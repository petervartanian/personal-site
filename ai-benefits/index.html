AI-B-001 · Two hikers located and guided to safety
AI-B-001Record link ↗

Two hikers located and guided to safety

Dead Horse Gap walking track, near Jindabyne, New South Wales, Australia

Read the original source ↗

Official operational account · 2026-06-25

Reported benefit
Both hikers were located and guided to safety.
AI’s contribution
The agency attributes initial identification in the bush to the drone's AI software; thermal imaging confirmed the find.
People’s contribution
The drone operator confirmed the find using thermal imagery and used a spotlight to guide Police and State Emergency Service rescuers. The hikers also signaled with a phone light.
Evidence and attribution limits

The report does not isolate AI's contribution from thermal imaging, the hikers' signal, or rescuers. It does not establish injuries or deaths avoided, or time saved.

No independent event verification or comparative causal evaluation is supplied.

Beneficiaries and context

The two hikers are the reported direct beneficiaries. Rescuers are contributing practitioners; a separate benefit to their workload or safety is not established.

System
Fire and Rescue NSW drone with AI detection software and thermal camera; model and vendor not identified.
Event date
Reported overdue June 23, 2026; exact rescue date unreported. Official account published June 25, 2026.
Benefit magnitude
Unassessed. No magnitude grade has been assigned.
Conditions for replication

Test whether trained operators, effective imaging, ground-team coordination, and clear confirmation procedures enable similar rescues.

These are hypotheses to investigate, not validated instructions.

Tradeoffs and missing evidence

No adverse effects are reported; costs, surveillance implications, and false detections are not evaluated.

Source checked 2026-10-19. Benefit labels are editorial classifications. The event has not been independently verified by this database.

AI-B-002 · Firefighters alerted before the first 911 call
AI-B-002Record link ↗

Firefighters alerted before the first 911 call

Near Grass Valley, California, United States

Read the original source ↗

First-party program account · 2023-10-24

Reported benefit
AI alerted at 5:19 a.m.; firefighters were on scene before the first 911 call at 6:01 a.m. The fire remained under a quarter acre.
AI’s contribution
Detected a possible ignition in the Wolf Mountain 1 camera feed and alerted firefighters.
People’s contribution
The program uses trained watchstanders to confirm detections. Firefighters responded and contained this fire.
Evidence and attribution limits

Earlier notification and containment are observed. A larger fire, specific losses, or casualties avoided are not established. The comparison is with the first 911 call, not every possible detection route.

The comparator is the first 911 call. No independent causal evaluation establishes response time without AI or losses avoided.

Beneficiaries and context

Earlier notification to firefighters is the reported operational benefit. Nearby residents are intended beneficiaries, but an individual or community welfare improvement is not measured.

System

Event date
September 11, 2023. Day and local alert time explicitly reported.
Benefit magnitude
Unassessed. No magnitude grade has been assigned.
Conditions for replication

Test whether camera coverage, human confirmation, reliable dispatch, and available crews produce earlier effective responses elsewhere.

These are hypotheses to investigate, not validated instructions.

Tradeoffs and missing evidence

No event-specific adverse effects are reported; false alarms, response costs, and surveillance effects are not evaluated.

Incidental lung cancer diagnosed and treated

Turkey/India multicenter study; individual center unreported

Peer-reviewed research · 2026-03-09

Reported benefit

Flagged a lung nodule on a chest X-ray obtained before anti-TNF treatment for ulcerative colitis.

Benefit scale

Four degrees of benefit, with zero as the reference point.

0 · No material change

Assessment finds no material change in the outcome being rated. Zero requires an assessment; missing evidence is not zero.

Unassessed is separate

Unknown magnitude is not zero. None of the current records has been graded.

Assess the depth of benefit for a specified beneficiary. Record reach, duration, and distribution separately.

Color accessibility

A modeled view, not a guarantee of individual perception. Numbers and labels remain the primary identifiers.

Proposed ordinal anchors. Increasingly negative numbers indicate greater benefit; they are not equal units. The number of levels and their definitions remain open to refinement.

Method

Inspectable sources and specific outcomes.

What belongs here

A real episode with a reported benefit, identifiable beneficiaries, an explicit AI role, and an accessible public source. Useful operational outcomes qualify, but are distinguished from downstream improvements to health or welfare.

How evidence is described

Research, operational accounts, and institutional patient accounts retain their source type. Checking a source does not independently verify an event. A reported benefit does not establish how much of it was caused by AI.

Who benefits

Beneficiary groups are informed by Trustible’s benefits framework: people, organizations, and society. Episode labels and assignments here are editorial judgments, not Trustible endorsements.

How benefits can recur

Each case identifies conditions worth investigating for replication over time and across settings. A selected collection cannot establish prevalence, comparative effectiveness, or a benefit’s long-term durability.
