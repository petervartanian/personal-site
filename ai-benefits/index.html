A scale for consequences

Benefits extend below zero; harms extend above it. A shared zero preserves the possibility of no material change. Select a grade to explore its meaning.

These are proposed reference grades. Assess depth for a stated beneficiary and outcome; document reach, duration, distribution, and evidence separately. The collection’s records are not yet graded.

Reading the scale and its colors

Zero requires an assessment; missing evidence does not count as zero. Record benefits and harms separately when an episode produces both. They cannot be canceled or added. A near miss is a separate event status.

Color becomes darker away from zero on each side. The palette is Fabio Crameri’s vik, sampled at nine fixed positions. Simulations use the Machado et al. model through Colorspacious. Color provides visual order; it does not validate the grade definitions or make welfare comparable. Palette attribution and license.

02 / Documentation

What earns a record?

A bounded episode, an identifiable outcome, an explicit AI role, and a source that can be inspected.

Suggest a case or correction ↗
Outcomes and protective responses
1 · Benefit created or restored
A reported improvement for an identifiable beneficiary, such as diagnosis, recovery, or rescue. AI’s causal contribution may remain uncertain.
2A · Specific harm prevented
Evidence links an intervention to stopping a particular harmful trajectory, with support for what would otherwise have happened. No initial record meets this threshold.
2B · Protective response
A response acted on a threat, but a particular loss avoided remains unproven.

These are outcome types, not magnitude grades. Studies, simulations, benchmarks, and broad deployment claims supply research context rather than individual event records.

Evidence and attribution

Each entry distinguishes the reported outcome from what AI and people contributed. Source review asks whether the source supports the description; it does not independently verify the event. Program and hospital accounts can be selective or promotional.

Diagnosis, treatment, and recovery are different outcomes. An early-stage diagnosis does not establish that AI saved a life. No reported harm does not establish the absence of harm. Event dates, publication dates, and uncertain dates remain distinct.

For context, the LungIMPACT trial found faster X-ray reporting without significantly faster lung cancer diagnosis. Individual episodes and comparative effectiveness answer different questions.

Depth, reach, and the long term

The proposed scale describes depth of consequence for a stated beneficiary and outcome. Reach, duration, distribution, reversibility, and longer-term consequences require their own evidence. A local benefit can be profound; a widespread effect can be small.

The four degrees on each side are provisional anchors for use and refinement. They are not an empirically established optimum. Raters should record their rationale and uncertainty, and preserve disagreement. Long-term benefit includes what can be created, restored, sustained, and repeated.

Learning how benefits can recur

Each record names conditions worth investigating: trained operators, human confirmation, accessible services, timely action, or institutional capacity. These are hypotheses, not validated instructions. The aim is to learn how beneficial outcomes might be replicated across settings and over time.

This selected collection cannot estimate prevalence, effectiveness, or net benefit. Such estimates need denominators, comparisons, costs, adverse outcomes, and distributional evidence. Related work includes Trustible’s AI benefits taxonomy and Hilliard, Kazim, and Ledain on benefits and risk perceptions.
