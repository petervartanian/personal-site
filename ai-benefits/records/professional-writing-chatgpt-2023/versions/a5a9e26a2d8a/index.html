Professional writing tasks took less time

Online experiment. January–February 2023.

In a randomized writing experiment, ChatGPT access reduced average task time by 40% and increased assessed output quality by 18%.

College-educated professionals doing the tested writing tasks, with larger quality gains among initially weaker performers.

Current recordDownload this version

Evidence

Randomized online experiment

Task
Drafting professional writing
Setting
Online recruitment through Prolific. Participant countries are unreported in the reviewed sources.
Compared with
Random assignment to ChatGPT signup and guidance between tasks, compared with an Overleaf signup control. The control group received no instruction to use ChatGPT, but some did.
Findings
453 college-educated professionals completed occupation-specific tasks. Assignment to ChatGPT occurred between two tasks. Three blinded professionals assessed each output.
Limits
Short tasks emphasized generic writing and did not require precise factual accuracy or organization-specific knowledge. About 10–20% of controls also used ChatGPT. This measures immediate task performance, not productivity across a whole job.
Over time
Immediate task outcomes. Later surveys tracked reported use after two weeks and two months, without measuring workplace productivity.
Other outcomes
Real work may require fact-checking, context gathering, and other effort absent from the experimental tasks. Effects on employment and pay were not measured.

Contributions

AI
Generated text in response to participants’ writing prompts.
People
Participants supplied prompts and chose what to submit. Most users submitted unedited or lightly edited AI output. Blinded professionals assessed the writing.
System
ChatGPTThe author manuscript identifies GPT-3.5 and the experiment dates. It does not give an exact model snapshot or ChatGPT release identifier.
Timing
Main experiment ran January 27–February 21, 2023.

Conditions

  • Participants worked on short assignments tailored to their occupations.
  • Treatment participants received ChatGPT signup instructions and example prompts.
  • Participants received quality-linked payments and output was graded by blinded professionals.

The measured gain should not be applied directly to research-heavy, fact-sensitive, or organization-specific writing, or to total job productivity.
