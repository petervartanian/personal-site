Historians reconstructed text more accurately

Ancient Greek inscriptions. Published March 2022.

On masked known text, historians’ top-choice reconstruction accuracy was 71.7% with Ithaca, compared with 25.3% unaided.

Participants: two graduate specialists in Greek epigraphy.

Current recordDownload this version

Evidence

Comparative research evaluation

Compared with
Known masked text was reconstructed by historians with and without Ithaca.
Findings
The evaluation removed 1–10 known characters, allowing reconstructions to be checked against the original. Results are reported in Nature, Table 1.
Limits
Two trained evaluators do not represent all historians. Accuracy on masked known text does not establish accuracy on genuinely unknown text. The paper’s authors include Ithaca’s developers, and the corpus reflects prior scholarship.
Over time
One evaluation. Lasting changes in scholarly productivity were not measured.
Other outcomes
Incorrect reconstructions and uneven corpus coverage are considerations for future use. This record does not estimate their downstream effects.

Contributions

AI
Provided 20 ranked reconstruction hypotheses.
People
Interpreted alternatives using historical knowledge and searchable textual parallels.
System
IthacaThe paper names Ithaca and describes its transformer architecture. JAX 0.2.9, Flax 0.3.0, and Haiku 0.0.4 are development-library versions. No Ithaca release or checkpoint identifier is reported.
Timing
Evaluation dates unreported.

Conditions

Digitized corpora, expert interpretation, and contextual checks are candidate conditions to test.
