What the numbers establish

Counts and shares describe incident records in one export. Counts of distinct events, reports, people, or exposed systems require separate verification. Primary tags count each record once. Additional tags can overlap. The calculations are reproducible, while the underlying labels still require independent validation.

How the shortlist was selected

The five starting examples were retained as an audited baseline. A search across 424 comparisons selected six additional candidates based on support, effect size, stability, and chart fit. A small-base emergence rule admitted the false/misleading-information finding. Published topics were routed to the claim audit to avoid presenting them as new discoveries.

Two further descriptive profiles cover the potential-causes diagram and national-security radar. The same comparison threshold applies. The shortlist identifies patterns for further review. Statistical significance was outside the selection criteria. The ledger records every selection decision. The selected set stayed the same when any individual weight moved by 0.1.

The model fits the four log completion rates, log(January count ÷ September count) ÷ elapsed days. It scales them to the remaining time and uses a Student-t predictive interval with three degrees of freedom, including uncertainty in the fitted average. The lower bound cannot fall below the current recorded count. Chart labels round the central 50% bounds to the nearest ten; the wider 90% bounds round outward to tens. The shaded band and downloadable evidence retain the unrounded bounds.

Reading the band

The solid daily curve, including 2026, counts the preceding 12 months. At December 31 that window equals the calendar-year total. The shaded segment connects the last observed point to the year-end interval. Only the year-end bounds are estimated. The observed 2026 calendar-year count remains separate. Category charts retain their timing-scenario ranges; they have no assigned probability and no adjustment for reporting delays.

Model limitations

The model transfers AIID reporting patterns to the Arcola export. It cannot anticipate changes in collection coverage, removals or reclassification, or incidents that never enter the database. It applies from September through December and recalculates from the new count and date. The training snapshots are bundled for reproducibility and must be refreshed as additional completed years become available.

The calendar-pace calculation, 231 × 365 ÷ 270 ≈ 312, remains in the evidence as a baseline. Separate editorial batches identify 74 records dated 2025 added during February–July 2026; this later backfill is outside the January forecast target.

Limits that apply to all candidates

  • Reporting and coverage
    • Voluntary reporting, uneven public attention, source detail, and changing coverage make the dataset unrepresentative.
    • There is no exposure denominator for estimating population risk.
  • Dates and comparisons
    • The daily curve continues through the 2026 observations. At each December 31, its 12-month total equals the annual count. A shaded segment connects the latest point to the year-end range.
    • Compare counts over equivalent time windows. Partial-year shares use the same window for numerator and denominator. Seasonality, reporting delays, and classification changes still affect comparisons.
    • Category-chart ranges show sensitivity to historical timing assumptions. They make no adjustment for reporting delays.
    • Incident dates may reflect legal, report, or publication events. One export cannot recover earlier classifications or entry dates.
    • Starting at a local peak or trough and selecting a small baseline affect which patterns appear.
  • Classification accuracy
    • The reviewed materials contain no representative held-out accuracy evaluation of the current pipeline. Model agreement and self-reported confidence require checking against independent reference judgments.
    • Taxonomy choices affect the patterns. Sensitivity checks show how results change under alternative assumptions. Reporting bias remains.
  • Severity
    • Direct and indirect scores classify reported harm. Inferred scores describe harm that the model imputes. Confirming that harm occurred requires source evidence.
    • Dimensions, thresholds, and zero handling must be specified.
