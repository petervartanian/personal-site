False or misleading information dominates the primary misinformation tags. Original source fields follow; the linked review contains the current assessment.

Caveats

  • Voluntary reporting and changing public attention shape this collection. It lacks a representative sample and a measure of population exposure, so it cannot establish prevalence.
  • Incident dates can come from legal events, reports, or publications. The export omits report dates, preventing estimates of reporting delays. ID order permits a conditional comparison, but establishing entry dates and historical backfill requires earlier exports or entry timestamps.
  • Tags can change with the model or prompt. Model agreement and self-reported confidence leave classification accuracy unresolved. Nearly every record has 'High' confidence, so that field provides little help in distinguishing reliable classifications.
  • The export omits source report URLs, dates, and IDs. The calculations have been checked against the export. Verifying the underlying allegations requires the source reports.
  • Selected by the data-driven discovery stage (score 0.930: effect 0.82, support 1.00, stability 1.00, chart_fit 0.90). Scores rank descriptive stability and chart usefulness; they are not significance tests.
  • The concentration also depends on the classifier’s use of the taxonomy. Some subdomains are rarely assigned.
