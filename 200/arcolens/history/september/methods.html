What the numbers establish

Counts and shares describe incident records in one export. The unit is not necessarily a distinct event, report, person, or exposed system. Primary tags count each record once; additional tags can overlap. The arithmetic can be reproduced without establishing the truth of every underlying label.

How the shortlist was selected

The five starting examples were retained as an audited baseline. A declared search across 424 comparisons selected six additional candidates using support, effect size, stability, and chart fit. A small-base emergence rule admitted the false/misleading-information finding. Published topics were routed to the claim audit to avoid presenting them as new discoveries.

Two further descriptive profiles cover the potential-causes diagram and national-security radar without relaxing the comparison threshold. The selection is exploratory, not a statistical-significance ranking. The full ledger records screened-out, merged, withheld, and selected comparisons. Weight-sensitivity checks did not change the selected set when an individual weight moved by 0.1.

Projecting 2026

The central estimate extends the current daily pace through December 31: 231 × 365 ÷ 270 ≈ 312 records. September 27 is day 270 of 2026 and is used as the cutoff because it is the snapshot date. This does not establish that all incidents through that date have been reported.

  • What is assumed
    • The average daily rate and category mix observed so far continue through the rest of 2026. Category counts and the total are scaled by the same factor, so the central projected shares equal the observed partial-year shares.
    • A projection may remain below 2025. Extending the earlier growth curve would ignore the 2026 records already available.
  • What the whiskers show
    • For each of 2021–2025, count records dated through September 27 and through December 31. Apply that year’s full-year-to-September multiplier to the current count. For shares, apply the same historical year’s category and total multipliers together.
    • The range spans the current-pace estimate and all five historical timing scenarios. It is a sensitivity range, not an 80% or 95% prediction interval. The eventual result can fall outside it.
  • Confidence and limitations
    • Low confidence. The central estimate does not adjust for reporting delays, backfill, changes in coverage, or changes in classification. Historical scenarios explore timing variation but cannot recover what earlier exports contained.
    • Five retrospective years do not establish stable seasonality or calibrate forecast uncertainty. Small category counts can produce wide scenario ranges.
    • These projections are exploratory supplements. Observed evidence and the reviewed publication wording retain their original values and date windows.

Projection calculations and contributing record IDs. Forecast uncertainty and probability intervals are discussed in Forecasting: Principles and Practice.

Limits that apply to all candidates

  • Reporting and coverage
    • Voluntary reporting, uneven public attention, source detail, and changing coverage make the dataset unrepresentative.
    • There is no exposure denominator for estimating population risk.
  • Dates and comparisons
    • Solid trend lines end at 2025. Dashed segments extend to a conditional full-year 2026 projection; the actual partial-year figures appear separately.
    • A partial-year count is not comparable with a full-year total. A partial-year share uses the same time window for numerator and denominator, but seasonality, reporting delays, and classification changes still limit comparisons.
    • 2026 projections extrapolate current record counts. They do not estimate all real-world incidents or resolve reporting bias.
    • Incident dates may reflect legal, report, or publication events. One export cannot recover earlier classifications or entry dates.
    • Starting at a local peak or trough and selecting a small baseline affect which patterns appear.
  • Classification accuracy
    • The current classification pipeline lacks a located representative held-out accuracy evaluation. Model agreement and self-reported confidence are not ground truth.
    • Taxonomy choices affect the patterns. Sensitivity checks inform interpretation; they do not remove reporting bias.
  • Severity
    • Direct, indirect, and inferred severity answer different questions. Inferred harm is not confirmation that harm occurred.
    • Dimensions, thresholds, and zero handling must be specified.
