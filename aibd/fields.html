AI Benefits Database / Method

Fields and evidence

What happened, for whom, with which system, and on what evidence.

Every case addresses the same 96 field groups: 91 public groups and five internal editorial groups. Six collection-wide definitions describe how the collection is assembled and maintained. These are structured questions, each of which may contain several observations.

Required coverage means that every question has a place. It does not mean that every answer is known. A case remains available while its open questions are assessed.

Seven ways to state what is known

Recorded value
A value is recorded with source, derivation, or curatorial provenance. This status alone does not certify truth or causality.
Not reported
The identified sources were checked for this question and do not report the answer. Record the source scope and review date.
Unresolved
Available reviewed evidence does not settle the answer. Preserve the competing possibilities and their evidence.
Not assessed
The question remains unreviewed. It remains a gap and cannot satisfy completed curation.
Not applicable
The question has no meaningful referent for the case or related object. Record the scope reason. Source silence is insufficient.
Withheld
A value is known internally but is restricted in the public view. Preserve authorized internal access and disclosure rules.
Source unavailable
A relevant identified source could not be inspected. Record the source, limitation, access-attempt date, and follow-up status.

A value, its evidence, its review status, and the realization of the benefit are separate. A recorded claim does not establish that the outcome occurred or that AI caused it. Unassessed questions cannot count toward completed curation.

The public case fields

Case identity and dates 10

Stable AIBD identifier
Preserve the existing identifier and URL through corrections.
Kind of case
Individual experience, deployment, program/activity, evaluation/study, or another defined unit. Link components of the same project.
Related cases and counting relationship
Link the same deployment, evaluation, replication, duplicate, or incident. Do not count additional articles as additional benefits.
Title
Name the activity and claimed or observed benefit plainly.
Short account
State what happened, for whom, and the main qualification.
Sector and application domain
Support sector and task filtering without forcing one exclusive category.
Intended purpose
Distinguish the deployment's purpose from a benefit that emerged unexpectedly.
Dates and precision
Separate occurrence start/end, first publication, database discovery, added date, and last checked date. Allow unknown dates and day/month/year precision.
Stage of use
Proposed, simulated, laboratory test, pilot, routine use, scaling, retired, or unknown. This is separate from benefit evidence.
Record version
Identify the exact published version that a user cited.

AI system and use 12

AI product or system
Identify the application actually used, including an in-house system. A vendor name alone is insufficient.
Underlying model
Record the model behind the application when known. Allow several components with different roles.
Exact model version
Preserve the observed version, checkpoint, release, or snapshot. Do not infer it from the latest product version.
Model or system provider
Distinguish the model provider from the application developer and organization operating it.
Role in the system
Identify generation, retrieval, classification, planning, verification, perception, or another component function.
Actual configuration
Application/interface version, wrapper, retrieval, fine-tuning, tools, inference budget, agent count/coordination, and weight/API access when they materially affect the result.
Inputs and outputs
Describe data types, languages, provenance, and outputs relevant to the task, subject to access restrictions.
Autonomy and permissions
Record what the AI could decide or execute, tool permissions, and escalation or approval boundaries.
Human and organizational workflow
Explain who used the AI, what they did before and after it, and which decisions remained human.
Human review and safeguards
Review steps, responsible roles, override options, training, and controls in the actual deployment.
How the benefit relates to AI
Direct AI use, AI-related education, AI-related convening, institutional activity, another identified relationship, or unresolved. Record the actual route to benefit.
AI contribution and alternative explanation
Distinguish AI's contribution from human work, ordinary digitization, or other simultaneous changes. The contribution may be unassessed.

Organizations and geography 6

Actors and roles
Identify developer, model provider, deployer, operator, evaluator, funder, and beneficiaries where relevant. A single actor can hold several roles.
Places with defined roles
Separate development, compute/hosting, physical site, deployment, beneficiary location, actor headquarters, and applicable jurisdiction. Include place resolution and source.
Ownership or effective control
Record ownership or control only where documented and relevant. Headquarters does not establish nationality, state control, or allegiance.
Physical and digital dependencies
Link hosting/compute, chips, connectivity, energy, water, suppliers, data access, and locations when material to use or replication.
Cross-border relationships
Record sourced flows of data, compute, services, funding, or value, with origin, destination, and relationship type.
Geopolitical interpretation
Specify the country or institution whose interests are being assessed, security/economic domain, assessed capability change, rationale, confidence, sources, and date.

Benefits and beneficiaries 11

Outcome identifier
Give each distinct stakeholder-specific outcome a stable identifier within the case.
Benefit statement
State the particular claimed or observed change. Retain multiple outcomes without duplicating the deployment count.
Benefit category
Use a versioned benefit category and preserve source wording where it differs.
Outcome status
Intended, observed, mixed, not demonstrated, reversed, or unclear. Distinguish status from confidence and deployment stage.
Beneficiary class
Personal, organizational, or societal. Apply the existing shape grammar to the relevant outcome.
Specific beneficiaries
Describe the people or organizations that benefit, rather than stopping at an abstract class.
Beneficiary count and reach
Count actually benefiting, merely exposed, eligible, and intended populations separately. Preserve unit, period, estimation basis, and overlap limits.
Distribution and access
Record relevant differences by subgroup, geography, income, language, disability, or access conditions when evidence supports them.
Duration and persistence
Distinguish a one-time result from recurring or sustained benefit and state the observation horizon.
Expected or unexpected
Expected, unexpected, partly unexpected, or unknown. Do not infer intention from the presence of a positive outcome.
Costs, burdens, and harms to stakeholders
Record costs or harms to the same beneficiaries and to other parties, with timing and evidence. Link relevant incident records where available.

Measurement and comparison 11

Evaluation identifier
Identify an evaluation independently of its reports and the outcomes that it measures.
Evaluation design
Randomized, quasi-experimental, controlled observational, before/after, qualitative evaluation, case study, simulation, testimonial, forecast, or other. Record the actual design.
Measurement method
Instrument, administrative data, observation, interview, survey, model estimate, or another method. Preserve qualitative findings without forcing a number.
Setting and sample
Population, eligibility, sample size, sites, recruitment, allocation, and study period where reported.
Measured outcome
Metric name, exact operational definition, value, unit, numerator, denominator, and period. Retain source wording and distinguish proxy from ultimate outcome.
Comparator and baseline
No AI, prior workflow, human-only work, another AI system, another intervention, or no comparator. Record the exact baseline and comparison period.
Difference from the comparator
Absolute and relative effects with their units, uncertainty interval, calculation, and source. Do not combine incompatible measures.
Increment attributable to AI
Assess changes in cost, time, quality, scale, access, or capability relative to the alternative. Separate estimated contribution from total observed improvement.
Uncertainty and limitations
Record sampling uncertainty, confounding, missing observations, selection, failed comparisons, and bounds on causal interpretation.
Exposure denominator
Population at risk or eligible to benefit, uses, agent-hours, task-hours, transactions, distance, or another matched denominator, with source and period.
Reproduction materials
Protocols, logs, prompts, action traces, code, data, and evaluation artifacts with model/configuration and permitted use.

Monetary value and costs 11

Kind of monetary value
Cash saving, labor capacity freed, revenue, profit, recovered money, avoided loss, recipient transfer, willingness to pay, or another stated valuation. Preserve these distinctions.
Amount
Store the numerical figure with its original quoted amount. Distinguish an outcome level from an incremental gain. Zero differs from unknown.
Currency and price basis
Original currency, price year when relevant, nominal/real basis, and any documented conversion rate/date. Retain the original amount after conversion.
Period and unit
One-time or recurring, period covered, per-user/per-task/per-organization/total basis, and population or volume to which the estimate applies.
Realization and estimation status
Observed cash flow, measured resource saving, estimated monetary value, forecast, or target. A time saving is not automatically a cash saving.
Who benefits and who pays
Identify the recipient of value and each cost bearer. Separate organizational savings from worker or societal effects.
Gross or net
Gross, net, or unspecified. State which cost categories the estimate includes or excludes.
Cost components
Purchase, integration, training, infrastructure, operation, maintenance, human review, compliance, transition, and other documented costs, with time basis.
Economic comparator
Specify the alternative against which costs or gains are measured. Preserve differences in quality, volume, and time horizon.
Calculation and assumptions
Show the source's method, rates, unit conversions, discount rate if applicable, uncertainty, and sensitivity. Label any AIBD calculation explicitly.
Return or payback
Retain a source-reported ROI or payback period, or a transparent calculation with matched inputs. State numerator, denominator, and horizon.

Sources and claim evidence 10

Source identifier
Identify each report or evidence artifact separately from the case.
Citation and exact locator
Title, author/publisher, URL or persistent identifier, publication date, page/paragraph/table, and a short supporting excerpt when permitted.
Source language and translation
Original language, translation status/method, and original wording needed to verify a consequential claim.
Source relationship and interests
Maker, deployer, beneficiary, evaluator, independent reporter, funder, other, or unclear relationship. Unknown does not default to independent. Record disclosed funding/conflicts separately from research design.
Shared evidence lineage
Identify reports that repeat the same press release, study, witness, or dataset. Several articles may constitute one evidentiary origin.
Access and source version
Access date, source version, archive/persistent link when permitted, corrections or retractions, and availability limits.
Precisely supported claim
Tie evidence to the exact factual assertion, outcome, actor attribution, or AI-contribution claim. Publicly identify source statements, analyst inferences, and generated summaries.
Supporting and contradicting evidence
Link the exact passage and distinguish support, contradiction, and contextual evidence.
Evidence assessment
Assess occurrence, outcome, AI contribution, and actor attribution separately where relevant, with reasons and an explicit unassessed state.
Missing information
Distinguish not reported, unknown after review, not assessed, not applicable, withheld, and disputed. Do not substitute zero or false.

Ratings and interpretations 8

Assessment target
Identify the exact benefit and beneficiary to which the rating applies.
Depth of benefit
Keep the current depth scale and color mapping. Money, beneficiary count, confidence, and strategic significance remain separate dimensions.
Rating rationale
Explain why the observed or intended benefit fits the stated depth for that stakeholder.
Observed or intended basis
State whether the depth describes a realized, intended, projected, or unclear outcome. Assess support separately: a claim can concern a realized result. Keep unrealized benefits eligible.
Rubric and taxonomy version
Identify the definitions and rules under which the assessment was made.
Assessor and assessment date
Store who assessed the claim and when. Use a public role or pseudonym where disclosure requires it.
Disagreement and alternatives
Preserve materially different ratings, rationales, and unresolved questions rather than averaging them away.
Confidence shown by fill
Support for the beneficial outcome and its stated relationship to AI. Certainty that someone made a claim or that a benefit failed is separate. Retain unassessed. Do not infer confidence from publisher prestige alone.

Replication, follow-up, and decisions 8

Conditions needed to repeat the benefit
Skills, data, language, infrastructure, workflows, controls, and contextual prerequisites that evidence identifies.
Attempts to reproduce the result
Setting, population, model/version, protocol differences, observed result, source, and independent/same-team relationship. Include failed attempts.
Subsequent outcomes
Track persisted, scaled, mixed, reversed, withdrawn, or unknown outcomes and relevant changes in model or context.
Access and availability
Whether and how the system/activity can be accessed, procurement or license conditions, cost, geographic/language availability, and as-of date.
Institution and decision
Identify who was deciding whether to adopt, fund, scale, regulate, or stop a use, and the task that evidence served.
Decision grounds and alternatives
Relevant mandate, jurisdiction, threshold, evidence considered, alternatives, reasons to act or not act, and constraints.
Action and observed consequence
Record what was done, who owned it, when, and what later evidence showed. Distinguish intended response from tested effectiveness.
What would show success
Preserve the measurable question that would establish whether the adoption or intervention worked, with unanswered follow-up questions.

Interoperability and publication 4

Other databases and identifiers
Link original collection entries, risk/incident records, studies, registries, and standards without implying that they are equivalent records.
Versioned category mappings
Record exact, broader, narrower, partial, or no-match mappings with rationale and versions. Retain disagreement.
Citable case brief
Present the main result, comparison, beneficiary, material limitations, and source links. Preserve qualifications in export.
Field-level changes
Changed field, old/new values or recoverable versions, date, source, editor, and correction reason.

When a question depends on another record

Evaluations, monetary observations, strategic assessments, and institutional decisions can each have multiple records. Their fields are required when those records exist. A documented family assessment can establish that none applies. An unreviewed family remains open.

The collection

Collection scope
Define countries, languages, sectors, dates, source classes, and inclusion rules actually searched.
How candidates were found
Queries, languages, channels, sampling or ranking rules, referrals, search dates, and updates. Separate submitted from actively sought material.
Counting unit and duplicate rules
State whether a count describes cases, deployments, outcomes, sources, evaluations, or beneficiaries and explain matching/overlap.
Coverage limits
Explain selective publicity, language/geography gaps, unavailable private data, and source bias beside aggregate comparisons.
Collection and review cadence
Distinguish source collection frequency from review backlog and record freshness.
Population and exposure context
Store matched denominators and their collection rules where available. A benefits collection alone cannot establish a success rate.

The five internal editorial groups cover publication terms, review status, information gaps, inclusion decisions, and capture quality. Their contents are excluded from public downloads.
