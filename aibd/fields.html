AI Benefits Database / Method

Fields and evidence

What each record establishes.

Review symbols

Fully reviewed
The field’s review is complete.
Partly reviewed
The review addresses part of the question.
Awaiting review
Information has been recorded and awaits review.
Not reviewed
No field review is recorded.

A complete review can conclude that the sources do not report an answer or that a question remains unresolved.

Qualifications to an answer

These symbols describe the answer or its sources, separately from the extent of review. An answer marked Inferred can also be Unresolved when the evidence supports a likely answer without settling the question.

Inferred
The answer is a reasoned judgment, with its basis explained in a note.
Unresolved
The available evidence does not settle the question.
Not reported
The inspected sources do not supply this information.
Source unavailable
The source could not be inspected.
Not applicable
The question does not apply to this record.
Withheld
The information is intentionally omitted from the public record.

Seven ways to state what is known

Recorded value
A value is recorded with source, derivation, or curatorial provenance. This status alone does not certify truth or causality.
Not reported
The identified sources were checked for this question and do not report the answer. Record the source scope and review date.
Unresolved
The available information does not settle the answer. Identify what remains unknown and the evidence needed. Review status is recorded separately.
Not assessed
The question remains unreviewed. It remains a gap and cannot satisfy completed curation.
Not applicable
The question has no meaningful referent for the case or related object. Record the scope reason. Source silence is insufficient.
Withheld
A value is known internally but is restricted in the public view. Preserve authorized internal access and disclosure rules.
Source unavailable
A relevant identified source could not be inspected. Record the source, limitation, access-attempt date, and follow-up status.

The public record fields

Record identity and dates 10

Stable AIBD identifier
Each record keeps one identifier and one web address. Both stay the same when the record is corrected, so citations continue to work.
What the record describes
This field shows what unit the record describes: an individual experience, a deployment, a program or activity, an evaluation or study, or another defined unit. Records that form parts of the same project are linked to each other.
Related records and counting relationship
Links show which other records concern the same deployment, evaluation, replication, duplicate, or incident. Further articles about the same event do not add to the count of benefits.
Title
The title names the activity and the benefit that was claimed or observed, in plain words.
Short account
The short account covers what happened and for whom. It also gives the main qualification that applies to the record.
Sector and application domain
Sector and application domain show the field in which the AI was used. A record can belong to more than one sector. Readers can filter records by sector and by task.
Intended purpose
The intended purpose is what the deployment was meant to do. It is recorded apart from any benefit that emerged unexpectedly.
Dates and precision
Separate dates mark when the events began and ended, when they were first published, when the record was found for the database, when it was added, and when it was last checked. Each date carries its precision to the day, month, or year, and any date can be unknown.
Stage of use
The stage of use shows how far the use of AI had gone: proposed, simulated, tested in a laboratory, piloted, in routine use, scaling, retired, or unknown. The stage is separate from the strength of the evidence for a benefit.
Record version
The record version identifies the exact published version of the record that a reader cited.

AI system and use 12

AI product or system
This field names the AI application that was used, including systems built in-house. A vendor name on its own does not identify the application.
Underlying model
The underlying model is the model behind the application, when it is known. A record can list several components, each with its own role.
Exact model version
Here the record gives the version, checkpoint, release, or snapshot of the AI model that was used in this record. A product name, a device generation, a publication revision, or the current commit in a code repository does not establish that version. The hardware model appears with the configuration that was deployed. A revision of a paper appears with the access and source version.
Model or system provider
The provider is the organization that supplied the model or system. It is recorded apart from the developer of the application and from the organization that operated it.
Role in the system
Each component has a role in the system: generation, retrieval, classification, planning, verification, perception, or another function.
Actual configuration
The configuration records the hardware model and the application or interface version apart from the AI model version. It also covers wrappers, retrieval, fine-tuning, tools, inference budget, coordination between agents, and access to weights or an API when these materially affect the result. A configuration published for reproducibility is marked apart from a verified snapshot of the deployment.
Inputs and outputs
Inputs and outputs describe the data that went into the system and what came out: data types, languages, provenance, and outputs that matter for the task. Some details are withheld when access is restricted.
Autonomy and permissions
This field shows what the AI could decide or carry out on its own and which tools it could use. It also shows the points at which escalation or approval was required.
Human and organizational workflow
The workflow describes who used the AI and what they did before and after using it. It also shows which decisions stayed with people.
Human review and safeguards
Safeguards in the deployment appear here: review steps, the roles responsible for them, options to override the AI, training, and other controls.
How the benefit relates to AI
This field shows the route by which the benefit relates to AI: direct use of AI, education about AI, meetings convened around AI, institutional activity, another identified relationship, or an unresolved relationship.
AI contribution and alternative explanation
The contribution of AI is kept apart from human work, ordinary digitization, and other changes that happened at the same time. The contribution can be marked as unassessed.

Organizations and geography 6

Actors and roles
Actors are the organizations and people involved, each with a role such as developer, model provider, deployer, operator, evaluator, funder, or beneficiary. One actor can hold several roles.
Places with defined roles
Each place carries a defined role: where the system was developed, where computing or hosting took place, the physical site, where it was deployed, where beneficiaries are located, where an actor has its headquarters, or which jurisdiction applies. Every place shows its level of detail and its source.
Ownership or effective control
Ownership or effective control appears only where it is documented and relevant. The location of a headquarters does not establish nationality, state control, or allegiance.
Physical and digital dependencies
This field links the physical and digital resources that use or replication depended on, such as hosting and computing, chips, connectivity, energy, water, suppliers, data access, and locations. Only dependencies that matter to use or replication are listed.
Cross-border relationships
Sourced flows of data, computing, services, funding, or value across borders are recorded here. Each flow shows its origin, its destination, and the type of relationship.
Geopolitical interpretation
A geopolitical interpretation names the country or institution whose interests are assessed and the security or economic domain in question. It also gives the assessed change in capability, the rationale, the confidence, the sources, and the date.

Benefits and beneficiaries 11

Outcome identifier
Each distinct outcome for a particular stakeholder has its own identifier, which stays stable within the record.
Benefit statement
The benefit statement gives the specific change that was claimed or observed. A record can hold several outcomes. The deployment is still counted once.
Benefit category
The benefit category places the benefit in a versioned set of categories. Where the source describes the benefit in different words, the wording of the source is kept.
Outcome status
Outcome status shows whether a benefit was intended, observed, mixed, not demonstrated, reversed, or unclear. Status is separate from confidence and from the stage of use.
Beneficiary class
The beneficiary class shows whether an outcome is personal, organizational, or societal. The class also sets the shape of the mark for that outcome.
Specific beneficiaries
Specific beneficiaries are the people or organizations that benefit, named in more detail than the broad class.
Beneficiary count and reach
The count keeps people who benefited, people who were merely exposed, eligible populations, and intended populations separate. Each figure carries its unit, its period, the basis of the estimate, and any limits on overlap between groups.
Distribution and access
Distribution shows how a benefit differed by subgroup, geography, income, language, disability, or conditions of access, when evidence supports those differences.
Duration and persistence
Duration shows whether a benefit was a one-time result or a recurring or sustained one. It also gives the period over which the outcome was observed.
Expected or unexpected
This field marks whether an outcome was expected, unexpected, partly unexpected, or unknown. A positive outcome is not taken as evidence that someone intended it.
Costs, burdens, and harms to stakeholders
Costs and harms to the same beneficiaries and to other parties are recorded here, with their timing and evidence. Related incident records are linked where they exist.

Measurement and comparison 11

Evaluation identifier
Each evaluation has its own identifier, separate from the reports that describe it and the outcomes that it measures.
Evaluation design
The evaluation design is the kind of study that was carried out: randomized, quasi-experimental, controlled observational, before and after, qualitative, case study, simulation, testimonial, forecast, or other.
Measurement method
The measurement method shows how the outcome was measured, for example by instrument, administrative data, observation, interview, survey, or model estimate. Qualitative findings are kept as they were reported, without being turned into numbers.
Setting and sample
Setting and sample describe who was studied and where: the population, eligibility, sample size, sites, recruitment, allocation, and study period, where the source reports them.
Measured outcome
The measured outcome gives the name of the metric, its exact operational definition, value, unit, numerator, denominator, and period, in the wording of the source. It also shows whether the metric is a proxy or the outcome that ultimately matters.
Comparator and baseline
The comparator is what the AI use was measured against: no AI, the prior workflow, human-only work, another AI system, another intervention, or no comparator. The exact baseline and the comparison period are recorded with it.
Difference from the comparator
The difference from the comparator appears in absolute and relative terms, each with its unit, uncertainty interval, calculation, and source. Measures that cannot be compared are kept apart.
Increment attributable to AI
This field shows how much of a change in cost, time, quality, scale, access, or capability can be attributed to AI, relative to the alternative. The estimated contribution of AI is kept separate from the total improvement that was observed.
Uncertainty and limitations
Limits on the findings are recorded here: sampling uncertainty, confounding, missing observations, selection, failed comparisons, and the bounds on any causal reading.
Exposure denominator
The exposure denominator is the matched base that a result is measured against, such as the population at risk or eligible to benefit, uses, agent-hours, task-hours, transactions, or distance. Each denominator shows its source and period.
Reproduction materials
Reproduction materials are the protocols, logs, prompts, action traces, code, data, and evaluation artifacts that would let someone repeat the evaluation. Each item notes the model and configuration that it applies to and the terms of permitted use.

Monetary value and costs 11

Kind of monetary value
This field shows what kind of monetary value a figure represents: cash saving, labor capacity freed, revenue, profit, recovered money, avoided loss, transfer to recipients, willingness to pay, or another stated valuation. These kinds are kept distinct.
Amount
The amount gives the figure as a number alongside the amount as the source quoted it. It also shows whether the figure is an outcome level or an incremental gain. A recorded zero means something different from an unknown amount.
Currency and price basis
Currency and price basis give the original currency, the price year where it matters, and whether the figure is nominal or real. Any conversion shows its documented rate and date, and the original amount stays visible after conversion.
Period and unit
Period and unit show whether a value is one-time or recurring and the period that it covers. They also show whether it applies per user, per task, per organization, or in total, and the population or volume that it covers.
Realization and estimation status
Realization status shows whether a value is an observed cash flow, a measured saving of resources, an estimated monetary value, a forecast, or a target. A saving of time does not automatically count as a saving of cash.
Who benefits and who pays
This field identifies who receives the value and who bears each cost. Savings to an organization are kept apart from effects on workers or on society.
Gross or net
A monetary figure is marked as gross, net, or unspecified. The record also shows which categories of cost the estimate includes or leaves out.
Cost components
Cost components are the documented costs, such as purchase, integration, training, infrastructure, operation, maintenance, human review, compliance, and transition, each with its time basis.
Economic comparator
The economic comparator is the alternative against which costs or gains are measured. Differences in quality, volume, and time horizon between the two are kept in view.
Calculation and assumptions
Calculation and assumptions show how a value was calculated in the source: its method, rates, unit conversions, discount rate where one applies, uncertainty, and sensitivity. Any calculation added by the editors of the database is labeled as such.
Return or payback
A return on investment or a payback period appears either as the source reported it or as a transparent calculation from matched inputs. The numerator, the denominator, and the time horizon are shown with it.

Sources and claim evidence 10

Source identifier
Each report or other piece of evidence has its own identifier, separate from the identifier of the record.
Citation and exact locator
The citation gives the title, author or publisher, web address or persistent identifier, and publication date. It also points to the exact page, paragraph, or table, with a short supporting excerpt when permission allows.
Source language and translation
This field gives the original language of the source and whether and how it was translated. The original wording is kept where it is needed to check an important claim.
Source relationship and interests
The source relationship shows how a source relates to the record: maker, deployer, beneficiary, evaluator, independent reporter, funder, other, or unclear. An unknown relationship is never treated as independent. Disclosed funding or conflicts appear apart from the research design.
Shared evidence lineage
Shared lineage traces reports that repeat the same press release, study, witness, or dataset. Several articles can share one origin and count as one piece of evidence.
Access and source version
Access and source version record when a source was accessed and which version was used, with an archived or persistent link where permitted. Corrections, retractions, and limits on availability are noted as well.
Precisely supported claim
Each claim ties evidence to one exact factual assertion, outcome, attribution to an actor, or claim about the contribution of AI. The record shows publicly whether a statement comes from a source, from an inference by an analyst, or from a generated summary.
Supporting and contradicting evidence
Links point to the exact passage behind a claim. Each link shows whether the passage supports the claim, contradicts it, or gives context.
Evidence assessment
The evidence for occurrence, outcome, AI contribution, and attribution to an actor is assessed separately where each is relevant, and each assessment comes with reasons. A claim that has not been assessed is marked as unassessed.
Missing information
This field shows why information is missing: not reported, unknown after review, not assessed, not applicable, withheld, or disputed. Missing values never appear as zero or false.

Ratings and interpretations 8

Assessment target
The assessment target is the exact benefit and beneficiary that a rating applies to.
Depth of benefit
Depth places a benefit on the depth scale of the database. Each level of the scale has its own color. Money, number of beneficiaries, confidence, and strategic significance are separate dimensions.
Rating rationale
The rationale gives the reasons that the observed or intended benefit fits the stated depth for that stakeholder.
Observed or intended basis
This field shows whether the depth describes a realized, intended, projected, or unclear outcome. Support for the claim is assessed separately. A claim that concerns a realized result still needs its own assessment of support. Benefits that have not yet been realized remain eligible for the database.
Rubric and taxonomy version
The rubric and taxonomy version identifies the definitions and rules under which an assessment was made.
Assessor and assessment date
This field records who assessed a claim and when. A public role or a pseudonym appears where the rules on disclosure require one.
Disagreement and alternatives
Ratings, rationales, and open questions that differ materially are all kept. They are never averaged into a single rating.
Confidence shown by fill
The fill of a mark shows how well evidence supports the beneficial outcome and its stated relationship to AI. The fill can remain unassessed. Certainty that someone made a claim or that a benefit failed is a separate matter. The prestige of a publisher alone is not a basis for confidence.

Replication, follow-up, and decisions 8

Conditions needed to repeat the benefit
Conditions for repeating the benefit are the prerequisites that the evidence points to, such as skills, data, language, infrastructure, workflows, controls, and context.
Attempts to reproduce the result
Each attempt to reproduce the result records the setting, the population, the model and version, differences in protocol, the result, and the source. It also shows whether an independent team or the same team made the attempt, and failed attempts are included.
Subsequent outcomes
Subsequent outcomes show what happened later: whether the benefit persisted, scaled, turned mixed, reversed, was withdrawn, or is unknown. Relevant changes in the model or the context are noted with them.
Access and availability
Availability shows whether and how the system or activity can be accessed, under what procurement or license conditions, at what cost, and in which places and languages. Each entry carries the date on which it was true.
Institution and decision
This field names who was deciding whether to adopt, fund, scale, regulate, or stop a use of AI. It also names the task that the evidence served in that decision.
Decision grounds and alternatives
Decision grounds record the basis for a decision: the relevant mandate, jurisdiction, threshold, evidence considered, alternatives, reasons to act or not to act, and constraints.
Action and observed consequence
This field records what was done, who was responsible, when it happened, and what later evidence showed. An intended response is kept apart from one whose effect has been tested.
What would show success
The success criterion is the measurable question that would show whether the adoption or intervention worked. Follow-up questions that remain unanswered appear with it.

Interoperability and publication 4

Other databases and identifiers
Links to other databases point to the entries in the collections where the record first appeared, to risk and incident records, and to studies, registries, and standards. A link does not mean that the other record is equivalent to this one.
Versioned category mappings
Category mappings connect the categories of a record to other taxonomies as exact, broader, narrower, partial, or no match, each with a rationale and the versions involved. Disagreements about a mapping are kept.
Citable summary
The citable brief presents the main result, the comparison, the beneficiary, the important limitations, and links to sources. These qualifications stay attached when the brief is exported.
Field-level changes
The revision history logs each change to the record: the part that changed, the old and new values or a recoverable earlier version, the date, the source, the editor, and the reason for the correction.

When a question depends on another record

Evaluations, monetary observations, strategic assessments, and institutional decisions can each have multiple records. Their fields apply when those records exist.

How the collections are assembled

AI Benefits Database

Collection scope
Reported and intended benefits of AI, including individual experiences, experiments, and deployments. The collection also retains proposals, disputed findings, and uses whose contribution from AI remains unresolved.
How candidates were found
Candidates came from searches by subject, source citations, and reviews of the ITU AI for Good publication and the Local Government Association case-study bank. Cases added on 29 October 2026 came from searches for independent evaluations: randomized and controlled studies in Europe PMC, FDA De Novo decision summaries, and reports by regulators, auditors and courts. The retained research files do not provide a complete log of queries, search dates, languages, or rejected search results.
Counting unit and duplicate rules
One entry per AIBD identifier. Several sources about the same benefit remain together. Studies, individual experiences, and aggregate reports all occur in the database; an entry count is therefore not a count of people or deployments.
Coverage limits
The collection is selective. Countries, languages, and sectors were not sampled uniformly, and the total number of eligible benefits is unknown. Intended benefits, contested findings, and unfavorable results may appear. Source availability affects what can be assessed.
Collection and review cadence
Entries are added or revised as research proceeds. Revision histories identify changes to an entry; individual source reviews have their own dates. There is no fixed refresh interval for every entry.
Population and exposure context
The count describes entries in AIBD. It is not a success rate: the database does not enumerate every deployment, attempted use, or person exposed to AI.

Fructus

Collection scope
Monetary statements attached to AIBD entries. Published statements require a checked primary source or primary-source excerpt, a supported connection to AI, and a matching entry. Value created or saved and money paid are shown separately.
How candidates were found
Candidates were drawn from monetary figures in AIBD and reviewed against their sources. Unresolved candidates and contextual amounts remain in separate review lists. Their presence there does not qualify them for the published collection.
Counting unit and duplicate rules
The headline count is the number of published monetary statements, not the number of AIBD entries. One statement may contain several amounts. A converted or source-equivalent amount is another expression of the same amount, not additional value.
Coverage limits
Payments, savings, avoided losses, and modeled values have different boundaries. The collection does not estimate the total economic benefit of AI. A payment connected to an AI-assisted project does not establish how much value AI created.
Collection and review cadence
Publication follows source review. Pending candidates and contextual amounts retain separate statuses. Currency conversions use the exchange-rate dates shown with the amounts; they are not live quotations.
Population and exposure context
Each monetary statement retains its own period, unit, recipients, and accounting boundary. The amounts are not summed across entries. Missing costs or counterfactuals prevent a net-benefit or return calculation unless separately established.

Querist

Collection scope
AIBD entries with court or regulator material, legal proceedings, challenges, reversals, or withdrawals. Inclusion identifies scrutiny; it does not mean that a court or regulator confirmed an AI benefit.
How candidates were found
Selection uses the source types and later developments recorded in AIBD. Identified trial registrations, catalog entries, and reposts that do not establish substantive scrutiny are excluded. A reviewed regulator announcement can document a decision.
Counting unit and duplicate rules
Each AIBD identifier counts once in this collection. Several sources or beneficiary groups do not create additional entries. Collections overlap, so their counts cannot be added.
Coverage limits
This is a view of AIBD, not a comprehensive register of litigation, approvals, or adverse findings. The underlying legal document may concern a drug, organization, or outcome rather than the AI system itself.
Collection and review cadence
Membership is recalculated when the site is published. The evidence is reviewed separately; a new publication of the site does not mean that every source was checked again.
Population and exposure context
The count describes entries in AIBD. It is not a success rate: the database does not enumerate every deployment, attempted use, or person exposed to AI.

Viator

Collection scope
AIBD entries in which at least one recorded case jurisdiction differs from the developer's jurisdiction. Entries can involve several case jurisdictions.
How candidates were found
Selection compares the developer country with the countries identified in the recorded location. Country aliases are normalized. An unknown developer jurisdiction or an unresolved case location cannot establish a cross-border relationship.
Counting unit and duplicate rules
Each AIBD identifier counts once in this collection. Several sources or beneficiary groups do not create additional entries. Collections overlap, so their counts cannot be added.
Coverage limits
A difference between jurisdictions does not establish a cross-border transfer of data, computing, funding, or ownership. The view depends on the geographic information recorded in AIBD and may omit deployments whose locations remain unresolved.
Collection and review cadence
Membership is recalculated when the site is published. The evidence is reviewed separately; a new publication of the site does not mean that every source was checked again.
Population and exposure context
The count describes entries in AIBD. It is not a success rate: the database does not enumerate every deployment, attempted use, or person exposed to AI.

Adyt

Collection scope
AIBD entries classified as a specific harm prevented or a protective response. A protective response can qualify even when prevention has not been demonstrated.
How candidates were found
Selection follows the two protective categories assigned in AIBD. The collection retains the recorded comparison as measured, assumed, or undocumented.
Counting unit and duplicate rules
Each AIBD identifier counts once in this collection. Several sources or beneficiary groups do not create additional entries. Collections overlap, so their counts cannot be added.
Coverage limits
A warning, intervention, or repair does not by itself prove that harm would otherwise have occurred. The records retain that distinction and any reported costs or harms. The collection is not an inventory of every safeguard in use.
Collection and review cadence
Membership is recalculated when the site is published. The evidence is reviewed separately; a new publication of the site does not mean that every source was checked again.
Population and exposure context
The count describes entries in AIBD. It is not a success rate: the database does not enumerate every deployment, attempted use, or person exposed to AI.

Chanticleer

Collection scope
Security, threat detection, and the operation of essential services. Topics include cybersecurity, defense and borders, energy and water, disasters, public and environmental security, financial security, and communications and transport.
How candidates were found
Entries were selected individually from AIBD according to their purpose and the activity described. Membership is explicit; a passing mention of security or a threat does not qualify an entry.
Counting unit and duplicate rules
Each AIBD identifier counts once in this collection. Several sources or beneficiary groups do not create additional entries. Collections overlap, so their counts cannot be added.
Coverage limits
The collection describes reported or intended uses. Inclusion does not endorse an operation or establish that its benefits outweighed its harms. Evidence about prevention, proportionality, and affected people varies by entry.
Collection and review cadence
Membership is recalculated when the site is published. The evidence is reviewed separately; a new publication of the site does not mean that every source was checked again.
Population and exposure context
The count describes entries in AIBD. It is not a success rate: the database does not enumerate every deployment, attempted use, or person exposed to AI.

Collection fields

Collection scope
Collection scope shows which countries, languages, sectors, dates, kinds of source, and inclusion rules were searched for a collection.
How candidates were found
This field shows how candidate records were found: the queries, languages, channels, sampling or ranking rules, referrals, search dates, and updates. Material that people submitted is kept apart from material that was actively sought.
Counting unit and duplicate rules
Counting rules show whether a count describes records, deployments, outcomes, sources, evaluations, or beneficiaries. They also show how matching records and overlaps are handled.
Coverage limits
Coverage limits sit beside any comparison across a collection and describe gaps such as selective publicity, missing languages or regions, private data that is unavailable, and bias in the sources.
Collection and review cadence
Cadence shows how often sources are collected, how large the review backlog is, and how current the records are, each as a separate measure.
Population and exposure context
Matched denominators for population and exposure appear here where they exist, with the rules used to collect them. A collection of benefits alone cannot show a success rate.
