About

I want more people to benefit from AI that has already helped someone else.

I collect these cases so that others can judge whether a similar use of AI could help them. Each case includes sources that readers can check and makes clear whether the benefit has been reported or is still proposed.

  1. In 2024, AI-governance researchers Airlie Hilliard, Emre Kazim, and Stephan Ledain wrote in AI and Ethics that “there is a lack of an equivalent for positive outcomes” to the databases that document AI harms.

    Airlie Hilliard et al., “Are the Robots Taking Over? On AI and Perceived Existential Risk,” AI and Ethics 5, no. 3 (June 2025): 2937, § 4, https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-024-00600-9.

  2. Following a joint workshop in 2024, Amazon Web Services and Minerva Technology Policy Advisors recommended establishing a centralized “AI Success Database” to “document and share positive case studies of AI adoption in the public sector.”

    Amazon Web Services and Minerva Technology Policy Advisors, AI, Everywhere, All at Once: A New Policy Agenda for AI Success through Faster Adoption (workshop report, n.d.), 12, § 9.2, https://adopt-ai.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/AIAI_0325_AI-Everywhere-All-At-Once_Updated-Final.pdf.

  3. In 2026, Valerie Wirtschafter, a fellow at the Brookings Institution, called for “documenting and sharing AI success stories and lessons learned” in her assessment of federal AI adoption.

    Valerie Wirtschafter, “Assessing the State of AI Adoption across the Federal Government,” Brookings Institution, April 15, 2026, 21, ¶ 1, https://www.brookings.edu/articles/assessing-the-state-of-ai-adoption-across-the-federal-government/.

    See esp. her proposal for a public repository on AI.gov showing how federal agencies use AI to benefit the public.
