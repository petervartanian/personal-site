How a record is madeClose

AI that helps people in one setting may be useful elsewhere. Examine the evidence behind each case to judge whether a similar approach could help in your own circumstances.

Edited by Peter H. Vartanian.

Admission

Cases concern reported or intended benefits with an identifiable AI contribution. These include AI-assisted learning, research, accessibility, and operational improvements. An AI-related topic alone is insufficient. Reported outcomes and intended benefits have distinct labels. Every case has a beneficiary shape, a depth color, and a confidence fill. Gray means that depth is unassessed.

Kinds of event

A record describes one of four kinds of case: a benefit created or restored, a specific harm prevented, a protective response whose prevention is unproven, or a general benefit case. General cases can include launches, simulations, accuracy studies, aggregate claims, cohorts, AI-assisted learning, and proposed applications. Their category does not determine their depth or confidence.

Depth

Each beneficiary group that benefited is rated from 1 to 5, the depth of the benefit for that group: 1, a convenience, or time or cost saved, with life otherwise unchanged; 2, a measurable improvement in a result or a capability, such as a test score or a crop yield; 3, a lasting change, a capability restored or a risk removed; 4, a serious harm averted or a serious condition resolved in time; 5, a life or a livelihood preserved, or a change documented across a population. Every rating has a reason. Depth describes the magnitude of the benefit that the source reports; it does not establish that the claim is independently verified. Aggregate outcomes and gains in capability can be rated. Intended benefits remain explicit, and a group stays gray when the extent of its benefit cannot be established. Corrections appear in the revision history.

The system's part

What the system contributed to the benefit is assessed separately from whether the benefit occurred, and may be left unrated. A system that was present is not thereby responsible.

Evidence and confidence

Every record cites its sources, each marked with its kind and with who wrote it: the system's makers, its deployer, the beneficiary, or someone independent of all three. The strongest independent source determines the evidence grade: found, when a court or a regulator has made a finding; studied, when an independent peer-reviewed study, case report or official report supports it; reported, when the independent material is a news report, preprint or dataset; claimed, when the only sources are the makers', the deployer's or the beneficiary's own. The mark's shade follows the grade: full for found and studied, paler for reported, outlined for claimed. A registry entry is one source among others.

Source notes distinguish independent evidence from accounts by the makers, deployers, and beneficiaries. They identify gaps in the cited evidence. Inferences are labeled.

Briefs

Briefs by Peter H. Vartanian examine the evidence and its gaps. Their dates appear in the revision history.

Replication

Every record states which conditions produced the benefit and which of them would have to hold again. Attempts to make the benefit happen again are recorded with their setting, model version, and outcome, and drawn on the case's timeline. An outcome that nobody followed up is shown as unknown.

Harm on record

Where an incident database holds an entry about the same kind of system harming the same kind of stakeholder, the record links to it, so that a reader weighing benefit against risk can open both. No severity is shown for the harm.

Exploring the cases

Cases can be filtered and viewed as a list, table, map, timeline, or table of benefit types. The view and the filters are kept in the address, so that a view can be shared.

Candidate reviews

The review log lists exclusions and their reasons. Each case requires a specific reported or intended benefit and an identifiable AI contribution. Unfavorable results remain visible. Multiple sources about the same benefit belong to one case. Intended outcomes, aggregate results, and weak evidence can qualify.

Dates

A case distinguishes an observed event date from the publication date, the date through which a source says that its information is current, or the date on which the source was checked. Month-only dates display the month without an invented day. The timeline uses that labeled date as its sorting anchor.

Coverage

Counts on this site describe the database's coverage. They do not measure how beneficial AI is, and a trend in records is a trend in reporting.

The map in the Gazetteer draws Natural Earth's 1:110m countries with de facto boundaries in an equirectangular projection. A country's circle has an area in proportion to its cases; a route runs from the country where a system was made to the country where the case happened. Cases that state no country are not on the map.

Not admitted

Excluded candidates and their reasons. An unresolved AI contribution can warrant further evidence. Intended benefits, aggregate results, and weak evidence can qualify.

DataClose

Data

The dataset is one file, records.json, released under CC BY 4.0. Three flat tables are derived from it: records.csv, one row per record; ratings.csv, one row per beneficiary group rated; and echoes.csv, one row per attempt to make a benefit happen again. The schema describes every field. Each record is also available on its own page as JSON.

Feed of new and revised cases

Required coverage

Each case has 96 required field groups: 91 public and five internal editorial groups. Six collection-wide definitions apply through versioned collection profiles. Every field carries a value or an explicit assessment status. Unassessed questions remain visible and incomplete. Explore the fields and their meanings.

Download the public case profiles, field dictionary, and collection profiles. The coverage table lists each public field and its status for every case.

Fructus

AI outcomes, in money. A separately curated collection of financial claims, with original currencies, periods, recipients, comparisons, and supporting sources. JSON and CSV.

Counting

One system, one setting, one benefit is one record. A benefit reported in several sources is one record with several sources. A benefit that happened again elsewhere is an echo on the original record, not a new record.

Fields

id
A stable identifier.
title, summary, date_observed, account
What happened, in one line and in one paragraph, and when.
brief
A brief from Peter H. Vartanian: his reading of the evidence and its gaps, where one has been written, with its sidenotes in brief_notes.
headline, metric
A short title that adds no fact (asterisks around a name set it in italics), and the AI's quantified effect, copied verbatim from the summary or account (null when the record states no number).
date_basis, date_precision
Whether date_observed identifies an event, source publication, source information cutoff, or source access. Precision is day, month, year, or unassessed. A partial date uses the first day of its month or year only as a sorting anchor.
benefit_status
Reported, intended, mixed, or unclear. This is separate from evidence confidence.
category
The kind of event: benefit created, harm prevented, protective response (prevention unproven), or general benefit (including aggregate results and proposed applications).
unexpected, purpose
Whether the benefit was what the system was built to do, and what it was built to do.
system, model_version, developer, deployer, location, domain, segment
The system, its version where known, who made and ran it, and where it was used.
benefit_type, taxonomy_note
The benefit type from the taxonomy, with a note where the fit is loose.
unrated_because
For an event with no depth, the reason in a clause: the good is denied, or the AI's part is undocumented.
ratings
For each beneficiary group that benefited, a depth from 1 to 5 or null for unrated, and the reason.
ai_role
What the system contributed, assessed separately, or marked not assessed.
evidence, confidence
The sources, each with kind, citation, link, date, a short quote, and who wrote it (the makers, the deployer, the beneficiary, someone independent of them, or a relationship that remains unassessed); confidence (found, studied, reported, claimed) is computed from them as the method page describes, and shade (high, medium, low) is the mark's fill that follows it.
comparator
Better than what: the baseline the benefit is measured against (against), the figure without and with the AI (before, after), whether the source measured or assumed the difference (basis), and the passage that states it (quote, url). Null when no comparison is recorded. The case profile distinguishes an unassessed question from a reviewed absence.
later
What happened after the first report: persisted, scaled, reversed, withdrawn, litigated or unknown, with the date, a note, and the passage that states it. Unknown means that no later outcome is established in the record.
value
A financial benefit stated by a source: the amount verbatim with its currency, what it measures, who gains or pays, whether it is an estimate, and the supporting passage.
claim_status, claim_basis
The strongest source's authority, the cited evidence by kind and author, and its limitations. Both are computed from the evidence.
replication
The conditions that produced the benefit, the conditions that must hold again, and the echoes (replications): each attempt to repeat it, with setting, model version, and outcome.
paired_incident
Harm on record: an entry in an incident database about the same kind of system harming the same kind of stakeholder, with the database's name and a one-sentence basis for the link.
source_collection, source_entry
The imported collection and the stable entry key that AIBD assigns within it. These entry keys are not publisher-assigned identifiers.
registry_ids
Identifiers of the same event in other registries.
revisions
Every change, with its date, source, and editor.
