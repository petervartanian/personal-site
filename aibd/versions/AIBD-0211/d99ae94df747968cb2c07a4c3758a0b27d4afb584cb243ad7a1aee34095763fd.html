Boston Children's uses OpenAI's o3 to find 11 new rare-disease diagnoses

OpenAI's o3 Deep Research helped Boston Children's Hospital reanalyze 376 locally unresolved rare-disease cases, which yielded 18 confirmed diagnoses: 11 new and seven rediscoveries.

Workflow

Already-sequenced unsolved cases, mostly child-parent trios, assembled into a de-identified packet. Each packet held Human Phenotype Ontology terms, any clinician notes, age and sex. It also held a filtered variant table with rarity, predicted effect, ClinVar class and family signal quality. A general-purpose reasoning model asked for the single most plausible molecular explanation and its evidence, not just a ranked gene. The prompt was refined first on cases of known diagnosis. Time elapsed since the original analysis, so that newly published gene-disease links and reclassified variants existed to be found. Expert adjudication under ACMG/AMP criteria by at least two reviewers, CLIA-laboratory confirmation, and return of results by the clinical team.

Relation to AI

O3 Deep Research read each de-identified case packet and proposed the most plausible molecular explanation with its supporting evidence. It diagnosed no one. Humans did the rest. At least two reviewers adjudicated each candidate under ACMG/AMP criteria, a CLIA-certified laboratory confirmed each variant, and the clinical team returned results to families. Its distinct contribution was surfacing candidates in cases that commercial and institutional analyses and multidisciplinary teams had already reviewed. In one early-psychosis case it inferred a 22q11.2 deletion absent from the input data, which genome sequencing then confirmed. Seven of the 18 were rediscoveries already established externally. The study was retrospective, and reviewers were not blinded to model confidence. The authors call for prospective evaluation with comparator baselines, so the gain over renewed human reanalysis is unmeasured.

Purpose

OpenAI o3 Deep Research is a general-purpose reasoning and research model. The Manton Center team built an explanation-first reanalysis workflow around it to propose candidate molecular diagnoses for unsolved cases. These diagnoses were the workflow's intended output. OpenAI states that the study does not describe or endorse a customer use of o3 for diagnosis. Brownstein said that she was shocked that a commercial system could find new diagnoses in genomes analyzed many times.
