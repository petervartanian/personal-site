Reasons

People: The sources report an aggregate yield and name no person who gained from it. Organizations: HMRC reports about £4.6 billion of extra yield from Connect cases in 2024–25. In a 2015 case study that draws on HMRC's own annual report, the National Audit Office says that Connect let HMRC cut its Risk and Intelligence staff by 40% while it collected more compliance revenue. Both are measurable gains in HMRC's results. The figures are HMRC's own estimates, and the Comptroller and Auditor General describes compliance yield as such. Society: No source traces the extra revenue to an effect on the public, such as funded services or a smaller tax gap.
