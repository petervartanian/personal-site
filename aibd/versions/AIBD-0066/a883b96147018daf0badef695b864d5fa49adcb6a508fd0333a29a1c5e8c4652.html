Confirmed TREWS sepsis alerts are linked to 3.3% lower mortality

In a five-hospital study by the TREWS developers, patients whose sepsis alert was confirmed within three hours had 3.3% absolute lower in-hospital mortality, a link that outside researchers question.

Relation to AI

TREWS flagged likely sepsis, and providers decided whether to confirm the alert and treat. The study compares patients whose alert was confirmed within 3 hours with those whose alert was not, with adjustment for presentation and severity but not for provider factors. Kennedy and Rudd (2022) note that faster confirmation may mark better care teams, and Nemati and colleagues (2022) argue that the result is likely to be severely biased. The sources include no randomized comparison of TREWS against usual care.

Original evidence

The benefit rests on one prospective cohort study by the developers of TREWS, who compared confirmation within three hours with later or absent confirmation among 6,877 sepsis patients out of 590,736 monitored. Researchers at the University of California San Diego, in a preprint, and at the University of Pittsburgh, in a commentary, question that comparison. The Food and Drug Administration, which cleared Bayesian Health's sepsis flagging device on 30 April 2026, made no finding on mortality. [1] [2] [3] [4]

  2. Preprint Independent author Context or contrary evidence

    Nemati S, Shashikumar SP, Holder AL, Wardi G, Owens RL, Randomized Clinical Trials or Convenient Controls: TREWS or FALSE?, medRxiv, 9 August 2022. The authors work at the University of California San Diego and have no stated role in TREWS; two of them co-founded Healcisio, a UC San Diego startup. With data from two other health systems, they argue that the TREWS comparison does not adjust for confounding by processes of care.

    Using data from over 650,000 patient encounters across two distinct healthcare systems, we show that the findings of Adams et al. are likely to be severely biased

  3. Other Independent author Context or contrary evidence

    Kennedy JN, Rudd KE, A sepsis early warning system is associated with improved patient outcomes, Cell Reports Medicine 3(9):100746, 20 September 2022. The authors are critical care researchers at the University of Pittsburgh with no role in TREWS; their commentary summarizes the study and notes that the comparison may reflect better care teams.

    it is possible that prompt response to the alert was simply indicative of a more attentive or better resourced care team in which we would already expect improved patient outcomes

  4. Regulatory document Developer Context or contrary evidence

    Bayesian Health, 510(k) summary for the Bayesian Health Sepsis Flagging Device (K250680), with the FDA clearance letter of 30 April 2026. Bayesian Health holds a license agreement with Johns Hopkins and wrote the summary; the FDA found substantial equivalence to an earlier sepsis device and made no finding on mortality.

    This submission designates Prenosis Inc.'s legally marketed Sepsis ImmunoScore (DEN230036) as the predicate device.
