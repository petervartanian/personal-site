Corsano CardioWatch detects a cardiac arrest in post-processing

In post-processing, the CardioWatch photoplethysmography algorithm detected cardiac arrest at 09:50:17 after loss of pulse in a cyclist; his implanted defibrillator ended it, and no alert was sent.

Workflow

Continuous wristband wear with 32 Hz photoplethysmography and accelerometry uploaded to a study cloud. A shockable arrest with clear loss of pulsations during cycling, in a patient whose ICD provided the reference and the rescue. Blinded after-the-fact processing by the developers' data scientist.

Awaiting review

Human oversight

A Corsano Health data scientist who was blinded to the event ran the exported data through the algorithm after the arrest. The algorithms did not run live and sent no alert. The patient's implantable defibrillator ended the arrest. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Relation to AI

The pulse-loss detector was run afterwards on exported data by a blinded Corsano data scientist. It flagged the loss of pulse, and the fall detector flagged the collapse. Neither ran live or sent an alert, and the ICD shock restored circulation. The group describes its pulse detector as rule-based. Its DETECT-2 paper describes a machine-learning fall detector, though the case report does not name the fall model it used. The developers' own papers are the only full sources; an independent editorial restates the case without new data.

Citable summary

Radboudumc DETECT-3 case: a 64-year-old's VF arrest while cycling was ended by his ICD; the CardioWatch PPG algorithm later detected pulselessness at 09:50:17 and the fall; no live alert was sent. The wristband classified the arrest only after the event and did not trigger treatment. The ICD terminated the arrhythmia. This is evidence of retrospective detection feasibility, not a life saved by the wristband. False alarms and the detector's AI classification remain separate unresolved issues.
