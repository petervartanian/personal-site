Dentbird software designs crowns faster but still needs human review

In university tests, Imagoworks' Dentbird software let technicians design crowns about four times faster, and Ohio State researchers concluded that its designs needed human review.

Results

Study 1: Fit was equivalent at the buccal, mesial and distal margins and internally; Dentbird crowns had larger lingual margin gaps and larger occlusal discrepancies. Study 2: Design time fell from 397.2 to 99.4 min for the premolar and from 516.4 to 97.6 min for the molar. The occlusal deviation between technicians' crowns fell from 275.5 to 25.7 µm. [2] [3]

Limitations

Study 1: Interim resin crowns; one technician as the comparator; no measure of design time or patient-reported outcomes. Study 2: One model, five technicians and twenty crowns; a single measurer; the reported design times are long for a single crown and the paper does not explain how time was counted. [2] [3]

A design produced in minutes is not a standardized comparison of total technician time or clinical turnaround. Sources

  1. Peer-reviewed study Independent author Context or contrary evidence

    Cho JH, Çakmak G, Choi J, Lee D, Yoon HI, Yilmaz B, Schimmel M, Deep learning-designed implant-supported posterior crowns: Assessing time efficiency, tooth morphology, emergence profile, occlusion, and proximal contacts, Journal of Dentistry 147:105142, 19 June 2024. University authors with no declared competing interests; the abstract reports that the deep-learning design was faster than a technician's design but does not name the software.

  2. Peer-reviewed study Independent author

    Nagata K, Inoue E, Nakashizu T, et al., Verification of the accuracy and design time of crowns designed with artificial intelligence, Journal of Advanced Prosthodontics, 24 February 2025. The authors work at Kanagawa Dental University with no stated tie to Imagoworks; five technicians designed crowns with both conventional CAD and Dentbird on a model, which shows a time saving in a laboratory setting.

    The AI-based CAD system significantly reduced design time and enabled the fabrication of uniform crowns regardless of the dental technician's experience and skill.

  3. Peer-reviewed study Independent author

    Win TT, Mai HN, Kim SY, et al., Fit accuracy of complete crowns fabricated by generative artificial intelligence design: a comparative clinical study, Journal of Advanced Prosthodontics, 19 August 2025. University authors in Korea, the United States, Thailand and Vietnam, funded by the Korea Medical Device Development Fund, with no author from Imagoworks; the study shows that Dentbird crowns fitted patients about as well as conventional crowns, with larger lingual and occlusal discrepancies.

    Each patient received two crown designs in the same prepared tooth: one with a web-based automated 3D GAID software program (Dentbird Crown version V.3.X.X; Imagoworks, Seoul, Korea)

  4. Peer-reviewed study Independent author Context or contrary evidence

    Sawangsri K, Bekkali M, Lutz N, et al., Acceptability and deviation of finish line detection and restoration contour design in single-unit crown: Comparative evaluation between 2 AI-based CAD software programs and dental laboratory technicians, Journal of Prosthetic Dentistry, 14 April 2025. Ohio State University authors with no stated tie to either vendor; six prosthodontists rated Dentbird crown designs below those of technicians and of a rival AI program.

    The DT and AM groups demonstrated significantly higher acceptability scores for finish line detection and restoration design than the DB group (P<.001).

  5. Peer-reviewed study Independent author

    Sawangsri K, Bekkali M, Hsieh YL, et al., Acceptability, deviation, and efficiency of 2 fully automated CAD software programs in designing 3-unit fixed dental prostheses: A comparative study, Journal of Prosthetic Dentistry, 5 December 2025. Ohio State University authors with no stated tie to either vendor; they found that Dentbird saved design time on 30 bridges but underperformed on esthetic and functional criteria.

    Although both systems improved design efficiency, human oversight remains essential for occlusal and proximal contact, connector design, and anterior FDPs.

  6. Report Developer

    International Telecommunication Union, AI for Good: Innovate for Impact (2025). Project-submitted account, current as of June 2025. ITU provides no independent outcome evaluation.
