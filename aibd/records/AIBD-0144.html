Project Amber fills 5,145 Blackpool potholes for about £450,000

Blackpool's Project Amber imagery/AI surveys: 5,145 potholes filled for ~£450k; pothole claim payouts fell to £719 (DfT, 2024)

ⓘ

What happened

Blackpool Council's Project Amber, launched in 2019 under the Local Council Roads Innovation Group, uses high-definition road imagery to detect potholes and prioritize repairs. The Blackpool Unlimited page calls it artificial-intelligence technology piloted in Anchorsholme and Bispham. It states that since summer 2019, 5,145 potholes were filled in North Shore for just under £450,000 against £1.5 million by traditional methods. The Department for Transport's release of 26 September 2024 says Blackpool was the first authority to use the technology. It had paid up to £1.5 million a year to settle pothole claims and “last year” paid out only £719. The release dates the work only as “last year”. The reduction is credited to the whole program, including surface treatments.

Purpose. Survey road condition from imagery to find and prioritize pothole and surface defects for repair

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. Neither source isolates the detection software's contribution. The claims reduction and savings are credited to Project Amber as a whole, which combines imagery surveys with preventive surface treatments. The Blackpool Unlimited page is undated and does not state its operator, so it is treated as a council-adjacent promotional page, not an official report.

Further evidence

Setting. Imagery-based detection of potholes, cracks and surface damage across the network. Color-coded prioritization used by engineers to schedule treatments. Preventive surface treatments funded through the program.

Conditions. Regular imagery capture of the road network. Budget for follow-up treatments. Claims handling that records payouts over time.
