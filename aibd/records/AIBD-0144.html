Blackpool uses AI road imagery to fill 5,145 potholes for about £450,000

Blackpool Council credits Project Amber, which detects potholes in road imagery with AI, with 5,145 repairs for just under £450,000 and claim payouts cut to £719; complaints later nearly doubled.

What happened

Blackpool Council launched Project Amber in 2019 under the Local Council Roads Innovation Group. The project uses high-definition road imagery to detect potholes and to prioritize repairs. The Blackpool Unlimited page calls it artificial-intelligence technology piloted in Anchorsholme and Bispham. It states that since summer 2019, 5,145 potholes were filled in North Shore for just under £450,000 against £1.5 million by traditional methods. On 3 February 2020 BBC News reported that the technology had been piloted in summer and rolled out permanently. On 5 January 2024 the BBC's Local Democracy Reporting Service reported that Blackpool Council's pothole payouts fell from £1.6m in 2008/09 to £719 the previous year. Officials said that about 95% of claims are now defended. The Department for Transport's release of 26 September 2024 says that Blackpool was the first authority to use the technology. It says that the council had paid up to £1.5 million a year to settle pothole claims and “last year” paid out only £719. The release dates the work only as “last year”. The reduction is credited to the whole program, including surface treatments. On 4 March 2026 the Local Democracy Reporting Service reported that complaints about potholes in 2023 to 2025 were nearly twice those of the three years before. It reported that 8,139 potholes were fixed in those three years against 9,123 in the three years before. The council leader called roads the top issue raised by residents and pledged a further £3.5m.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Road images were analyzed for surface defects and used to prioritize repairs.

Workflow

Imagery-based detection of potholes, cracks and surface damage across the network. Color-coded prioritization used by engineers to schedule treatments. Preventive surface treatments funded through the program.

AI contribution

No source isolates the contribution of the detection software. The claims reduction and savings are credited to Project Amber as a whole, which combines imagery surveys with preventive surface treatments.
