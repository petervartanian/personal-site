Transpara cut mammogram reading work in a Swedish randomized trial

In the MASAI trial of 105,934 women in Sweden, Transpara-guided triage cut screen readings by 44.2% and raised cancer detection from 5.0 to 6.4 per 1,000.

What happened

The MASAI trial randomized 105,934 women screened at four sites in southwest Sweden (Malmö, Lund, Landskrona and Trelleborg) between 12 April 2021 and 7 December 2022. In the intervention arm, Transpara version 1.7.0 scored each examination, and the score decided whether one or two radiologists read it. The software also marked suspicious findings. The control arm used standard double reading without AI. The AI arm needed 61,248 screen readings against 109,692 in the control arm, a 44.2% reduction in workload. Cancer detection was 6.4 per 1,000 women with AI support and 5.0 per 1,000 without, with more small node-negative invasive cancers found. A later analysis found that the interval cancer rate with AI was non-inferior and that sensitivity was higher (80.5% against 73.8%), with equal specificity. Lund University researchers led the trial with funding from the Swedish Cancer Society and Swedish government research funds.

Sector

Purpose

Transpara is built to score mammograms for cancer risk and mark suspicious findings. Using its score to triage exams to single or double reading was the trial's way of cutting workload, which is a stated aim.
