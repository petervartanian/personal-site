A Tesla Cybertruck may have pulled over after its driver passed out

Rishi Vohra wrote that Tesla's Full Self-Driving pulled his Cybertruck onto a freeway shoulder after he passed out on 18 February 2026; no vehicle data has been published.

What happened

In an X post of 19 February 2026, Rishi Vohra wrote that he passed out while driving on 18 February. He was on a freeway in his Tesla Cybertruck, mid-call with his wife. He wrote that he had passed out after a severe allergic reaction, following a 17-hour unintentional fast and medicine. He wrote that Full Self-Driving was engaged. He wrote that its driver monitoring system detected his loss of consciousness. By his account the truck slowed, put on hazards and pulled onto the shoulder without a crash. His wife used Life360 to send emergency services, who reached him in five minutes. He wrote that the car then drove him to an emergency room, where he was stabilized overnight and discharged on 19 February. Elon Musk replied “Glad you're ok!”. By 29 October 2026 no vehicle data or official confirmation had been published, and no police, hospital or Tesla account of the event had appeared. In an earlier case, on 30 July 2021, Norwegian police reported that a Tesla had stopped in a tunnel with a drunk 24-year-old man asleep at the wheel. Electrek reports that Autopilot was engaged and that the car stopped by itself.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

FSD (Supervised) requires driver supervision, per Storyboard18. An unconscious driver is outside its intended operating condition. Whatever stopped the truck was a fallback, not a substitute for an attentive driver. The account is Vohra's alone. No Tesla vehicle data, police or emergency-service record, hospital statement, freeway location, FSD version or footage of the pull-over has been published. The replies from Elon Musk and Tesla's account acknowledge the outcome without confirming the mechanism. Vohra chose to have the car take him to the emergency room after responders had reached him. Emergency services, not the car, were the documented first response.

Sector

Road vehicle crash avoidance. Automotive.

Purpose

Tesla sells Full Self-Driving (Supervised) as driver assistance, and Storyboard18 notes that the system still requires driver supervision. Its cabin driver-monitoring system watches the driver. Vohra credits that monitoring with detecting his loss of consciousness. He credits the system with slowing, putting on hazards and pulling onto the shoulder. Keeping the car out of a crash is what the system is built to do. Tesla has not described whether the stop was a designed fallback for an unresponsive driver. The later drive to the emergency room used the system's ordinary point-to-point driving.
