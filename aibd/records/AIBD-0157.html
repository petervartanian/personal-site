Rishi Vohra says Tesla FSD pulled over after he passed out

depth 3 for a person

Rishi Vohra says FSD pulled his Cybertruck onto a freeway shoulder after he passed out, 18 Feb 2026; Musk replied, no data published.

What happened

In an X post of 19 February 2026, Rishi Vohra wrote that on 18 February he passed out while driving his Tesla Cybertruck on a freeway, mid-call with his wife, after a severe allergic reaction following a 17-hour unintentional fast and medicine. Full Self-Driving (Supervised) was engaged, he wrote; its driver monitoring system detected his loss of consciousness, and the truck slowed, put on hazards and pulled onto the shoulder without a crash. His wife used Life360 to send emergency services, who reached him in five minutes; the car drove him to an emergency room, where he was stabilized overnight and discharged on 19 February. Musk replied 'Glad you're ok!'; no vehicle data or official confirmation has been published.

What it was built to do. Tesla sells Full Self-Driving (Supervised) as driver assistance that still requires driver supervision, as Storyboard18 notes, and its cabin driver-monitoring system watches the driver. Vohra credits that monitoring with detecting his loss of consciousness and the system with slowing, putting on hazards and pulling onto the shoulder; keeping the car out of a crash is what the system is built to do, though Tesla has not described whether the stop was a designed unresponsive-driver fallback. The later drive to the emergency room used the system's ordinary point-to-point driving.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A Tesla Cybertruck with Full Self-Driving (Supervised) engaged on a freeway, by Vohra's account. A cabin driver-monitoring system that, on his account, registered his loss of consciousness, after which the truck slowed, put on its hazards and pulled onto the shoulder. A wife on the phone who used Life360 to send emergency services to him within five minutes, and responders who attended to him before he had the car continue to the emergency room.

What must hold again. FSD (Supervised) requires driver supervision (Storyboard18); an unconscious driver is outside its intended operating condition, so whatever stopped the truck was a fallback, not a substitute for an attentive driver. The account is Vohra's alone: no Tesla vehicle data, police or emergency-service record, hospital statement, freeway location, FSD version or footage of the pull-over has been published, and the replies from Elon Musk and Tesla's account acknowledge the outcome without confirming the mechanism. Vohra chose to have the car take him to the emergency room after responders had reached him; emergency services, not the car, were the documented first response.
