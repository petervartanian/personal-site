Tesla FSD may have pulled over after Rishi Vohra passed out

Rishi Vohra says FSD pulled his Cybertruck onto a freeway shoulder after he passed out, 18 February 2026; Musk replied, no data published.

ⓘ

What happened

In an X post of 19 February 2026, Rishi Vohra wrote that he passed out while driving on 18 February. He was on a freeway in his Tesla Cybertruck, mid-call with his wife. He had passed out after a severe allergic reaction, following a 17-hour unintentional fast and medicine. Full Self-Driving (Supervised) was engaged, he wrote. Its driver monitoring system detected his loss of consciousness. The truck slowed, put on hazards and pulled onto the shoulder without a crash. His wife used Life360 to send emergency services, who reached him in five minutes. The car drove him to an emergency room, where he was stabilized overnight and discharged on 19 February. Musk replied “Glad you're ok!”. No vehicle data or official confirmation has been published.

Purpose. Tesla sells Full Self-Driving (Supervised) as driver assistance that still requires driver supervision, as Storyboard18 notes. Its cabin driver-monitoring system watches the driver. Vohra credits that monitoring with detecting his loss of consciousness. He credits the system with slowing, putting on hazards and pulling onto the shoulder. Keeping the car out of a crash is what the system is built to do. Tesla has not described whether the stop was a designed unresponsive-driver fallback. The later drive to the emergency room used the system's ordinary point-to-point driving.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. Everything known about what the system did comes from Vohra's post. FSD (Supervised) was engaged, and the driver monitoring system registered that he had lost consciousness. The truck slowed, put on its hazards and pulled onto the shoulder. After responders had attended to him, it drove him to the emergency room. The post carries a video that The American Bazaar describes as showing emergency services responding, not the pull-over itself. Tesla has published no vehicle data or statement. Its account replied with a heart and Elon Musk wrote “Glad you're ok!”. Those replies acknowledge the outcome, not the mechanism. No police, emergency-service or hospital record, freeway location or FSD version has been published.

Further evidence

Setting. A Tesla Cybertruck with Full Self-Driving (Supervised) engaged on a freeway, by Vohra's account. A cabin driver-monitoring system that, on his account, registered his loss of consciousness. The truck then slowed, put on its hazards and pulled onto the shoulder. A wife on the phone who used Life360 to send emergency services to him within five minutes. Responders attended to him before he had the car continue to the emergency room.

Conditions. FSD (Supervised) requires driver supervision, per Storyboard18. An unconscious driver is outside its intended operating condition. Whatever stopped the truck was a fallback, not a substitute for an attentive driver. The account is Vohra's alone. No Tesla vehicle data, police or emergency-service record, hospital statement, freeway location, FSD version or footage of the pull-over has been published. The replies from Elon Musk and Tesla's account acknowledge the outcome without confirming the mechanism. Vohra chose to have the car take him to the emergency room after responders had reached him. Emergency services, not the car, were the documented first response.
