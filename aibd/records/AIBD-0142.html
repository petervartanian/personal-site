ChatGPT ties a mother's 18-month cough to her blood pressure drug

ChatGPT listed a blood pressure drug among possible causes of a woman's 18-month cough; a doctor confirmed it and changed the drug, and her daughter says that she is healing.

  • 2 a person

    One identifiable patient, the poster's mother, had an 18-month cough with internal bleeding that doctors had warned could become fatal. After the medication change her daughter reported her “finally healing”. Material for one person. It rates below life-changing because the recovery was in progress. Nothing beyond the daughter's post documents the diagnosis or the outcome.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
ChatGPT generated the hypothesis. Among several possible causes it listed blood pressure medication. When told the mother was on such medication, it asked whether the prescription contained a specific ingredient, which it did. The confirmation and the prescription change were made by a doctor (“He confirmed it instantly and changed the meds”). NDTV, Mint, Mashable India and the Financial Express quote a commenter. They say cough is a known side effect of BP medicine that doctors “should've checked”. That frames the contribution as surfacing a documented side effect rather than a novel diagnosis. No clinician comments on the chatbot's role. The whole sequence rests on the daughter's account.
Limits
The medication change and improvement rest on a daughter's account, with no identified drug, clinical records, or treating-clinician statement. The claimed six-month fatal prognosis is likewise unverified. Improving after a prescription change does not establish that ChatGPT saved a life.

What happened

An X account posting as Shreya.tsx (@Life_of_coder, profile location “india”) was created on 27 January 2025, according to the archived copy of its post. On 23 July 2025 the user wrote about her mother. Her mother had had a nonstop cough for 1.5 years. That was despite top doctors, big hospitals in and outside their city, homeopathy, ayurveda and allopathy. Internal bleeding had begun. Doctors had warned that the condition could be fatal within six months. She described everything to OpenAI's ChatGPT, which listed possible causes including blood pressure medication. It asked whether the prescription contained a specific ingredient. It did. A doctor, told of this, confirmed it at once and changed the medication. Her mother was “finally healing”. Four Indian outlets reported the post on 24 July 2025. By October 2026 the author had deleted the post, and the account, under the same user ID, posted as Cipher (@Cipher_twt).

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The daughter described her mother's cough and then confirmed that she was taking a blood-pressure medicine. ChatGPT proposed a medication side effect for a doctor to check.

Workflow

A family member with an 18-month symptom history (nonstop cough, then internal bleeding). She described it to ChatGPT after top doctors, big hospitals, homeopathy, ayurveda and allopathy had not helped. ChatGPT listed several possible causes, including blood pressure medication. Told the mother was on BP medication, it asked whether it contained a specific ingredient, which it did. A doctor who, told of the suggestion, confirmed it at once and changed the prescription.

Human oversight

A doctor reportedly confirmed the suspected medication side effect and changed the prescription. The daughter used the chat to raise the possibility during care.

AI contribution

ChatGPT suggested that a blood-pressure medication might explain a persistent cough. The daughter reported that a doctor confirmed the explanation and changed the prescription.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The chatbot's suggestion is taken to a prescribing doctor who confirms it before any medication is changed. The post credits the doctor with both the confirmation and the change. The cough must actually be the drug's side effect. The post names neither the drug nor the “ingredient”, so the mechanism cannot be checked against the drug's label. The account rests on one since-deleted post by the patient's daughter, relayed by independent outlets. No clinician, hospital or record corroborates it.

Purpose

ChatGPT is OpenAI's general-purpose conversational assistant. OpenAI did not build it as a diagnostic or drug-safety tool. Surfacing a medication side effect as the likely cause of a chronic cough was outside what it was built for. So the benefit is marked unexpected.
