AI colonoscopy flags a polyp; stage I cancer removed in Chiayi

After a positive fecal occult blood test, Fujifilm AI endoscopy at St. Martin De Porres Hospital, Chiayi, marked a 1 to 1.5 cm adenoma; pathology: stage I colorectal cancer, removed endoscopically.

  • 3 a person

    A stage I colorectal cancer inside a 1 to 1.5 cm adenoma was removed through the endoscope without surgery, according to all three reports. The sources do not demonstrate a survival benefit for this early-stage cancer.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

Reported benefit. The hospital-linked report describes an AI alert during endoscopy, pathology-confirmed early cancer, and endoscopic removal.

The system’s part
Not isolated. The hospital says that the AI system marked the suspicious polyp with a colored frame and a sound during the examination. The physician then diagnosed and removed it. No source says that the physician would have missed a 1 to 1.5 cm polyp in a patient already referred after a positive stool test. Randomized trials of Fujifilm's CAD EYE have produced mixed results: authors of a 2024 Japanese trial found higher adenoma detection with AI, while authors of a 2026 Spanish trial in patients referred after a positive stool test found none. So the AI's share of this detection cannot be separated from the colonoscopy itself.
Limits
A positive stool test had already prompted the examination, and pathology established the cancer. No source shows that the endoscopist would otherwise have missed the lesion or that the AI spared the man more invasive treatment. The authors of the 2026 trial in Palencia found that CAD EYE did not raise adenoma detection in patients referred after a positive stool test. The sources report no recurrence or survival data.

What happened

Three Taiwanese outlets reported on 19 April 2024 on a man in his 50s. He attended free colorectal cancer screening at St. Martin De Porres Hospital in Chiayi City. He tested positive for fecal occult blood and was scheduled for an AI-assisted endoscopy (Liberty Times Net health). Gastroenterologist Zheng Xu-sheng said that the hospital had introduced a Fujifilm 7000-series endoscope in 2023. The hospital installed what it calls the nation's first Fujifilm AI system (Liberty Times Net; Taiwan Good News). During the examination the AI system marked a suspicious polyp with a colored frame and sounded an alert. It was diagnosed as a 1 to 1.5 cm adenoma that biopsy confirmed as stage I colorectal cancer. It was removed immediately through the endoscope (Taiwan Good News; Guan Chuan Media). Zheng said that polyps over 1 cm risk malignant change. He advised free screening every two years after 50 (Guan Chuan Media). The procedure date is not given. Randomized trials of Fujifilm's CAD EYE have produced mixed results. Saitama Medical University authors reported in January 2024 that AI raised adenoma detection. A team in Palencia, Spain, reported in March 2026 that it found no gain among patients referred after a positive stool test, as this man was.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Colonoscopy video produced a colored frame and audible warning around a suspicious polyp.

Autonomy

The endoscopist inspected and removed the lesion, while pathology established the cancer diagnosis.

Workflow

A positive fecal occult blood test in a free screening program, followed by colonoscopy. A Fujifilm AI system that marks suspicious polyps with a colored frame and a sound during the examination. Endoscopic removal and pathology at the same hospital.

Human oversight

The AI system marked the suspicious polyp with a colored frame and a sound. The physician then diagnosed and removed it. The sources describe no other safeguard. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Benefits

Reach

One patient, a man in his 50s, benefited in this case. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Duration

The sources report one detection and removal, on a date that they do not give. No source reports follow-up on recurrence or survival. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Measurement

Measurement

Adenoma detection rate and advanced neoplasia detection rate by colonoscopy and pathology. [6]

Setting and sample

361 people aged 50 to 74 with a positive fecal immunochemical test, one hospital in Palencia, Spain, October 2023 to February 2025. [6]

Measures

Adenoma detection rate. [6]

Results

61.5% with AI against 69.8% without AI (p = 0.097); advanced neoplasia 37.9% against 36.3%. [6]

Additional value from AI

No significant gain in detection; CADx specificity was 66.7% against 51.9% for optical diagnosis by endoscopists. [6]

Limitations

Single center with high baseline detection rates; the authors call for multicenter studies. [6]

Denominators

CAD EYE-assisted colonoscopy. [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Patients attend screening and follow a positive stool test with colonoscopy. The endoscopist inspects and acts on the AI's marks. Bowel preparation adequate for the camera to see the mucosa.

Evidence

Original evidence

Three Taiwanese outlets reported the case on 19 April 2024 and quoted the gastroenterologist Zheng Xu-sheng. Liberty Times Net credits its photographs to the hospital. The hospital's own release and the OECD Monitor entry tell the same story. The two randomized trials of CAD EYE, published in January 2024 and March 2026, concern other patients.
