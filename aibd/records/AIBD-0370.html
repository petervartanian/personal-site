AI colonoscopy flags a polyp; stage I cancer removed in Chiayi

After a positive fecal occult blood test, Fujifilm AI endoscopy at St. Martin De Porres Hospital, Chiayi, marked a 1 to 1.5 cm adenoma; pathology: stage I colorectal cancer, removed endoscopically.

ⓘ

What happened

Three Taiwanese outlets reported on 19 April 2024 on a man in his 50s. He attended free colorectal cancer screening at St. Martin De Porres Hospital in Chiayi City. He tested positive for fecal occult blood and was scheduled for an AI-assisted endoscopy (Liberty Times Net health). Gastroenterologist Zheng Xu-sheng said the hospital introduced a Fujifilm 7000-series endoscope in 2023. It installed what it calls the nation's first Fujifilm AI system (Liberty Times Net; Taiwan Good News). During the examination the AI system marked a suspicious polyp with a colored frame and sounded an alert. It was diagnosed as a 1 to 1.5 cm adenoma that biopsy confirmed as stage I colorectal cancer. It was removed immediately through the endoscope (Taiwan Good News; Guan Chuan Media). Zheng said polyps over 1 cm risk malignant change and advised free screening every two years after 50 (Guan Chuan Media).

The procedure date is not given.

Purpose. The Fujifilm AI system is built to mark suspicious polyps on the endoscopic image with a colored frame and sound. The physician then inspects and removes them. Flagging this adenoma was that function.

AI’s contribution

Taiwan Good News and Guan Chuan Media state that the AI system marked the suspicious polyp with a colored frame and sound during the examination. The physician then diagnosed and removed it. No source says the physician would have missed a 1 to 1.5 cm polyp in a patient already referred for a positive stool test. So the AI's share of this detection cannot be separated from the colonoscopy itself. All three reports relay the hospital's account. No clinical study of the system at this hospital was cited.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    A stage I colorectal cancer inside a 1 to 1.5 cm adenoma was removed through the endoscope without surgery, per all three reports. A survival benefit is plausible for an early-stage cancer but is not demonstrated in the sources.

Further evidence

Setting. A positive fecal occult blood test in a free screening program, followed by colonoscopy. A Fujifilm AI system that marks suspicious polyps with a colored frame and a sound during the examination. Endoscopic removal and pathology at the same hospital.

Conditions. Patients attend screening and follow a positive stool test with colonoscopy. The endoscopist inspects and acts on the AI's marks. Bowel preparation adequate for the camera to see the mucosa.
