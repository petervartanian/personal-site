FAST-PETase degrades 51 post-consumer PET products in a week

MutCompute, a structure-based machine-learning model, predicted stabilizing mutations for FAST-PETase, which almost completely degraded 51 post-consumer PET products in a week in laboratory tests.

  • 1 an organization

    Independent groups at Texas A&M University and the University of Waterloo reused the enzyme in their own laboratory systems. The sources report bench work and no production deployment.

  • Not rated society

    Near-complete degradation of post-consumer PET was shown only in the laboratory, and under larger-scale conditions FAST-PETase converted 18.1 percent of post-consumer PET in 24 hours. No source reports that any plastic waste was recycled outside a laboratory.

What happened

In a Nature paper of 27 April 2022, researchers at the University of Texas at Austin used a structure-based machine-learning algorithm. The algorithm, an ensemble of three three-dimensional convolutional neural networks, selected stabilizing mutations in a polyethylene terephthalate (PET) hydrolase, producing FAST-PETase. They reported that untreated post-consumer PET from 51 different thermoformed products was almost completely degraded within one week at 50 degrees Celsius. The authors report that FAST-PETase “shows superior PET-hydrolytic activity relative to both wild-type and engineered alternatives between 30 and 50 °C and a range of pH levels.” The researchers then resynthesized PET from the recovered monomers. In a paper of 26 September 2023, Carbios scientists and academic co-authors reported that FAST-PETase converted 18.1 percent of post-consumer PET in 24 hours under larger-scale conditions, against 97.3 percent for the enzyme LCC-ICCG. They concluded that its low rate may not permit use at larger reaction scales. In September 2024 a University of Waterloo group reported that FAST-PETase made by engineered wastewater bacteria degraded about 40 percent of a commercial PET film in four days at 50 degrees Celsius. The group reports that the enzyme also degraded part of a post-consumer PET product. An independent group at Texas A&M University used FAST-PETase together with MHETase in a yeast whole-cell biocatalyst (Environmental Science and Technology Letters, 19 March 2025). The yeast system produced 4.95 mM terephthalic acid from PET film after seven days at 30 degrees Celsius, with no accumulation of MHET intermediates. The authors report complete depolymerization of PET into its monomers. Their reported yield does not establish that the entire starting film was degraded.

Details

AI system

Model

MutCompute.

Role

The model selected candidate mutations for enzyme experiments.

Inputs and outputs

An ensemble of three 3D convolutional networks proposed stabilizing mutations in a PET-degrading enzyme, producing the FAST-PETase candidate for laboratory testing.

Workflow

A structure-based machine-learning model trained to predict stabilizing mutations. A wild-type PET hydrolase as the starting scaffold. Untreated post-consumer PET tested at moderate temperatures.

Human oversight

Researchers introduced the proposed mutations, tested degradation of plastic products, and resynthesized PET from recovered monomers.

Benefits

Reach

No source names or counts beneficiaries. The benefit is a laboratory capability for the groups that engineered and reused the enzyme. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The authors of the original study report near-complete degradation of thermoformed PET within one week at 50 degrees Celsius. Under larger-scale conditions, Carbios scientists and academic co-authors report that FAST-PETase converted 18.1 percent of post-consumer PET in 24 hours. The Waterloo group reports about 40 percent degradation of a commercial film in four days. The Texas A&M system yielded 4.95 mM terephthalic acid after seven days. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Measurement

Measurement

Laboratory comparison of four enzymes under one standardized protocol; conversion measured by base consumption and by residual dry weight. [4]

Setting and sample

Post-consumer colored-flake PET waste powder at 16.5 percent weight in a bioreactor. [4]

Measures

Percent of PET converted to monomers after 24 hours. [4]

Results

FAST-PETase converted 18.1 percent of the PET in 24 hours, against 97.3 percent for LCC-ICCG. [4]

Additional value from AI

Not measured. [4]

Limitations

Five authors work for Carbios, which develops LCC-ICCG; each enzyme ran near its own working temperature; one reaction setup. [4]

Denominators

24 hours. [4]
