FAST-PETase degrades 51 post-consumer PET products in a week

degraded 51 post-consumer PET products in a week

A 3D-CNN ensemble engineered FAST-PETase, which degraded 51 post-consumer PET products in a week; Texas A&M later reported complete depolymerisation using it with MHETase.

What happened

In a paper published in Nature on 27 April 2022, researchers at the University of Texas at Austin used a structure-based machine-learning algorithm, an ensemble of three three-dimensional convolutional neural networks, to select stabilising mutations in a PET-hydrolysing enzyme, producing FAST-PETase. They reported that untreated post-consumer PET from 51 different thermoformed products was almost completely degraded within one week at 50 degrees Celsius and that the enzyme outperformed wild-type and engineered alternatives between 30 and 50 degrees Celsius; they closed the loop by resynthesising PET from the recovered monomers. An independent group at Texas A&M University, publishing in Environmental Science and Technology Letters on 19 March 2025, used FAST-PETase together with MHETase in a yeast whole-cell biocatalyst and reported complete depolymerisation of a PET film in seven days at 30 degrees Celsius.

What it was built to do. Use a structure-based machine-learning model to predict stabilising mutations that make a PET hydrolase active and robust at moderate temperatures.

The system's part

The machine-learning model chose which mutations to introduce; the enzymatic activity was measured experimentally by the designers and later reproduced by a separate group at another institution that used FAST-PETase as a component of its own system.

Replication

What produced it

  • A structure-based machine-learning model trained to predict stabilising mutations
  • A wild-type PET hydrolase as the starting scaffold
  • Untreated post-consumer PET tested at moderate temperatures

What must hold again

  • Moderate reaction temperatures (roughly 30-50 degrees Celsius)
  • Amorphous or thermoformed PET substrate
  • Enzyme expressed and purified or displayed at sufficient activity
