FAST-PETase degrades 51 post-consumer PET products in a week

What happened

In a Nature paper of 27 April 2022, researchers at the University of Texas at Austin used a structure-based machine-learning algorithm. The algorithm, an ensemble of three three-dimensional convolutional neural networks, selected stabilizing mutations in a polyethylene terephthalate (PET) hydrolase, producing FAST-PETase. They reported that untreated post-consumer PET from 51 different thermoformed products was almost completely degraded within one week at 50 degrees Celsius. The enzyme outperformed wild-type and engineered alternatives between 30 and 50 degrees Celsius. They closed the loop by resynthesizing PET from the recovered monomers. An independent group at Texas A&M University used FAST-PETase together with MHETase in a yeast whole-cell biocatalyst (Environmental Science and Technology Letters, 19 March 2025).

It reported complete depolymerization of a PET film in seven days at 30 degrees Celsius.

What it was built to do. Use a structure-based machine-learning model to predict stabilizing mutations that make a PET hydrolase active and robust at moderate temperatures.

Since then
Persisted on 19 March 2025: An independent Texas A&M group adopted FAST-PETase in a yeast whole-cell biocatalyst and reported complete depolymerization of a PET film in seven days.Co-immobilization of FAST-PETase and MHETase on a surface-displayed trifunctional scaffoldin led to complete depolymerization of PET into its constituent monomers TPA and EG, with no accumulation of reaction intermediates. In our system, the presence of MHETase along with FAST-PETase led to a nearly 5-fold boost in product formation, and attachment of FAST-PETase at two sites on the trifunctional scaffoldin further boosted yields by up to 1.8-fold. TPA yields of 4.95 mM were obtained in 7 days on an amorphous PET film with no pretreatment requirement. source

The system's part

The machine-learning model chose which mutations to introduce. The designers measured the enzymatic activity experimentally. A separate group at another institution later reproduced it, using FAST-PETase as a component of its own system.

Replication

What produced it. A structure-based machine-learning model trained to predict stabilizing mutations. A wild-type PET hydrolase as the starting scaffold. Untreated post-consumer PET tested at moderate temperatures.

What must hold again. Moderate reaction temperatures (roughly 30–50 degrees Celsius). Amorphous or thermoformed PET substrate. Enzyme expressed and purified or displayed at sufficient activity.
