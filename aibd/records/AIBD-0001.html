Brain-spine interface lets Gert-Jan Oskam walk by thought

Brain-spine interface let Gert-Jan Oskam, paralyzed since 2011, stand, walk and climb stairs by thought (Nature, May 2023)

ⓘ

What happened

In Lausanne's STIMO-BSI trial, a 38-year-old man (named in press as Gert-Jan Oskam) received two WIMAGINE 64-electrode cortical implants and a spinal paddle lead. He had an incomplete C5/C6 spinal cord injury from a cycling accident ten years earlier. A decoder trained on his cortical signals controlled spinal stimulation, letting him stand, walk, climb stairs and cross complex terrain. Performance stayed stable for over a year, including independent use at home. Lorach et al. reported in Nature on 24 May 2023 that he regained the ability to walk with crutches with the interface switched off. News reports place the implant surgery in 2021. As only the year is given, 1 January 2021 is used as the observation date.

Purpose. Built to restore volitional control of leg movement after spinal cord injury by decoding movement intentions from cortical recordings and driving epidural spinal stimulation. Standing and walking were the intended benefit. The recovery of crutch-assisted walking with the interface switched off was a secondary finding the authors attribute to BSI-supported neurorehabilitation.

AI’s contribution

The AI is the decoder: a Hidden-Markov/multilinear model calibrated on the participant's cortical signals. It predicts intended movement and modulates stimulation amplitude in real time. The paper reports hip-muscle control within two minutes of calibration. Surgery and stimulation hardware are conventional. The walking benefit depends on the decoder translating intention into stimulation.

Further evidence

Setting. Incomplete cervical spinal cord injury with preserved cortical movement intention and residual lumbosacral circuits that epidural stimulation can recruit. Epidural cortical recording (WIMAGINE) paired with lumbosacral epidural stimulation, decoder calibrated to the individual. Supervised neurorehabilitation within a regulated feasibility trial (STIMO-BSI, NCT04632290).

Conditions. Decoder calibration remains fast and decoding performance stable over months. Spinal stimulation still recruits leg muscles (intact circuits below the lesion). Participant can tolerate implanted hardware and use the wireless system independently.
