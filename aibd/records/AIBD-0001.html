Brain-spine interface lets Gert-Jan Oskam walk by thought

What happened

In Lausanne's STIMO-BSI trial, a 38-year-old man (named in press as Gert-Jan Oskam) received two WIMAGINE 64-electrode cortical implants and a spinal paddle lead. He had an incomplete C5/C6 spinal cord injury from a cycling accident ten years earlier. A decoder trained on his cortical signals controlled spinal stimulation, letting him stand, walk, climb stairs and cross complex terrain. Performance stayed stable for over a year, including independent use at home. Lorach et al. reported in Nature on 24 May 2023 that he regained the ability to walk with crutches with the interface switched off. News reports place the implant surgery in 2021. As only the year is given, 1 January 2021 is used as the observation date.

What it was built to do. Built to restore volitional control of leg movement after spinal cord injury by decoding movement intentions from cortical recordings and driving epidural spinal stimulation. Standing and walking were the intended benefit. The recovery of crutch-assisted walking with the interface switched off was a secondary finding the authors attribute to BSI-supported neurorehabilitation.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Life-changing benefit to one identifiable person: standing, walking, stair climbing and independent home use restored after ten years of paralysis. Some recovery persists when the device is switched off.

After
the BSI enabled walking with gait features that were markedly closer to those quantified in healthy individuals; WISCI II 16 after STIMO-BSI

The source measured it.Compared to stimulation alone, the BSI enabled walking with gait features that were markedly closer to those quantified in healthy individuals (Extended Data Fig. 4a). The BSI ensured a continuous link between the intended movement and the modulation of stimulation protocols, which translated into the ability to walk overground source

The system's part

The AI is the decoder: a Hidden-Markov/multilinear model calibrated on the participant's cortical signals. It predicts intended movement and modulates stimulation amplitude in real time. The paper reports hip-muscle control within two minutes of calibration. Surgery and stimulation hardware are conventional. The walking benefit depends on the decoder translating intention into stimulation.

Replication

What produced it. Incomplete cervical spinal cord injury with preserved cortical movement intention and residual lumbosacral circuits that epidural stimulation can recruit. Epidural cortical recording (WIMAGINE) paired with lumbosacral epidural stimulation, decoder calibrated to the individual. Supervised neurorehabilitation within a regulated feasibility trial (STIMO-BSI, NCT04632290).

What must hold again. Decoder calibration remains fast and decoding performance stable over months. Spinal stimulation still recruits leg muscles (intact circuits below the lesion). Participant can tolerate implanted hardware and use the wireless system independently.
