LEADERG's AI door god flags smoke; temple fire held to 10 m²

depth 2 for an organization

held to 10 m², no injuries, main hall intact

AI camera analytics flagged smoke in an empty temple office a minute before a battery fire; held to 10 m², no injuries, main hall intact.

What happened

At about 05:30 on 5 February 2026 the AI門神 (AI door god) system that 立達軟體 (LEADERG Inc.) runs over the CCTV of Beigang Wude Temple, Yunlin County, Taiwan, flagged smoke in a first-floor office and machine room, empty after hours, and messaged the front desk, the general manager and chairman 林安樂, in Japan, by LINE. The night-duty clerk reached the room with an extinguisher within a minute; at 05:33 overheated lithium batteries beside a tool charger burst and flames spread. Staff called 119 at 05:36; Yunlin County Fire Bureau crews arrived at 05:44, controlled it at 06:10 and put it out at 06:14. Ten square meters burned, nobody was hurt, the main hall and statues untouched.

What it was built to do. AI門神 ('AI door god: temple AI fire-and-theft prevention system') was built to run AI image recognition over a temple's existing CCTV, detect excess smoke, flame and suspicious behavior, and alert staff by SMS, LINE or email; CTS reported in December 2024 that Wude Temple's door-god system would sound an alarm at once on abnormal smoke. Flagging smoke before a fire was exactly its designed purpose.

Who benefited

  • 2
    organizational

    The temple's loss was the contents of one first-floor office of about ten square meters (staff lockers, some tea, sweets, incense and joss paper); the main hall, statues and worship areas were untouched and the temple stayed open in the run-up to the Lunar New Year crowds. The fire bureau credited the reinforced-concrete structure and its own eight-minute arrival for the containment, and a conventional smoke alarm sounded as well, so the share attributable to the AI alert is a minute or two of earlier warning, not the whole saved building.

The system's part

The system's documented contribution is the first alert. By the temple's own log it detected smoke at 05:30, a minute before the lithium batteries burst at 05:33, and it messaged the front desk, the general manager and the chairman in sequence over LINE, which is how a chairman asleep in Nara came to follow the fire on the temple's cameras. The suppression was human: the clerk's extinguisher failed once the batteries burst, and the Yunlin County Fire Bureau's eight trucks and eighteen firefighters, called at 05:36, controlled the fire at 06:10. UDN reports that a residential smoke alarm (住警器) sounded as well, and the Third Brigade commander 陳岳謄 attributed the containment to the building's RC structure and the crews' quick arrival without mentioning the AI. The LINE relay from the vendor is the chairman's account, not the bureau's.

Replication

What produced it. A camera-analytics system (AI門神) already watching the office over the temple's existing CCTV, set to alert on small amounts of smoke and to message a named chain of people (front desk, general manager, chairman) over LINE. A staffed night watch: the temple has kept someone on duty every night since it moved to the site in 1980 (民國69年), so the alert had a clerk a minute away with an extinguisher. A slow start to the fire: a tool charger and lithium batteries smoked before the batteries burst at 05:33, which gave the alert a minute's lead. A fire brigade eight minutes away and a reinforced-concrete annex that held the fire to one room, as the Third Brigade commander said.

What must hold again. The alert reaches a person who can act within minutes; at an unstaffed site the lead time is worth little. The camera covers the rooms where fires start (here an office and machine room with chargers, beside stored joss paper), not only the shrine. The AI alert complements conventional detectors rather than replacing them; a residential smoke alarm sounded here as well.
