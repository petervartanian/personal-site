What happened

St Helens Borough Council built an AI-integrated catalog of technology-enabled care using Microsoft Copilot Studio. Care professionals co-curated the catalog. Predictive matching connects care-assessment information to suitable technology options, and generative AI drafts personalized care plans and costings. The workflow is intended to help residents return home and live independently. The Care Quality Commission, in its assessment published on 8 May 2025, recorded that St Helens had launched a technology hub using AI systems in November 2024. The regulator rated the council’s adult social care Good but did not assess what the AI tools achieved. Reported medication-dispenser savings and in-house development costs concern other parts of the service. On 20 March 2026, the council’s cabinet member for adult social care wrote that AI-supported care planning tools were still in use and saved staff time. He gave no figures. Improvements in care and independent living remain unevaluated.

Details

AI system

Role

Predictive matching connects care-assessment information to technology options. Generative AI produces personalized care plans and costings. The source does not separately identify the underlying models. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Care assessments are matched against a catalog of assistive equipment to draft care plans and cost estimates. [1]

Workflow

The council used Microsoft Copilot Studio to combine care-assessment information with a technology catalog co-curated by care professionals. Predictive matching selects relevant technology options and generative AI drafts personalized care plans and costings.

Human oversight

Care professionals co-curated the technology catalog. No source describes who reviews or approves the care plans that the AI drafts. [1] [2] [3]
