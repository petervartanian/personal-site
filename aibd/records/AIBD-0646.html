In a two-hospital Seoul trial of 998 patients, colonoscopy with Ainex's ENAD-DET-01 found adenomas in 52.3% of patients against 36.1% with standard colonoscopy.

Brief from Peter

A randomized trial has tested ENAD-DET-01, a polyp-detection system from the South Korean company Ainex. At two Seoul hospitals between May 2023 and April 2025, 998 patients were assigned at random to colonoscopy with or without the system, which draws a circle on the monitor when it detects a polyp. Adenomas were found in 52.3% of patients with the system and 36.1% without.1 For sessile serrated lesions and cancers, the difference between the groups was not statistically significant. A control rate of 36.1% left room for improvement, and units that already detect more may see a smaller gain. Elsewhere, researchers found no gain in a randomized trial of computer-aided polyp detection at a Japanese community hospital. Both hospitals used one Olympus platform, so the trial does not test the system on other equipment.2 The Korea Medical Device Development Fund paid for the study, and the authors report no conflicts of interest.

  1. 1 H. J. Jeon and colleagues, “Clinical Efficacy of Real-Time Artificial Intelligence-Assisted Colonoscopy in Colorectal Polyp Detection: A Prospective Multicenter Randomized Controlled Trial,” Gut and Liver, November 27, 2025. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
  2. 2 The paper states that the system receives digital input directly from the endoscopy processor and that both centers used the same colonoscopy system from Olympus. H. J. Jeon and colleagues, Gut and Liver, November 27, 2025, methods section. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Details

AI system

Workflow

The system marked suspected polyps on the live endoscopy monitor. Endoscopists at two tertiary hospitals used it in screening, surveillance and diagnostic colonoscopy. The control group's adenoma detection rate was 36.1%.
