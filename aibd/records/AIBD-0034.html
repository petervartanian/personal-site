What happened

Xie and colleagues at Pennsylvania State University interviewed 14 visually impaired users of Be My AI. That is the large-multimodal-model image-description feature of the Be My Eyes app. They analyzed image descriptions gathered from 25 September 2023 to 31 March 2024. The paper appeared at CHI 2025. Individual uses are recorded. “P2 utilized it to identify gate numbers at the airport”. “P10 used it to recognize her surroundings when disoriented in her neighborhood”. P13 received a description locating “a hair tie in the upper right hand corner” of a carpet. A separate ASSETS 2025 interview study of 21 participants (November 2024 to January 2025) records P9 using Be My AI to read a handwritten check. The date observed is Be My AI's public release date, 25 September 2023. That starts the first paper's social-media collection window.

The interview participants' uses are undated and outcomes self-reported.

What it was built to do. Be My AI was built to describe photographed scenes and objects to blind and low-vision users. These task completions were that purpose. Xie et al. state that Be My AI is built on OpenAI's GPT-4 models.

The system's part

The multimodal model generated the descriptions participants acted on. No independent per-task accuracy check was made. Participants themselves reported risk (P9: “I knew there was a risk”).

Replication

What produced it. Smartphone photograph submitted to Be My AI by a blind or low-vision user. Task in a familiar or low-stakes setting. User interprets a text description and acts on it.

What must hold again. The model does not hallucinate in the specific scene (both studies document hallucinations). User cross-checks high-stakes outputs such as medication or finances.
