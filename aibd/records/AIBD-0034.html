Be My AI reads airport gate numbers and a check for blind users

Blind and low-vision users of Be My AI, the GPT-4 image-description feature of the Be My Eyes app, report in peer-reviewed studies that it read airport gate numbers and a handwritten check.

  • 1 a person

    Participants who are identified by number report that they identified airport gate numbers, recognized their surroundings, located a dropped hair tie and read a check on their own. The results are small and self-reported, and the authors of the same papers document frequent errors.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

Reported benefit. Participants reported assistance with everyday visual tasks through Be My AI.

The system’s part
The multimodal model generated the descriptions participants acted on. No independent per-task accuracy check was made. Participants themselves reported risk (P9: “I knew there was a risk”).
Limits
The evidence consists of interview accounts, descriptions of use and an analysis of low-star app store reviews. The cited sources report no independent measure of the accuracy of individual tasks, of unsuccessful attempts or of any change in independence.
Costs and harms
Users carry the risk of acting on descriptions that they cannot check. P9 said of reading a check with Be My AI that “it could've exposed bank info”. Brigham and Kohno report that low-star app reviews complain of inaccurate descriptions and paternalistic guardrails, and they name hallucinations as a problem with Be My AI.
Later
Persisted, 30 June 2025. An independent interview study run from November 2024 to January 2025 recorded blind participants still using Be My AI for everyday reading tasks such as a handwritten check.

What happened

Be My AI is the image-description feature of the Be My Eyes app, and it runs on a large multimodal model. It was publicly released on 25 September 2023. Xie and colleagues at Pennsylvania State University analyzed image descriptions gathered in a social-media collection window from that day to 31 March 2024. They also interviewed 14 visually impaired users of Be My AI. In October 2024 Adnin and Das at Northeastern University reported at ASSETS that 19 blind participants in their interview study used ChatGPT and Be My AI in their everyday practices. A separate interview study of 21 participants, run from November 2024 to January 2025 and published at ASSETS 2025, records P9 using Be My AI to read a handwritten check. The paper by Xie and colleagues appeared at CHI 2025 and records individual uses. It reports that “P2 utilized it to identify gate numbers at the airport” and that “P10 used it to recognize her surroundings when disoriented in her neighborhood”. P13 received a description that located “a hair tie in the upper right hand corner” of a carpet. The interview participants' uses are undated, and the participants report the outcomes themselves. On 30 September 2026 Brigham and Kohno reported that low-star app reviews of Be My Eyes complain of inaccurate descriptions and paternalistic guardrails, and they name hallucinations as a problem with Be My AI.

Details

AI system

Model

GPT-4.

Role

The model described images for people with impaired vision.

Inputs and outputs

Users submitted smartphone photographs to Be My AI and received generated descriptions of text, objects, or surrounding scenes.

Workflow

Smartphone photograph submitted to Be My AI by a blind or low-vision user. Task in a familiar or low-stakes setting. User interprets a text description and acts on it.

Human oversight

Users interpreted and acted on the descriptions, including identifying airport gates, recognizing surroundings, and locating small objects.

Benefits

Reach

The three interview studies cover 14, 21 and 19 participants, and their uses are self-reported. The sources give no count of all the Be My AI users who benefited. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

Xie and colleagues analyzed descriptions gathered from 25 September 2023 to 31 March 2024. An independent interview study run from November 2024 to January 2025 recorded blind participants still using Be My AI for everyday reading tasks such as a handwritten check. On 30 September 2026 Brigham and Kohno reported that low-star app reviews of Be My Eyes complain of inaccurate descriptions. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The model does not hallucinate in the specific scene (both studies document hallucinations). User cross-checks high-stakes outputs such as medication or finances.

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.

Availability

Be My AI was publicly released on 25 September 2023 within the Be My Eyes app.

Evidence

Original evidence

Three independent interview studies supply the uses: Xie and colleagues interviewed 14 users for CHI 2025, Sharma and colleagues interviewed 21 participants for ASSETS 2025, and Adnin and Das interviewed 19 blind people for ASSETS 2024. Brigham and Kohno separately analyzed 1,428 one- to three-star app store reviews of Be My Eyes. The Penn State Pure entry confirms only the venue of the study by Sharma and colleagues. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
