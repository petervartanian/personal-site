Be My AI reads airport gate numbers and a check for blind users

Peer-reviewed interview studies record blind users completing everyday tasks with Be My AI, e.g., airport gate numbers, a handwritten check.

What happened

Xie and colleagues at Pennsylvania State University interviewed 14 visually impaired users of Be My AI, the large-multimodal-model image-description feature of the Be My Eyes app, and analysed image descriptions gathered from 25 September 2023 to 31 March 2024; the paper appeared at CHI 2025. Individual uses are recorded: 'P2 utilized it to identify gate numbers at the airport', 'P10 used it to recognize her surroundings when disoriented in her neighborhood', and P13 received a description locating 'a hair tie in the upper right hand corner' of a carpet. A separate ASSETS 2025 interview study of 21 participants (November 2024 to January 2025) records P9 using Be My AI to read a handwritten check. The date observed is Be My AI's public release date, 25 September 2023, which is the start of the first paper's social-media collection window; the interview participants' uses are undated. Outcomes are self-reported.

What it was built to do. Be My AI was built to describe photographed scenes and objects to blind and low-vision users; these task completions were that purpose. Xie et al. state that Be My AI is built on OpenAI's GPT-4 models.

The system's part

The multimodal model generated the descriptions participants acted on; no independent per-task accuracy check was made, and participants themselves reported risk (P9: 'I knew there was a risk').

Replication

What produced it
  • Smartphone photograph submitted to Be My AI by a blind or low-vision user
  • Task in a familiar or low-stakes setting
  • User interprets a text description and acts on it
What must hold again
  • The model does not hallucinate in the specific scene (both studies document hallucinations)
  • User cross-checks high-stakes outputs such as medication or finances
