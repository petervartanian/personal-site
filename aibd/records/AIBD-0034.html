Be My AI reads airport gate numbers and a check for blind users

Peer-reviewed interview studies record blind users completing everyday tasks with Be My AI, e.g., airport gate numbers, a handwritten check.

ⓘ

What happened

Xie and colleagues at Pennsylvania State University interviewed 14 visually impaired users of Be My AI. That is the large-multimodal-model image-description feature of the Be My Eyes app. They analyzed image descriptions gathered from 25 September 2023 to 31 March 2024. The paper appeared at CHI 2025. Individual uses are recorded. “P2 utilized it to identify gate numbers at the airport”. “P10 used it to recognize her surroundings when disoriented in her neighborhood”. P13 received a description locating “a hair tie in the upper right hand corner” of a carpet. A separate ASSETS 2025 interview study of 21 participants (November 2024 to January 2025) records P9 using Be My AI to read a handwritten check. Be My AI was publicly released on 25 September 2023. That starts the first paper's social-media collection window. The interview participants' uses are undated and outcomes self-reported.

Purpose. Be My AI was built to describe photographed scenes and objects to blind and low-vision users. These task completions were that purpose. Xie et al. state that Be My AI is built on OpenAI's GPT-4 models.

AI’s contribution

The multimodal model generated the descriptions participants acted on. No independent per-task accuracy check was made. Participants themselves reported risk (P9: “I knew there was a risk”).

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Everyday tasks (gate numbers, orientation, locating a dropped item, reading a check) completed independently by identifiable participants. Small, self-reported, and the same papers document frequent errors.

Further evidence

Setting. Smartphone photograph submitted to Be My AI by a blind or low-vision user. Task in a familiar or low-stakes setting. User interprets a text description and acts on it.

Conditions. The model does not hallucinate in the specific scene (both studies document hallucinations). User cross-checks high-stakes outputs such as medication or finances.
