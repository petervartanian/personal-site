What happened

Researchers at the University of California, San Francisco, the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, Stanford University, Harvard Medical School and The Rockefeller University docked ultralarge chemical libraries against unrefined AlphaFold2 models of the sigma-2 receptor and the serotonin 2A (5-HT2A) receptor, and against experimental structures of the same receptors. They synthesized and tested hundreds of top-ranked molecules. For sigma-2, 64 of 119 molecules from the AlphaFold2 model were active, a hit rate of 54%, against 51% for the crystal structure. For 5-HT2A, 42 of 161 molecules from the AlphaFold2 model bound, a hit rate of 26%, against 23% for the cryo-EM structure, and the three strongest binders (15 to 24 nM) came from the AlphaFold2 campaign. The differences were not statistically significant. A cryo-EM structure of one new agonist with 5-HT2A agreed closely with the docked pose. Science published the study on 21 June 2024. The authors note that retrospective tests with known ligands had favored the experimental structures and that functional hit rates for 5-HT2A were low, in the range of 1 to 5%. The authors tested two receptors.
