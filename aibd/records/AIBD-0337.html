ATO estimates myTax prompts had a $37 million revenue impact

depth 1 for an organization, depth 1 for society

adjustments estimated to have a revenue impact of around $37 million

ATO page: at tax time 2020 nearly 340,000 myTax users (7.5%) got real-time prompts from nearest-neighbor checks; adjustments had an estimated revenue impact of about $37 million.

What happened

The Australian Taxation Office's page 'How we use data and analytics' states that the ATO uses a common form of artificial intelligence known as machine learning, and that nearest neighbor methods compare amounts being entered into myTax with those of other people in similar circumstances; if a claim is significantly different from what is expected, a message prompts the taxpayer to check the figures. The page reports that during tax time in 2020 nearly 340,000 taxpayers, around 7.5% of myTax users, received a pop-up message suggesting they review a specific label, and that these prompts caused taxpayers to make adjustments estimated to have a revenue impact of around $37 million. The same page says the ATO also uses machine-learning algorithms to forecast call-center volumes and speech recognition to transcribe inbound calls, and separately that in 2020 it automated 93,000 franking-credit refunds worth over $42 million for self-preparers over 60 (not described as machine learning). The only non-ATO source fetched is The Mandarin's report of 25 February 2025 that the Australian National Audit Office found the ATO's AI governance arrangements only partly effective; it gives no outcome figures. The earlier verifier found no non-vendor primary source with a recovered figure attributable to machine learning, and that remains the case: the $37 million is the ATO's own estimate. The page is undated; the outcome concerns tax time 2020, and the date used is 14 July 2021, the earliest Internet Archive capture of the page that already carries the 340,000 and $37 million figures (a publication-date proxy, not the event date).

What it was built to do. Built to prompt myTax users in real time to check claims that differ from those of similar taxpayers before lodgment; the recorded adjustments are that purpose.

The system's part

The ATO page states that nearest-neighbor methods generate the prompts and that the ATO uses machine learning; it attributes the adjustments to the prompts, while the taxpayers themselves make the changes. Only the deployer's own account was fetched for the outcome: the ATO built and runs the system in-house, so its page is both the developer's and the deployer's account (marked independent by the database's convention for public bodies describing their own tools). The ANAO audit reported by The Mandarin found AI governance 'partly effective' and offers no outcome figures in the fetched excerpt.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Large volumes of pre-filled third-party data (over 85 million pieces in 2020) giving a comparison base. Nearest-neighbor comparison of entered amounts against taxpayers in similar circumstances. A real-time prompt inside myTax before lodgment. Taxpayers' own decision to adjust a flagged label.

What must hold again. Comparison groups and thresholds that keep prompts credible to taxpayers. Continued use of myTax self-preparation at scale. AI governance that the ANAO found only partly effective in 2025 being maintained or improved. The ATO's method of estimating revenue impact remaining the only measure, since no independent evaluation was fetched.
