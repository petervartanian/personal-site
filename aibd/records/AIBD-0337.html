Prompts in Australia's myTax lead taxpayers to adjust unusual claims

At tax time 2020, nearest-neighbor checks in the Australian Taxation Office's myTax prompted nearly 340,000 users to review a claim; the office puts the revenue impact at about $37 million.

  • Not rated a person

    Taxpayers who corrected claims are not individually documented; the page gives no individual outcome beyond the aggregate prompt count.

    The ATO's own estimate of about $37 million revenue impact from one tax time, with no independent verification and no comparison with total collections given.

  • 1 society

    Protects public revenue at modest scale: prompts reached 7.5% of myTax users and the stated impact is an estimate.

The system’s part
The models compare each claim with the claims of similar taxpayers and decide which claims trigger a message. The taxpayer then decides whether to change the figure. The ATO's randomized trial compares taxpayers who received a message with taxpayers who did not, so it measures the effect of the message. It does not compare model-selected prompts with prompts chosen by simple rules. The ANAO found that the ATO did not document the weights behind the real-time analytics model, and its audit does not test the revenue estimates.
Limits
The $37 million and the later $92.6 million are the ATO's own estimates, and the Australian National Audit Office did not test them. The ATO's randomized trial measures the effect of a message and does not compare model-selected prompts with prompts chosen by simple rules. No source isolates the costs of the prompts or their effect on valid claims.
Costs and harms
The sources document no cost or harm to taxpayers. The ATO says that it is cautious about adding more messages because more messages may weaken their effect.
Later
Scaled up. For the 2023-24 income year the ATO reports over 712,000 prompts and an estimated $92.6 million of revenue protected, against nearly 340,000 prompts and about $37 million at tax time 2020. The ATO says that it is cautious about adding more messages because more messages may weaken their effect. Both sets of figures are the ATO's own estimates.

What happened

The Australian Taxation Office (ATO) page “How we use data and analytics” states that the ATO uses machine learning, a common form of artificial intelligence. Nearest neighbor methods compare amounts being entered into myTax with those of other people in similar circumstances. If a claim is significantly different from what is expected, a message prompts the taxpayer to check the figures. The page reports that during tax time in 2020 nearly 340,000 taxpayers, around 7.5% of myTax users, received a pop-up message. The message suggested that they review a specific label. These prompts caused taxpayers to make adjustments estimated to have a revenue impact of around $37 million. The $37 million is the ATO's own estimate. The same page says that the ATO also uses machine-learning algorithms to forecast call-center volumes and speech recognition to transcribe inbound calls. Separately, in 2020 it automated 93,000 franking-credit refunds worth over $42 million for self-preparers over 60 (not described as machine learning). The page is undated, and the outcome concerns tax time 2020. An Internet Archive copy dated 14 July 2021 contains the 340,000 and $37 million figures. The Australian National Audit Office (ANAO) audit of 24 February 2025 found that the real-time analytics component uses nearest neighbor analysis at lodgment. It found that the ATO did not document the weights behind that component. The Mandarin reported on 25 February 2025 that the ANAO found that the ATO's governance arrangements for artificial intelligence were only partly effective. The report gives no outcome figures. No source independent of the ATO gives a recovered figure attributable to machine learning. In its State of the Service Report 2024-25, the Australian Public Service Commission (APSC) reported ATO figures of over 712,000 prompts and an estimated $92.6 million of revenue protected for the 2023-24 income year. It also reported that an ATO randomized trial found that nudged taxpayers were 3 times more likely to adjust their data than taxpayers without a message.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Nearest-neighbor comparisons checked entered myTax amounts against similar taxpayers and returned a prompt to review an unusual claim.

Autonomy

Taxpayers chose whether to revise their entries after the prompt. The system did not automatically rewrite the claim.

Workflow

Large volumes of pre-filled third-party data (over 85 million pieces in 2020) giving a comparison base. Nearest-neighbor comparison of entered amounts against taxpayers in similar circumstances. A real-time prompt inside myTax before lodgment. Taxpayers' own decision to adjust a flagged label.

Human oversight

The taxpayer decides whether to change a figure after a prompt. The Australian National Audit Office found that the ATO's governance of artificial intelligence was only partly effective and that the ATO did not document the weights behind the real-time analytics model. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

Reach

Nearly 340,000 taxpayers, around 7.5% of myTax users, received a prompt at tax time 2020. This is a count of people prompted, and no source counts the taxpayers who adjusted their claims. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The ATO reports nearly 340,000 prompts at tax time 2020 with an estimated revenue impact of around $37 million. For the 2023-24 income year it reports over 712,000 prompts and an estimated $92.6 million of revenue protected. Both sets of figures are the ATO's own estimates. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

ATO randomized control trial of real-time nudge messages in myTax, as summarized by the APSC. [5]

Setting and sample

Self-lodging myTax users, 2023-24 income year; sample size not stated. [5]

Measures

Likelihood that a taxpayer reviews the return and adjusts the data. [5]

Results

2.5 times more likely to review the return and 3 times more likely to adjust the data. [5]

Additional value from AI

Not isolated: the trial tests the message, and the models choose which claims are flagged. [5]

Limitations

The ATO ran and reported the trial itself; the APSC summary gives no sample size, confidence interval or effect on revenue from the trial. [5]

Denominators

Over 712,000 prompts in the 2023-24 income year. [5]

Money

Kind of value

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Comparison groups and thresholds that keep prompts credible to taxpayers. Continued use of myTax self-preparation at scale. AI governance that the ANAO found only partly effective in 2025 being maintained or improved. The ATO's method of estimating revenue impact remaining the only measure, since no independent evaluation is among the sources.
