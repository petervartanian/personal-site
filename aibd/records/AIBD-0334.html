Colombia's Constitutional Court adopts PretorIA to sort tutela rulings

What happened

In January 2020 the court's president, Gloria Ortiz, described the forthcoming tool to El Tiempo as Prometea, the Argentine system that inspired the initial proof of concept. Semana (3 July 2020) reported that the program was developed by the court with the Universidad de Buenos Aires and the Universidad del Rosario. In the same period the court unanimously decided to stop receiving paper files and to accept electronic filing from 30 July 2020. On 27 July 2020 Colombia's Corte Constitucional presented PretorIA at a virtual press conference organized by Corferias, according to Ámbito Jurídico. It is an artificial-intelligence system meant to support the selection of tutela (constitutional protection) rulings for review. The court receives about 620,242 tutela files a year (more than 2,500 rulings a day). PretorIA was to group, analyze and classify them by criteria that the court prioritizes. It was to offer search, categorization (initially for health rulings) and statistics. Developers claimed that it could process rulings in under two minutes, where a person needs at least a day to read 30 files. The algorithm registry of the Universidad de los Andes records a 2018 pilot and a 2020 deployment without quantified metrics. On 19 April 2021 Dejusticia published independent research with Derechos Digitales. The researchers found that PretorIA was still in a testing phase with court staff. It performed four functions (classification of lower-court rulings, search, non-narrative summaries and statistics), supported by a four-person in-house AI and data team. The court's 2022 management report shows that PretorIA stayed in use, with 42 trained categories, a new search engine and training sessions for the chambers. In a 2024 article, G. de J. Sierra Cadena, a member of the Universidad del Rosario team that built PretorIA, reported that the system reached 80 percent reliability in its implementation phase. In Sentencia T-323 of 2 August 2024 the court described PretorIA as a tool that classifies and searches the more than 3,400 tutelas that it receives each day. It stated that the tool neither selects nor prioritizes cases. No court statement of a verified outcome is among the sources. The court's PretorIA page shows no text.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

PretorIA organized tutela rulings into searchable categories and structured summaries for court staff. [1] [4]

Autonomy

Court staff and judges retained the decision about which cases to select for review.

Workflow

Transfer of the Prometea approach from the Universidad de Buenos Aires to the court, with the Universidad del Rosario. Electronic filing of tutela files to the court from 30 July 2020, replacing paper. A four-person in-house AI and data-science team at the court. Classification categories defined by the court, starting with health rulings.

Human oversight

The court states that PretorIA neither selects nor prioritizes cases. Selection stays with the chambers. Court staff still fill in a record sheet for each tutela. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Benefits

Duration

Dejusticia reported on 19 April 2021 that PretorIA was still in user testing with court staff. The court's 2022 management report shows the tool in use with 42 trained categories. In Sentencia T-323 of 2 August 2024 the court described it as a tool that it applies. No source measures an outcome over that period. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Rulings arriving in digital, machine-readable form. Classification accuracy acceptable to selection staff (not published). Human selection staff using the tool's output rather than bypassing it. Continued institutional ownership after the launch partners withdrew.
