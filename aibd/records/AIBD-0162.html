Pano AI camera alerts crews, who hold a campsite fire to one-eighth acre

A Pano AI camera funded by Xcel Energy alerted crews to a campsite fire near a Breckenridge neighborhood; firefighters said that the early notice kept it to one-eighth of an acre.

What happened

The Wellington fire broke out on Sunday, 7 July 2024, at an unattended campsite just east of a Breckenridge neighborhood. Summit Daily reported on 8 July 2024 that crews were dispatched around 12:30 p.m. The officials whom it interviewed credited fuels reduction work completed 18 months earlier for the containment within hours. They did not mention the camera. Summit Daily gave the fire's size as up to a quarter-acre. CBS Colorado reported on 9 July 2024 that the fire was spotted by a Pano AI camera financially supported by Xcel Energy. The camera sits on a cell tower with a 360-degree view and submits an alert to emergency crews if it notices smoke. Firefighters said that this was the first instance in Summit County where a wildfire was caught early thanks to the camera. They said that the early notice kept it to one-eighth of an acre. Earlier alerts had been false alarms from legal campfires. Mathew Benedict of Red White and Blue Fire's Wildland Division said that a location given as latitude and longitude let crews drive straight to the fire. Otherwise they could spend up to an hour finding it. Michelle Aguayo of Xcel Energy said that the company had 28 Pano AI cameras installed. On 3 September 2024 CBS Colorado reported that Pano AI cameras had first spotted another one-eighth-acre fire near the Gold Hill Trailhead. KJCT8 reported on 5 May 2026 that Xcel Energy had more than 130 Pano AI cameras across Colorado.

Details

AI system

Workflow

A camera station with a 360-degree view covering a campsite near housing. Alerts routed to the local fire district with a lat/long. A fire district willing to respond to AI alerts despite earlier false alarms.

Human oversight

The camera sends a smoke alert with a location to emergency crews, who drive to the fire and contain it. Firefighters told CBS Colorado that earlier alerts had been false alarms from legal campfires. [1] [2] [3]

People and places

Coordinates helped crews reach the fire directly; human response and earlier vegetation management also mattered.
