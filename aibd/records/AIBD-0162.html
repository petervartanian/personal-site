What happened

CBS Colorado reported on 9 July 2024 on the Wellington fire, which broke out at an unattended campsite just east of a Breckenridge neighborhood. It was spotted by a Pano AI camera financially supported by Xcel Energy. The camera sits on a cell tower with a 360-degree view and submits an alert to emergency crews if it notices smoke. Firefighters said this was the first instance in Summit County where a wildfire was caught early thanks to the camera's early notice. It was kept to one-eighth of an acre. This followed earlier false alarms from legal campfires. Mathew Benedict of Red White and Blue Fire's Wildland Division said a location with an actual lat/long let crews drive straight to the fire. Otherwise they could spend up to an hour finding it. Xcel's Michelle Aguayo said the company had 28 Pano AI cameras installed. The fire's date is not given.

The date used is the article's publication date.

What it was built to do. Pano AI cameras scan for smoke and alert emergency crews with a location. Catching a fire near homes early was the intended use.

The system's part

The camera's AI smoke alert was the trigger for dispatch, according to the fire division quoted by CBS Colorado. The only source is a single local news report with named sources. The fire's date, cause of containment and any official report are not documented.

Replication

What produced it. A camera station with a 360-degree view covering a campsite near housing. Alerts routed to the local fire district with a lat/long. A fire district willing to respond to AI alerts despite earlier false alarms.

What must hold again. Camera coverage of the ignition. Crew availability to reach the location quickly. Fire conditions that allow a small crew to contain a sub-acre fire.
