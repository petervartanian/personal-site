AI yield estimates speed crop insurance claims for some Indian farmers

YES-TECH feeds satellite and weather data to a machine-learning model whose yield estimates set crop insurance claims in Madhya Pradesh; some farmers report faster payment and others lower claims.

  • 1 a person

    CSE reports that some farmers in Sehore, Vidisha and Bhopal valued faster payment of claims than before. That is time saved. Other farmers in the same districts report lower claims than their losses.

  • Not rated an organization

    District officials told CSE that claims for similar crop losses had become more consistent since the technology came in. They gave no figures and also called the process opaque. The PIB states that settlement is faster and gives no figures.

  • Not rated society

    The PIB reports adoption by nine states, and CSE later reports twelve. No source documents an outcome at scale. Down To Earth reports that payouts in three districts fell sharply between 2018 and 2024.

The system’s part
Official PIB documents (deployer, not the model's maker) state YES-TECH employs AI-driven analytics and that Madhya Pradesh relies fully on technology-based yield estimates. CSE's presentation says kharif yields come from an “AI/ML model” whose results are used to disburse claims. The model output thus directly determines payouts. Whether this produced a benefit is contested. PIB asserts faster, more transparent settlement without data. CSE and Down To Earth document reduced claims, protests and rollback calls among large soybean farmers in Sehore, Vidisha and Bhopal. They also record some farmers' appreciation of timeliness and insurers' claim that manual yields were under-reported. No independent quantified beneficial outcome was found.
Limits
No source gives a quantified result for payout speed or estimate accuracy. Without the true losses, lower payouts alone do not show whether the estimates improved. Faster settlement is distinct from adequate compensation.
Costs and harms
Faster settlement could come with lower payments and reduced farmer confidence when model estimates differed from familiar crop-cutting results.
Later
Scaled up, 18 May 2026. CSE reported in May 2026 that twelve states were implementing YES-TECH with varying weightage, up from nine in January 2025. In 2024-25 farmers and unions in Madhya Pradesh protested low payouts, and some called for a return to manual crop-cutting experiments.

What happened

On 21 July 2023 India's Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare launched the YES-TECH manual. It covers a technology-driven yield estimation system under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), the government crop insurance scheme. The system was piloted in 100 districts. It estimates yields at gram panchayat level for claim assessment. A Press Information Bureau (PIB) release of 17 February 2025 says that YES-TECH was introduced from kharif 2023. It blends technology-based yield estimates with manual crop-cutting experiments (CCEs). A PIB backgrounder of 14 February 2026 says that YES-TECH uses remote sensing and AI-driven analytics. It was introduced for paddy and wheat in kharif 2023 and extended to soybean in kharif 2024 with a minimum 30 percent weightage. As of January 2025 nine states had adopted it, and Madhya Pradesh had transitioned fully. The backgrounder says that this enables timely loss assessment and faster claim settlement, and it gives no figures. A Centre for Science and Environment (CSE) presentation by Chhavi Mahaur at a webinar of 18 May 2026 describes the Madhya Pradesh implementation. The Madhya Pradesh Council of Science and Technology (MPCST) runs it with the National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC) in Hyderabad. Satellite and weather data are run into a machine learning model (the “AI/ML model”) for kharif and a “semi-physical model” for rabi. Insurers use the results to disburse PMFBY claims. Madhya Pradesh is the only major state that gives YES-TECH 100 percent weightage. CSE reported that twelve states were implementing YES-TECH. CSE's field assessment covered some 50 farmers in Sehore, Vidisha and Bhopal. Large soybean farmers reported lower claims than under manual CCEs, and some urged a rollback. Some farmers appreciated faster disbursement. Insurers and officials blamed the lower claims on historically under-reported manual yields. District officials told CSE that claims paid for similar crop losses had become more consistent since the technology came in. They also called the process opaque. Down To Earth reported on 20 August 2026 that kharif payouts in the three districts fell from Rs 217.91 crore to Rs 55.2 crore between 2018 and 2024. It reported that farmers and unions protested in 2024–25. No official document with a quantified beneficial outcome is among the sources.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Satellite and weather data fed seasonal yield models whose estimates were used to calculate crop-insurance claims.

Autonomy

Madhya Pradesh gave the technology estimate full weight in yield assessment, leaving insurers to disburse claims under the program rules.

Workflow

A national YES-TECH manual and guidelines mandating technology-based yield estimates with at least 30 percent weightage. A state Technology Implementation Partner (MPCST) running satellite, weather and ground-truth data through AI/ML and semi-physical models. Ground-truthing points (130,989 in Madhya Pradesh as of August 2025, per CSE) and a YES-TECH committee reviewing periodic reports. Insurers computing claims from estimated actual yield against threshold yield.

Human oversight

CSE reports that a YES-TECH committee reviews periodic reports and that Madhya Pradesh had 130,989 ground-truthing points as of August 2025. Insurers compute claims from the estimated yields. CSE found that farmers in three districts lacked transparency about how input data are validated. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

AI contribution

YES-TECH produced estimates of crop yields that Madhya Pradesh used to calculate insurance payments. The cited CSE presentation describes an AI or machine-learning model for kharif crops.

Benefits

Reach

No source counts the farmers who benefited. CSE's field assessment covered some 50 farmers in three districts, and some of them appreciated faster payment. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

Madhya Pradesh has used YES-TECH since kharif 2023. Nine states had adopted it by January 2025, and CSE reported twelve in May 2026. Farmers and unions in Madhya Pradesh protested low payouts in 2024–25. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Accurate historical yield data, since threshold yields and model training depend on past CCE records (CSE). Open satellite data (Sentinel-1) and weather-station data availability. Farmer trust and transparency about input data validation, which CSE found lacking in three districts. State decision on weightage (100 percent in Madhya Pradesh, 30–50 percent elsewhere).

Purpose

Estimate crop yields from satellite, weather and model data to replace or supplement manual crop-cutting experiments. The aim: insurance losses assessed accurately and claims paid on time. The claimed benefit (faster, more transparent settlement) is that purpose and is contested.
