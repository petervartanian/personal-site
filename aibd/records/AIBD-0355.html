Officials say YES-TECH speeds PMFBY claims; farmers contest cuts

kharif payouts in the three districts fell from Rs 217.91 crore to Rs 55.2 crore

Launched 21 Jul 2023; nine states by Jan 2025; Madhya Pradesh 100% weightage from kharif 2023. PIB claims faster claims; CSE and DTE report lower payouts and rollback calls in three districts.

What happened

On 21 July 2023 India's Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare launched the YES-TECH manual for a technology-driven yield estimation system under the Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY), developed after piloting in 100 districts, to estimate yields at gram panchayat level for claim assessment. A PIB release of 17 February 2025 states YES-TECH was introduced from kharif 2023 to blend technology-based yield estimates with manual crop-cutting experiments (CCEs); a PIB backgrounder of 14 February 2026 says it employs remote sensing and AI-driven analytics, was introduced for paddy and wheat in kharif 2023 and extended to soybean in kharif 2024 with a minimum 30 percent weightage, and that as of January 2025 nine states had adopted it with Madhya Pradesh fully transitioning, which the backgrounder says enables timely loss assessment and faster claim settlement; no figures are given. A Centre for Science and Environment (CSE) presentation by Chhavi Mahaur (webinar of 18 May 2026) describes the Madhya Pradesh implementation by the Madhya Pradesh Council of Science and Technology with NRSC Hyderabad: satellite and weather data are run into an 'AI/ML model' for kharif and a 'semi-physical model' for rabi, the results are used by insurers to disburse PMFBY claims, and Madhya Pradesh is the only major state giving YES-TECH 100 per cent weightage. CSE's field assessment of about 50 farmers in Sehore, Vidisha and Bhopal found large soybean farmers reporting lower claims than under manual CCEs and some advocating a rollback, while some farmers appreciated faster disbursement; insurers and officials attributed lower claims to historically under-reported manual yields. Down To Earth (20 August 2026) reports that kharif payouts in the three districts fell from Rs 217.91 crore to Rs 55.2 crore between 2018 and 2024 and that farmers and unions protested in 2024-25. No official document with a quantified beneficial outcome was fetched.

What it was built to do. Estimate crop yields from satellite, weather and model data to replace or supplement manual crop-cutting experiments so that insurance losses are assessed accurately and claims paid on time; the claimed benefit (faster, more transparent settlement) is that purpose and is contested.

The system's part

Official PIB documents (deployer, not the model's maker) state YES-TECH employs AI-driven analytics and that Madhya Pradesh relies fully on technology-based yield estimates; CSE's presentation states kharif yields come from an 'AI/ML model' whose results are used to disburse claims, so the model output directly determines payouts. Whether this produced a benefit is contested: PIB asserts faster, more transparent settlement without data; CSE and Down To Earth document reduced claims, protests and rollback calls among large soybean farmers in Sehore, Vidisha and Bhopal, alongside some farmers' appreciation of timeliness and insurers' claim that earlier manual yields were under-reported. No independent quantified beneficial outcome was found.

Replication

What produced it

  • A national YES-TECH manual and guidelines mandating technology-based yield estimates with at least 30 percent weightage
  • A state Technology Implementation Partner (MPCST) running satellite, weather and ground-truth data through AI/ML and semi-physical models
  • Ground-truthing points (130,989 in Madhya Pradesh as of August 2025, per CSE) and a YES-TECH committee reviewing periodic reports
  • Insurers computing claims from estimated actual yield against threshold yield

What must hold again

  • Accurate historical yield data, since threshold yields and model training depend on past CCE records (CSE)
  • Open satellite data (Sentinel-1) and weather-station data availability
  • Farmer trust and transparency about input data validation, which CSE found lacking in three districts
  • State decision on weightage (100 per cent in Madhya Pradesh, 30-50 per cent elsewhere)
