Germany deploys IdentiFlight to protect lesser spotted eagles

The OECD AI Incidents Monitor records Germany's deployment of IdentiFlight AI cameras to detect lesser spotted eagles near wind turbines and slow the blades (September 2022); no German outcome data.

ⓘ

What happened

The OECD AI Incidents entry summarizes press coverage of 20 and 21 September 2022 (The Guardian, Mother Jones, The Register and others, not among the sources). It says Germany is deploying AI-powered camera systems developed by IdentiFlight to detect endangered lesser spotted eagles near wind turbines. They slow the blades to prevent fatal collisions, as wind-farm expansion raises the risk to the species. The monitor's rationale cites the 82% reduction reported from the Wyoming study rather than any German measurement. Only the registry page is among the sources; the sites, operators and any fatality data in Germany are not documented.

Purpose. IdentiFlight is built to detect eagles and curtail turbines; protecting lesser spotted eagles at German wind farms is the intended use.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. The deployment is reported through a registry summary of press coverage. No evaluation of the system's effect in Germany was found, and the only effect size cited is the contested Wyoming estimate.

Since then
OekoFor's validation page reports, in an entry dated September 2025 (day not given), that the latest IdentiFlight version is under evaluation in Germany and its recognition model is still being trained, after training on further species in April 2024.The latest version of the IdentiFlight system is being evaluated. At the same time, the underlying AI recognition model is being continuously trained and optimised. source

Further evidence

Setting. Wind-farm expansion into lesser spotted eagle habitat creating a regulatory need for mitigation. Availability of the IdentiFlight system already evaluated in the United States.

Conditions. Site-specific monitoring of eagle fatalities before and after installation. Operators accepting curtailment losses.
