Mia flags a 6 mm cancer missed by NHS Grampian radiologists

depth 3 for a person, depth 2 for an organization

Mia helped find 12% more cancers than routine practice

In NHS Grampian's GEMINI pilot, Mia flagged Barbara's 6 mm breast cancer missed by radiologists; surgery plus five days' radiotherapy.

What happened

Mia, an AI mammography reader developed by Kheiron Medical Technologies, was used alongside radiologists in NHS Grampian's GEMINI breast-screening evaluation in Aberdeen, Scotland, covering 10,889 women. BBC News reported on 21 March 2024 that Barbara, an Aberdeen patient, was one of 11 women whose cancer Mia flagged but hospital radiologists had not spotted on her scan; because her 6 mm tumor was caught so early she had an operation and needed only five days of radiotherapy. Dr Gerald Lip, clinical director of breast screening at NHS Grampian, led the evaluation; a University of Aberdeen statement the same day reported that Mia helped find 12% more cancers than routine practice. The date of Barbara's screening is not given.

What it was built to do. Mia was built to read screening mammograms and flag suspicious findings alongside human readers; detecting a cancer missed by readers is exactly its intended function.

Who benefited

  • 3
    personal

    A 6 mm cancer was treated at an early stage with surgery and a short radiotherapy course; Dr Lip states that cancers picked up under 15 mm carry around 95% survival, so the benefit is potentially life-saving but not demonstrated as such for this patient.

  • 2
    organizational

    Across the 10,889-woman evaluation Mia helped find 12% more cancers, and the University of Aberdeen statement says this would not put more women through additional tests.

The system's part

Mia flagged a lesion that the hospital radiologists had not identified on the same scan; clinicians then confirmed and treated the cancer. Both the BBC and the University of Aberdeen attribute the detection to Mia. The counterfactual is that the cancer would have been found later, at a larger size, rather than never.

Replication

What produced it. Deployment of Mia alongside routine human double reading in a screening program. A safety-net workflow in which AI-flagged, reader-negative cases are re-reviewed.

What must hold again. Clinicians act on AI-only flags rather than discarding them. The AI's recall threshold is set so that extra flags do not overwhelm the service.
