Mia flags a 6 mm breast cancer that NHS Grampian radiologists missed

In NHS Grampian's GEMINI evaluation, Mia flagged a 6 mm breast cancer that radiologists had missed in a patient named Barbara, and she had surgery and needed only five days of radiotherapy.

  • 3 a person

    A 6 mm cancer was treated at an early stage with surgery and a short radiotherapy course. Dr Lip states that cancers picked up under 15 mm carry around 95% survival. The benefit is potentially life-saving but not demonstrated as such for this patient.

  • 2 an organization

    Across the 10,889-woman evaluation Mia helped find 12% more cancers. The University of Aberdeen statement says this would not put more women through additional tests.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
Mia flagged a lesion that the hospital radiologists had not identified on the same scan. Clinicians then confirmed and treated the cancer. Both the BBC and the University of Aberdeen attribute the detection to Mia. The counterfactual is that the cancer would have been found later, at a larger size, rather than never.
Limits
The case shows that an additional cancer was found and treated. It does not show that the patient's cancer would otherwise have caused death. It does not explain why her radiotherapy lasted five days. The GEMINI authors report that three-year follow-up for interval cancers was not yet available.
Costs and harms
The live additional-reading pathway produced 1,345 extra clinical reviews and 55 recalls to find 11 additional cancers. Workload increased by 6%, while recall rates increased by 11.3% relative to routine screening.

What happened

Mia, an AI mammography reader by Kheiron Medical Technologies, was used alongside radiologists in National Health Service (NHS) Grampian's GEMINI breast-screening evaluation. It covered 10,889 women in Aberdeen, Scotland, who were screened between 27 February and 5 October 2023. Dr Gerald Lip, clinical director of breast screening at NHS Grampian, led the evaluation. On 21 March 2024 BBC News described Barbara, an Aberdeen patient. She was one of 11 women whose cancer Mia flagged and hospital radiologists had not spotted on the scan. Her 6 mm tumor was caught so early that she had an operation and needed only five days of radiotherapy. The date of Barbara's screening is not given. A University of Aberdeen statement the same day said that Mia helped find 12% more cancers than routine practice. In the peer-reviewed GEMINI paper, published in Nature Cancer on 10 March 2026, Clarisse de Vries, Gerald Lip and colleagues report the screening dates. They confirm that 11 additional cancers were found with Mia, seven of them invasive and four ductal carcinoma in situ. They report that three-year follow-up for interval cancers was not yet available. Three of the authors were Kheiron employees, and the authors state that the vendor could not access the workspace where the analysis was run.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Mia assessed screening mammograms and flagged lesions for review, including Barbara's six-millimeter tumor that the routine readers had not identified.

Workflow

Deployment of Mia alongside routine human double reading in a screening program. A safety-net workflow in which AI-flagged, reader-negative cases are re-reviewed.

Human oversight

Clinicians re-reviewed AI-flagged scans, confirmed cancer, and selected surgery and radiotherapy.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Barbara received treatment for a small breast cancer, and the evaluation identified ten other women whose cancers routine readers had not spotted.

Reach

Mia helped find cancers in 11 women whom routine double reading had not recalled, among 10,889 women screened. Barbara is one of them. Seven of the 11 cancers were invasive. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

BBC News reported Barbara's treatment on 21 March 2024. No cited source describes her later course. In the GEMINI paper of 10 March 2026, the authors confirm the 11 additional cancers. They report that three-year follow-up for interval cancers was not yet available. [1] [2] [3]

Measurement

Measurement

Mia read every eligible mammogram in the background. Cases that Mia flagged but routine double reading did not recall went to an additional human arbitration review. Cancers were confirmed by histology after surgery or biopsy. Triage workflows were simulated on the same data. [3]

Setting and sample

NHS Grampian breast screening, Aberdeen, Scotland, in a program that invites women aged 50 to 71; 10,889 women screened between 27 February and 5 October 2023. [3]

Measures

Cancer detection rate per 1,000 women, recall rate, reader workload. [3]

Results

11 additional cancers detected beyond the 106 found by routine screening; the primary workflow raised cancer detection by 10.4 percent (1 per 1,000) and kept the recall rate level. [3]

Additional value from AI

The authors released the AI opinion to the arbitration readers only after the routine double reading decision. They argue that this design gives a true reflection of what the AI added to cancer detection. [3]

Limitations

One site and one AI tool. Three-year follow-up for interval cancers was not yet available, so the authors could not assess the true sensitivity of readers and AI. Triage workflows were simulated. Three authors were employed by Kheiron. [3]

Denominators

Mia v3 at a higher-sensitivity operating point, which flagged 12.4 percent of cases (1,345) for additional review. [3]

Materials for replication

The evaluation and statistical analysis plans are in the Supplementary Information, with bootstrapped confidence intervals as Supplementary Data. The R code is on GitHub and Zenodo. Images and data stay in the Grampian Data Safe Haven and are available on approved request. [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Clinicians act on AI-only flags rather than discarding them. The AI's recall threshold is set so that extra flags do not overwhelm the service.

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.
