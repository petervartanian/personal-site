Neural networks recreate the strips cut from The Night Watch in 1715

The Rijksmuseum used neural networks to reconstruct the strips cut from The Night Watch in 1715, and it displayed the painting complete from 23 June 2021.

What happened

On 23 June 2021 the Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam displayed Rembrandt's The Night Watch with reconstructions of the strips cut from all four sides in 1715. The canvas had been trimmed to fit a wall in the Town Hall. Rijksmuseum senior scientist Robert Erdmann trained artificial neural networks on the original and on a seventeenth-century copy attributed to Gerrit Lundens. The networks generated the missing sections in Rembrandt's style. Printed reconstructions were mounted around the original for the following months. The remaining original canvas measures 379.5 by 436 centimeters. The museum gives the result as a reconstruction of 393.1 by 507.4 centimeters, with strips of 64.4 cm on the left, 7 cm on the right, 23.3 cm at the top and 11.3 cm at the bottom. Director Taco Dibbits said: “Thanks to this reconstruction, we can now see that the composition as it was painted by Rembrandt was even more dynamic.” Hyperallergic reported the project independently on 24 June 2021. On 18 August 2026, two scholars at the Repin Academy of Fine Arts in St Petersburg, who have no tie to the museum, published an analysis of the project. They describe the neural network as a tool that extended the expert's analysis, and they argue that the art historian remains responsible for the key professional and ethical tasks. They do not test the accuracy of the reconstruction.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Neural networks used high-resolution images of the original painting and a seventeenth-century copy to generate the missing border sections.

Workflow

A contemporaneous copy showing the lost sections. High-resolution imaging of the original. Neural networks trained to map the copy's style to the original's.

Human oversight

Museum researchers selected the historical reference and mounted printed reconstructions around the original canvas for temporary display.
