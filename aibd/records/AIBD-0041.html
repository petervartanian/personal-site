Rijksmuseum neural networks restore The Night Watch's strips

Rijksmuseum used neural networks to reconstruct The Night Watch's 1715-trimmed edges, displayed complete from 23 June 2021.

What happened

On 23 June 2021 the Rijksmuseum in Amsterdam put Rembrandt's The Night Watch on display with reconstructions of the strips cut from all four sides in 1715, when the canvas was trimmed to fit a wall in the Town Hall. Rijksmuseum senior scientist Robert Erdmann trained artificial neural networks on the original and on a seventeenth-century copy attributed to Gerrit Lundens to generate the missing sections in Rembrandt's style; the printed reconstructions were mounted around the original for the following months. Director Taco Dibbits said: 'Thanks to this reconstruction, we can now see that the composition as it was painted by Rembrandt was even more dynamic.' Hyperallergic reported the project independently on 24 June 2021.

What it was built to do. The networks were built specifically to reconstruct the trimmed sections from the Lundens copy; the display was that purpose.

The system's part

The neural networks generated the pixel content of the missing strips in Rembrandt's style from the Lundens copy; the copy's existence and curatorial choices were preconditions.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • A contemporaneous copy showing the lost sections
  • High-resolution imaging of the original
  • Neural networks trained to map the copy's style to the original's

What must hold again

  • The copy is reliable for composition
  • The museum presents the output as reconstruction, not original
