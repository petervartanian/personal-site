A texting assistant helps more admitted students enroll at Georgia State

AdmitHub’s text-message assistant Pounce raised enrollment by 3.3 percentage points among students already committed to Georgia State in 2016.

What happened

Georgia State University used AdmitHub’s Pounce to help admitted students complete enrollment tasks from April through August 2016. Researchers assigned 7,489 students to the assistant or to usual support. Pounce used university records to send relevant reminders, answered questions and referred unresolved questions to counselors. Among 1,948 students who had already committed to attend, the researchers found that enrollment increased by 3.3 percentage points from a control rate of 84.6%. The overall enrollment effect was not statistically significant. The study evaluated the complete service, including staff assistance. It did not measure degree completion. In two later randomized trials with enrolled students at a two-year and a four-year college, Page, Meyer, Lee and Gehlbach found that chatbot outreach worked best for discrete and time-sensitive tasks such as filing financial aid forms or registering for courses (EdWorkingPaper, August 2023). Georgia State’s research partnership with Mainstay (formerly AdmitHub) continued. In two randomized trials of course-specific Pounce chatbots in introductory courses, Meyer and colleagues found that the chatbot raised the share of students who earned a course grade of B or higher by 5 to 6 percentage points and reduced course drops (EdWorkingPaper 22-564, revised September 2023).

Details

AI system

Model

The authors describe supervised learning with convolutional neural networks and a university-specific knowledge base. [1]

Provider

AdmitHub. [1]

Role

Pounce selected relevant reminders and answered questions about enrollment. [1]

Configuration

The SMS assistant used Georgia State’s student records and knowledge base to tailor messages about unfinished tasks. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Inputs included student questions and completed enrollment tasks. Outputs were text replies, reminders, and requests for staff help. [1]

Autonomy

Pounce sent messages and routine replies automatically. Questions that it could not answer went to university staff. [1]

Workflow

Pounce checked which tasks remained, sent reminders, and answered questions. Counselors handled unresolved questions by email through the platform. [1]

Human oversight

Staff supplied answers and reviewed conversations. Their corrections helped train the system and expand its knowledge base. [1]

Relation to AI

The assistant used machine learning to interpret questions and choose answers. [1]

AI contribution

The trial compared the complete Pounce service with usual enrollment support. It did not isolate machine learning from reminders and staff assistance. [1]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Admitted students who had already committed to attend Georgia State. [1]

Reach

The trial assigned 7,489 students. The subgroup with the reported enrollment gain contained 1,948 students. [1]

Distribution and access

The effect was concentrated among students already committed to Georgia State. The overall enrollment effect was not statistically significant. [1]

Duration

The outcome was enrollment for fall 2016. The trial did not measure degree completion. [1]

Expected?

Reducing the loss of committed students before enrollment was the intended benefit. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

Researchers used university administrative records and National Student Clearinghouse enrollment data. [1]

Setting and sample

There were 3,745 students assigned to Pounce and 3,744 controls. All had a U.S. Mobile number and had agreed to receive texts. [1]

Measures

Enrollment, financial-aid tasks, and other requirements for entering college. [1]

Results

In the committed subgroup, enrollment rose by 3.3 percentage points from a control rate of 84.6%. [1]

Additional value from AI

The trial did not separate automated conversation from personalized reminders and counselor support. [1]

Limitations

The gain applies to the committed subgroup at one university. Overall enrollment rose by 1.2 percentage points, which was not statistically significant. [1]

Denominators

Of students assigned to Pounce, 96.2% received texts, 85.1% replied, and 6.6% opted out. [1]

Money

Kind of value

Platform price and a modeled annual budget. [1]

Amount

The platform cost US$7–15 per student per year, plus staff work. The authors illustrated a Georgia State budget of about US$53,000 a year. [1]

Currency and prices

U.S. Dollars at the prices discussed in the 2017 article. A separate price-base year is not given. [1]

Period and unit

Per student per year, or per university year for the illustrative budget. [1]

Realization

The price range and annual budget are estimates, not an audited statement of Georgia State’s payments. [1]

Recipients and payers

Universities pay for the platform and contribute staff time. [1]

Gross or net

The figures are costs. The paper does not give a measured net financial benefit. [1]

Included costs

The subscription excludes staff time for setup, monitoring, and answers that require a counselor. [1]

Comparison

The authors compare the price with earlier counseling and text-message interventions. Those programs were not cost arms in this trial. [1]

Method

The illustrative annual budget applies a US$15 per-student price. Additional tuition income is discussed but not quantified. [1]

People and places

Participants

AdmitHub built the assistant, Georgia State provided student information and counseling, and academic researchers evaluated enrollment. [1]

Ownership and control

AdmitHub supplied the platform. Georgia State supplied its institutional knowledge and student records. [1]

Dependencies

The service needed SMS access, current student records, a maintained knowledge base, and counselors for unresolved questions. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Universities need accurate task records, students who can receive texts, and staff who can answer unresolved questions. [1]

Replication attempts

Two later Georgia State trials reported 5–6 percentage-point increases in grades of B or higher. Their courses and students differed from this enrollment trial. [3]

Availability

Georgia State offered Pounce through its enrollment-support service during the 2016 trial.

Two later working papers by Page, Meyer and colleagues report separate trials with enrolled students. One concerns time-sensitive tasks and the other course performance. [1] [2] [3]
