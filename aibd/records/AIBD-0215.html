AdmitHub's Pounce chatbot lifts on-time enrollment 3.3 points

off the scale

3.3 percentage points more likely to enroll on time

Page and Gehlbach's field experiment at Georgia State University: students assigned to AdmitHub's conversational AI assistant Pounce were 3.3 points more likely to enroll on time.

What happened

Lindsay Page and Hunter Gehlbach tested Pounce, an artificially intelligent text-message assistant developed by AdmitHub for Georgia State University (GSU), in a field experiment with GSU-committed students during the summer before college, when tasks such as financial-aid paperwork cause 'summer melt'. Their AERA Open article (volume 3, issue 4, October 2017; online 12 December 2017 per Crossref) reports that treated students exhibited greater success with pre-enrollment requirements and were 3.3 percentage points more likely to enroll on time, with enrollment impacts comparable to prior interventions but substantially reduced burden on university staff. In a Brookings piece of 11 September 2018 the two researchers describe the chatbot as answering many questions automatically with machine-learning algorithms and routing a small fraction to counselors. WABE reported on 14 June 2018 that GSU uses Pounce to answer students' questions. The date observed is the article's online publication date; the fetched sources do not state the summer in which the experiment ran or the number of students.

What it was built to do. Pounce was deployed to guide admitted students through pre-enrollment tasks; higher on-time enrollment is that purpose.

The system's part

The chatbot delivered personalized reminders and answered questions automatically, routing only a small fraction to counselors (Brookings); the authors compare the effect to earlier human-delivered outreach, so the gain is in scale and staff burden as much as in the AI itself.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A university knowledge base of enrollment tasks and deadlines behind a text-message assistant. Personalised outreach to each admitted student about the tasks they had not completed. Human counselors receiving the questions the chatbot could not answer.

What must hold again. Students reachable by text message during the summer. A population for whom pre-enrollment tasks, especially financial aid, are the barrier. Staff capacity to handle escalated questions.
