SoundTalks health score falls with high swine influenza A loads

In a blinded study at a German pig nursery, the SoundTalks cough monitor's respiratory health score fell with high swine influenza A loads; no intervention outcome is reported.

  • Not rated an organization

    The monitors were blinded, so no alert was acted on, and the study documents associations without a changed treatment or production outcome.

  • Not rated a person

    No outcome for the farmer or animals is reported.

What happened

Veterinarians at Ludwig-Maximilians-University Munich ran a blinded prospective study on a conventional one-site piglet-producing farm in Germany. Its nursery holds 600 pigs per airspace in 20 pens. Two batches were monitored for 39 days each. Two SoundTalks monitors (SoundTalks NV, Leuven) counted coughs around the clock and distinguished pig coughs from other barn noise. They computed a daily respiratory health status from 0 to 100, with yellow alerts at 40–59 and red at 0–39. The LEDs were set to a blinded “science mode” so that the investigators were not cued. Oral fluids and bioaerosol samples were screened by qPCR for swine influenza A virus, PRRSV, Mycoplasma hyopneumoniae, Actinobacillus pleuropneumoniae and PCV2. All but M. hyopneumoniae were detected. High loads of swine influenza A virus RNA were significantly associated with a decrease in the AI-calculated respiratory health score. Boehringer Ingelheim Vetmedica funded the study, and three of its employees are co-authors. The paper was published on 6 October 2024. In a separate study published on 20 December 2024, researchers at Sunchon National University with no external funding kept 80 pigs in two houses under normal or poor conditions of heat, humidity and gases. The SoundTalks respiratory score averaged 97.55 under normal conditions and 74.41 under poor conditions. In 2026, a trial at Konkuk University in Korea used the same device's respiratory health status as an outcome measure across 2,000 grower-finisher pigs.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Microphones recorded pig sounds continuously, and SoundTalks converted coughing patterns into daily respiratory-health scores.

Workflow

Two SoundTalks monitors in a 600-pig nursery airspace. Parallel oral fluid and bioaerosol qPCR sampling. Circulating swine influenza A virus producing coughing.

Human oversight

The researchers kept the monitors in science mode so that visible alerts did not reveal the automated assessment during observation.

Benefits

Reach

No beneficiary count is reported. The German study monitored two batches in a nursery that holds 600 pigs per airspace. The Sunchon study kept 80 pigs, and the Konkuk trial covered 2,000 grower-finisher pigs. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

Each of the two batches was observed for 39 days.

Measurement

Measurement

Two SoundTalks monitors counted coughs around the clock in a blinded mode; veterinarians scored coughing by hand and screened oral fluids and air samples by qPCR.

Setting and sample

One conventional piglet-producing farm in Germany; a nursery designed for 600 pigs per airspace; two batches, each monitored for 39 days.

Measures

Correlation between the daily AI respiratory health status and the human clinical coughing score; association of pathogen loads with the score.

Results

Spearman's rho of -0.478 (batch 1) and -0.468 (batch 2) between the AI score and the human coughing score; high swine influenza A loads were associated with a lower score.

Limitations

One farm and two batches; association only, with no alert acted on; Boehringer Ingelheim funded the study and three of its employees are co-authors.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Monitors able to separate pig coughs from barn noise. Pathogen loads high enough to depress the score.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2.
