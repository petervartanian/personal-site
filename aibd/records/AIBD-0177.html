VeriPol flags false robbery reports in 2017; police drop it in 2024

The Spanish National Police's VeriPol text classifier flagged 25 false robbery reports in Murcia and 39 in Málaga in a June 2017 pilot week; the police stopped using it on 21 October 2024.

  • 2 an organization

    The developers report that in one pilot week police in Murcia and Málaga detected and closed 25 and 39 false robbery reports. In June of the years 2008 to 2016 the two cities had averaged 3.33 and 12.14 per month. That is a measured gain in a police result. The developers ran the pilot themselves, and independent researchers note that the paper gives no case counts or confusion matrix.

  • Not rated society

    No source measures any effect on insurance fraud, on police workload or on people wrongly flagged.

The system’s part
The peer-reviewed paper describes a natural-language-processing and machine-learning classifier with L1 feature selection that outputs the probability a report is false. The paper's authors include the police developer. AlgorithmWatch and Civio report that no independent verification was done before the police stopped using it in October 2024.
Limits
The pilot comparison lacks a contemporaneous matched control and relies on cases classified as false after investigation or confession. The record does not independently validate ground truth, time savings, or fairness, and later withdrawal limits claims of continuing operational benefit.

Later
Withdrawn, 21 October 2024. The Interior Ministry told Civio that the National Police stopped using VeriPol on 21 October 2024. It said that the tool lacks validity in judicial proceedings, and it refused to release usage data.

What happened

VeriPol, a model for detecting false robbery reports from their text, was developed by researchers at the Complutense and Carlos III universities with police officer Miguel Camacho-Collados. A pilot study in Murcia and Málaga ran for one week in June 2017. The model appeared in Knowledge-Based Systems (Quijano-Sánchez, Liberatore, Camacho-Collados and Camacho-Collados, 2018). The abstract reports a validation accuracy of 91% on more than 1,000 reports from 2015, an improvement of more than 15% over expert officers. It reports that the pilot study showed a lower bound on empirical precision of about 83%. Civio reported that the Interior Ministry announced nationwide use in 2018. Scientific American (1 February 2019) reported that the one-week pilot in Murcia and Málaga detected 25 and 39 false robberies respectively, against 3 and 12 in the same month over the previous decade. It quoted Camacho Collados on colleagues wasting time on cases that never occurred. According to Civio, the police analyzed 49,702 complaints in 2019 and deemed 2,338 of them false. AlgorithmWatch (27 October 2020) found no independent verification of effectiveness since deployment. Civio reported that in 2022 the police analyzed 3,762 complaints and deemed 511 of them false. In a University of Valencia report of September 2024, Lucía Martínez Garay and colleagues criticized the training sample of 1,122 reports, the missing officer training and the missing regional language variation. They found that the 2017 pilot paper gave neither case counts nor a confusion matrix. The police told them that VeriPol still needed an evaluation of its performance that had not been possible to carry out. The Interior Ministry told Civio that the police stopped using VeriPol on 21 October 2024. Civio (25 March 2025) reported that the Ministry said that the police stopped because the tool was not valid in judicial proceedings. Civio also reported that the police said that in 83.54% of reports flagged false the complainant confessed.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

VeriPol scored the text of robbery reports for the probability that a report was false, using selected language features and heuristic rules.

Workflow

Training on 1,122 robbery reports from 2015 (534 genuine, 588 false) labeled by one experienced officer. Text features selected by L1 penalization plus heuristic rules, scoring reports at the police station.

Human oversight

VeriPol gave officers the probability that a report was false to support police decisions. The authors of the University of Valencia report faulted the missing officer training. The Interior Ministry said that the tool was not valid in judicial proceedings. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Benefits

Reach

The sources count false reports detected: 25 in Murcia and 39 in Málaga in the pilot week, 2,338 of 49,702 complaints in 2019 and 511 of 3,762 in 2022. No source counts the officers or the people who benefited. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

The Interior Ministry announced nationwide use in 2018, and the police stopped using VeriPol on 21 October 2024. The pilot result covers one week in June 2017, and no source reports a later evaluation of its performance. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Measurement

Measurement

Two expert officers installed VeriPol at police stations in each city, trained local officers and supervised its use for one week. A report counted as false only when the complainant confessed. [1]

Setting and sample

Urban police stations in Murcia and Málaga, Spain, one week in June 2017; the number of reports checked is not given. [1]

Measures

False robbery reports detected and closed; share of interrogated complainants who confessed. [1]

Results

25 and 39 in one week against monthly averages of 3.33 and 12.14; confession rates of 81.58% in Murcia and 84.78% in Málaga, which the authors give as a lower bound on precision of 83.54%. [1]

Additional value from AI

Not isolated: the experts who installed the tool also trained local officers and supervised all activity during the pilot week. [1]

Limitations

The developers, one of them a police officer, ran the pilot. It lasted one week in two cities, and it compares one week with whole-month historical averages. The paper reports no case counts or confusion matrix. Confessions under interrogation stand in for ground truth. A satisfaction survey drew 21 participants. [1]

Denominators

One week of use in two cities. [1]

Materials for replication

The paper lists the model's text features and weights; the 2015 training reports and the pilot data are not public. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Reports written in the same language register as the training data (the Valencia report faulted missing regional variation). Officer training and protocols for acting on scores. Independent field evaluation, absent before discontinuation.

Replication attempts

3 follow-up entries. Outcome unresolved: 2. Benefit did not recur: 1.

Availability

The Spanish National Police stopped using VeriPol on 21 October 2024 after nationwide deployment.

Evidence

Original evidence

The pilot figures trace to the developers' 2018 paper, whose authors include Miguel Camacho-Collados, the police officer among its developers. The usage counts for 2019 and 2022 and the 2024 discontinuation come from the police and the Interior Ministry through Civio. On 1 April 2025 AlgorithmWatch published an article by the same Civio authors. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]
