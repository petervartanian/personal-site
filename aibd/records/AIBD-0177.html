What happened

VeriPol, a model for detecting false robbery reports from their text, was developed by researchers from the Complutense and Carlos III universities with police officer Miguel Camacho-Collados and published in Knowledge-Based Systems (Quijano-Sánchez, Liberatore, Camacho-Collados and Camacho-Collados, 2018); the abstract reports a validation accuracy of 91% on more than 1,000 reports from 2015, an improvement of more than 15% over expert officers, and a pilot study in June 2017 showing a lower bound on empirical precision of about 83%. Scientific American (1 February 2019) reported that the one-week pilot in Murcia and Málaga detected 25 and 39 false robberies respectively, against 3 and 12 in the same month over the previous decade, and quoted Camacho Collados on colleagues wasting time on cases that never occurred. Civio (25 March 2025) reported that the police said that in 83.54% of reports flagged false the complainant confessed, that the Interior Ministry announced nationwide use in 2018, that 49,702 complaints were analyzed in 2019 with 2,338 deemed false and 3,762 in 2022 with 511 deemed false, and that use stopped on 21 October 2024 because the tool was not valid in judicial proceedings; a University of Valencia report (September 2024) criticized the 1,122-report training sample, missing officer training and regional language variation. AlgorithmWatch (27 October 2020) found no independent verification of effectiveness since deployment. The pilot month carries no day, so Scientific American's publication date is used.

What it was built to do. Estimate the probability that a robbery report is false from its text to support police decisions.

The system's part

The peer-reviewed paper describes a natural-language-processing and machine-learning classifier with L1 feature selection that outputs the probability a report is false; the paper's authors include the police developer, and AlgorithmWatch and Civio report that no independent verification was done before the police stopped using it in October 2024.

Replication

What produced it. Training on 1,122 robbery reports from 2015 (534 genuine, 588 false) labeled by one experienced officer. Text features selected by L1 penalization plus heuristic rules, scoring reports at the police station.

What must hold again. Reports written in the same language register as the training data (the Valencia report faulted missing regional variation). Officer training and protocols for acting on scores. Independent field evaluation, absent before discontinuation.
