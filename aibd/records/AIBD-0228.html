Nigeria's biometric matching flags 2,780,756 registrations as ineligible

Nigeria's electoral commission reported on 26 October 2022 that its Automated Biometric Identification System (ABIS) flagged 2,780,756 (22.6%) of 2021–22 registrations as ineligible.

What happened

On 12 September 2022, Festus Okoye, a commissioner of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), said that 1,126,359 of 2,523,458 registrants (28 June 2021 to 14 January 2022) were found invalid and delisted. Punch reported that ABIS processing of later registrations was ongoing. The case is dated to 26 October 2022, when INEC's chairman, Professor Mahmood Yakubu, addressed political parties in Abuja. The Nigerian Democratic Report reproduced the address. He said that the Continuous Voter Registration data (28 June 2021 to 31 July 2022) was cleaned with the Automated Biometric Identification System (ABIS). In this cleaning, 2,780,756 registrations (22.6%) were identified as ineligible and invalidated. Among them were double or multiple registrants, underage persons and fake registrations that failed INEC's business rules. That left 9,518,188 valid registrations after ABIS cleaning. INEC also identified 23 registration officers for sanctions. On 20 January 2023 BusinessDay argued that some fake and underage registrants evaded ABIS and that invalidation rates were regionally lopsided. The European Union Election Observation Mission reported on 27 June 2023 that more than 2.8 million double, underage and fake registrations were removed. It called this a positive development. It also reported that stakeholders widely acknowledged that underage registrants remained, in unknown numbers, and that the register has never been audited. The mission recommended an independent audit that includes the quality of biometric data. On 5 October 2026 INEC's chairman said that ABIS had again screened the registrations made for the 2027 elections. The sources do not name the vendor of ABIS.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

ABIS processed voter-registration biometrics and business-rule checks to identify duplicate or otherwise ineligible registrations for removal.

Workflow

Biometric capture (fingerprints, face) at registration with a central de-duplication pass. Business rules defining ineligible entries applied after matching. Public display of the register for claims and objections.

Human oversight

The sources describe no human review of individual ABIS matches. INEC's chairman said that the register would be displayed publicly for claims and objections by citizens before the election. INEC identified 23 registration officers for sanctions. The European Union observer mission reported that the register has never been audited. [1] [2] [3] [4]
