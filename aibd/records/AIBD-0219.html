Kyukyu VoiceTra translates for ambulance crews across Japan

Kyukyu VoiceTra, a speech-translation app for ambulance crews, reached 690 of Japan's 720 fire headquarters by 1 January 2025; more than 70% of Sapporo crews said that it sped up communication.

What happened

NICT, the National Institute of Information and Communications Technology, announced on 18 April 2017 that Kyukyu VoiceTra would be provided to fire headquarters nationwide from that month. The speech-translation app for ambulance crews was developed with the Fire and Disaster Management Agency (FDMA) and its Fire Research Center on NICT's VoiceTra engine. It adds medical terms to improve the accuracy of free-text translation. It also adds 46 fixed phrases for emergency scenes in 15 languages. The Sapporo Fire Department had used it since February 2017. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications' 2017 white paper covers the research and development, part of the action plan toward 2020. It records completion as Kyukyu VoiceTra in April 2017 and the start of provision to all fire headquarters. Its download figures (205,421 by the end of fiscal 2015 and 621,221 by the end of fiscal 2016) are for the general VoiceTra app. The trade publication risktaisaku.com (8 May 2017) described the Android release and a planned iOS version. It also described on-screen text that helps communication with hearing-impaired patients. The Fire Research Center and NICT developers wrote in September 2017 that more than 70% of Sapporo ambulance crews said in a survey that the app made communication faster and better than existing methods. More than 90% wanted to keep using it. The developers also wrote that 180 fire headquarters had started using the app by 7 August 2017. Their own field tests of the general VoiceTra app had found that translated exchanges took about twice as long as exchanges in Japanese, which led them to add fixed phrases. Ninomiya Town's fire service announced its own adoption on 6 December 2022. By 1 January 2025, 690 of Japan's 720 fire headquarters (95.8%) used the app, according to the Fire and Disaster Management Agency. No cited source documents an outcome for a patient.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Ambulance crews entered speech or selected an emergency phrase, and the app returned spoken and written translations in a supported language.

Workflow

A general-purpose national speech-translation engine (VoiceTra) adapted with domain vocabulary. Fixed phrases for the commonest emergency-scene exchanges, with touch-screen answers translated back. Free distribution by the national fire agency to local fire headquarters.

Human oversight

Ambulance crews operate the app at the scene and can choose fixed phrases, which reduce reliance on free-text translation. No independent evaluation of its accuracy is among the sources. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Benefits

Reach

No source counts foreign patients who were helped. The counts are of fire headquarters that use the app: 180 by 7 August 2017 and 690 of 720 on 1 January 2025. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Distribution and access

The service could assist patients who spoke one of its 15 languages and patients who could read the displayed text when spoken communication was difficult.

Duration

Uptake grew from 180 fire headquarters in August 2017 to 690 of 720 (95.8%) on 1 January 2025, according to the Fire and Disaster Management Agency. No source repeats the crews' survey in a later year. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Measurement

Measurement

A survey of ambulance crews by the Sapporo Fire Bureau after trial use, asking whether speed and quality of communication changed compared with existing methods. [5]

Setting and sample

Sapporo Fire Bureau ambulance crews who used the app from January 2017; the number of respondents is not given. [5]

Measures

Share of crews who said that speed and quality of communication improved, and share who wanted to keep using the app. [5]

Results

More than 70% said that both speed and quality improved; more than 90% wanted to keep using the app. [5]

Additional value from AI

Not separated: the survey compares the whole app, including its 46 fixed phrases, with earlier methods. [5]

Limitations

Self-reported views of crews in one city, reported by the developers; no count of respondents; Sapporo crews had used the app since development, as the authors note; no patient outcome. [5]

Denominators

Trial use from January 2017, including 29 cases in Sapporo. [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Smartphones available to ambulance crews. Coverage of the patient's language among the 15 supported. Crew training (several municipal pages describe drills).

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Outcome unresolved: 1.

Availability

Distribution to fire headquarters nationwide began in April 2017; Sapporo and Ninomiya have documented adoption.
