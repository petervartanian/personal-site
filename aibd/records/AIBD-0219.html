Kyukyu VoiceTra translates for ambulance crews across Japan

depth unassessed

From April 2017 NICT and the FDMA provided Kyukyu VoiceTra (VoiceTra with medical terms and 46 fixed phrases, 15 languages) to fire headquarters across Japan; Sapporo used it from February 2017.

What happened

NICT announced on 18 April 2017 that Kyukyu VoiceTra would be provided to fire headquarters nationwide from that month. The speech-translation app for ambulance crews was developed with the Fire and Disaster Management Agency (FDMA) and its Fire Research Center on NICT's VoiceTra engine. It adds medical terms to improve free-text translation accuracy and 46 fixed phrases for emergency scenes in 15 languages. The Sapporo Fire Department had used it since February 2017. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications' 2017 white paper covers the R&D, part of the action plan toward 2020. It records completion as Kyukyu VoiceTra in April 2017 and the start of provision to all fire headquarters. Its download figures (205,421 by end-FY2015 and 621,221 by end-FY2016) are for the general VoiceTra app.

The trade publication risktaisaku.com (8 May 2017) described the Android release, a planned iOS version and on-screen text that also helps communication with hearing-impaired patients. Ninomiya Town's fire service announced its own adoption on 6 December 2022. No cited source documents a patient-level outcome or a count of adopting headquarters.

What it was built to do. Built to let ambulance crews communicate with foreign patients quickly at emergency scenes. The nationwide provision is that purpose, without a documented outcome.

The system's part

The app performs speech recognition and translation at the scene. Fixed phrases reduce reliance on free-text translation. Developer (NICT, a public research institute) and the agency that distributes it (FDMA) are both public bodies. The deploying fire headquarters are local governments. No independent evaluation of accuracy or outcomes is among the sources.

Replication

What produced it. A general-purpose national speech-translation engine (VoiceTra) adapted with domain vocabulary. Fixed phrases for the commonest emergency-scene exchanges, with touch-screen answers translated back. Free distribution by the national fire agency to local fire headquarters.

What must hold again. Smartphones available to ambulance crews. Coverage of the patient's language among the 15 supported. Crew training (several municipal pages describe drills).
