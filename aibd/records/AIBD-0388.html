Ghanaian researchers propose smartphone AI to detect malaria parasites

CSIR-INSTI and Bolgatanga Technical University propose a smartphone-over-microscope TinyML model to help health workers detect malaria parasites; they have published no field test or accuracy result.

What happened

The ITU entry, current as of June 2025, names the CSIR Institute for Scientific and Technological Information (CSIR-INSTI) and Bolgatanga Technical University in Ghana as the developers. The project combines microscope imagery, a smartphone and a compact malaria-detection model built with TinyML, a method for running machine-learning models on small devices. Its cited testbed is the Lacuna Malaria Detection Challenge, and the team plans further data collection with local clinics. The proposed benefit is diagnostic support for community health workers. The code repository that the team cites holds only a README created on 29 May 2025. The team has published no field test or accuracy result for the system, and no source reports a field diagnosis or a treatment outcome.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

A smartphone would photograph blood slides through a microscope eyepiece. A compact model would identify malaria parasites and white blood cells in the images.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

People and places

Dependencies

The proposed setup required a microscope, stable phone mount, sufficient camera quality, battery power, and a trained model that could run on the phone.
