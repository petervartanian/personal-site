GPT-4 flags a cause of a dog's anemia that a second vet then confirms

ChatGPT Plus running GPT-4 listed immune-mediated hemolytic anemia among causes of a dog's worsening anemia; a second veterinarian confirmed it, and the owner reported an almost full recovery.

The system’s part
From the owner's typed description and two sets of blood results, GPT-4 explained the trend in the bloodwork. When asked what else could underlie the anemia, it listed candidate causes including IMHA. The owner chose IMHA to put to a second veterinarian, who confirmed it after further tests and treated the dog. Whether that vet would have reached IMHA unprompted is unknown. The owner himself notes IMHA is a common complication of babesiosis, so the counterfactual is unproven. The owner reported that GPT-3.5 did not reach the diagnosis. He said the tool “should be a tool to be used by professionals”.
Limits
The owner reports that GPT-4 listed IMHA and that a second veterinarian confirmed and treated it. No veterinarian or medical record corroborates the account in the material on record. Recovery after treatment does not establish that the dog would otherwise have died or that the diagnosis depended on AI.

What happened

A Twitter user, Cooper (@peakcooper), had a Border Collie, Sassy, that had been treated for babesiosis but worsened. She had pale gums and more severe anemia. The first vet found no cause and advised waiting. He entered two sets of bloodwork into ChatGPT Plus running GPT-4. It said that it was not a veterinarian, then listed possible underlying causes including immune-mediated hemolytic anemia (IMHA). At a second clinic he asked about IMHA. The vet agreed after further tests, treatment began, and Cooper reported an almost full recovery. He wrote about it on Twitter on 25 March 2023. GPT-4 reached ChatGPT Plus subscribers on 14 March 2023, so the consultation took place between 14 and 25 March 2023. The New York Post, India Today and Business Insider reported the thread (27 to 30 March). Cooper did not respond to Insider, and no veterinarian has spoken publicly.

Details

AI system

Model

GPT-4.

Role

GPT-4 interpreted blood-test results and suggested possible causes of the dog’s anemia.

Inputs and outputs

The owner entered two sets of blood results and the dog's illness and treatment history into GPT-4, which listed possible causes of worsening anemia, including IMHA.

Workflow

The owner had two sets of actual blood results plus the babesiosis diagnosis and treatment history. He entered them, with which tests had been run, into GPT-4 via ChatGPT Plus rather than GPT-3.5. The first vet had no working diagnosis and advised waiting, which left the owner looking for a second opinion. A second veterinarian was willing to test for the owner-raised hypothesis and confirmed it before treating.

Human oversight

The owner took the IMHA hypothesis to a second veterinarian, who reportedly performed further tests and selected treatment.

Conditions for replication

A licensed clinician confirms the model's suggestion with tests before any treatment is started. The model's output is treated as a hypothesis to raise with a clinician, not as a diagnosis, consistent with its own disclaimer. The underlying data entered are real laboratory results, not a lay description of symptoms alone.
