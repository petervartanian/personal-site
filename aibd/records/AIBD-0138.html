GPT-4 lists IMHA among causes of a dog's anemia; vet confirms

depth 2 for a person

GPT-4 listed IMHA among causes of a dog's worsening anemia; a second vet confirmed and treated; the dog recovered, per the owner's thread.

What happened

A Twitter user, Cooper (@peakcooper), wrote on 25 March 2023 that his Border Collie, Sassy, had been treated for babesiosis but worsened, with pale gums and more severe anemia, while the first vet found no cause and advised waiting. He entered two sets of bloodwork into ChatGPT Plus running GPT-4, which said it was not a veterinarian, then listed possible underlying causes including immune-mediated hemolytic anemia (IMHA). At a second clinic he asked about IMHA; the vet agreed after further tests, treatment began, and Cooper reported an almost full recovery. The New York Post, India Today and Business Insider reported the thread (27 to 30 March); Cooper did not respond to Insider, and no veterinarian has spoken publicly.

What it was built to do. GPT-4, served through ChatGPT, is a general-purpose conversational model built to answer questions and follow instructions; answering a diagnostic question about a dog's bloodwork is an instance of that general use, not a designed veterinary function, and the model itself prefaced its answer by saying it is not a veterinarian.

The system's part

From the owner's typed description and two sets of blood results, GPT-4 explained the trend in the bloodwork and, when asked what else could underlie the anemia, listed candidate causes including IMHA; the owner chose IMHA to put to a second veterinarian, who confirmed it after further tests and treated the dog. Whether that vet would have reached IMHA unprompted is unknown, and the owner himself notes IMHA is a common complication of babesiosis, so the counterfactual is unproven. The owner reported that GPT-3.5 did not reach the diagnosis and said the tool 'should be a tool to be used by professionals'.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. The owner had two sets of actual blood results plus the babesiosis diagnosis and treatment history, and entered them, with which tests had been run, into GPT-4 via ChatGPT Plus rather than GPT-3.5. The first vet had no working diagnosis and advised waiting, which left the owner looking for a second opinion. A second veterinarian was willing to test for the owner-raised hypothesis and confirmed it before treating.

What must hold again. A licensed clinician confirms the model's suggestion with tests before any treatment is started. The model's output is treated as a hypothesis to raise with a clinician, not as a diagnosis, consistent with its own disclaimer. The underlying data entered are real laboratory results, not a lay description of symptoms alone.
