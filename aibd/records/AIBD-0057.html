People with low vision complete more daily tasks with OrCam readers

In independent studies of 6 to 100 participants, people scored higher on reading and daily tasks with OrCam readers; in a Dutch study only three of 20 patients kept using the device.

  • 2 a person

    Study participants showed measured gains in reading and daily-living task scores. In the 2015 study, 12 patients went from 2.5 to 9.5 of 10 tasks with the device. These are test results, and no lasting change in a person's life is reported. In the Dutch study only three of 20 patients kept using the device.

  • Not rated society

    The authors tested usability in cohorts at several sites and report no societal outcome that can be attributed to the devices.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The devices' computer-vision recognition of text, faces and colors is the function measured in every study. Comparisons were against no aid or existing low-vision aids. The 2016 test instrument was non-validated, so effects are task-level rather than clinical outcomes.
Limits
The studies involve different devices, conditions, and task measures. Small cohorts and short follow-up limit generalization, and improvements on reading or recognition tasks do not by themselves establish sustained independence or a combined pooled effect.
Costs and harms
In the Dutch study the patients who stopped using the device most often cited other text-to-speech tools such as Seeing AI, the price or missing features.
Later
Persisted, 3 April 2026. Independent clinics kept testing the devices through 2026, when an NIH team reported that all nine children with CLN3 disease completed the basic tasks. A Dutch study found that 17 of 20 patients did not keep using the device; the reasons that they gave most often were other text-to-speech tools such as the Seeing AI app, the price and missing features.

What happened

The authors of independent clinical studies of OrCam's wearable and handheld text-and-object readers report gains for groups of users and attribute no outcome to an individual. Between 1 July and 30 September 2015, 12 patients with acuity of 20/200 or worse in an ophthalmology department in the United States improved on a 10-item daily-living test from 2.5 to 9.5 with the device at the first visit. They scored 9.8 after a week of use at home (JAMA Ophthalmology, 2016). In a Dutch study of 20 patients with advanced retinal dystrophies (Acta Ophthalmologica, 26 September 2021), the authors found that the OrCam MyEye 2.0 improved near-activity and reading scores after about five weeks. Only three patients kept using it, and the others most often cited other text-to-speech tools such as Seeing AI, the price or missing features. Six patients with stroke or brain tumors at the University of California, Davis, used OrCam Read for a month in 2021 (Neuro-Ophthalmology, 2024). The authors of a five-center study of 100 users found that reading and face recognition improved (Journal of Medical Systems, 2023). At the National Institutes of Health (NIH), nine children with CLN3 disease completed all level-A text, color and face tasks by day five (Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases, 2026). OrCam MyEye 2 and OrCam Read are listed with the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as class I electronic vision aids that are exempt from premarket review, so no FDA finding on benefit exists.

Details

AI system

Role

The devices recognized text and objects in camera images and described them aloud.

Configuration

The studies used different OrCam products, including MyEye, MyEye 2, and Read.

Inputs and outputs

The devices converted camera images of text, faces, colors, or objects into spoken information that users could hear.

Workflow

Camera-based OCR and object recognition with audio output. Short in-clinic training followed by home use. Task batteries simulating daily living.

Human oversight

Users activated the device and chose the text or objects to inspect. Study staff taught them how to operate it, and the month-long OrCam Read study included weekly technical support.

Benefits

Duration

The earliest cohort was tested at the first visit and after a week of use at home. The Dutch authors measured gains after about five weeks. The stroke and brain-tumor cohort used OrCam Read for a month. The children with CLN3 disease completed the level-A tasks by day five. Only three of the 20 Dutch patients kept using the device. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Study 1: Non-validated 10-item test that simulates daily-living tasks. Study 2: Dutch NEI-VFQ, Participation and Activity Inventory and an OrCam Function Questionnaire at baseline and after about five weeks. [2] [5]

Setting and sample

Study 1: 12 patients with best-corrected acuity of 20/200 or worse in a US ophthalmology department, 1 July to 30 September 2015. Study 2: 20 Dutch patients with retinitis pigmentosa or cone-rod dystrophy and acuity of 20/400 or worse. [2] [5]

Measures

Study 1: Number of the 10 tasks completed. Study 2: Questionnaire subscale scores, above all near activities and reading. [2] [5]

Results

Study 1: Mean 2.5 tasks without the device, 9.5 with it at the first visit and 9.8 after a week of home use. Study 2: Significant gains on the near-activities and reading measures; 3 of 20 patients kept using the device afterward. [2] [5]

Additional value from AI

Study 1: The full gain is attributed to the device, since the comparison is the same patients without it. [2] [5]

Limitations

Study 1: Small sample, no control group, a test that is not validated and one week of follow-up. Study 2: Small sample, no control group and self-reported measures; 17 of 20 patients stopped using the device and most often cited other text-to-speech tools such as Seeing AI, the price or missing features. [2] [5]

Denominators

Study 1: One clinic session and one week of home use. Study 2: About five weeks of use. [2] [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Adequate lighting and pointing ability. Device training provided by clinicians. Users with residual ability to aim the camera.

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.

Evidence

Original evidence

Each of the five cited studies comes from an independent clinical group. The 2016 and 2024 studies share the author Mannis. OrCam lent the devices for the Dutch study, and its authors state that the company had no role in the study. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
