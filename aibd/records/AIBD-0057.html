OrCam readers raise daily-living test scores from 2.5 to 9.5

improved on a 10-item daily-living test from 2.5 to 9.5

Independent cohorts (12, 100, 6 and 10 participants) show OrCam devices raising reading and daily-task scores; no individual outcome.

What happened

Independent clinical studies of OrCam's wearable and handheld text-and-object readers report cohort gains. In a US ophthalmology department between 1 July and 30 September 2015, 12 patients with acuity of 20/200 or worse improved on a 10-item daily-living test from 2.5 to 9.5 with the device at the first visit and 9.8 after a week of use at home (JAMA Ophthalmology, 2016). A five-centre study of 100 users found improved reading and face recognition (Journal of Medical Systems, 2023). Six UC Davis patients with stroke or brain tumours used OrCam Read for a month in 2021 (Neuro-Ophthalmology, 2024), and at the NIH nine children with CLN3 disease completed all level-A text, colour and face tasks by day five (Orphanet Journal of Rare Diseases, 2026). No individual outcome is attributed.

What it was built to do. Read text and recognise faces, objects and colours aloud for people with visual impairment

The system's part

The devices' computer-vision recognition of text, faces and colours is the function measured in every study; comparisons were against no aid or existing low-vision aids, and the 2016 test instrument was non-validated, so effects are task-level rather than clinical outcomes.

Replication

What produced it

  • Camera-based OCR and object recognition with audio output
  • Short in-clinic training followed by home use
  • Task batteries simulating daily living

What must hold again

  • Adequate lighting and pointing ability
  • Device training provided by clinicians
  • Users with residual ability to aim the camera
