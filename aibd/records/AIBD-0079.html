After a RapidAI stroke alert, doctors reopen a firefighter's artery fast

RapidAI's large vessel occlusion alert prompted a physician to mobilize the stroke team for Eric, 29; thrombectomy restored flow under 60 minutes from arrival; no deficits.

  • 3 a person

  • 2 an organization

    Dr Mehta states that the platform streamlines workflow and cuts time compared with the previous need to open scans at workstations.

The system’s part
Not isolated. RapidAI's automated LVO detection delivered the alert that prompted Dr Mehta to mobilize the cath lab team and fast-track thrombectomy. Under the Memorial stroke protocol EMS had already pre-notified the hospital and the neurointerventional team was ready before imaging. The occlusion would have been identified on the CT scan regardless. So the AI's contribution is speed at the point of care, and its share of the time saved cannot be separated from the pre-notification. The only counterfactual offered is Dr Mehta's general description of the pre-RapidAI workflow.
Limits
The hospital account reports rapid reperfusion and the patient's absence of deficits. It does not isolate time saved by RapidAI from emergency pre-notification, imaging, staffing, and thrombectomy. A personal success story cannot establish the proportion of similar patients helped.

What happened

Memorial Healthcare System began a RapidAI trial at Memorial Regional Hospital in 2017, according to its release. It then extended the platform to Memorial Hospital West, Memorial Hospital Miramar and Memorial Hospital Pembroke. Eric, a 29-year-old firefighter and paramedic, felt unwell after a workout. He tried to send a text and could not move his left thumb, then his left leg went numb. His trainer noticed that he was slurring and called 9-1-1. Emergency medical services (EMS) took him to Memorial Hospital West's Comprehensive Stroke Center. Under the Memorial stroke protocol the paramedics had already alerted the hospital. The neurointerventional surgery team was ready to do a computed tomography (CT) scan and treat in the catheterization laboratory. As soon as scan results were available, Dr Brijesh Mehta received the RapidAI alert on his smartphone and smartwatch. It showed the software's automated detection of a large vessel occlusion (LVO). The alert prompted him to mobilize the catheterization laboratory team and fast-track Eric for mechanical thrombectomy. The blocked artery was fully opened and blood flow restored under 60 minutes from his arrival. Eric described himself as alive with no deficits. The hospital published the account on 12 May 2022. The release gives neither the date nor the city of Eric's stroke, and it gives no measured time saved by the alert.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

RapidAI analyzed the CT scan for a large-vessel occlusion and sent the finding to the neurointerventionalist's smartphone and smartwatch.

Workflow

RapidAI LVO detection pushing alerts to the on-call neurointerventionalist's phone and watch. A comprehensive stroke center with an in-house thrombectomy team.

Human oversight

Paramedics first alerted the stroke center. Dr. Mehta used the imaging alert to mobilize the catheterization team, which performed thrombectomy.

Benefits

Reach

The case concerns one patient. The release says that RapidAI had identified more than 8,400 strokes across Memorial hospitals by May 2022, and it does not say how many of those patients benefited. [1]

Duration

No later report on Eric or on Memorial's RapidAI program was found, so whether his recovery lasted is not known. The release, published on 12 May 2022, quotes him describing himself as alive with no deficits. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Rapid recognition of stroke signs and EMS transport to a thrombectomy-capable center. Team mobilization on the AI alert rather than after a formal radiology read.
