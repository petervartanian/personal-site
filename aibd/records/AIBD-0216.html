Stanford's Tutor CoPilot lifts math mastery 4 percentage points

What happened

Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb and Demszky of Stanford built Tutor CoPilot, a language-model system that gives human tutors expert-like suggestions during live sessions. They tested it with FEV Tutor, a virtual tutoring provider, and a large southern U.S. school district serving over 30,000 students. Nine schools took part. The district identified 1,787 students in grades 3–8, 80% Hispanic, who had performed below grade level. The preprint (arXiv, 3 October 2024) says the study started at the end of March 2024 and lasted two months. The 900 tutors were evenly split between treatment and control. Students of treated tutors were 4 percentage points more likely to master topics. Students of lower-rated tutors gained 9 points, at about $20 per tutor per year.

Education Week (31 October 2024), quoting co-author Susanna Loeb, reported that tutors using the tool were more likely to prompt students to explain. They were less likely to give away solutions. The 74 (7 October 2024) and MIT Technology Review (28 October 2024) reported that the system runs on OpenAI's GPT-4. They gave pass rates of 66% versus 62% on exit assessments and quoted outside researchers' caveats about the basic level of the mathematics. The preprint says the study started at the end of March 2024.

What it was built to do. Tutor CoPilot was built to scale expert tutoring guidance to novice tutors in real time. Higher student mastery is that purpose.

The system's part

The model advised the human tutor and never taught the student directly. The measured change runs through tutor behavior (more prompting for explanations, fewer given-away solutions). Outside researchers quoted by MIT Technology Review note the mathematics was relatively basic.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. A language model prompted with a taxonomy of expert tutoring moves rather than answers. Integration into a live virtual-tutoring platform where tutors can request suggestions mid-session. Tutor-level randomization (900 tutors) with preregistered analysis.

What must hold again. Human tutors willing to use suggestions in real time. Basic-level mathematics content (outside researchers' caveat). Students below grade level in under-served schools, the population studied.
