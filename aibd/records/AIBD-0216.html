Stanford's Tutor CoPilot lifts math mastery 4 percentage points

off the scale

students of treated tutors were 4 percentage points more likely to master topics

RCT with 900 tutors and 1,800 grade 3-8 students in a southern US district: students whose tutors had Tutor CoPilot were 4 points more likely to master topics; 9 points for lower-rated tutors.

What happened

Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb and Demszky of Stanford built Tutor CoPilot, a language-model system that gives human tutors expert-like suggestions during live sessions, and tested it with FEV Tutor, a virtual tutoring provider, and a large southern U.S. school district serving more than 30,000 students; nine schools took part and the district identified 1,787 students in grades 3-8, 80% Hispanic, who had performed below grade level. The preprint (arXiv, 3 October 2024) states that the study started at the end of March 2024 and lasted two months, with 900 tutors evenly split between treatment and control; students of treated tutors were 4 percentage points more likely to master topics, and students of lower-rated tutors gained 9 points, at about $20 per tutor per year. Education Week (31 October 2024) reported that tutors using the tool were more likely to prompt students to explain and less likely to give away solutions, quoting co-author Susanna Loeb; The 74 (7 October 2024) and MIT Technology Review (28 October 2024) reported that the system runs on OpenAI's GPT-4, gave pass rates of 66% versus 62% on exit assessments, and quoted outside researchers' caveats about the basic level of the mathematics. The date observed is the first day of the month in which the trial began, used as a placeholder; the preprint says the study started at the end of March 2024.

What it was built to do. Tutor CoPilot was built to scale expert tutoring guidance to novice tutors in real time; higher student mastery is that purpose.

The system's part

The model advised the human tutor and never taught the student directly; the measured change runs through tutor behavior (more prompting for explanations, fewer given-away solutions). Outside researchers quoted by MIT Technology Review note the mathematics was relatively basic.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A language model prompted with a taxonomy of expert tutoring moves rather than answers. Integration into a live virtual-tutoring platform where tutors can request suggestions mid-session. Tutor-level randomization (900 tutors) with preregistered analysis.

What must hold again. Human tutors willing to use suggestions in real time. Basic-level mathematics content (outside researchers' caveat). Students below grade level in under-served schools, the population studied.
