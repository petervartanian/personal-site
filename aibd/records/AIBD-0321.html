ABAIR's Irish voices power an NVDA screenreader for blind pupils

SLPAT 2022 paper by the ABAIR team (Trinity College Dublin): Irish NVDA plugin with Braille output, NCBI-funded, built with a blind researcher, tested with visually impaired pupils; no dated outcome.

ⓘ

What happened

Ní Chasaide, Barnes, Ní Chiaráin, McGuirk, Morrin, Nic Corcráin and Cummins of the ABAIR initiative described three assistive applications of ABAIR's Irish speech technology. This was at the Ninth Workshop on Speech and Language Processing for Assistive Technologies (SLPAT-2022, 27 May 2022). The first is “A screenreading facility for Irish has been developed as a plugin for the NVDA screenreader, prompted by urgent pleas from parents and grandparents of visually impaired children in the Gaeltacht or attending Irish-medium schools”. Only three books existed in Irish Braille for them. “With funding from the National Council for the Blind in Ireland (NCBI) a blind researcher worked with the ABAIR team to develop the NVDA screenreader plugin”. It adds Braille output through the Liblouis translator and lets users choose a male or female voice and the reading speed.

The paper states “The system was extensively tested with visually-impaired school children and a full technical description is available in McGuirk (2005)”. The reference list has McGuirk 2015, an M.Phil. dissertation. It names no child, school, date or measured result and describes the voices as text-to-speech synthesis without stating the synthesis method. No source independent of the developers was found.

Purpose. Built so that blind and visually impaired pupils can hear and read Irish text through a screenreader with Braille output. Use by school children was that intended purpose.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. The evidence is limited to the developers' account. The ABAIR team's SLPAT 2022 paper says the Irish NVDA plugin with their synthetic voices was built with a blind researcher. It was extensively tested with visually impaired school children, without any outcome data. The paper does not describe the synthesis method. So neither the system's AI character nor its contribution to any pupil's reading can be assessed from cited sources.

Further evidence

Setting. Multidialect Irish text-to-speech voices from the ABAIR initiative, funded under the 20-Year Strategy for the Irish Language. A blind researcher co-developing the NVDA plugin with NCBI funding, with Liblouis Braille output.

Conditions. Users and their support networks (teachers, family) are involved in design and testing, which the authors state as a guiding principle. The free NVDA screenreader is the pupil's platform.
