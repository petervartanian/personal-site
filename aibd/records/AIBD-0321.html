ABAIR's Irish voices power an NVDA screenreader for blind pupils

off the scale

SLPAT 2022 paper by the ABAIR team (Trinity College Dublin): Irish NVDA plugin with Braille output, NCBI-funded, built with a blind researcher, tested with visually impaired pupils; no dated outcome.

What happened

Ní Chasaide, Barnes, Ní Chiaráin, McGuirk, Morrin, Nic Corcráin and Cummins of the ABAIR initiative described, at the Ninth Workshop on Speech and Language Processing for Assistive Technologies (SLPAT-2022, 27 May 2022, the date used here), three assistive applications of ABAIR's Irish speech technology, the first being 'A screenreading facility for Irish has been developed as a plugin for the NVDA screenreader, prompted by urgent pleas from parents and grandparents of visually impaired children in the Gaeltacht or attending Irish-medium schools', for whom only three books existed in Irish Braille. 'With funding from the National Council for the Blind in Ireland (NCBI) a blind researcher worked with the ABAIR team to develop the NVDA screenreader plugin', which adds Braille output through the Liblouis translator and lets users choose a male or female voice and the reading speed. The paper states 'The system was extensively tested with visually-impaired school children and a full technical description is available in McGuirk (2005)' (the reference list gives McGuirk 2015, an M.Phil. dissertation). It names no child, school, date or measured result, and describes the voices as text-to-speech synthesis without stating the synthesis method. No source independent of the developers was found in this pass.

What it was built to do. Built so that blind and visually impaired pupils can hear and read Irish text through a screenreader with Braille output; use by school children was that intended purpose.

not assessed Only the developers' own account could be fetched: the ABAIR team's SLPAT 2022 paper says the Irish NVDA plugin with their synthetic voices was built with a blind researcher and extensively tested with visually impaired school children, without any outcome data. The paper does not describe the synthesis method, so neither the system's AI character nor its contribution to any pupil's reading can be assessed from fetched sources.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Multidialect Irish text-to-speech voices from the ABAIR initiative, funded under the 20-Year Strategy for the Irish Language. A blind researcher co-developing the NVDA plugin with NCBI funding, with Liblouis Braille output.

What must hold again. Users and their support networks (teachers, family) are involved in design and testing, which the authors state as a guiding principle. The free NVDA screenreader is the pupil's platform.
