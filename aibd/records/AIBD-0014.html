Google's neural network finds an eighth planet around the star Kepler-90

A deep convolutional neural network built by Google and the University of Texas at Austin found Kepler-90i, an eighth planet around the star Kepler-90, in archival Kepler data.

  • 2 an organization

    NASA's Paul Hertz said that discoveries were lurking in archived Kepler data awaiting the right tool. The archive yielded two newly validated planets without new telescope observations by the authors. Their validation used Kepler data and follow-up observations that other observers had already uploaded to the Kepler Community Follow-up Program.

  • 2 society

    Astronomy gained a validated eighth planet and the first exoplanetary system known to have eight planets, tying the Solar System. The gain is knowledge for a specialist community of astronomers.

What happened

Christopher Shallue (Google Brain) and Andrew Vanderburg (University of Texas at Austin, NASA Sagan Fellow) built a deep convolutional neural network to classify Kepler transit signals. It was trained on about 15,000 previously vetted Kepler signals and correctly identified true planets and false positives 96 percent of the time in testing. It was then applied to 670 known multi-planet systems to search for weak transit signals missed by earlier methods. In archival Kepler Space Telescope data it identified an eighth planet around Kepler-90, Kepler-90i. Kepler-90i is a rocky planet about 30 percent larger than Earth with a 14.4-day orbit. It was statistically validated together with Kepler-80g, an Earth-sized sixth planet in a resonant chain. The authors posted their paper on arXiv on 13 December 2017. On 14 December 2017 NASA announced that the network had identified the planet. On the same day Science News reported that Jeff Coughlin of the SETI Institute, who was not an author, explained that earlier programs detected exoplanet signals with hand-coded rules. The peer-reviewed paper appeared in the Astronomical Journal on 30 January 2018. On 25 March 2019 the same group reported that it had adapted the network as AstroNet-K2 for the K2 mission and had used it to identify and validate two previously unknown exoplanets. Astronomers who were not among the authors wrote of Kepler-90 as an eight-planet system in 2025. As of 29 October 2026 the NASA Exoplanet Archive lists Kepler-90 i as a confirmed planet.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The network classified folded stellar light curves and ranked weak transit candidates in 670 known multi-planet systems.

Workflow

Large labeled set of vetted transit signals and false positives. Convolutional network trained on folded light curves. Application to systems with known planets to look for weaker signals. Statistical validation of high-confidence candidates.

Human oversight

The astronomers assessed the model's candidates and used conventional statistical validation to rule out alternative explanations before identifying the planets.
