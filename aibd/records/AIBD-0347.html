Analysts help Indonesia seize the blacklisted fishing vessel MV NIKA

Indonesia seized the MV NIKA on 12 July 2019 after INTERPOL tracking aided by OceanMind and Global Fishing Watch; no source names AI software or documents an AI detection.

What happened

The MV NIKA was suspected of illegal fishing in the Southern Ocean. Its owner also owned the blacklisted STS 50, which was intercepted in Indonesia in 2018. Global Fishing Watch says that its analyst Imam Prakoso helped track the vessel from February 2019, after it illegally switched off its tracking system. INTERPOL tracked the vessel's movement with support from the UK not-for-profit OceanMind and sent daily reports to Indonesia, New Zealand, Panama, the UK and the US. As the vessel approached Indonesian waters, hourly updates aided by Global Fishing Watch followed. Global Fishing Watch says that a live multi-way communication between Indonesian naval and fishery officials, INTERPOL and its analyst Charles Kilgour exchanged information. Its analysts helped predict the vessel's course and identify rendezvous points. Indonesian patrol boats ORCA 2 and ORCA 3 intercepted it on 12 July 2019 at 08:06 local time. Agence France-Presse reported on 16 July 2019 that the navy had intercepted the vessel on 12 July in the Strait of Malacca. Citing the Jakarta Post, it reported that 18 Russians and 10 Indonesians were aboard. The fisheries minister said that INTERPOL had sought the vessel in many countries. INTERPOL reported on 22 July 2019 that Indonesian authorities had detained the MV NIKA, and Global Fishing Watch published its account on 23 July 2019. In 2020 the Commission for the Conservation of Antarctic Marine Living Resources listed NIKA (IMO number 8808654) as an illegal fishing vessel for fishing without authorization on 8 June 2019. With the Indonesian Supreme Court's verdict of 27 February 2020, the captain was convicted, the owner fined Rp300,000,000 and the vessel forfeited. This court outcome comes from a UK Government Blue Belt blog of 5 June 2021, which was no longer available as of 20 October 2026. The commission's 2025/26 list still names the vessel. None of the sources describes a machine-learning or AI detection. OceanMind's homepage says only, in general terms, that it uses satellites, AI and human expertise.

AI system

Workflow

Analysts followed the vessel, shared increasingly frequent updates, and helped Indonesian officials choose an interception point for patrol boats.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

A coastal state willing and able to intercept and prosecute. Analyst capacity to follow a vessel that has switched off its tracking system.
