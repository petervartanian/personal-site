Veesion alert may have led police to a Szeged shoplifter

depth 2 for an organization

Independently reported, 5 sources.

AI cameras in Szeged's unstaffed-at-night Coop flagged a shopper hiding goods; police caught them within hours, the operators said.

What happened

Coop Szeged Zrt. and Laurel Kft. opened the 247 Shop on Szent István tér in Szeged at the end of May 2025. It is staffed by day, automated and unstaffed outside normal hours. Cameras running the French company Veesion's software analyze shoppers' movements without identifying faces. They alert a remote dispatch service to gestures such as concealing goods. On 9 October 2025 the operators issued a press release relayed by Index.hu and MTI. It said over four and a half months the system had exposed three theft attempts. In one, a person hid products under their clothing during night mode. Police caught the perpetrator within hours using the footage. Dates, recovered goods and any police statement are not reported.

What it was built to do. Veesion's gesture-recognition software is built to spot shoplifting movements such as concealing goods and to raise an immediate alert. In Laurel's 247 Shop it is the loss-prevention layer for unstaffed operation. Flagging a concealment was its intended use.

The system's part

Not assessed. By the operators' account, the Veesion analysis flagged the shopper's concealment gesture in real time and alerted the remote dispatcher. The camera footage let police catch the perpetrator within hours. No police statement, loss figure or description of what the dispatcher did at the moment of the alert was found. Whether the theft was stopped in progress or only documented, and whether the goods came back, is unknown. The claim rests on a joint press release by the deployer and its integrator, relayed by independent outlets.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Gesture-recognition analysis watching every shopper during the hours when no staff are present, tuned to concealment movements rather than faces. A remote dispatch service that receives the alert immediately, and camera footage that can be handed to police. A sales floor small enough (150 square meters, 4,000 SKUs) for the cameras to cover it entirely.

What must hold again. The flagged gesture is a real theft rather than an innocent movement. The operators report no false-alarm figures. Police are willing and able to act on the footage quickly, here within hours. The system keeps analyzing movements without identifying faces, as the operators describe it.
