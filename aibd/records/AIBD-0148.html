Veesion alert leads police to a Szeged shoplifter, operators say

depth 2 for an organization

over four and a half months the system had exposed three theft attempts

AI cameras in Szeged's unstaffed-at-night Coop flagged a shopper hiding goods; police caught them within hours, the operators said.

What happened

Coop Szeged Zrt. and Laurel Kft. opened the 247 Shop on Szent István tér in Szeged at the end of May 2025: staffed by day, automated and unstaffed outside normal hours. Cameras running software from the French company Veesion analyze shoppers' movements without identifying faces and alert a remote dispatch service to gestures such as concealing goods. On 9 October 2025 the operators announced, in a press release relayed by Index.hu and MTI, that over four and a half months the system had exposed three theft attempts; in one, a person hid products under their clothing during night mode and police caught the perpetrator within hours using the footage. Dates, recovered goods and any police statement are not reported.

What it was built to do. Veesion's gesture-recognition software is built to spot shoplifting movements such as concealing goods and to raise an immediate alert; in Laurel's 247 Shop it is the loss-prevention layer for unstaffed operation, so flagging a concealment was its intended use.

Who benefited

  • 2
    organizational

    The store operator gained a flagged concealment, footage that led police to the perpetrator within hours, and three exposed attempts over four and a half months of unstaffed night trading; the operators also say shrinkage did not rise. All figures are the companies' own, and no loss amount or recovery is reported.

The system's part

not assessed By the operators' account, the Veesion analysis flagged the shopper's concealment gesture in real time and alerted the remote dispatcher, and the camera footage let police catch the perpetrator within hours. No police statement, loss figure or description of what the dispatcher did at the moment of the alert was found, so whether the theft was stopped in progress or only documented, and whether the goods came back, is unknown; the claim rests on a joint press release by the deployer and its integrator, relayed by independent outlets.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Gesture-recognition analysis watching every shopper during the hours when no staff are present, tuned to concealment movements rather than faces. A remote dispatch service that receives the alert immediately, and camera footage that can be handed to police. A sales floor small enough (150 square meters, 4,000 SKUs) for the cameras to cover it entirely.

What must hold again. The flagged gesture is a real theft rather than an innocent movement; the operators report no false-alarm figures. Police are willing and able to act on the footage quickly, here within hours. The system keeps analyzing movements without identifying faces, as the operators describe it.
