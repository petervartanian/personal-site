Veesion cameras flag a Szeged shoplifter; police catch them in hours

The operators said that Veesion's gesture-recognition software flagged a shopper hiding goods in their Szeged store at night, and that police caught the perpetrator within hours.

What happened

Coop Szeged Zrt. and Laurel Kft. opened the 247 Shop on Szent István tér in Szeged at the end of May 2025. It is staffed by day, automated and unstaffed outside normal hours. Cameras running the French company Veesion's software analyze shoppers' movements without identifying faces. They alert a remote dispatch service to gestures such as concealing goods. On 8 October 2025 the Szeged daily Délmagyar reported that a security guard had been placed in the store after closing because of thefts. Thieves had followed customers in and out after the customers opened the door with the app. A thief known to police came in at least twice and was caught quickly with camera footage. The operators said that the guard was temporary while the system was fine-tuned, and that the rate of abuse was no higher than in traditional shops. On 9 October 2025 the operators issued a press release, relayed by Index.hu and MTI. It said that over four and a half months the system had exposed three theft attempts. In one, a person hid products under their clothing during night mode. Police caught the perpetrator within hours using the footage. Dates, recovered goods and any police statement are not reported. The operators also said that 8,500 people had shopped in the automated night system in four and a half months, and that shrinkage had not risen despite the longer opening hours. On 30 September 2026 the two companies opened a second store that is unstaffed after hours, a full supermarket on Temesvári körút, after more than 27,000 unstaffed purchases in a year at the first store. The report on the opening describes camera monitoring and a remote dispatch service and does not name Veesion.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Veesion analyzed shopping movements in camera footage and alerted remote staff to suspected concealment. The stored footage then supported a police report.

Workflow

Gesture-recognition analysis watching every shopper during the hours when no staff are present, tuned to concealment movements rather than faces. A remote dispatch service that receives the alert immediately, and camera footage that can be handed to police. A sales floor small enough (150 square meters, 4,000 SKUs) for the cameras to cover it entirely.

Human oversight

A remote dispatch service received the alert. The store operator reported the incident, and police used the footage to locate the suspected thief.

AI contribution

By the operators' account, the Veesion software flagged a shopper's concealment gesture and alerted the remote dispatcher. Police then caught the perpetrator within hours from the camera footage. Délmagyar's report of a thief caught quickly credits the footage and does not mention an alert. No source separates what the alert added from what recorded footage alone would have given police. Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The flagged gesture is a real theft rather than an innocent movement. The operators report no false-alarm figures. Police are willing and able to act on the footage quickly, here within hours. The system keeps analyzing movements without identifying faces, as the operators describe it.
