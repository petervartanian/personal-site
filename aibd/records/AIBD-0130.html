Google alerts Crete phones seconds before the M6.0 Kasos quake

depth 1 for a person

Android users in Crete and the Dodecanese got Google's quake alert seconds before the M6.0 (Greek press 6.1) Kasos quake of 14 May 2025.

What happened

At 01:51 local time on Wednesday 14 May 2025 (22:51 UTC on 13 May) a magnitude 6.0 (USGS; Greek outlets 6.1) earthquake struck 20 km south-south-east of Fry on Kasos, between Kasos and Karpathos, at 76 km depth; USGS logged 931 felt reports and a green alert level. Newsbomb, GizChina Greece, in.gr and Alphafreepress reported on 14 and 15 May that Google's Android Earthquake Alerts System, which in Europe detects quakes from phone accelerometers rather than seismographs, had notified users in Crete, the Dodecanese and farther away seconds before the shaking, by message and an audible signal similar to the 112 alert. None of the reports names a recipient, gives a lead time in seconds, or mentions damage or injuries.

What it was built to do. Built to detect earthquakes from the accelerometers of Android phones, combine the signals on Google's servers and push an alert to users in the expected shaking zone ahead of the slower seismic waves, for quakes of magnitude 4.5 and above; this alert was that intended function.

The system's part

If the reports are right, the alert was produced end to end by Google's system: accelerometer triggers from many phones combined on Google's servers to detect the quake, then alerts pushed ahead of the slower waves, as Google's 2020 description and the 2025 Science paper set out. Google's own pages do not use the words AI or machine learning; the Science abstract speaks of smartphone-based earthquake detection algorithms, and the OECD Monitor classifies the system as AI doing event detection. That alerts arrived seconds before shaking rests on unattributed statements in four Greek outlets published within a day of the quake; none quotes a recipient, a seismologist or Google, and no Google per-event confirmation was found.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A magnitude 6.0 (USGS) quake above the system's 4.5 alert threshold, centered offshore south of Kasos at about 76 km depth, so a push alert could outrun the slower waves to Crete and the Dodecanese. Enough Android phones near the epicentre with the Earthquake Alerts setting on to trigger Google's server-side detection; in Europe the system uses the accelerometers in phones instead of seismographs (in.gr). Receiving phones with location (GPS) enabled and an internet connection, which Alphafreepress lists as requirements for the alerts.

What must hold again. The server's magnitude and location estimate must be right and early: the 2025 Science paper reports that only 36% of alert recipients got the alert before shaking. Recipients must know what to do with seconds of notice; the Greek reports document no protective action by anyone who received the alert. The phone must have the Earthquake Alerts setting enabled (Alphafreepress: Settings > Safety & Emergency > Earthquake Alerts) and the feature must be supported in the region.
