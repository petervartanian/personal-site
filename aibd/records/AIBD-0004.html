Brain implant decodes a woman's attempted speech at 62 words per minute

A Stanford speech neuroprosthesis used a recurrent neural network and a 125,000-word language model to decode Pat Bennett's attempted speech into text at 62 words per minute.

Configuration

Inputs and outputs

The speech decoder used recordings from the two arrays in ventral premotor cortex. An RNN predicted phonemes, and a 125,000-word language model assembled text.

Later findings

A Stanford-led study in Cell reported that T12 was one of four participants whose inner speech was decoded, with data from post-implant days 412 to 995. Real-time decoding of her imagined sentences on a 50-word vocabulary reached a 24% word error rate. The study reports that she still communicated mainly with a writing board or an iPad, so it shows continued research use and no everyday use. [5]
