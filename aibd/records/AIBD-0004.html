Brain implant decodes a woman's attempted speech at 62 words per minute

A Stanford speech neuroprosthesis used a recurrent neural network and a 125,000-word language model to decode Pat Bennett's attempted speech into text at 62 words per minute.

  • 2 a person

    One identifiable person communicated from a large vocabulary at conversational pace in research sessions. The team called the work a proof of concept. The authors of a 2025 study report that she still communicated mainly with a writing board or an iPad.

The system’s part
An RNN decoder plus an n-gram language model is the mechanism translating neural activity into text. The Stanford neurosurgeon describes the language model as “a sophisticated auto-correct”. The benefit is attributable to the decoding system.

What happened

At Stanford University, a participant with bulbar-onset amyotrophic lateral sclerosis took part in the BrainGate2 trial. News reports identify her as Pat Bennett, 68. Stanford's release dates her surgery to 29 March 2022, when four 64-electrode arrays went into ventral premotor cortex (area 6v) and area 44. The release says that research sessions began about a month after the surgery. A recurrent neural network converted her neural activity into phoneme probabilities, and a 125,000-word language model assembled sentences. She produced text at 62 words per minute. The word error rate was 9.1% on a 50-word vocabulary and 23.8% on the full vocabulary. The paper dates sessions by post-implant day (119, 136 and 141 for the reported data) and gives no calendar dates. The Stanford release says that 62 words per minute came after four months, which places the result in about August 2022. Willett and colleagues reported the results in Nature on 23 August 2023. The team described the work as a proof of concept in research sessions. In a Nature commentary of the same day, Nick Ramsey and Nathan Crone, who were not part of the study, called this result and the result from the University of California, San Francisco, a turning point for restoring communication. On 14 August 2025 a Stanford-led team reported in Cell that she took part in later inner-speech decoding sessions from post-implant day 412 to day 995. Real-time decoding of her imagined sentences on a 50-word vocabulary reached a 24% word error rate. The authors report that she still communicated mainly with a writing board or an iPad.

Details

AI system

Model

A five-layer recurrent neural network with gated recurrent units, combined with a trigram language model.

Role

The decoder and language model converted neural activity into text.

Configuration

Four arrays, each with 64 electrodes, recorded activity in cortical areas 6v and 44. The language model used a vocabulary of 125,000 words.

Inputs and outputs

The speech decoder used recordings from the two arrays in ventral premotor cortex. An RNN predicted phonemes, and a 125,000-word language model assembled text.

Workflow

Preserved articulatory code for phonemes in ventral premotor cortex years after loss of speech. Four intracortical arrays in speech-related cortex. RNN decoder plus large-vocabulary language model trained on the participant's attempted speech. Regulated trial setting (BrainGate2) with research support.

Human oversight

Researchers collected 260–480 prompted sentences at the start of each evaluation day and retrained the decoder using those and earlier recordings. Data collection and training averaged 140 minutes, including breaks.

Benefits

Reach

One person benefited: the single participant in this study, a woman with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis in the BrainGate2 trial at Stanford. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The result of 62 words per minute comes from sessions on post-implant days 119, 136 and 141. The authors of the 2025 study in Cell report that she kept taking part in decoding sessions from post-implant day 412 to day 995. They also report that she still communicated mainly with a writing board or an iPad. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Real-time decoding of attempted speech of held-out sentences, scored by word error rate and words per minute. [1]

Setting and sample

One woman with bulbar-onset ALS in the BrainGate2 pilot trial at Stanford, research sessions on post-implant days 119 to 141 (late July to mid August 2022). [1]

Measures

Word error rate and words per minute. [1]

Results

62 words per minute; word error rate 9.1% on a 50-word vocabulary and 23.8% on a 125,000-word vocabulary. [1]

Limitations

One participant, and the authors are the developers. The team called it a proof of concept, and the sessions were supervised research sessions. [1]

Materials for replication

Neural data for 10,850 attempted sentences are public on Dryad (doi:10.5061/dryad.x69p8czpq). [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Array signal quality remains adequate. Participant can attempt speech and tolerate recording sessions. Decoder retraining available across sessions.

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.

Availability

The system was an implanted research proof of concept used in supported sessions, not a generally available communication product.
