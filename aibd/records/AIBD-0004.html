Stanford neuroprosthesis decodes Pat Bennett's speech at 62 wpm

produce text at 62 wpm, 23.8% word error rate

Four 64-electrode microelectrode arrays and an RNN with a 125,000-word language model let Pat Bennett (ALS) produce text at 62 wpm, 23.8% word error rate

What happened

At Stanford University, a participant with bulbar-onset amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (identified in news reports as Pat Bennett, 68) had four 64-electrode arrays implanted in ventral premotor cortex (area 6v) and area 44 under the BrainGate2 trial; Stanford's release dates the surgery to 29 March 2022. A recurrent neural network converted her neural activity into phoneme probabilities and a 125,000-word language model assembled sentences: she produced text at 62 words per minute, with a 9.1% word error rate on a 50-word vocabulary and 23.8% on the full vocabulary (Willett et al., Nature, 23 August 2023). The work was described as a proof of concept in research sessions. The paper dates its sessions by post-implant day (119, 136 and 141 for the reported data) rather than by calendar date; the Stanford release says research sessions began about a month after the 29 March 2022 surgery and that 62 words per minute was reached after four months, which places the result in about August 2022, so 1 August 2022 is used as the observation date.

What it was built to do. Built to restore communication by decoding attempted speech from intracortical recordings into text; large-vocabulary text communication was the intended benefit and is what occurred.

The system's part

An RNN decoder plus an n-gram language model is the mechanism translating neural activity into text; the Stanford neurosurgeon describes the language model as 'a sophisticated auto-correct'. The benefit is attributable to the decoding system.

Replication

What produced it

  • Preserved articulatory code for phonemes in ventral premotor cortex years after loss of speech
  • Four intracortical arrays in speech-related cortex
  • RNN decoder plus large-vocabulary language model trained on the participant's attempted speech
  • Regulated trial setting (BrainGate2) with research support

What must hold again

  • Array signal quality remains adequate
  • Participant can attempt speech and tolerate recording sessions
  • Decoder retraining available across sessions
