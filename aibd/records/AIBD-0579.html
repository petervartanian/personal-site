COMPOSER sepsis alerts are linked to lower mortality

At UC San Diego, mortality after COMPOSER deployment was 9.49%, versus a modeled 11.39% without it.

Benefits

Benefit

Sepsis mortality after deployment was lower than the study’s modeled estimate for care without COMPOSER. [1]

Beneficiary groups

People.

Beneficiaries

Adults with sepsis in the Hillcrest and La Jolla emergency departments. [1]

Reach

The study analyzed 6,217 sepsis encounters. Its estimate of 22 additional survivors comes from the counterfactual model. [1]

Distribution and access

The mortality difference was statistically significant at Hillcrest but not at La Jolla. The two hospitals served different patient populations. [1]

Duration

The post-deployment evaluation ran from 7 December 2022 through 30 April 2023. Mortality was measured during hospitalization. [1]

Expected?

Yes

Costs and harms

Nurses received about 235 alerts per month across both sites. Unnecessary treatment among patients without sepsis, and its costs or harms, were not evaluated. [1]

AI system

System

COMPOSER with a nurse-facing alert in Epic. [1] [3]

Model

COMPOSER, a feedforward neural network with conformal prediction. [1] [3]

Release

The deployment report does not give a release or checkpoint identifier. [1] [3]

Provider

University of California San Diego. [1] [3]

Role

COMPOSER estimated sepsis risk over the next four hours and reported the measurements that contributed to each alert. [1] [3]

Configuration

The system assessed risk over the next four hours and issued nurse alerts in Epic. Repeat alerts were suppressed for eight or twelve hours after a response. [1]

Inputs and outputs

COMPOSER analyzed clinical measurements and their timing to estimate sepsis risk and identify the main contributing factors. [1] [3]

Autonomy

Nurses acknowledged the alert and could notify a physician. Clinicians decided on tests and treatment. [1] [3]

Workflow

Continuous analysis of electronic patient data and alerts delivered to nursing staff. Nurse review, physician assessment, and treatment at UC San Diego Medical Center and Jacobs Medical Center.

Human oversight

Nurses reviewed an alert and chose whether to notify a physician, note that care was already underway, or record that infection was unlikely. Physicians decided on treatment. [1]

Relation to AI

COMPOSER analyzed patient data and alerted nurses to possible sepsis. Nurses reviewed patients with physicians, who selected treatment. The outcome concerns AI-assisted clinical care.

AI contribution

COMPOSER analyzed patient data and alerted nurses to possible sepsis. Nurses reviewed patients with physicians, who selected treatment. The outcome concerns AI-assisted clinical care.

Measurement

Studies

UCSD IRB 805726 [1]

Study design

A prospective, nonrandomized before-and-after study. A Bayesian time-series model estimated what would have happened without deployment. [1]

Measurement

Researchers used electronic records to identify Sepsis-3 cases, inpatient deaths, organ dysfunction, and completion of the sepsis care bundle. [1]

Setting and sample

The analysis included 6,217 eligible sepsis encounters: 5,065 before deployment and 1,152 afterward. [1]

Measures

In-hospital mortality, completion of the sepsis care bundle, and change in organ dysfunction over 72 hours. [1]

Comparison

A modeled post-deployment counterfactual based on the historical period, with adjustment for measured differences. [1]

Results

Mortality was 9.49%, versus 11.39% in the modeled comparison: a difference of 1.9 percentage points (95% CI, 0.3–3.5). [1]

Additional value from AI

The estimate concerns COMPOSER, nurse alerts, preparation, and subsequent care together. It does not isolate the prediction model from those changes. [1]

Limitations

There was no randomized or concurrent untreated group. Unmeasured changes in care could affect the comparison. Results covered five months at one health system. [1]

Denominators

The historical period ran from 1 January 2021 to 6 December 2022. Deployment began on 7 December 2022. [1]

Materials for replication

The supplement describes implementation and statistical methods. De-identified data and analysis code are available only through the authors and institutional approval. [1]
