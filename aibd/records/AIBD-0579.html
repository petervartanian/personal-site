Fewer sepsis patients die after COMPOSER alerts UC San Diego nurses

After UC San Diego Health deployed COMPOSER, a deep-learning model that alerts nurses to sepsis, mortality was 9.49%, against a modeled 11.39% without it.

Brief from Peter

COMPOSER, a deep-learning model that flags possible sepsis to nurses, went live in two emergency departments at UC San Diego Health on 7 December 2022. The evaluation covered 6,217 adults with sepsis seen before and after that date. Mortality after deployment was 9.49%, against a modeled 11.39% without COMPOSER, an adjusted reduction of 1.9 percentage points with a 95% confidence interval of 0.3–3.5. Raw mortality before deployment was 10.3%. The comparison rests on historical controls, with no randomization and no concurrent untreated group. The estimated benefit reached statistical significance at only one of the two hospitals.1 A meta-analysis by Yale authors, published on 13 May 2025, pooled seven randomized trials of other sepsis alert systems and found no significant effect on mortality.2 The COMPOSER team reported in August 2026 that the alerts stayed in use in both emergency departments through October 2025, with a large language model layer added in July 2024. The mortality result is an association, reported by the team that built COMPOSER and the health system that deployed it.

Details

AI system

Model

COMPOSER, a feedforward neural network with conformal prediction. [1] [3]

Provider

University of California San Diego. [1] [3]

Role

COMPOSER estimated sepsis risk over the next four hours and reported the measurements that contributed to each alert. [1] [3]

Configuration

The system assessed risk over the next four hours and issued nurse alerts in Epic. Repeat alerts were suppressed for eight or twelve hours after a response. [1]

Inputs and outputs

COMPOSER analyzed clinical measurements and their timing to estimate sepsis risk and identify the main contributing factors. [1] [3]

Autonomy

Nurses acknowledged the alert and could notify a physician. Clinicians decided on tests and treatment. [1] [3]

Workflow

Continuous analysis of electronic patient data and alerts delivered to nursing staff. Nurse review, physician assessment, and treatment at UC San Diego Medical Center and Jacobs Medical Center.

Human oversight

Nurses reviewed an alert and chose whether to notify a physician, note that care was already underway, or record that infection was unlikely. Physicians decided on treatment. [1]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Adults with sepsis in the Hillcrest and La Jolla emergency departments. [1]

Reach

The study analyzed 6,217 sepsis encounters. Its estimate of 22 additional survivors comes from the counterfactual model. [1]

Distribution and access

The mortality difference was statistically significant at Hillcrest but not at La Jolla. The two hospitals served different patient populations. [1]

Duration

The post-deployment evaluation ran from 7 December 2022 through 30 April 2023. Mortality was measured during hospitalization. The COMPOSER team reported in August 2026 that the alerts remained in use in both emergency departments through October 2025. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Researchers used electronic records to identify Sepsis-3 cases, inpatient deaths, organ dysfunction, and completion of the sepsis care bundle. [1]

Setting and sample

The analysis included 6,217 eligible sepsis encounters: 5,065 before deployment and 1,152 afterward. [1]

Measures

In-hospital mortality, completion of the sepsis care bundle, and change in organ dysfunction over 72 hours. [1]

Results

Mortality was 9.49%, versus 11.39% in the modeled comparison: a difference of 1.9 percentage points (95% CI, 0.3–3.5). [1]

Additional value from AI

The estimate concerns COMPOSER, nurse alerts, preparation, and subsequent care together. It does not isolate the prediction model from those changes. [1]

Limitations

There was no randomized or concurrent untreated group. Unmeasured changes in care could affect the comparison. Results covered five months at one health system. [1]

Denominators

The historical period ran from 1 January 2021 to 6 December 2022. Deployment began on 7 December 2022. [1]

Materials for replication

The supplement describes implementation and statistical methods. De-identified data and analysis code are available only through the authors and institutional approval. [1]

People and places

Participants

UC San Diego researchers developed COMPOSER. Nurses and physicians at UC San Diego Health acted on its alerts. [1]

Dependencies

COMPOSER ran on AWS with electronic-record feeds and an Epic nurse alert. The deployment also required clinical staffing and monitoring of data delivery. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The deployment required reliable electronic records, nurse training, physician response, and continuing checks of model performance and data delivery. [1]

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.

Availability

The paper describes clinical use at UC San Diego and a startup formed by several authors. It does not establish general public access to the deployed system. [1]

Institutional decision

UC San Diego Health introduced a nurse alert for possible sepsis. [1]

Grounds

The project received IRB approval 805726 and ACQUIRE approval 609, with a waiver of informed consent. [1]

Action and result

The team trained staff, deployed the alerts, and monitored the system during clinical use. [1]

Success criterion

The evaluation examined mortality, sepsis treatment, and organ dysfunction rather than a single formal pass-or-fail threshold. [1]

Evidence
