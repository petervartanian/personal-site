Fewer sepsis patients die after COMPOSER alerts UC San Diego nurses

After UC San Diego Health deployed COMPOSER, a deep-learning model that alerts nurses to sepsis, mortality was 9.49%, against a modeled 11.39% without it.

Brief from Peter

COMPOSER, a deep-learning model that flags possible sepsis to nurses, went live in two emergency departments at UC San Diego Health on 7 December 2022. The evaluation covered 6,217 adults with sepsis seen before and after that date. Mortality after deployment was 9.49%, against a modeled 11.39% without COMPOSER, an adjusted reduction of 1.9 percentage points with a 95% confidence interval of 0.3–3.5. Raw mortality before deployment was 10.3%. The comparison rests on historical controls, with no randomization and no concurrent untreated group. The estimated benefit reached statistical significance at only one of the two hospitals.1 A meta-analysis by Yale authors, published on 13 May 2025, pooled seven randomized trials of other sepsis alert systems and found no significant effect on mortality.2 The COMPOSER team reported in August 2026 that the alerts stayed in use in both emergency departments through October 2025, with a large language model layer added in July 2024. The mortality result is an association, reported by the team that built COMPOSER and the health system that deployed it.

Brief

COMPOSER, a deep-learning model that flags possible sepsis to nurses, went live in two emergency departments at UC San Diego Health on 7 December 2022. The evaluation covered 6,217 adults with sepsis seen before and after that date. Mortality after deployment was 9.49%, against a modeled 11.39% without COMPOSER, an adjusted reduction of 1.9 percentage points with a 95% confidence interval of 0.3–3.5. Raw mortality before deployment was 10.3%. The comparison rests on historical controls, with no randomization and no concurrent untreated group. The estimated benefit reached statistical significance at only one of the two hospitals.[1] A meta-analysis by Yale authors, published on 13 May 2025, pooled seven randomized trials of other sepsis alert systems and found no significant effect on mortality.[2] The COMPOSER team reported in August 2026 that the alerts stayed in use in both emergency departments through October 2025, with a large language model layer added in July 2024. The mortality result is an association, reported by the team that built COMPOSER and the health system that deployed it.

The authors work at the University of Toronto and Harvard Medical School and took no part in COMPOSER. In this editorial they accept the reported association and note that the mortality benefit appeared at one of the two hospitals.

    Here, the authors found that using COMPOSER within two EDs at UC San Diego (UCSD) Health was associated with a 17% relative reduction in in-hospital mortality and a 10% increase in sepsis bundle compliance.

  5. Peer-reviewed study Independent author Context or contrary evidence

    Ein Alshaeba S, Marhoffer EA, Holleck JL, Theisen-Toupal J, et al., The Effect of Early Warning Systems for Sepsis on Mortality: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis, Journal of General Internal Medicine, 13 May 2025. The authors work at Yale and George Washington University and have no tie to COMPOSER; their meta-analysis of seven randomized trials of sepsis alert systems found no significant effect on mortality, and it did not include the COMPOSER study, which was not randomized.

    The pooled odds ratio for mortality for patients randomized to early warning systems was 0.84 (95% CI, 0.60, 1.18).

Benefit marks

Depth

The number and color show the depth of a benefit.

  1. 1Depth 1. A convenience, or time or money saved.
  2. 2Depth 2. A measurable improvement in a result or ability.
  3. 3Depth 3. A lasting change, an ability restored, or a risk removed.
  4. 4Depth 4. A serious harm averted or a serious condition resolved in time.
  5. 5Depth 5. A life or livelihood preserved, or a change documented across a population.

Beneficiary

  • Circle: a person
  • Square: an organization
  • Triangle: society

Evidence

  • Solid. A court or regulator found a benefit, or researchers independently studied it.

  • Hatched. Independent reporting.

  • Outline. Claimed by the parties. Only the people or organizations involved, or sources whose independence has not been assessed, support the benefit.

The fill changes with the evidence. The depth color stays the same.
