What happened

Erdős problem #38, an open question in additive number theory asking for a set B that is not an additive basis yet raises the Schnirelmann density of every set A, was solved by OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Pro. On 25 April 2026 a user (gebyjaff) posted the model's solution on the erdosproblems.com forum, with a PDF cleanup by Liam Price; on 26 April forum commenter Nat Sothanaphan reported that a standard check found no issues in the write-up, and on 1 May the Aristotle system autoformalised the argument in Lean, which Sothanaphan confirmed matches the paper. Site maintainer Thomas Bloom added an alternative sketch on 2 May 2026, and the problem page now records the positive solution by GPT 5.5 Pro. Terence Tao's wiki lists it as an AI-standalone full solution.

What it was built to do. General-purpose reasoning model prompted to solve an open research problem

The system's part

The model produced the proof after a human prompt; humans cleaned up the write-up, checked it, and the maintainer accepted it; a separate AI system (Aristotle) produced the Lean formalization that a human confirmed matches the paper. Tao's wiki classifies the case as AI standalone with non-significant human involvement. The attribution of GPT-5.5 Pro to OpenAI rests on the chatgpt.com share link in gebyjaff's forum post; no cited source names OpenAI in prose. The cited sources do not name Aristotle's developer.

Replication

What produced it. A user prompting GPT-5.5 Pro with the problem statement. Human cleanup of the written proof. Independent human check and Lean autoformalisation by Aristotle. Acceptance by the site maintainer.

What must hold again. The problem has a precise, formalized statement. The proof is checkable by humans or in Lean. A community venue reviews and records AI-produced solutions.
