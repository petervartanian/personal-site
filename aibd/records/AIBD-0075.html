What happened

Erdős problem #38 was an open question in additive number theory. It asks for a set B that is not an additive basis yet raises the Schnirelmann density of every set A. OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Pro solved it. On 25 April 2026 a user (gebyjaff) posted the model's solution on the erdosproblems.com forum, with a PDF cleanup by Liam Price. On 26 April forum commenter Nat Sothanaphan reported that a standard check found no issues in the write-up. On 1 May the Aristotle system autoformalized the argument in Lean, and Sothanaphan confirmed that the formalization matches the paper. The same day Terence Tao changed the status of problem 38 in the community Erdős problem database from open to proved with a Lean proof. On 2 May 2026 site maintainer Thomas Bloom posted an alternative sketch. In it he wrote that the argument is short and mostly classical, and that it is close to Ruzsa's construction of thin essential components. The problem page now records the positive solution by GPT 5.5 Pro. Terence Tao's wiki lists it as an AI-standalone full solution.
