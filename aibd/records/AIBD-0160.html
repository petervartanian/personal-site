Istanbul police use AVCI to detain 325 and shut 69 dealer groups

Istanbul police first used their AI-supported AVCI messaging-analysis system on 10 February 2026, in a drug operation across 14 provinces: 325 detained and remanded, 69 groups closed.

The system’s part
By the police account relayed by Anadolu Agency, AVCI infiltrated closed messaging groups and decoded slang and coded language. It preserved messages by recording group traffic before users deleted it, linked profiles through IP analysis, and mapped group relationships. That work produced the suspects and groups targeted on 10 February. Police and courts carried out the detentions, referrals and remands. The sources include no independent evaluation of the system. No official is quoted on how many of the 325 were identified by AVCI rather than by conventional work. The sources name no developer beyond the police's own software team.
Limits
The police account does not say how many suspects AVCI identified and how many came from other investigative work. The counts differ by date: 305 detained on 10 February, 324 remanded by 13 February and 325 detained and remanded in the report of 6 April 2026. No source reports charges or convictions, and no source measures any reduction in drug-related harm or gives an error rate for the system.

What happened

AVCI (Açık Kaynak İstihbaratı Analiz Sistemi, Open Source Intelligence Analysis System) is an AI-supported program. The Istanbul Police Department's narcotics and cybercrime divisions built it in-house with a special software team. Police chief Selami Yıldız ordered the work. It infiltrates closed groups and channels on end-to-end encrypted messaging apps. It analyzes messages, voice notes, photos and location data. It also decodes dealer slang, links profiles and maps group relationships. By copying traffic to its own servers while the traffic is accessible, it preserves material that users later delete. Police first used AVCI on 10 February 2026, in a drug operation that was centered on Istanbul and ran across 14 provinces. Anadolu Agency reported that day that the simultaneous raids had taken place that morning in 14 provinces and 35 districts and that 305 of 346 sought suspects had been detained. On 13 February 2026 the Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor's Office said that 324 suspects had been caught, 21 of them group administrators, and that criminal judgeships of peace had remanded all of them. Anadolu Agency reported on 6 April 2026 that 325 suspects had been detained in the operation. All 325 referred to court were remanded in custody. 69 messaging-app groups were closed.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

AVCI analyzed messages, images, voice notes, locations, and account relationships from groups that police accessed. It produced intelligence reports, identity links, and maps of suspected group roles.

Workflow

In-house police software that infiltrates closed groups on encrypted messaging apps and analyzes messages, media and location data with natural language processing. A coordinated same-day operation across 14 provinces in which the identified suspects were detained and referred to court.

Human oversight

Police reviewed the reports and used them in field investigations. Officers detained suspects, and courts decided remand.

Benefits

Reach

No source counts beneficiaries. The figures in the record count suspects detained and remanded and messaging groups closed. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Duration

Anadolu Agency reported on 17 July 2026 that police had used AVCI again in an Istanbul-based operation across 35 provinces, in which 110 suspects were detained and 65 Telegram groups and channels were closed. No source follows the groups closed in February or the cases of the 325 suspects. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

People and places

Dependencies

The reported retrieval of deleted messages depended on copying group traffic to police-controlled servers before deletion. The account does not demonstrate that AVCI broke end-to-end encryption.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Police must gain access to relevant groups and capture their traffic before messages are deleted. Courts remand the suspects. The sources report all 325 remanded, but no later charging or conviction outcomes. The attribution of the suspects to AVCI, rather than to conventional investigation, is the police's own. No independent evaluation exists.
