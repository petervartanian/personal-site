ASENA flags a car at Yüksekova carrying 52.5 kg of explosives

The Turkish police's ASENA analysis program flagged a car at Yüksekova on 20 March 2022, and officers found 52.5 kg of explosives that the interior minister said were bound for Istanbul.

  • 4 society

    Police seized 52.5 kg of explosive assessed as PETN-RDX before it was used. The minister said that the target was Istanbul. The court convicted five men of supplying weapons to a terrorist organization. The averted harm was potentially mass-casualty, although the reporting did not establish the specific plan.

  • 2 an organization

    The Hakkari police obtained a lead that produced a seizure, five arrests and five convictions. The minister counted it among 15 similar actions stopped that year.

Brief from Peter

On 20 March 2022 officers of the Hakkari Provincial Police Directorate stopped a car at a checkpoint in Yüksekova, Turkey, after work on the directorate's ASENA analysis program had flagged it. A hidden compartment under the spare-wheel well held 133 packets, 52.5 kg in all, of factory-made explosive assessed as PETN-RDX.1 The driver was a police officer posted in Istanbul, and on 20 November 2023 the Hakkari 2nd Heavy Penal Court sentenced him to 21 years in prison and four co-defendants to 17.5 years each for supplying weapons to a terrorist organization.2 ASENA was built to flag drug crime, so this find lay outside its purpose. Interior Minister Süleyman Soylu said on 21 March 2022 that police using analysis programs, “what they call artificial intelligence”, had identified a suspicious vehicle thought to be carrying drugs.3 In June 2022 the head of the police Narcotics Crimes Department listed the explosives among the finds of operations that, by his account, had started with ASENA.4 No source describes the data that ASENA matched for this car or whether an analyst checked the flag before the stop. The driver spoke in court of “those who informed”, but no source reports an informant.5

Details

AI system

Autonomy

ASENA supplied a suspicious-vehicle lead. Officers stopped and searched the car, and forensic and judicial processes established the resulting case.

Workflow

A police analysis program integrated with national identity, justice and interior databases that ranks vehicles or persons for interdiction. A fixed checkpoint on the Yüksekova-Van road at which a flagged car could be stopped and searched the same evening. Officers prepared to search a concealed compartment on a narcotics lead rather than a terrorism lead.

Human oversight

Trained analysts used approved queries and passed leads to field officers. The police program director described judicially coordinated searches after analysis, while officers carried out this vehicle stop.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The flag must be followed by a lawful stop and physical search; the software only prioritizes. Seized material must be forensically assessed and the case prosecuted, as here with a 2023 conviction, for the counterfactual to stand. Analytic leads must be checked before a stop; the sources do not report the program's false-positive rate.

Entry details

Sector

Public safety and counter-terrorism policing. Government, security, and defense.

Purpose

ASENA (Analiz Sistemleri Narkotik Ağı) is the Turkish National Police's narcotics analysis software, in service since March 2021. Built to flag and investigate drug crime from integrated police and justice databases. The Hakkari flag was worked as a suspected drug shipment and the search found explosives instead, so this benefit was outside the system's design purpose.
