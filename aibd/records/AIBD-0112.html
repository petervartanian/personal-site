ASENA flags a car at Yüksekova carrying 52.5 kg of explosives

depth 4 for society, depth 2 for an organization

133 packets, 52.5 kg, of factory-made explosive

Police analysis software flagged a car at Yüksekova on 20 Mar 2022; it held 52.5 kg of explosives officials said were bound for Istanbul.

What happened

On 20 March 2022 at about 19:00, officers of the Hakkari Provincial Police Directorate stopped a car at the Yüksekova-Van checkpoint exit in Yüksekova, Turkey, after work on the directorate's ASENA analysis program flagged it as suspicious, initially as a suspected drug shipment. A search of a hidden compartment under the spare-wheel well found 133 packets, 52.5 kg, of factory-made explosive assessed as PETN-RDX. The driver, A.K., a police officer posted in Istanbul, was remanded on 22 March 2022. Interior Minister Süleyman Soylu said on 21 March that the target was Istanbul. On 20 November 2023 the Hakkari 2nd Heavy Penal Court gave A.K. 21 years for supplying weapons to a terrorist organization and four co-defendants 17.5 years each.

Not what it was built to do. ASENA (Analiz Sistemleri Narkotik Ağı) is the Turkish National Police's narcotics analysis software, in service since March 2021, built to flag and investigate drug crime from integrated police and justice databases. The Hakkari flag was worked as a suspected drug shipment and the search found explosives instead, so this benefit was outside the system's design purpose.

Who benefited

  • 4
    societal

    52.5 kg of explosive assessed as PETN-RDX was seized before use; the minister said the target was Istanbul and the court convicted five men of supplying weapons to a terrorist organization, so the averted harm was potentially mass-casualty, though the specific plan was not established in the reporting.

  • 2
    organizational

    The Hakkari police obtained a lead that produced a seizure, five arrests and five convictions; the minister counted it among 15 similar actions stopped that year.

The system's part

The program supplied the lead and nothing more. The minister said the analysis programs, 'what they call artificial intelligence', had identified a suspicious vehicle thought to be carrying drugs, and the Hakkari police chief called him on the evening of 20 March. Officers then stopped the car at a fixed checkpoint, searched the concealed compartment and arrested the driver; prosecutors and the court did the rest. No source describes the program's inputs, the data it matched, or whether an analyst reviewed the flag before the stop. The defendant spoke in court of 'those who informed', but no source reports an informant.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A police analysis program integrated with national identity, justice and interior databases that ranks vehicles or persons for interdiction. A fixed checkpoint on the Yüksekova-Van road at which a flagged car could be stopped and searched the same evening. Officers prepared to search a concealed compartment on a narcotics lead rather than a terrorism lead.

What must hold again. The flag must be followed by a lawful stop and physical search; the software only prioritizes. Seized material must be forensically assessed and the case prosecuted, as here with a 2023 conviction, for the counterfactual to stand. Analytic leads must be checked before a stop; the sources do not report the program's false-positive rate.
