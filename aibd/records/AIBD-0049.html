Exomiser helps diagnose 25% of 2,183 probands in the NHS pilot

off the scale

Genetic diagnoses were found in 25% of probands, rising to 35% for monogenic disorders

Phenotype-driven ML prioritization helped diagnose 25% of 2,183 probands in the NHS pilot; a quarter of diagnoses were immediately actionable.

What happened

The 100,000 Genomes Project pilot on rare-disease diagnosis, published in the New England Journal of Medicine on 11 November 2021, applied Exomiser's phenotype-based prioritization to genome sequences from 2,183 families in the English NHS. Genetic diagnoses were found in 25% of probands, rising to 35% for monogenic disorders, and 92% of the 490 diagnoses came from applied gene panels or Exomiser's top five candidates. Clinicians reported that 134 of the 533 genetic diagnoses, 25%, were of immediate clinical actionability, including medication changes, surveillance recommendations, clinical trial eligibility and reproductive planning.

What it was built to do. Score and rank candidate causative variants against patient phenotypes to make genome-scale rare-disease diagnosis tractable for clinical teams.

The system's part

Exomiser scored and ranked candidate variants against each proband's phenotype; the pilot reports that diagnoses were detected in 77% of top-ranked candidate variants and that panels and Exomiser's top five were complementary. Clinical confirmation and reporting were performed by NHS diagnostic laboratories and clinicians, not by the software. Neither cited source says who developed Exomiser; the pilot's prioritization analysis was led by Damian Smedley's team at Queen Mary University of London and Genomics England, who are among the paper's authors, so an independent evaluation is cited alongside it.

Replication

What produced it. Genome sequencing with family data across a national pilot. Structured phenotype terms of adequate quality. Exomiser ranking used alongside applied gene panels, with clinical review before reporting.

What must hold again. Genome or exome sequencing with family data where available. Structured HPO phenotype terms of adequate quality. Clinical review of ranked candidates before reporting.
