ChatGPT sends a woman with a spider bite to the ER; staff agree

ChatGPT sent Holli of Wolfforth, Texas to the ER for a worsening spider bite on 27 August 2025; doctors agreed and said the tissue was dying.

ⓘ

What happened

Holli posts on TikTok as @hair.queen.holli from Wolfforth, Texas. She said a spider had bitten her “a little over a week ago”. The bite was getting worse. She had vomited for days and could not keep water down. She woke with a numb arm. She asked ChatGPT whether she should go to the emergency room (ER). It answered that her symptoms were “a ton of red flags” and that she should go immediately. At the ER, she said, staff told her “Yeah, you should be here”. They said the skin tissue was dying or dead. Her video, recorded at the ER, was posted on 27 August 2025. The hospital, diagnosis and treatment were not reported. No clinician has spoken.

Purpose. ChatGPT is a general-purpose conversational assistant. Answering a user's question, here whether to go to the emergency room, is its core function. OpenAI told the Mirror that its services “are not intended for use in the diagnosis or treatment of any health condition”. The Economic Times quoted it. The system gave triage advice rather than a diagnosis.

AI’s contribution

ChatGPT's contribution, on her account, was an unambiguous instruction to go immediately when she was unsure. The assessment and any treatment were the ER's. Whether earlier arrival changed her outcome is unknown. Everything traces to her TikTok, relayed by Reach plc tabloids and Indian lifestyle desks. Hindustan Times states that it “has not independently verified the claims”.

Further evidence

Setting. Escalating symptoms (days of vomiting, inability to keep water down, new numbness in the arm) in a person hesitating to seek care. A yes/no triage question put to a chatbot that answered without hedging: “you need to go immediately”. An emergency department within reach that could assess the bite the same day.

Conditions. The user acts on the advice promptly rather than waiting for a second opinion. The advice is to escalate to clinicians, who then examine and diagnose. The chatbot made no diagnosis. Such cases are self-published. No clinician, hospital or document has confirmed this one. The record stands or falls with her account.
