What happened

Holli posts on TikTok as @hair.queen.holli from Wolfforth, Texas. She said that a spider had bitten her “a little over a week ago”. The bite was getting worse. She had vomited for days and could not keep water down. She woke with a numb arm. She asked ChatGPT whether she should go to the emergency room. It answered that her symptoms were “a ton of red flags” and that she should go immediately. At the emergency room, she said, staff told her “Yeah, you should be here”. They said that the skin tissue was dying or dead. Her video, recorded at the emergency room, was posted on 27 August 2025. The Express and the Daily Star retold it on 2 September 2025, and the Hindustan Times and The Economic Times on 3 September 2025. The hospital, diagnosis and treatment were not reported. No clinician has made a public statement. In a randomized study published in Nature Medicine on 9 February 2026, researchers at the University of Oxford and other institutions found that members of the public who used GPT-4o or two other chatbots chose the right level of care no more often than people who used their usual sources. The study does not concern Holli's case.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Holli described a worsening bite, prolonged vomiting, inability to keep water down, and new arm numbness. ChatGPT advised immediate emergency assessment.

Autonomy

The chatbot recommended escalation. Holli chose to attend the ER, where clinicians examined her.

Workflow

Escalating symptoms (days of vomiting, inability to keep water down, new numbness in the arm) in a person hesitating to seek care. A yes/no triage question put to a chatbot that answered without hedging: “you need to go immediately”. An emergency department within reach that could assess the bite the same day.

Human oversight

Holli decided to act on the chatbot's advice, and emergency room staff examined the bite. The chatbot made no diagnosis, and the assessment and any treatment were the emergency room's. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]
