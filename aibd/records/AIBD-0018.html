Berkeley CNN finds 72 unseen pulses from FRB 121102

found 72 previously undetected pulses from the repeating fast radio burst FRB 121102

A Berkeley CNN found 72 unseen pulses from FRB 121102 in Green Bank data, published in ApJ and announced by GBO (Sep 2018).

What happened

On 10 September 2018 the Green Bank Observatory announced that a convolutional neural network developed by Gerry Zhang, a graduate student at the University of California, Berkeley, with Breakthrough Listen collaborators had found 72 previously undetected pulses from the repeating fast radio burst FRB 121102 in a five-hour, 400-terabyte Green Bank Telescope observation made on 26 August 2017. Conventional algorithms had found 21 bursts in the same data. The combined 93 pulses, including 45 within the first 30 minutes, were reported in a peer-reviewed paper in the Astrophysical Journal (Zhang et al. 2018) and used to search for periodicity. The detections come from a re-analysis of the team's own data; no independent re-detection by another group was found in the fetched sources.

What it was built to do. Built to detect fast radio burst pulses in Breakthrough Listen radio data that classical search algorithms miss; the 72 bursts were that purpose.

The system's part

The CNN flagged 72 pulses in data that a classical de-dispersion search had already processed, finding 21; the detections were vetted by the authors and accepted in peer review. The result is a verified discovery but the candidate's wording 'independently confirmed' is not met: no re-detection by another group was fetched.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • A long, high-time-resolution radio observation of a known repeater
  • A CNN trained on simulated and real pulses
  • Re-analysis of data already searched by classical methods
What must hold again
  • Candidate pulses must survive radio-frequency-interference rejection and visual vetting
  • Independent confirmation would require re-detection by a second pipeline or group
