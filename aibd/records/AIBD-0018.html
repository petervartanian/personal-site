Berkeley CNN finds 72 unseen pulses from FRB 121102

A Berkeley CNN found 72 unseen pulses from FRB 121102 in Green Bank data, published in ApJ and announced by GBO (Sep 2018).

ⓘ

What happened

On 10 September 2018 the Green Bank Observatory announced a convolutional neural network found 72 previously undetected pulses from repeating fast radio burst FRB 121102. They were in a five-hour, 400-terabyte Green Bank Telescope observation made on 26 August 2017. Gerry Zhang, a University of California, Berkeley graduate student, built it with Breakthrough Listen collaborators. Conventional algorithms had found 21 bursts in the same data. The combined 93 pulses, including 45 within the first 30 minutes, were reported in a peer-reviewed Astrophysical Journal paper (Zhang et al. 2018). They served to search for periodicity. The detections come from a re-analysis of the team's own data. No independent re-detection by another group was found in the sources.

Purpose. Built to detect fast radio burst pulses in Breakthrough Listen radio data that classical search algorithms miss. The 72 bursts were that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The CNN flagged 72 pulses in data that a classical de-dispersion search had already processed, finding 21. The detections were vetted by the authors and accepted in peer review. The result is a verified discovery, but the wording “independently confirmed” is not met. No re-detection by another group is among the sources.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    Breakthrough Listen recovered 72 additional bursts from an existing 400-terabyte Green Bank dataset without new telescope time.

  • society

    The FRB research community gained a much larger burst sample (93 pulses in five hours) from the only known repeating FRB at the time.

Further evidence

Setting. A long, high-time-resolution radio observation of a known repeater. A CNN trained on simulated and real pulses. Re-analysis of data already searched by classical methods.

Conditions. Candidate pulses must survive radio-frequency-interference rejection and visual vetting. Independent confirmation would require re-detection by a second pipeline or group.
