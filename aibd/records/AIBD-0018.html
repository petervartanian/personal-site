Neural network finds 72 missed pulses from a repeating fast radio burst

A convolutional neural network built at the University of California, Berkeley, found 72 missed pulses from fast radio burst FRB 121102; a 2023 search of part of the data re-detected 12 of them.

  • 2 an organization

  • 2 society

    The FRB research community gained a much larger burst sample (93 pulses in five hours) from the only known repeating FRB at the time.

What happened

On 26 August 2017 Breakthrough Listen made a five-hour, 400-terabyte observation with the Green Bank Telescope. Conventional algorithms found 21 bursts in these data. Gerry Zhang, a University of California, Berkeley graduate student, built a convolutional neural network with Breakthrough Listen collaborators to search the same data again. The network found 72 previously undetected pulses from the repeating fast radio burst FRB 121102. The team reported the combined 93 pulses, including 45 within the first 30 minutes, in a peer-reviewed Astrophysical Journal paper (Zhang et al. 2018). The team used the pulses to search for periodicity. On 10 September 2018 the Green Bank Observatory announced the result. The detections came from a re-analysis of the team's own data. In November 2019 researchers at Nanjing University with no tie to Breakthrough Listen used the 93 pulses as the largest sample of FRB 121102 in their own analysis of burst statistics. They did not re-check the detections. In a Nature Astronomy paper published on 19 October 2023, astronomers at the University of Amsterdam and ASTRON, joined by Breakthrough Listen's Vishal Gajjar, searched the first 30 minutes of the same observation again with a different method. They re-detected all 18 bursts that the classical search had found in that span and 12 pulses that only the neural network had found. They report that the 16 network candidates that they missed were faint pulses whose astrophysical nature is unclear. The rest of the observation was not searched again. No source reports a re-detection by a group without a Breakthrough Listen author.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The convolutional network searched recorded radio observations for pulse candidates that conventional processing had missed.

Workflow

A long, high-time-resolution radio observation of a known repeater. A CNN trained on simulated and real pulses. Re-analysis of data already searched by classical methods.

Human oversight

The authors vetted the network's detections before they analyzed the pulses for periodicity, and peer reviewers accepted them. In 2023 a mostly separate team searched the first 30 minutes again with a different method and re-detected 12 pulses that only the network had found. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The immediate beneficiaries were researchers studying FRB 121102: the larger pulse sample gave them more observations with which to test timing patterns.

Duration

The detections are a result from a single observation made on 26 August 2017. A 2019 study still used the 93 pulses as the largest sample of FRB 121102. In 2023 a re-search of the first 30 minutes re-detected 12 pulses that only the network had found and missed 16 faint candidates. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Candidate pulses must survive radio-frequency-interference rejection and visual vetting. Independent confirmation would require re-detection by a second pipeline or group.
