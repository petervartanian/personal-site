An autonomous eye exam passed its trial in primary care clinics

In the trial that FDA reviewed for the IDx-DR De Novo grant, the AI found more than mild diabetic retinopathy with 87.4% sensitivity and 89.5% specificity in primary care.

  • 2 an organization

    Camera operators with no earlier imaging experience obtained a screening result for 96.1% of graded participants, and the software found 87.4% of people with more than mild retinopathy.

  • Not rated society

    FDA reviewers describe wider access to retinopathy screening in primary care as a potential benefit, and no realized effect on access or vision across the population is reported.

What happened

IDx, LLC of Coralville, Iowa, tested its IDx-DR software in a pivotal trial that enrolled 900 adults with diabetes at 10 primary care sites in the United States between January and July 2017. Camera operators with no earlier imaging experience, trained to a standard protocol, took four retinal photographs with a Topcon NW400 camera, and the software decided on its own whether more than mild diabetic retinopathy was present. A reading center graded separate images of the same eyes, taken by a professional photographer, as the reference standard. FDA reviewers report that the software found 173 of 198 people with the disease, an observed sensitivity of 87.4%, and that its observed specificity was 89.5%. It gave a usable result for 96.1% of participants with a reading center grade. FDA granted the De Novo request on 11 April 2018 and found that the probable benefits outweigh the probable risks. The trial measured accuracy against a reference standard. It did not measure vision outcomes, and 73 of the 892 participants who completed the procedures could not be fully analyzed.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Previously untrained camera operators took four retinal photographs with a Topcon NW400 camera after standardized training. The software graded the images without a physician. Some participants needed pupil dilation to obtain usable images.

Measurement

Measurement

Prospective diagnostic accuracy study against reading center grading of fundus photographs and OCT. [2]

Setting and sample

900 adults with diabetes enrolled at 10 US primary care sites; 819 fully analyzable. [2]

Measures

Sensitivity and specificity for more than mild diabetic retinopathy. [2]

Results

Sensitivity 87.4%, specificity 89.5%, imageability 96.1%. [2]

Additional value from AI

Not isolated; the software is the whole screening test. [2]

Limitations

No usual-care comparison arm; 73 participants could not be fully analyzed; vision outcomes not measured. [2]

Denominators

One screening exam per participant. [2]

Materials for replication

Protocol available with the npj Digital Medicine article. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The patient has diabetes and no earlier diagnosis of diabetic retinopathy. The images meet the quality standard of the software. Patients with a positive result are referred to an eye care professional.

Entry details

Sector

Healthcare.

Purpose

IDx-DR was built to detect more than mild diabetic retinopathy in primary care without a physician reading the images. The measured benefit is that purpose.
