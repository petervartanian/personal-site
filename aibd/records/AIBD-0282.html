AresGW finds eight new gravitational-wave candidates in LIGO O3

eight new O3 candidates with p_astro > 0.5 (cumulative 5.94)

Koloniari et al. (Mach. Learn.: Sci. Technol., 27 Feb 2025): the ResNet pipeline AresGW found eight new O3 candidates with p_astro > 0.5 (cumulative 5.94); none is confirmed by another pipeline.

What happened

Alexandra Koloniari, Evdokia Koursoumpa, Paraskevi Nousi, Paraskevas Lampropoulos, Nikolaos Passalis, Anastasios Tefas and Nikolaos Stergioulas of the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki and the Swiss Data Science Center (ETH Zurich) posted 'New Gravitational Wave Discoveries Enabled by Machine Learning' on arXiv on 10 July 2024; it was published open access in Machine Learning: Science and Technology 6(1) 015054 on 27 February 2025 (received 20 September 2024, accepted 13 February 2025). Applying their ResNet-based code AresGW, with a new hierarchical classification of triggers by noise and frequency filters, to 6.7 months of LIGO Hanford-Livingston O3 data, they write: 'we identify eight new GW candidates in the O3 data, with p_astro > 0.5 and a cumulative astrophysical probability of 5.94. This is the first identification of new coincident events in GW searches with a network of detectors by a machine-learning-based algorithm.' The arXiv table lists the candidates (GW190511_125545, p_astro 1.00, false alarm rate 0.27 per year; GW190614_134749, 0.99; GW190607_083827, 0.99; GW190904_104631, 0.72; GW190523_085933, 0.68; GW200208_211609, 0.55; GW190705_164632, 0.51; GW190426_082124, 0.50); the text and Figure 2 call the first event GW190511_135545. With the new detections, AresGW's total within its effective training range reached 42 events, against 34 and 36 for the two IAS catalogs cited. The paper reports no confirmation of the candidates by other pipelines, and the earlier verifier found none; a September 2025 follow-up by the same group on the model's sensitivity 'reveal[s] notable performance variations' across month-long datasets. The date observed is the journal publication date.

What it was built to do. AresGW was built to detect binary black hole signals in LIGO data faster than matched filtering; finding new candidates was that purpose.

The system's part

The deep-learning pipeline produced the candidate list and the ranking statistic; the authors computed p_astro from injections into O3 data. The developers are the authors, and the only source for the discovery is their own peer-reviewed paper and preprint; the paper itself calls the events candidates and reports no external confirmation.

Replication

What produced it

  • A ResNet trained on LIGO Hanford and Livingston data with hierarchical classification of triggers by noise and frequency filters
  • 6.7 months of coincident O3 data; significance from a logarithmic ranking statistic and injections into O3 data

What must hold again

  • Sources within the effective training range (component masses 7 to 50 solar masses, chirp masses 10 to 40 solar masses)
  • Two-detector coincidence; the network was not trained on Virgo data
  • A follow-up by the same group found sensitivity varies notably between month-long datasets, so detection counts may not transfer
