Smartwatch AF alerts in a 25-year-old lead to cardioversion

atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response at 123 bpm

A 25-year-old's smartwatch notified him several times of an irregular rhythm consistent with AF; ECG showed AF with rapid ventricular response, treated by TEE-guided cardioversion (Cureus, 2023).

What happened

Bedi, Al Masri, Al Hennawi, Qadir and Ottman, internal medicine physicians at Abington Jefferson Hospital, Abington, USA (with a co-author at Alfaisal University, Riyadh), reported in Cureus on 9 March 2023 (the event date is not given, so the publication date is used) a 25-year-old man with anxiety and depression who presented to an emergency department with 24 hours of chest pressure and palpitations after his smartwatch had notified him several times of an irregular heart rhythm consistent with atrial fibrillation, which the authors link to weekend binge drinking. He was tachycardic above 120 bpm; the ECG showed atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response at 123 bpm. Intravenous diltiazem and oral metoprolol did not restore rhythm; enoxaparin was started; BNP was 796 with a clear chest X-ray. He was admitted, and on the cardiologist's recommendation underwent transoesophageal-echo-guided electrical cardioversion the next day, restoring sinus rhythm. Despite a CHA2DS2-VASc score of 0 he was given apixaban for four weeks, advised on alcohol cessation and gradual return to exercise, and scheduled for monthly cardiology follow-up. The report does not name the watch's brand or model; it describes smartwatches generically as using photoplethysmography with deep neural network algorithms.

What it was built to do. Smartwatch irregular-rhythm notifications are built to prompt the wearer to seek medical review; this benefit was that purpose.

The system's part

The watch's own notification, repeated several times, is what the report says prompted the visit; clinicians confirmed AF on a 12-lead ECG and chose cardioversion. The brand is unknown, so the algorithm behind the notification cannot be identified; the authors describe smartwatch rhythm detection generically as deep-neural-network analysis of pulse waves.

Replication

What produced it
  • A wearer with symptomatic AF whose smartwatch issued repeated irregular-rhythm notifications
  • The wearer acting on the notifications by attending an emergency department
  • Clinicians confirming the rhythm on a 12-lead ECG and treating it
What must hold again
  • The watch's notification corresponds to a true arrhythmia (the authors cite a positive predictive value of 84% for irregular-pulse notifications in the Apple Heart Study)
  • The wearer treats the notification as a reason to seek care
