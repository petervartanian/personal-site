Smartwatch AF alerts in a 25-year-old lead to cardioversion

A 25-year-old's smartwatch notified him several times of an irregular rhythm consistent with AF; ECG showed AF with rapid ventricular response, treated by TEE-guided cardioversion (Cureus, 2023).

ⓘ

What happened

Bedi, Al Masri, Al Hennawi, Qadir and Ottman are internal medicine physicians at Abington Jefferson Hospital, Abington, USA, with a co-author at Alfaisal University, Riyadh. They reported in Cureus on 9 March 2023 a 25-year-old man with anxiety and depression. The event is undated. He came to an emergency department with 24 hours of chest pressure and palpitations. His smartwatch had notified him several times of an irregular rhythm consistent with atrial fibrillation (AF). The authors link this to weekend binge drinking. He was tachycardic above 120 bpm. The electrocardiogram (ECG) showed AF with rapid ventricular response at 123 bpm. Intravenous diltiazem and oral metoprolol did not restore rhythm. Enoxaparin was started. BNP was 796 with a clear chest X-ray.

He was admitted and, on the cardiologist's recommendation, underwent transesophageal-echo-guided electrical cardioversion the next day, restoring sinus rhythm. Despite a CHA2DS2-VASc score of 0 he was given apixaban for four weeks. He was advised on alcohol cessation and gradual return to exercise and scheduled for monthly cardiology follow-up. The report names neither the watch's brand nor model. It describes smartwatches generically as using photoplethysmography with deep neural network algorithms.

Purpose. Smartwatch irregular-rhythm notifications are built to prompt the wearer to seek medical review. This benefit was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The watch's own notification, repeated several times, is what the report says prompted the visit. Clinicians confirmed AF on a 12-lead ECG and chose cardioversion. The brand is unknown, so the algorithm behind the notification cannot be identified. The authors describe smartwatch rhythm detection generically as deep-neural-network analysis of pulse waves.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    A 25-year-old received an ECG-confirmed AF diagnosis, cardioversion to sinus rhythm and four weeks of anticoagulation. A material benefit to one person, not described as life-saving.

Further evidence

Setting. A wearer with symptomatic AF whose smartwatch issued repeated irregular-rhythm notifications. The wearer acting on the notifications by attending an emergency department. Clinicians confirming the rhythm on a 12-lead ECG and treating it.

Conditions. The watch's notification corresponds to a true arrhythmia (the authors cite a positive predictive value of 84% for irregular-pulse notifications in the Apple Heart Study). The wearer treats the notification as a reason to seek care.
