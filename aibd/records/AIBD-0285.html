Smartwatch alerts get a 25-year-old treated for atrial fibrillation

A smartwatch of unstated brand notified a 25-year-old man several times of an irregular rhythm, and physicians confirmed atrial fibrillation and restored normal rhythm.

  • 2 a person

    A 25-year-old man received a diagnosis of atrial fibrillation confirmed by electrocardiogram. He then had cardioversion to sinus rhythm and four weeks of anticoagulation. The report does not describe the outcome as life-saving.

The system’s part
The watch's own notification, repeated several times, is what the report says prompted the visit. Clinicians confirmed AF on a 12-lead ECG and chose cardioversion. The brand is unknown, so the algorithm behind the notification cannot be identified. The authors describe smartwatch rhythm detection generically as deep-neural-network analysis of pulse waves.
Limits
The patient also had chest discomfort and palpitations, so the watch’s incremental effect on seeking care is not isolated. The device and algorithm are unnamed, and a generic description of AI-enabled watches does not establish the model that generated this alert. One case cannot establish accuracy or prevention of a future cardiac event.

What happened

Bedi, Al Masri, Al Hennawi, Qadir and Ottman are internal medicine physicians at Abington Jefferson Hospital in Abington, United States, with a co-author at Alfaisal University in Riyadh. On 9 March 2023 they reported in Cureus the case of a 25-year-old man with anxiety and depression. The report gives no date for the event. The man came to an emergency department with 24 hours of chest pressure and palpitations. His smartwatch had notified him several times of an irregular rhythm consistent with atrial fibrillation. The authors link the arrhythmia to weekend binge drinking. His heart rate was above 120 beats per minute (bpm). The electrocardiogram showed atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response at 123 bpm. Intravenous diltiazem and oral metoprolol did not restore the rhythm. Enoxaparin was started. His B-type natriuretic peptide level was 796, and a chest X-ray was clear. He was admitted. On the cardiologist's recommendation, he underwent electrical cardioversion guided by transesophageal echocardiography the next day, which restored sinus rhythm. Despite a CHA2DS2-VASc score of 0, he was given apixaban for four weeks. He was advised to stop drinking alcohol and to return gradually to exercise. Monthly cardiology follow-up was scheduled. The report names neither the brand nor the model of the watch. It describes smartwatches generically as using photoplethysmography with deep neural network algorithms.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

The watch prompted attention; clinicians confirmed the rhythm and decided on drugs, cardioversion and follow-up.

Workflow

Repeated rhythm warnings and symptoms prompted emergency care, where ECG confirmed atrial fibrillation and clinicians restored sinus rhythm by cardioversion.

Human oversight

Clinicians confirmed atrial fibrillation on a 12-lead electrocardiogram, and a cardiologist recommended the cardioversion. The authors recommended monthly cardiology follow-up so that the patient would not rely on his smartwatch alone. [1]
