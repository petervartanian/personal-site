Apple Watch calls an ambulance for an unconscious motorcyclist

After a hit-and-run left a Singapore motorcyclist unconscious, Apple Watch Fall Detection called an ambulance and alerted his girlfriend.

  • 3 a person

    An unconscious hit-and-run victim on a quiet road at night had an ambulance summoned within what Wanbao calls the golden hour. He had abrasions, a neck injury needing a collar and a suspected bleed at the back of the head. His girlfriend was told where he was. The family and the Wanbao headline call it life-saving. No clinician or official says so and a passer-by did reach him, so life-saving is not established.

The system’s part
The watch's fall-detection classifier read the collision's impact as a hard fall. With the wearer unresponsive, it placed the automatic ambulance call through the paired phone and sent the location texts and calls to his girlfriend. SCDF paramedics treated and conveyed him. The counterfactual is unproven. A passer-by later picked up his phone and spoke to the girlfriend. The family at first assumed that person had called the ambulance, and Fitri was regaining consciousness as paramedics arrived.

What happened

On the evening of 25 September 2021, Muhammad Fitri, 24, was riding home when a gray van struck his motorcycle. That was at the junction of Ang Mo Kio Avenue 6 and Ang Mo Kio Street 31, Singapore. The driver fled; Fitri lost consciousness on a near-empty road. His Apple Watch's Fall Detection registered the impact. Via the paired phone it automatically called an ambulance and texted and phoned his emergency contact, his girlfriend. The Singapore Civil Defence Force said it was alerted around 8:20pm. Fitri was regaining consciousness as paramedics arrived. He was conveyed, conscious, to Khoo Teck Puat Hospital with abrasions, a neck collar and a suspected bleed at the back of the head. Police confirmed a hit-and-run.

Details

AI system

Role

The watch detected a hard fall and called emergency services when the wearer did not respond.

Configuration

The reported feature was Apple Watch Fall Detection. The record does not identify the watch generation.

Autonomy

Fitri's family reported that the watch detected the crash, called emergency services through his paired phone, and notified his girlfriend.

Workflow

Fall Detection is enabled on a worn Apple Watch (Series 4 or later) with a connected paired iPhone or cellular service. A collision throws the wearer down with an impact the classifier reads as a hard fall. The wearer stays immobile for about a minute without dismissing the alert. The ambulance service and the emergency contact can act on the automatic call and the shared location.

Human oversight

The watch places the call only when the wearer stays immobile for about a minute without dismissing the alert. Paramedics of the Singapore Civil Defence Force then treated him and took him to Khoo Teck Puat Hospital. [1] [2] [3]

Benefits

Reach

One person benefited, the motorcyclist for whom the watch summoned an ambulance. The watch also told his girlfriend where he was. [1] [2] [3]

People and places

Dependencies

The watch, its connection to the phone, the phone service, and emergency response all had to remain usable after the impact.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The watch and phone keep working and connected after the impact (here the watch screen cracked but still functioned). Nobody else present is able to call for help, otherwise the watch's call adds little. An ambulance can reach the location within the window in which treatment matters.

Entry details

Sector

Emergency response / road traffic injury. Consumer wearables.

Purpose

Fall Detection is built to recognize a hard fall. If the wearer stays immobile for about a minute, it calls emergency services and messages emergency contacts with the location. That is what it did. The trigger here was a motorcycle collision. Apple's documentation notes only that high-impact activity may register as a fall.
