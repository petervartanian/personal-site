curl fixes several hundred bugs flagged by ZeroPath and other AI tools

Bug reports made mostly with the ZeroPath AI code analyzer led curl to merge about 50 fixes from a first list, and by 23 December 2025 AI analyzer reports had led to several hundred.

  • 2 an organization

    The curl maintainer reports that the project merged about 50 bugfixes from the first list and removed code that carried a potential overflow. By 23 December 2025 he counted several hundred bugs fixed from AI analyzer reports.

  • 1 society

    curl is widely deployed, and the sources report no averted incident that affected users. The merged fixes are described as mostly small mistakes.

What happened

Reports to curl arrived from mid-September 2025. The curl lead maintainer, Daniel Stenberg, writes that a security report received on 16 September accurately identified a possible stack-based buffer overflow in curl's Kerberos file transfer protocol (FTP) code. It was not turned into a CVE only because the code could not be reached. The project then removed krb5-ftp support. The reporter, Joshua Rogers, then forwarded a large list of further potential issues found mostly with ZeroPath, an AI-powered code analyzer. The tool produced over two hundred possible problems despite curl's existing compilers, static analyzers and fuzzing. From the initial list about 50 separately identifiable bugfixes were merged. Stenberg told The Register on 2 October 2025 that about 50 bugfixes based on Rogers' reports had been merged. Second and third lists of 47 and 158 items followed, according to Stenberg's post of 10 October 2025. Rogers states in his retrospective of 19 October 2025 that about 98% of the curl bugs reported and fixed were discovered by ZeroPath. He puts the running total of fixed bugs around 150. In his review of 2025, published on 23 December 2025, Stenberg wrote that curl had fixed several hundred bugs as a direct result of reports from AI analyzers, primarily ZeroPath and Aisle Research.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

ZeroPath scans generated candidate defects for Rogers and curl maintainers to review.

Workflow

A large, mature C codebase already covered by conventional analyzers and fuzzing. An expert researcher filtering scanner output before reporting. Maintainers willing to work through long candidate lists.

Human oversight

The curl maintainers reviewed the lists that Rogers forwarded and merged about 50 bugfixes from the first one. By 23 December 2025 the project had fixed several hundred bugs from AI analyzer reports. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Reach

The beneficiary is the curl project, and the sources give no count of the people who use curl. The counts concern fixed bugs: about 50 from the first list, around 150 by 19 October 2025 and several hundred by 23 December 2025. [1] [2] [3] [4]
