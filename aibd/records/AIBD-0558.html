Peterborough reports quicker advice through Hey Geraldine

Peterborough City Council reports that Hey Geraldine, an AI assistant modeled on an experienced occupational therapist, saves the therapy team 15 minutes per conversation.

What happened

The BBC reported on 28 July 2025 that Peterborough City Council began developing Hey Geraldine in 2023. The tool is a social-care knowledge assistant modeled on an experienced occupational-therapy colleague. In its account of 18 December 2024, the council reports 15 minutes saved per conversation. A deputy therapy manager describes obtaining needed equipment information while the real Geraldine was on leave. The reported 1,200 questions include testing and demonstrations. The government AI Knowledge Hub listed the tool as live in July 2025. On 4 August 2025 People Management quoted employment advisers who discussed consent and the risk that the tool could replace the employee whom it was modeled on. In April 2026 the developer Datnexa claimed that the assistant had cleared the backlog of the technology-enabled care team and avoided £250,000 in costs in its first year. Datnexa also wrote that the assistant answered over 1,200 questions in a six-week trial and released more than 300 hours of occupational therapy time. No independent source has checked these figures.

Details

AI system

Role

Hey Geraldine answers staff questions about technology-enabled care using locally maintained content. [1]

Workflow

The council and partners developed the assistant in six weeks and gathered staff feedback. The source describes genuine staff questions alongside testing and demonstration interactions.

Human oversight

Occupational therapists can update the assistant’s content and inspect its query dashboard. [1]
