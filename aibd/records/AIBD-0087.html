ALICE alert leads to a cut of about R$100 million in a highway tender

ALICE, the federal comptroller's AI alert tool, flagged a highway tender in Pará, Brazil, and the auditors' findings led the contracting agency to cut almost R$100 million from the tender's budget.

The system’s part
The CGU audit report states that ALICE raised the alert that started the preventive audit, and that the audit was limited to the risks in that alert. CGU auditors confirmed the alert, examined the budget and sent findings to the DNIT. DNIT staff and the design consortium made the corrections. The figure of almost R$100 million comes from the auditors' five findings; the report does not credit ALICE itself with any part of it.
Limits
A lower reference value is a procurement adjustment. It does not prove that R$100 million was saved in executed spending, and the sources do not report the final contract price. The R$257.25 million annual total is a separate aggregate.

What happened

Brazil's federal comptroller, the Controladoria-Geral da União, runs ALICE, a robot that reads planning documents and notices on Compras.gov.br with artificial-intelligence techniques and issues risk alerts that auditors confirm. In May 2024 the Departamento Nacional de Infraestrutura de Transportes published Concorrência 154/2024, a federal road-works engineering tender for restoring the BR-158 highway in Pará between kilometer 699 and kilometer 889.58. An ALICE alert led the comptroller's auditors to examine the budget. They questioned the asphalt binder price, site and mobilization costs, truck sizes and other items. The department suspended the tender on 19 June 2024 and then corrected or justified every finding. The final budget of 19 August 2024 cut the reference value from R$549.66 million to R$436.47 million. In audit report 1632530, published on 17 December 2024, the comptroller attributes almost R$100 million of the cut to its auditors' findings. In its statement of 30 December 2024 on the year's results, the comptroller reported that federal managers had adjusted the budget after the alert, reducing the reference value by about R$100 million. That statement names neither the contracting agency nor the date of the case. Its wording “this year” refers to a separate example about water bottles. The same statement gives 2024 aggregates: 118,069 procurement processes analyzed and 206 preventive audits opened on purchases worth R$25.57 billion. It reports R$257.25 million saved in concluded audits. On 14 January 2025 the comptroller reported that ALICE alerts had led to audits of three federal hospital tenders in Rio de Janeiro, with one suspended, one revoked and one re-estimated.
