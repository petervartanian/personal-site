Smartwatch ECG app confirms AF at sea; CIRM diverts the ship

On 29 Sep 2023 a merchant ship with no ECG used a smartwatch ECG app under CIRM guidance; it confirmed AF within seconds, the ship diverted, hospital ECG confirmed AF and the seafarer was repatriated.

What happened

Ruocco, Amenta, Angeloni, Frisicale, Vaia and Sagaro, of the University of Camerino's Telemedicine and Telepharmacy Center, the Centro Internazionale Radio Medico (CIRM) research department and Italy's Ministry of Health, reported in International Maritime Health (2025;76(2):93-96; early publication date 5 March 2025 as printed on the journal page) a 43-year-old chief officer from the United Kingdom who fell ill with chest pain, malaise and palpitations on a merchant ship in the middle of the Indian Ocean. CIRM, the Italian Telemedical Maritime Assistance Service, received the request for assistance on 29 September 2023. Blood pressure was 115/80 then 110/70 mmHg with tachycardia and an irregular heartbeat, but the ship had no electrocardiograph and was too far from land for prompt rescue. The captain and the CIRM duty doctor used a smartwatch's ECG application; within seconds the application confirmed the diagnostic hypothesis of atrial fibrillation, the doctor reviewed the recording and recommended diversion to the nearest port, metoprolol 100 mg and aspirin 300 mg daily were given on board, and the hospital later confirmed atrial fibrillation. The seafarer, with no prior history, was repatriated safely without complications and diagnosed with new-onset AF. The report does not name the watch's brand or whose watch it was.

What it was built to do. Smartwatch ECG apps are built to record a single-lead ECG and classify it for atrial fibrillation; this use, relayed to a telemedicine doctor, was that purpose in an unusual setting.

The system's part

The ECG app's automatic reading confirmed the suspected AF 'within seconds' and the CIRM doctor then reviewed the trace himself and identified irregularities consistent with AF, so the classification and the human reading agreed; the diversion, drugs and hospital confirmation were human decisions. The brand is unknown, so the algorithm cannot be identified.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • A ship without an electrocardiograph but with a smartwatch carrying an ECG app on board
  • A telemedical maritime assistance service (CIRM) reachable from the ship and willing to act on a smartwatch trace
  • A captain able to run the app on the patient and to divert the vessel
What must hold again
  • The app's AF classification and the remote doctor's reading of the trace agree, as they did here and as the hospital ECG later confirmed
  • A port is within reach for diversion and the patient remains stable on the drugs available on board
