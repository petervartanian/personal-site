Smartwatch ECG app confirms AF at sea; CIRM diverts the ship

depth 2 for a person, depth 1 for an organization

Claimed by the parties, 2 sources.

On 29 Sep 2023 a merchant ship with no ECG used a smartwatch ECG app under CIRM guidance; it confirmed AF within seconds, the ship diverted, hospital ECG confirmed AF and the seafarer was repatriated.

What happened

Ruocco, Amenta, Angeloni, Frisicale, Vaia and Sagaro reported a case in International Maritime Health (2025;76(2):93–96). The early publication date is 5 March 2025 as printed on the journal page. They are at the University of Camerino's Telemedicine and Telepharmacy Center, the Centro Internazionale Radio Medico (CIRM) research department and Italy's Ministry of Health. A 43-year-old chief officer from the United Kingdom fell ill on a merchant ship in the middle of the Indian Ocean. He had chest pain, malaise and palpitations. CIRM, the Italian Telemedical Maritime Assistance Service, received the request on 29 September 2023. Blood pressure was 115/80 then 110/70 mmHg with tachycardia and an irregular heartbeat. The ship had no electrocardiograph and was too far from land for prompt rescue. The captain and the CIRM duty doctor used a smartwatch's electrocardiogram (ECG) application.

Within seconds it confirmed the diagnostic hypothesis of atrial fibrillation (AF). The doctor reviewed the recording and recommended diversion to the nearest port. Metoprolol 100 mg and aspirin 300 mg daily were given on board. The hospital later confirmed AF. The seafarer, with no prior history, was repatriated safely without complications and diagnosed with new-onset AF. The report does not name the watch's brand or whose watch it was.

What it was built to do. Smartwatch ECG apps are built to record a single-lead ECG and classify it for atrial fibrillation. This use, relayed to a telemedicine doctor, was that purpose in an unusual setting.

The system's part

The ECG app's automatic reading confirmed the suspected AF “within seconds”. The CIRM doctor then reviewed the trace himself and identified irregularities consistent with AF. So the classification and the human reading agreed. The diversion, drugs and hospital confirmation were human decisions. The brand is unknown, so the algorithm cannot be identified.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A ship without an electrocardiograph but with a smartwatch carrying an ECG app on board. A telemedical maritime assistance service (CIRM) reachable from the ship and willing to act on a smartwatch trace. A captain able to run the app on the patient and to divert the vessel.

What must hold again. The app's AF classification and the remote doctor's reading of the trace agree, as they did here and as the hospital ECG later confirmed. A port is within reach for diversion and the patient remains stable on the drugs available on board.
