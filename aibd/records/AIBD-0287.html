Smartwatch app helps a remote doctor identify atrial fibrillation at sea

An electrocardiogram app on a smartwatch of unstated brand helped a telemedicine physician identify atrial fibrillation in a seafarer and recommend a ship’s diversion to the nearest port.

  • 2 a person

    The seafarer received a diagnosis, rate control and aspirin at sea, a diversion to port and confirmation in the hospital. He was repatriated without complications. The authors say that the service “has the potential to save lives”. The report documents no life-threatening complication.

  • 1 an organization

    The CIRM duty doctor could base a rhythm diagnosis and a diversion decision on an electrocardiogram trace in addition to the symptoms. The report gives no measure beyond the single decision.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The smartwatch supplied an ECG that the CIRM physician interpreted as atrial fibrillation. The authors say that the app confirmed the hypothesis within seconds, which makes an automatic AF flag plausible, but they do not identify the device or distinguish an algorithmic classification from the physician’s reading. Diversion, treatment and hospital confirmation were human decisions.

The authors report that the app confirmed the AF hypothesis, but do not distinguish automatic classification from physician interpretation. The watch brand and algorithm remain unidentified, and the case does not establish a death prevented.

What happened

Ruocco, Amenta, Angeloni, Frisicale, Vaia and Sagaro reported a case in International Maritime Health (2025;76(2):93–96). The journal page prints an early publication date of 5 March 2025. The authors are at the University of Camerino's Telemedicine and Telepharmacy Center, the research department of the Centro Internazionale Radio Medico (CIRM) and Italy's Ministry of Health. A 43-year-old chief officer from the United Kingdom fell ill on a merchant ship in the middle of the Indian Ocean. He had chest pain, malaise and palpitations. CIRM, the Italian Telemedical Maritime Assistance Service, received the request for assistance on 29 September 2023. His blood pressure was 115/80 then 110/70 mmHg, with tachycardia and an irregular heartbeat. The ship had no electrocardiograph and was too far from land for prompt rescue. The captain and the CIRM duty doctor used a smartwatch's electrocardiogram application. The authors report that within seconds it confirmed the diagnostic hypothesis of atrial fibrillation (AF). The doctor reviewed the recording and recommended diversion to the nearest port. Metoprolol 100 mg and aspirin 300 mg daily were given on board. Some days later, the hospital confirmed AF. The seafarer, who had no prior history, was diagnosed with new-onset AF. He was repatriated safely without complications. The report does not name the watch's brand or whose watch it was.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

The CIRM doctor interpreted the watch ECG, prescribed treatment and recommended diversion; the captain arranged the ship’s response.

Workflow

A ship without an electrocardiograph but with a smartwatch carrying an ECG app on board. A telemedical maritime assistance service (CIRM) reachable from the ship and willing to act on a smartwatch trace. A captain able to run the app on the patient and to divert the vessel.

Human oversight

The CIRM duty doctor reviewed the smartwatch recording before recommending the diversion, and the captain diverted the ship. The hospital later confirmed atrial fibrillation. [1]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The chief officer gained access to a useful rhythm recording where the ship lacked an electrocardiograph and immediate rescue was unavailable.

Reach

One seafarer, a 43-year-old chief officer, benefited. [1]

Duration

The authors report that the hospital confirmed atrial fibrillation some days later and that the seafarer was repatriated without complications. No later report on the patient has appeared. [1]

People and places

Dependencies

The response required an ECG-capable watch, a way to share its recording with CIRM, available onboard medication and a reachable port.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The app's AF classification and the remote doctor's reading of the trace agree, as they did here and as the hospital ECG later confirmed. A port is within reach for diversion and the patient remains stable on the drugs available on board.

Healthcare (maritime telemedicine; cardiology). Consumer wearables.

Purpose

Smartwatch ECG apps are built to record a single-lead ECG and classify it for atrial fibrillation. This use, relayed to a telemedicine doctor, was that purpose in an unusual setting.
