Xuanwu Hospital implants China's first responsive neurostimulator

Xuanwu Hospital implanted the Epilcure responsive neurostimulator in a man with epilepsy in March 2022; its trial team reports a 61.1% mean seizure reduction in seven patients and no result for him.

What happened

On 9 March 2022 a team at Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University, Beijing, implanted the Epilcure closed-loop responsive neurostimulation system. Hospital president Zhao Guoguang and neurosurgeon Shan Yongzhi led it. The patient, a man with drug-resistant bilateral temporal lobe epilepsy, was the first enrollee in a hospital-led registration clinical trial for China's National Medical Products Administration (NMPA). cnBeta and Kuai Keji reported the operation on 12 March 2022, and both cited the neurosurgery department's WeChat account. The reports describe an AI chip implanted in the skull, with intracranial electrodes monitoring electroencephalography (EEG) rhythms around the clock. The reports say that the device predicts an imminent seizure and delivers an interfering rhythm to block seizure formation at the focus. They call bilateral temporal lobe epilepsy a relative contraindication for open surgery. The operation went smoothly, and the stimulator's indicators worked normally during surgery. Post-operative reconstruction showed precise electrode placement and clear EEG signals (cnBeta). The initial accounts give no seizure outcome, patient age or follow-up for this patient. In 2023 the Xuanwu Hospital team and two partner centers reported that 10 patients were enrolled in the first-in-human trial between March and November 2022. They reported that seven patients followed over three months had a mean seizure reduction of 61.1%, with no serious adverse events. A later eight-center report by the same group describes 19 patients through June 2023, with a median reduction of 58% at month 12 and seizure-free periods in four patients. That paper identifies the maker as Hangzhou Genlight MedTech. It describes five clinician-configured detection algorithms that trigger stimulation when signal measures cross set thresholds. It does not establish that a learned model predicted seizures. Neither paper gives the first patient's own outcome. The 2022 news reports relay the hospital's WeChat post.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

The implant was intended to monitor intracranial EEG continuously and trigger stimulation automatically when configured detectors recognized abnormal activity. The later clinical protocol describes signal thresholds, rather than establishing a learned seizure-prediction model.

Workflow

Intracranial electrodes at the epileptic focus feeding continuous EEG to a chip implanted in the skull. Clinician-configured EEG detection algorithms that trigger stimulation when signal measures cross set thresholds, as described in the later clinical paper. Enrollment in the hospital-led NMPA registration trial for patients whose epilepsy is drug-resistant and unsuited to open surgery.

Human oversight

In the later clinical protocol, clinicians used recorded EEG to select detection algorithms and thresholds, then adjusted stimulation settings. Patients or family members could switch the device on or off and monitor its battery.

Benefits

Reach

The first implantation concerned one patient. The trial enrolled 10 patients at three centers between March and November 2022 and 19 patients at eight centers by June 2023. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

The eight-center report gives a median seizure reduction of 58% at month 12 for the cohort. No source follows this first patient's own outcome over time. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Measurement

Measurement

Monthly seizure counts compared with the mean of the three months before implantation; the first month after stimulation began served to tune settings, and efficacy counts began in the second month. [6]

Setting and sample

10 patients with drug-resistant focal epilepsy at Xuanwu Hospital (3), Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University (6) and Huashan Hospital (1), enrolled March to November 2022. [6]

Measures

Seizure frequency reduction from baseline. [6]

Results

Mean reduction of 61.1% in seven patients followed over three months (P = 0.0156); one patient became seizure-free. [6]

Limitations

Small sample, short follow-up, no control group, and authors from the implanting teams; no individual result for the first Xuanwu patient. [6]

Denominators

Mean follow-up of 5.1 months. [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Electrode placement is precise and signals are clear, as the post-operative reconstruction reported here. Detection and stimulation settings must be appropriate for the patient; a seizure outcome for this first implantation is not established by the inspected sources.
