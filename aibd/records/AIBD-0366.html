What happened

On 9 March 2022 a team at Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University, Beijing, implanted the Epilcure closed-loop responsive neurostimulation system. Hospital president Zhao Guoguang and neurosurgeon Shan Yongzhi led it. The patient, a man with drug-resistant bilateral temporal lobe epilepsy, was the first enrollee in a hospital-led NMPA registration clinical trial. Sources: cnBeta and Kuai Keji (12 March 2022), both citing the neurosurgery department's WeChat account. The reports describe an AI chip implanted in the skull, with intracranial electrodes monitoring electroencephalography (EEG) rhythms around the clock. On predicting an imminent seizure, it delivers an interfering rhythm to block seizure formation at the focus. Bilateral temporal lobe epilepsy is called a relative contraindication for open surgery.

The operation went smoothly and the stimulator's indicators worked normally during surgery. Post-operative reconstruction showed precise electrode placement and clear EEG signals (cnBeta). No seizure outcome, patient age or follow-up is reported; the device's maker is not named.

What it was built to do. Epilcure was built to detect the onset of seizures from continuously monitored intracranial EEG and deliver stimulation to abort them in people with drug-resistant epilepsy. Implanting it for that purpose was the intended use, though no seizure outcome is yet reported.

The system's part

Not assessed. The hospital, as relayed by the reports, describes the AI element as an on-skull chip. It monitors intracranial EEG continuously and predicts imminent seizures to trigger interfering stimulation. The read reports establish only that the implant functioned during surgery and that electrodes were placed precisely. No detection or stimulation event, seizure count or follow-up is reported. What the AI contributed to the patient cannot be assessed from these sources.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. Intracranial electrodes at the epileptic focus feeding continuous EEG to a chip implanted in the skull. A seizure-prediction routine on the chip that triggers an interfering stimulation rhythm when it predicts an imminent seizure. Enrollment in the hospital-led NMPA registration trial for patients whose epilepsy is drug-resistant and unsuited to open surgery.

What must hold again. Electrode placement is precise and signals are clear, as the post-operative reconstruction reported here. The prediction fires early enough and specifically enough to abort seizures, which no cited source yet documents.
