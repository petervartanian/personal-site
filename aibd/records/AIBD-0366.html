IdentiFlight automated curtailment at Top of the World wind facility, Wyoming: 82% eagle fatality reduction claimed, cut to 50% with a CI spanning an increase by a USGS reanalysis

McClure et al. 2021 reported an 82% (75-89%) lower eagle fatality rate after IdentiFlight camera-triggered curtailment in Wyoming; Huso & Dalthorp 2023 recalculated 50% (-159%, 89%). Contested.

What happened

McClure et al. (Journal of Applied Ecology, published online 31 January 2021) tested an automated curtailment system, a camera system that detects flying objects, classifies them and decides whether to curtail individual turbines, at the Top of the World Windpower Facility in Wyoming, USA, in a before-after-control-impact design against a control site 15 km away; after correcting for carcass detection, they estimated fatalities fell 63% at the treatment site while rising 113% at the control site, 'an 82% (75%-89%) reduction in the fatality rate at the treatment site relative to the control site'. The same authors, in Ecological Solutions and Evidence (2022), reanalysed with additional data and reported an 85% reduction (95% highest density interval 12% to 100%) with the IdentiFlight system. Huso and Dalthorp of the US Geological Survey (Journal of Applied Ecology, 30 August 2023) identified four major errors (ignoring annual variation, unfounded causal inference from one treatment year at one site, inflated effect size, inconsistent data with 7 of 42 fatalities excluded) and gave a corrected estimate of 50% (-159%, 89%) reduction or possible increase, concluding that the claim is not supported by the data. The activation date of the system is not given in the fetched abstracts; the date used is the 2021 publication date.

What it was built to do. Detect approaching eagles with cameras and classification software and curtail individual turbines to avoid collisions; the claimed reduction in fatalities is the intended effect.

The system's part

The camera-based detection and classification system is the mechanism that triggers curtailment, so any fatality reduction is attributable to it together with the curtailment policy; however, the independent USGS reanalysis (Huso and Dalthorp 2023) attributes the reported 82% effect to four methodological errors and finds little evidence of reduction once corrected, while the original authors' 2022 reanalysis reaffirms an 85% reduction with a wide interval. The benefit is therefore contested between peer-reviewed sources and not verified.

Replication

What produced it

  • Camera-based detection and classification of flying eagles at each turbine
  • Operator policy to curtail individual turbines on detections
  • Carcass searches with detection-probability correction at treatment and control sites
  • Before-after-control-impact study design

What must hold again

  • Several years of post-activation data at several sites (Huso and Dalthorp)
  • Consistent treatment of annual variation and site differences
  • Complete fatality data without unexplained exclusions
  • Turbine curtailment actually executed on detections
