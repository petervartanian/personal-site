Google FINDER flags restaurants 3.1 times as likely to be unsafe

52.3% were unsafe versus 24.7% of 10,786 other inspections (odds ratio 3.06)

Health departments inspected 132 FINDER-flagged restaurants in 2016-2017; 52.3% were unsafe versus 24.7% of 10,786 other inspections (odds ratio 3.06).

What happened

Google built FINDER, a machine-learned model that estimates the fraction of people who visited a restaurant and later searched for terms indicative of food poisoning, using anonymous and aggregated web search and location data. It was deployed in Las Vegas between May and August 2016 (5,038 inspections completed, 61 prompted by FINDER) and in Chicago between November 2016 and March 2017 (5,880 inspections, 71 prompted by FINDER). Each morning the Southern Nevada Health District and the Chicago Department of Public Health received a list of restaurants identified by FINDER and dispatched inspectors who were unaware of whether an inspection was prompted by the model. Of the 132 FINDER-identified restaurants, 69 (52.3%) were deemed unsafe, against 2,662 of 10,786 (24.7%) baseline restaurants, an odds ratio of 3.06 (95% CI 2.14-4.35); against routine inspections alone the unsafe rate was 22.7% and the odds ratio 3.16. In 38% of cases the restaurant potentially causing illness was not the last one visited. The sources give deployment months but no single event day, so the date used is the paper's publication date.

What it was built to do. Identify restaurants likely to be causing foodborne illness from anonymized, aggregated web-search and location data so that health departments can target inspections.

The system's part

FINDER generated the daily restaurant lists; inspectors did not know the source of the prompt. The comparison is observational (FINDER-prompted versus other inspections during the same period), not randomized. Google employees conceived and implemented the model and co-authored the peer-reviewed evaluation; health department staff oversaw implementation.

Replication

What produced it
  • Aggregated web search and location logs covering a city's restaurant patrons
  • Health departments able to act on a daily list
  • Inspection outcome records to compare against routine and complaint-based inspections
What must hold again
  • Access to anonymized, aggregated search and location data held by Google
  • Tolerance of the incubation-period lag before inference
  • Inspectors unaware of the prompt source
