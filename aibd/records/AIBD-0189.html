Restaurants that Google's FINDER flags are more often found unsafe

In Las Vegas and Chicago in 2016 and 2017, 52.3% of 132 restaurants that Google's FINDER model flagged were unsafe on inspection, against 24.7% of 10,786 other inspections.

What happened

Google built FINDER, a machine-learned model that estimates the fraction of people who visited a restaurant and later searched for terms indicative of food poisoning. It uses anonymous and aggregated web search and location data. It was deployed in Las Vegas between May and August 2016 (5,038 inspections completed, 61 prompted by FINDER). In Chicago it ran between November 2016 and March 2017 (5,880 inspections, 71 prompted by FINDER). Each morning the Southern Nevada Health District and the Chicago Department of Public Health received a list of restaurants identified by FINDER. They dispatched inspectors unaware of whether an inspection was prompted by the model. Of the 132 FINDER-identified restaurants, 69 (52.3%) were deemed unsafe, against 2,662 of 10,786 (24.7%) baseline restaurants, an odds ratio of 3.06 (95% CI 2.14–4.35). Against routine inspections alone the unsafe rate was 22.7% and the odds ratio 3.16. In 38% of cases the restaurant potentially causing illness was not the last one visited. The sources give deployment months but no exact day.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

FINDER combined aggregated restaurant visits with subsequent searches associated with food poisoning to produce daily lists of restaurants for inspection.

Workflow

Aggregated web search and location logs covering a city's restaurant patrons. Health departments able to act on a daily list. Inspection outcome records to compare against routine and complaint-based inspections.

Human oversight

Health departments dispatched inspectors, who determined whether restaurants were unsafe without knowing which inspections the model had prompted.

Benefits

Reach

No source counts the people who benefited. Health departments inspected 132 restaurants that FINDER identified, and 69 of them were deemed unsafe. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

Inspectors, unaware of whether FINDER prompted a visit, inspected each restaurant under the usual protocol. The authors labeled a restaurant unsafe when it received a grade that reflected a serious health code violation.

Setting and sample

Las Vegas (May to August 2016) and Chicago (November 2016 to March 2017); 132 FINDER-prompted inspections and 10,786 other inspections.

Measures

Share of inspected restaurants rated unsafe.

Results

52.3% of FINDER-prompted restaurants were unsafe against 24.7% of the others; odds ratio 3.06 (95% CI 2.14 to 4.35), adjusted for city and risk level.

Additional value from AI

The odds ratio compares the model's list with the existing ways of choosing restaurants to inspect.

Limitations

Not randomized; small FINDER sample; Google staff co-wrote the study; no measure of illness prevented.

Denominators

Daily lists of restaurants sent to the two health departments during the deployments.

Materials for replication

The search and location data are held by Google and are not public.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Access to anonymized, aggregated search and location data held by Google. Tolerance of the incubation-period lag before inference. Inspectors unaware of the prompt source.
