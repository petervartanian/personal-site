FSD v12 may have driven its owner 13 miles to a VA emergency room

By his own X account, FSD v12 drove Max Paul Franklin 13 miles to a VA emergency room at 2 a.m. on 2 April 2024; uncorroborated.

ⓘ

What happened

By his own account, posted on X on 10 April 2024, Max Paul Franklin, a North Carolina television producer, had severe dehydration. His blood glucose read 670 at 2 a.m. on 2 April 2024 after his insulin pump malfunctioned. He says he got into his Tesla Model Y and engaged the Full Self-Driving (Supervised) v12 trial unlocked the day before. He says it drove the 13 miles to the VA emergency room without intervention and offered to park. It is the Charles George VA Medical Center, Asheville, per Il Fatto Quotidiano. He reports a mild heart attack and discharge with no restrictions. Tesla and Elon Musk reposted it on 11 April 2024. No hospital or official source has confirmed it.

Purpose. FSD (Supervised) is built to navigate a Tesla to a chosen destination while an attentive driver supervises it. Driving Franklin to the destination he set is that function. Use by a driver impaired by a medical emergency is not an intended use. Hindustan Times notes the system depends on driver intervention when needed. A Tesla owner quoted by Tesla Oracle calls it a Level 2 system. It requires hands on the wheel and eyes on the road even when active.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. The only description of what FSD did is Franklin's. Engaged by a double click of the stalk, it drove 13 miles to the VA emergency room without intervention and offered to park on arrival. No Tesla vehicle data, dashcam footage, hospital record, emergency-service log or official statement has been found. No outlet reports having interviewed him or the hospital. Tesla's corporate account and Elon Musk reposted the story on 11 April 2024 but added no facts. The photos attached to the post are two hospital-bed selfies and a night shot of a Model Y in a wet car park. They show no route, timestamp or hospital name. Some syndicated coverage garbles the story (Sky TG24 places him in Virginia and describes low blood sugar). The counterfactual, calling emergency services, driving himself or not reaching care, is unexamined. Commenters cited by Il Fatto Quotidiano argued he should have called for help.

Further evidence

Setting. A Tesla Model Y with the FSD (Supervised) v12 trial active, which Franklin says Tesla had unlocked for all US cars on 1 April 2024. A driver who, by his account, could still reach the car, engage FSD by double-clicking the stalk and set the emergency room as destination. A 13-mile route to the VA emergency room that the system, on his account, completed without intervention and then offered to park.

Conditions. FSD (Supervised) requires an attentive driver with hands on the wheel and eyes on the road (Hindustan Times; Tesla Oracle quoting owner James Locke). A driver too impaired to supervise would be outside its intended use. The account is Franklin's alone. No hospital, emergency-service, police or vehicle-log record of the drive has been found. The photos attached to the post do not show the route, a timestamp or the hospital. Emergency services remain the documented first resort. Il Fatto Quotidiano reports commenters arguing that he should have called for help.
