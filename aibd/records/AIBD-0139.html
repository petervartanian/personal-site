Tesla software may have driven a sick owner 13 miles to a hospital

Tesla Full Self-Driving (Supervised) v12 drove Max Paul Franklin 13 miles to a VA emergency room at 2 a.m. on 2 April 2024, according to his own uncorroborated post on X.

  • 4 a person

    By his own account Franklin reached a VA emergency room during an insulin-pump failure with blood glucose at 670. He says that he was treated for a mild heart attack and left the hospital with no restrictions, which is a serious condition treated in time. No clinician, hospital or official source confirms the emergency or the drive.

The system’s part
Not isolated. The only description of what FSD did is Franklin's. Engaged by a double click of the stalk, it drove 13 miles to the VA emergency room without intervention and offered to park on arrival. No Tesla vehicle data, dashcam footage, hospital record, emergency-service log or official statement has been found. No outlet reports having interviewed him or the hospital. Tesla's corporate account and Elon Musk reposted the story on 11 April 2024 but added no facts. The photos attached to the post are two hospital-bed selfies and a night shot of a Model Y in a wet car park. They show no route, timestamp or hospital name. Some syndicated coverage garbles the story (Sky TG24 places him in Virginia and describes low blood sugar). The counterfactual, calling emergency services, driving himself or not reaching care, is unexamined. Commenters cited by Il Fatto Quotidiano argued he should have called for help.
Limits
The retained story rests on the driver's post and its republication. Vehicle logs, hospital confirmation, and a comparison with emergency transport are absent from the evidence. Reposts by Tesla or Musk add no independent verification.
Costs and harms
A medical crisis can impair the supervision on which this driving system depends. NHTSA told Tesla that the company's repost of the story conflicted with the company's own message that drivers must stay in control.
Later

NHTSA published the email with its information request of 5 November 2024. NHTSA questioned the promotion of the story and did not rule on whether the drive took place. No hospital or press confirmation of Franklin's account has appeared since.

What happened

By his own account, Max Paul Franklin, a North Carolina television producer, had severe dehydration at 2 a.m. on 2 April 2024. His blood glucose read 670 after his insulin pump malfunctioned. He says that he got into his Tesla Model Y and engaged the Full Self-Driving (Supervised) v12 trial unlocked the day before. He says that it drove the 13 miles to the VA emergency room without intervention and offered to park. Il Fatto Quotidiano names the hospital as the Charles George VA Medical Center in Asheville. He reports a mild heart attack and discharge with no restrictions. He posted this account on X on 10 April 2024. Tesla and Elon Musk reposted it on 11 April 2024. No hospital or official source has confirmed it. The Office of Defects Investigation of the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) raised Franklin's post with Tesla at a meeting on 15 April 2024 and in an email of 14 May 2024. It wrote that Tesla's repost conflicted with Tesla's own message that drivers must keep control. NHTSA published the email in November 2024 and did not rule on whether the drive took place. Owner accounts of similar rescues on 15 November 2025 and 18 February 2026 appeared in 2026.

Details

AI system

Release

Full Self-Driving v12. The exact installed build is not established.

Role

Franklin reported that the system drove his car to an emergency department.

Configuration

Franklin reported using Full Self-Driving v12 (Supervised), which was enabled on the car on 1 April 2024.

Editor’s note. Franklin identified FSD v12, but not the exact build. The Driven described 12.3.3 as the release available at that time, which does not establish the build installed in his car.

Autonomy

Franklin said that FSD completed the thirteen-mile drive without intervention, but the product was the supervised version and still required an attentive driver.

Workflow

A Tesla Model Y with the FSD (Supervised) v12 trial active, which Franklin says Tesla had unlocked for all US cars on 1 April 2024. A driver who, by his account, could still reach the car, engage FSD by double-clicking the stalk and set the emergency room as destination. A 13-mile route to the VA emergency room that the system, on his account, completed without intervention and then offered to park.

Human oversight

Franklin chose the destination and engaged the driving feature. No independent record establishes his ability to supervise throughout the trip.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

FSD (Supervised) requires an attentive driver with hands on the wheel and eyes on the road (Hindustan Times; Tesla Oracle quoting owner James Locke). A driver too impaired to supervise would be outside its intended use. The account is Franklin's alone. No hospital, emergency-service, police or vehicle-log record of the drive has been found. The photos attached to the post do not show the route, a timestamp or the hospital. Emergency services remain the documented first resort. Il Fatto Quotidiano reports commenters arguing that he should have called for help.

Purpose

Full Self-Driving (Supervised) is built to navigate a Tesla to a chosen destination while an attentive driver supervises it. Driving Franklin to the destination that he set is that function. Use by a driver impaired by a medical emergency is not an intended use. Hindustan Times notes that the system depends on driver intervention when needed. A Tesla owner quoted by Tesla Oracle calls it a Level 2 system. It requires hands on the wheel and eyes on the road even when active.
