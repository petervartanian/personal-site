Short account

Recorded; review pending

State what happened, for whom, and the main qualification.

Summary
Keith Thomas, paralyzed since 2020, regained arm movement and touch after a 15-hour brain-implant operation in Manhasset, New York.
Account
On 9 March 2023, surgeons led by Ashesh Mehta and Netanel Ben-Shalom implanted five microelectrode arrays in the brain of Keith Thomas, 45. The 15-hour operation was at North Shore University Hospital. A diving accident on 18 July 2020 had paralyzed him from the chest down. The Feinstein Institutes' double neural bypass decodes his movement intentions with recurrent neural networks and reinforcement learning. It pairs them with patterned spinal cord stimulation and restores touch by cortical microstimulation. By 28 July 2023 he could move his arms at will and feel his sister's hand. The Feinstein release of 16 July 2026 on the Nature Medicine paper reports 86% and 62% arm-strength gains, self-feeding and gains persisting beyond two years. The original paper distinguishes the earlier arm-strength gains from the AI-controlled hand tasks. Its grasp-force comparison reports 87% success with reinforcement learning and 27% without it.

Account
On 9 March 2023, surgeons led by Ashesh Mehta and Netanel Ben-Shalom implanted five microelectrode arrays in the brain of Keith Thomas, 45. The 15-hour operation was at North Shore University Hospital. A diving accident on 18 July 2020 had paralyzed him from the chest down. The Feinstein Institutes' double neural bypass decodes his movement intentions with recurrent neural networks and reinforcement learning. It pairs them with patterned spinal cord stimulation and restores touch by cortical microstimulation. By 28 July 2023 he could move his arms at will and feel his sister's hand. The Feinstein release of 16 July 2026 on the Nature Medicine paper reports 86% and 62% arm-strength gains, self-feeding and gains persisting beyond two years. The original paper distinguishes the earlier arm-strength gains from the AI-controlled hand tasks. Its grasp-force comparison reports 87% success with reinforcement learning and 27% without it.
Replication
Produced by
  • Chronic complete cervical spinal cord injury (C4 sensory/C5 motor) with movement intentions still readable from motor cortex
  • Five microelectrode arrays placed in motor and somatosensory cortex during a 15-hour mapped operation with the participant partly awake
  • An LSTM plus deep reinforcement-learning decoder paired with patterned spinal cord stimulation and intracortical microstimulation
  • Thirty-five weeks of supervised laboratory sessions inside an approved early-feasibility trial (NCT03680872)
Must hold
  • Decoder accuracy sustained over months (84.6% over five months) so intended movements reliably trigger stimulation
  • Neurosurgical, engineering and rehabilitation teams present throughout. The gains were measured in the laboratory
  • Follow-up with the system switched off to confirm that strength and sensation gains persist

Replication, follow-up, and decisions

Conditions needed to repeat the benefit

Recorded; review pending

Replication
Produced by
  • Chronic complete cervical spinal cord injury (C4 sensory/C5 motor) with movement intentions still readable from motor cortex
  • Five microelectrode arrays placed in motor and somatosensory cortex during a 15-hour mapped operation with the participant partly awake
  • An LSTM plus deep reinforcement-learning decoder paired with patterned spinal cord stimulation and intracortical microstimulation
  • Thirty-five weeks of supervised laboratory sessions inside an approved early-feasibility trial (NCT03680872)
Must hold
  • Decoder accuracy sustained over months (84.6% over five months) so intended movements reliably trigger stimulation
  • Neurosurgical, engineering and rehabilitation teams present throughout. The gains were measured in the laboratory
  • Follow-up with the system switched off to confirm that strength and sensation gains persist
