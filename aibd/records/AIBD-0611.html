Surgery-length predictions shortened patient waits at Sloan Kettering

In a 2018 Memorial Sloan Kettering randomized trial of 683 surgeries, a machine learning model cut scheduling error by 9.8 minutes and patient waits by 33.1 minutes.

What happened

Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center in New York randomized colorectal and gynecology operations scheduled between 7 April and 25 June 2018 on two surgical campuses. For half of the cases, schedulers used case durations predicted by an in-house machine learning model the day before surgery; the other half used the usual EHR estimate adjusted by surgeons or schedulers. Among 683 patients, the mean absolute error of the planned duration fell from 59.3 minutes to 49.5 minutes. The start delay for following cases, which is the time patients waited in the presurgical area, fell by 33.1 minutes per patient, from 49.4 to 16.3 minutes. Time between cases did not change. The model was built and tested by the hospital's own staff at a single cancer center, and the trial covered two surgical services.

Details

AI system

Workflow

The model drew on more than 300 data points per case from the EHR. Predictions ran each morning one weekday before surgery and went to schedulers.

Measurement

Measurement

Recorded patient-in-room to patient-out-of-room times against planned durations; presurgical wait times. [2]

Setting and sample

683 colorectal and gynecology cases on two Memorial Sloan Kettering campuses. [2]

Measures

Mean absolute error of planned case duration; wait time of following patients. [2]

Results

MAE 49.5 versus 59.3 minutes; wait 16.3 versus 49.4 minutes. [2]

Additional value from AI

9.8 minutes less error; 33.1 minutes less waiting. [2]

Limitations

Single center, two services, 11 weeks, model built by the same team. [2]

Denominators

Half of eligible cases. [2]

Materials for replication

Trial protocol and eMethods in supplements. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Data for each case are available before scheduling. Schedulers apply the predicted durations when they set patient times.

Entry details

Sector

Healthcare.

Purpose

The model was built to predict how long each operation will take so that schedulers can plan rooms and patient arrival times. Shorter patient waits follow from that purpose.
