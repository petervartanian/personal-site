Ithaca raises historians' accuracy from 25 to 72 percent

Ithaca restores and dates Greek inscriptions with measured accuracy gains; no specific accepted restoration verified.

ⓘ

What happened

On 9 March 2022 Nature published Ithaca, a deep neural network built by DeepMind with the Universities of Venice Ca' Foscari, Oxford and Athens. Authors include Assael, Sommerschield, Prag and de Freitas. It restores missing text in damaged ancient Greek inscriptions and attributes them to place and date. Ithaca restored text with 62 percent accuracy alone and raised historians' accuracy from 25 to 72 percent when they worked with it. It attributed inscriptions geographically with 71 percent accuracy and dated them to within about 30 years. Its average date of 421 BC for a group of disputed Athenian decrees agreed with recent independent re-dating. The cited sources report accuracy studies and a public interface, not a specific inscription whose restoration epigraphers have since accepted.

Purpose. Built to restore, geographically attribute and date ancient Greek inscriptions; the reported capability was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

Ithaca's outputs were evaluated against held-out ground truth and in a study of historians working with and without it. The 421 BC dating is a model prediction consistent with evidence established by other means, not an outcome the model caused.

Since then
DeepMind followed Ithaca with Aeneas, a successor model for Latin inscriptions published in Nature on 23 July 2025, extending the approach to a second language and to retrieval of parallel texts.It follows Google's previous archaeological tool Ithaca, which also used deep learning to reconstruct and contextualize ancient text, in its case Greek. But while Ithaca and Aeneas use some similar systems, Aeneas also promises to give researchers jumping-off points for further analysis. source

Further evidence

Setting. A large digitized corpus of inscriptions with known provenance and date. A neural network trained on artificially damaged text. Evaluation against held-out inscriptions and with historians in the loop.

Conditions. A verified event of a benefit created or restored would require an epigrapher-accepted restoration or re-dating attributable to the model and documented in a non-vendor source.
