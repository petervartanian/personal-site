Brain implant lets a paralyzed man converse at home for nearly two years

A speech neuroprosthesis at the University of California, Davis, decoded Casey Harrell's attempted speech into text, and he used it at home to communicate 1,960,163 words.

  • 3 a person

    One identifiable person regained conversation with family and colleagues. In the 2026 follow-up the team reports that he used the system for more than 3,800 hours at home without researchers. He communicated nearly two million words and kept working full time.

What happened

At the University of California, Davis, a 45-year-old man with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and severe dysarthria took part in the BrainGate2 trial (NCT00912041). Press identify him as Casey Harrell. The paper reports that before the implant he managed about 6.8 correct words per minute with expert listeners and 6.3 with a head mouse. In July 2023 four 64-electrode arrays were implanted in his left precentral gyrus. From August 2023 a decoder predicted phonemes every 80 milliseconds, and language models with a 125,000-word vocabulary converted his attempted speech to text. The paper gives only the month of the first session. Accuracy was 99.6% with a 50-word vocabulary on day one and 90.2% with the full vocabulary on day two. Prompted-speech accuracy reached 97.5%. Harrell used the system for 248.3 hours of conversation over 8.4 months. Selected conversation sessions had a 3.7% word error rate. On 14 August 2024 Intention to Treat, the podcast of the New England Journal of Medicine, broadcast interviews with Harrell and his wife, Levana Saxon. Saxon said that before the device she was nearly the only person who could understand him. Harrell said that the device lets him converse without a translator and keep working full time. The team's paper appeared in the journal's issue of 15 August 2024. On 12 June 2025 the team reported in Nature that the same arrays drove instantaneous voice synthesis for him, including intonation control and singing short melodies. On 15 June 2026 the team reported in Nature Medicine that over nearly two years he used the system for more than 3,800 hours at home with no researchers present. He communicated 1,960,163 words at an average rate of 56 words per minute and kept full-time employment.

Details

AI system

Model

A decoder identifies phonemes. Three language models convert them into words and sentences.

Role

The models translated attempted speech into text from cortical recordings.

Configuration

Four arrays recorded signals from 256 electrodes in the left precentral gyrus. The language models used a vocabulary of 125,000 words.

Inputs and outputs

The cortical decoder predicted phonemes every 80 milliseconds, and language models converted attempted speech into text across a 125,000-word vocabulary.

Workflow

Preserved speech-motor cortical activity during attempted speech despite severe dysarthria. 256-electrode intracortical recording in the ventral precentral gyrus. Phoneme decoder plus large-vocabulary language models calibrated on the participant's own data within minutes. Regulated trial setting (BrainGate2, NCT00912041) with ongoing research support.

Human oversight

Researchers calibrated the decoder on Harrell's neural signals within the BrainGate2 trial. The team reports that over nearly two years he then used the system for more than 3,800 hours at home with no researchers present. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The reported direct beneficiary is the 45-year-old participant with ALS in the BrainGate2 speech trial. [1]

Reach

One participant reportedly gained conversational speech through the system. The account does not establish a beneficiary count for family members or a wider population. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

Word error rate and phoneme error rate as Levenshtein distance, chosen before data collection, on prompted sentences and in self-paced conversation; communication rate in words per minute. [1]

Setting and sample

One 45-year-old man with ALS and severe dysarthria in the BrainGate2 trial, with sessions at his home over the 8.4 months after the July 2023 implant. [1]

Measures

Word error rate and words per minute. [1]

Results

97.5% accuracy over 248 hours of conversation in 8.4 months; 2.5% word error rate at 31.6 words per minute in the final five Copy Task evaluation sessions. [1]

Limitations

One participant, and the authors are the developers. The Copy Task figures come from prompted sentences in research sessions. [1]

Denominators

248 cumulative hours of conversational use over 8.4 months. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Array signal quality remains stable over months to years. Participant retains the ability to attempt speech and the cognition to operate the system. Decoder recalibration available as neural signals drift.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2.
