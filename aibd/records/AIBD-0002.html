UC Davis neuroprosthesis restores conversation for Casey Harrell

97.5% sustained accuracy over 248 hours of self-paced conversation

Four 64-electrode arrays plus RNN decoder let Casey Harrell (ALS) converse at 97.5% accuracy; later 1.96 million words at home

What happened

At the University of California Davis, a 45-year-old man with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and severe dysarthria (identified in press as Casey Harrell) had four 64-electrode arrays implanted in the left precentral gyrus in July 2023 under the BrainGate2 trial (NCT00912041). From August 2023, a decoder predicting phonemes every 80 ms, combined with language models over a 125,000-word vocabulary, converted his attempted speech to text: 99.6% accuracy with a 50-word vocabulary on the first day, 90.2% with the full vocabulary on day two, and 97.5% sustained accuracy over 248 hours of self-paced conversation with family, friends and colleagues (Card et al., NEJM, August 2024). The paper gives only the month for the first session, so 1 August 2023 is used.

What it was built to do. Built to restore communication by decoding attempted speech from intracortical activity into text; conversational communication was the intended benefit and is what occurred.

Who benefited

  • 3
    personal

    Life-changing benefit to one identifiable person: conversational communication with family and colleagues was restored, and the 2026 follow-up documents over 3,800 hours of unsupervised home use, nearly two million words and continued full-time employment.

The system's part

A neural-network decoder (phoneme prediction every 80 ms) combined with language models is the mechanism converting attempted speech into text; the benefit is attributable to the decoding system, with surgery and arrays as enabling hardware.

Replication

What produced it

  • Preserved speech-motor cortical activity during attempted speech despite severe dysarthria
  • 256-electrode intracortical recording in the ventral precentral gyrus
  • Phoneme decoder plus large-vocabulary language models calibrated on the participant's own data within minutes
  • Regulated trial setting (BrainGate2, NCT00912041) with ongoing research support

What must hold again

  • Array signal quality remains stable over months to years
  • Participant retains the ability to attempt speech and the cognition to operate the system
  • Decoder recalibration available as neural signals drift
