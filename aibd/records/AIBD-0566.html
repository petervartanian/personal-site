Tunbridge Wells museum uses AI editing to process artifact scans faster

Tunbridge Wells Borough Council reports that AI masking and editing in Adobe Lightroom made The Amelia Scott museum's scan and image processing 50% faster.

  • 2 an organization

What happened

Before it used AI, The Amelia Scott already made digital twins of artifacts by 3D scanning, a slow process that often produced scans with errors. The museum worked with PolyPlay to improve this workflow and uses AI masking and editing in Adobe Lightroom to prepare artifact images for digital twins and tactile display objects. In a case study published by the Local Government Association on 12 March 2025, Tunbridge Wells Borough Council reports that scan and image processing became 50% faster and that scan quality improved. The council does not say how it measured these results. It also reports interactive exhibitions and volunteers who progressed to paid roles at the museum. Training, partnerships and digital production contributed to these results together with the AI, so the effect of AI on employment and learning is unknown. Commercial revenue remains prospective, and the sources do not measure what visitors learned.
