The Amelia Scott reports better scans and expanded digital exhibits

The Amelia Scott reports 50% faster image processing, improved scans, interactive exhibits, and volunteers progressing to paid museum roles.

ⓘ

What happened

The Amelia Scott uses Adobe Lightroom AI to prepare artifact images for digital twins and tactile display objects. The council reports 50% faster scan/image processing, better scan quality, interactive exhibitions, and volunteers progressing to paid museum roles. Commercial revenue remains prospective, and visitor learning gains are unmeasured. Training, partnerships, and digital production contribute jointly to the results. AI’s effect on employment and learning is unknown.

Purpose. Improve artifact-imaging workflows and create digital and tactile museum experiences.

AI’s contribution

The source attributes faster masking, editing, and improved scan preparation to Lightroom AI within a broader production workflow. Training, partnerships, and other tools also contribute, so employment and visitor-learning effects cannot be isolated to AI.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    Improved digital twins and delivered interactive and tactile exhibits extend the museum’s capabilities.

  • a person

    The source reports skill development, volunteers obtaining paid roles, and expanded ways to experience artifacts. These are capability/opportunity gains without established livelihood preservation.

Further evidence

Setting. The museum worked with PolyPlay to improve existing 3D-scanning workflows. Digital twins were used in interactive exhibitions and tactile objects, with volunteers and learners involved in production.

Conditions. The conditions needed to reproduce the benefit are unspecified.
