What happened

Scientists at the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) set up Pangu-Weather, a machine-learning model that Huawei Cloud trained on ERA5 reanalysis data, on ECMWF computers. They started it from the same operational analyses as ECMWF's physics-based Integrated Forecasting System (IFS) and compared ten-day forecasts, mainly for the year from 1 March 2022 to 28 February 2023, with 2018 used for tropical cyclones. The authors report that accuracy was comparable for global scores and for extreme events, checked against IFS analyses and against station observations. For 2-meter temperature events over Europe in summer, Pangu-Weather discriminated events better than the IFS, with significant differences for thresholds between the 75th and 90th percentiles; in winter the two models performed alike. Differences in tropical cyclone track error were small and not significant. The authors also report weaknesses. The forecasts are too smooth, bias grows with lead time, and cyclone intensity is forecast poorly. The Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society published the study on 6 June 2024. The authors evaluated deterministic forecasts from one model and did not measure any use in warnings.

Details

AI system

Workflow

ECMWF ran Pangu-Weather from its own operational IFS analyses. Both models were verified with the same scores against analyses and station observations.

Measurement

Measurement

Standard verification scores (correlation, RMSE, area under the ROC curve, cyclone position and pressure error) against IFS analyses, synoptic observations and IBTrACS. [1]

Setting and sample

Ten-day forecasts from 1 March 2022 to 28 February 2023, with 2018 for tropical cyclones; Northern Hemisphere and Europe. [1]

Measures

Upper-air and 2-meter temperature skill; event discrimination; cyclone track and intensity error. [1]

Results

Comparable global skill; better summer event discrimination for the 75th to 90th percentiles; similar cyclone track error; worse cyclone intensity. [1]

Additional value from AI

Comparable accuracy at much lower computing cost; cost not quantified in the paper. [1]

Limitations

Deterministic forecasts from one model; smooth fields and growing bias; poor cyclone intensity; no measure of use by forecasters. [1]

Denominators

Research runs only. [1]

Materials for replication

Model weights public for noncommercial use; ECMWF toolkit public; ECMWF forecasts available. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The model needs high-quality initial conditions from a data assimilation system. The weather to be forecast must resemble the reanalysis period used for training.

Purpose
