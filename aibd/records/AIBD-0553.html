Leeds reports more planning applications processed with Xylo

Leeds City Council reports that planning officers using the Xylo Core AI workspace processed over 40% more householder and minor applications each month.

  • 2 an organization

    The council reports that each officer processed over 40% more householder and minor applications each month, a measurable gain in service capability.

  • 2 a person

    The council reports that all surveyed officers had improved stress and energy levels and did less research work. No source shows a lasting change.

The council describes retrieval of relevant planning material and API calls to language models that generate suggestions and draft text for officer review. What happened

Leeds and Xylo developed a workspace that helps planning officers research applications and prepare material for human review. The council's account in the Local Government Association bank states that the pilot launched in mid-October with eight officers, and the council's other records place that launch in 2025. The council’s technical disclosure of 27 November 2025 names the language models and requires officers to review the generated material. On 11 March 2026 Xylo described a separate pilot of its Patch tool with six officers and 33 pre-applications. On 12 March 2026 Xylo cofounder Habs Isabelle Kim reported a 40% increase against a control group. On 23 April 2026 UKAuthority reported that the government's Digital Planning program funded the project and that it was shortlisted for the 2026 Planning Awards. The outlet repeated the council's figure of one day a week saved and added no measurement of its own. In its account of 8 May 2026, the council reports that the pilot had expanded to 20 officers. The council reports that officer interviews and usage data showed over 40% more householder and minor applications processed monthly per officer. It also reports less research work and improved wellbeing among officers. Neither the council nor Xylo provides the throughput denominator, group assignment, baseline totals or analysis needed to assess that comparison. The Patch sample of six officers cannot automatically be treated as the denominator for the Core throughput result. The reported accuracy improvement concerns officers’ judgments of their work. No source reports an independently measured change in decision accuracy. These are original accounts from the council and the supplier, and no independent evaluation of the reported benefit has appeared.

Details

AI system

Model

The council listed GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4.

Editor’s note. The council’s disclosure dated 27 November 2025 lists these models. It does not identify which model produced each reported result.

Release

The council’s disclosure of 27 November 2025 lists gpt-5-2025-08-07, gemini-2.5-pro, and claude-sonnet-4@20250514. It does not connect individual results to one of those releases. [3]

Role

The models retrieved planning information and generated suggestions and draft text for officers to review.

Configuration

Xylo retrieves planning documents and calls model APIs. It reports no additional model fine-tuning. [3]

Autonomy

Officers must accept each suggestion. Xylo does not decide whether to approve an application. [3]

Workflow

Leeds and Xylo co-designed and tested the workspace over more than six months. The reported pilot focused on time, work quality, and officer wellbeing.

Human oversight

Planning officers had to check and accept suggestions, could reject or override them, and remained responsible for decisions. Xylo supplied sources and reasoning and kept an audit trail. [3]

Benefits

Reach

The pilot began with eight officers and had expanded to 20 by May 2026. No source counts the applicants affected. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The council reports that the pilot grew from eight to 20 officers by May 2026 and that it plans to extend Xylo Core across the wider planning team. No source gives dated follow-up of the throughput gain. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

People and places

Dependencies

The disclosed architecture uses AWS in London and a managed database, with model processing under EU residency agreements. [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Local planning policies and application material are available and kept current for retrieval. Planning officers review generated suggestions against the original material and retain responsibility for decisions. Integration limits, officer training, and the time required for review affect the achievable gains.
