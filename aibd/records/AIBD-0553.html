Leeds reports more planning applications processed with Xylo

Leeds reports over 40% more applications processed per officer, less research time, and improved officer wellbeing during its Xylo pilot.

ⓘ

What happened

Leeds and Xylo developed a workspace that helps planning officers research applications and prepare material for human review. The council reports that its pilot began with eight officers and later expanded to 20. Officer interviews and usage data reportedly showed over 40% more householder and minor applications processed monthly per officer, less research work, and improved wellbeing. Xylo cofounder Habs Isabelle Kim had reported a 40% increase against a control group on March 12, 2026. Neither account provides the throughput denominator, group assignment, baseline totals, or analysis needed to assess that comparison. The reported accuracy improvement concerns officers’ judgments of their work, rather than an independently measured change in decision accuracy. Xylo’s separate March 11 account describes six officers and 33 pre-applications using Patch.

That sample cannot automatically be treated as the denominator for the Core throughput result. The council’s earlier technical disclosure names the language models and requires officers to review the generated material. These are original accounts from the council and supplier, without an independent evaluation of the reported benefit.

Purpose. Help planning officers find and process relevant information while retaining professional judgment.

AI’s contribution

The council describes retrieval of relevant planning material and API calls to language models that generate suggestions and draft text for officer review. Its November 2025 disclosure identifies GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Claude Sonnet 4. The models do not determine planning merits. Reported changes in work and wellbeing come from project-side interviews and usage data. A supplier-reported control group lacks sufficient published methods to establish an independent causal comparison.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    Reported application throughput rises by over 40%, supporting a measurable service-capability gain.

  • a person

    All surveyed officers reportedly experienced improved stress and energy levels, alongside less research work. These are reported wellbeing/work-capability improvements without a demonstrated lasting change.

Further evidence

Setting. Leeds and Xylo co-designed and tested the workspace over more than six months. The reported pilot focused on time, work quality, and officer wellbeing.

Conditions. Local planning policies and application material are available and kept current for retrieval. Planning officers review generated suggestions against the original material and retain responsibility for decisions. Integration limits, officer training, and the time required for review affect the achievable gains.
