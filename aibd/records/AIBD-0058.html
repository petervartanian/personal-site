Superpower Glass briefly raises social scores of children with autism

In a 71-child trial, Stanford's Superpower Glass emotion classifier on Google Glass added 4.58 points on a socialization scale, and Spectrum reported that the gain was gone six weeks after treatment.

Sources

  1. Peer-reviewed study Developer

    Daniels J, Schwartz JN, Voss C, et al. (2018). Exploratory study examining the at-home feasibility of a wearable tool for social-affective learning in children with autism. npj Digital Medicine 1:32.

    Fourteen compliant families used Google Glass and Android software; five families were excluded for fewer than three weekly 20-minute sessions; parents reported recognition problems and altered facial expressions. Abstract, Results, and family feedback table

    Original

    12 of 14 families commented that they had observed an increase in eye contact from intake to conclusion during the semi-structured interview
    Children's total SRS-2 scores decreased an average of 7.38 points over the course of the study

  2. Peer-reviewed study Developer

    Voss C, Schwartz J, Daniels J, et al. (2019). Effect of Wearable Digital Intervention for Improving Socialization in Children With Autism Spectrum Disorder: A Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Pediatrics 173(5):446-454.

    showed significant improvements on the Vineland Adaptive Behaviors Scale socialization subscale compared with treatment as usual controls (mean [SD] treatment impact, 4.58 [1.62]; P = .005)

  3. Peer-reviewed study Independent author

    Perry N, Sun C, Munro M, Boulton KA, Guastella AJ, AI technology to support adaptive functioning in neurodevelopmental conditions in everyday environments: a systematic review, npj Digital Medicine 7:370, 19 December 2024. The University of Sydney authors declare no tie to Stanford or Cognoa. They report that the 71-child trial carried a low risk of bias, flag potential funding bias and low adherence, and collected no new outcome data.

    a wearable intervention utilizing AI, known as Superpower Glass, was found to result in significant improvements in social skills as measured by the Vineland Adaptive Behaviour Scale

  4. News report Independent author Context or contrary evidence

    Hannah Furfaro, Tech firm's 'Superpower Glass' for autism not so super, experts say, Spectrum (now The Transmitter), 2 May 2019. The outlet interviewed autism researchers with no role in the trial; they argue that the trial design was weak and report that the Vineland gain did not last.

    And six weeks after the treatment ended, the children's social improvements on the Vineland had disappeared.
