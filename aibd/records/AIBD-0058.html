What happened

Superpower Glass, developed at Stanford University, runs a machine-learning facial-expression classifier on Google Glass. It shows a child which emotion a facing person is displaying. An at-home feasibility study had intake between July and October 2016 (day not given, so 1 July is used). In it, 14 children with autism completed the protocol. Total SRS-2 scores fell an average of 7.38 points and 12 of 14 families reported more eye contact (npj Digital Medicine, 2018). A randomized trial enrolled 71 children aged 6 to 12 from 1 November 2016 to 11 April 2018. Those adding the device to applied behavioral analysis therapy gained 4.58 points more on the Vineland socialization subscale than controls (P = .005). Three other primary measures did not reach significance (JAMA Pediatrics, 2019). Results are cohort-level.

What it was built to do. Teach emotion recognition and facial engagement to children with autism through real-time wearable feedback

The system's part

The emotion classifier, trained on public facial-expression databases and tuned for home conditions, generates the real-time cues the intervention relies on. The Guess the Emotion game within the package does not use the classifier. So part of the effect may come from structured family interaction rather than the AI.

Replication

What produced it. Real-time emotion classification on a head-worn display. Structured 20-minute home sessions several times a week. Concurrent applied behavioral analysis therapy.

What must hold again. Child tolerates wearing Google Glass. Family adherence to the session schedule. Classifier accuracy under home lighting and pose variation.
