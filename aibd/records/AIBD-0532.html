Changan says that its voice agent sets up car scenarios in seconds

Changan reports that its Intelligent In-Vehicle Scenario Orchestration Agent cuts scenario setup from minutes to seconds; it reports no timing study.

  • 1 a person

    Changan states that scenario setup falls from minutes to seconds, a convenience for drivers. No timing study or independent user study supports the figure.

  • Not rated an organization

    The makers expect that manufacturers will integrate services more easily. No source measures a gain for any organization.

What happened

A Changan brochure for the Qiyuan E07 SUV, uploaded to the company's GitHub repository on 28 May 2025, markets scenarios that drivers program by voice through an AI large model. In its project account for the International Telecommunication Union, current as of June 2025, Changan reports that its vehicle agent demonstration reduces scenario configuration tasks from minutes to seconds. The account states that configuring a scenario typically takes several minutes and needs technical knowledge. It cites the brochure. It lists only a Douyin video as its testbed and reports no timing study. Development continues. No source measures safety or environmental benefits.

Details

AI system

Role

The agent configures in-vehicle scenarios through an orchestration interface; a demonstration reports reducing this setup from minutes to seconds.

Autonomy

The submission describes generated trigger-and-action chains that can invoke climate, entertainment, and driver-assistance functions. A fuller interface for issuing driving commands is future work, so the demonstrated control boundary remains unclear.

Workflow

Conditions that could produce the benefit are unspecified.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Automotive instruction data and vehicle-service interfaces.

Evidence

Original evidence

Both sources come from Changan. The first is its project account for the International Telecommunication Union, and the second is a brochure for the Qiyuan E07 SUV that the account cites. Neither source reports a measurement of time or of use by drivers. [1] [3]
