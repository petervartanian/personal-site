What happened

MediaNama reported on 30 December 2025 (Prabhanu Kumar Das) that a Right to Information response showed 23 banks had implemented MuleHunter.AI as of 10 December 2025, up from at least 15 in August and nearly 20 in November 2025, naming Canara Bank, Punjab National Bank, Bank of India, Bank of Baroda, AU Small Finance Bank and Federal Bank. The tool, developed by the Reserve Bank Innovation Hub, analyzes transaction and account activity patterns across banks to identify accounts likely used as mules in financial fraud. The RBI said that it cannot provide data on the number of mule accounts identified or acted upon through MuleHunter.AI because it holds such information in a fiduciary capacity with banks and disclosure could harm competitive interests; RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra is reported as saying that the tool is showing a good success rate, without figures.

RBIH's own project page returned no readable content, and Google News RSS headlines from May to September 2026 describe an I4C-RBIH memorandum and deployment across 31 banks, which were not read. The date used is the article's publication date.

What it was built to do. Identify bank accounts likely used as money mules in financial fraud.

The system's part

Not assessed. The tool is described as AI-based pattern analysis across banks, but no detection result is available to assess its contribution; RBIH, a central-bank subsidiary, developed it and commercial and public-sector banks deploy it.

Replication

What produced it. Cross-bank transaction and account-activity pattern analysis run by a central-bank-owned hub.

What must hold again. Bank adoption and data sharing with the hub. Public disclosure of detection outcomes, without which no evaluation is possible.
