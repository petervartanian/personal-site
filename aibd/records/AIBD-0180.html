What happened

MediaNama (Prabhanu Kumar Das) reported on 30 December 2025 on a Right to Information response. It showed 23 banks had implemented MuleHunter.AI as of 10 December 2025. That was up from at least 15 in August and nearly 20 in November 2025. The report named Canara Bank, Punjab National Bank, Bank of India, Bank of Baroda, AU Small Finance Bank and Federal Bank. The tool was developed by the Reserve Bank Innovation Hub (RBIH). It analyzes transaction and account activity patterns across banks to identify accounts likely used as mules in financial fraud. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) said that it cannot provide data on the number of mule accounts identified or acted upon through MuleHunter.AI. The reason given was that it holds such information in a fiduciary capacity with banks and disclosure could harm competitive interests.

RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra is reported as saying that the tool is showing a good success rate, without figures. RBIH's own project page was not available. News headlines from May to September 2026 describe an I4C-RBIH memorandum and deployment across 31 banks. The articles behind those headlines are not among the sources.

What it was built to do. Identify bank accounts likely used as money mules in financial fraud.

The system's part

Not assessed. The tool is described as AI-based pattern analysis across banks. No detection result is available to assess its contribution. RBIH, a central-bank subsidiary, developed it. Commercial and public-sector banks deploy it.

Replication

What produced it. Cross-bank transaction and account-activity pattern analysis run by a central-bank-owned hub.

What must hold again. Bank adoption and data sharing with the hub. Public disclosure of detection outcomes, without which no evaluation is possible.
