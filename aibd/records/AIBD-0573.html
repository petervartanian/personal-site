Nara proposes AI voice control for an accessible metaverse

A Nara Knowledge Information patent describes AI speech recognition for a metaverse that blind users control by voice; no source shows that its Blind Metaverse demo uses that function.

What happened

Nara Knowledge Information is developing audio and tactile interfaces to help blind users navigate virtual spaces. The public demo repository for Blind Metaverse holds two commits, both made on 11 February 2025. The demo integrates Google Cloud speech synthesis, and the sources do not specify the exact model behind its Korean Standard voice. A Nara patent dated 16 May 2025 describes deep-learning speech recognition that interprets spoken input from a Braille terminal and controls the computing device. The patent does not name the product. Its connection to Blind Metaverse is inferred from the assignee, the inventor, the purpose and the interface design. Nara's account in the International Telecommunication Union publication AI for Good: Innovate for Impact gives information current as of June 2025. It states that a blind planner took part in accessibility planning and that a blind person used the platform at a disability festival exhibition. The account gives no date for the exhibition and reports no results. The sources do not confirm that the patented speech-recognition function was deployed, and they do not measure its effect on users.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The demo converts descriptions of nearby virtual objects into spoken Korean and supports tactile presentation. [1] [2] [3]

Workflow

The patent proposes a deep-learning speech-recognition model that converts spoken input from a Braille terminal into control of the computing device supporting the metaverse interface.

AI contribution

Nara's patent describes deep-learning speech recognition that interprets spoken input and controls the computing device. No source shows that the Blind Metaverse demo uses that function. The demo integrates Google Cloud speech synthesis. No source measures a change in navigation or access for any blind user. [1] [2] [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The proposed design requires speech recognition, compatible audio input and Braille interfaces, and integration with the virtual environment. Conditions sufficient to improve access remain unknown.
