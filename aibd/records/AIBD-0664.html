A chat assistant helps new support agents resolve more issues

A GPT-based chat assistant raised issues resolved per hour by 15% on average among 5,172 support agents, with the largest gains for novice and low-skilled agents.

  • 2 an organization

    The firm's agents resolved 15% more issues per hour on average after gaining access.

What happened

Erik Brynjolfsson of Stanford and Danielle Li and Lindsey Raymond of MIT studied the staggered rollout of a generative AI chat assistant to the technical support agents of a Fortune 500 enterprise software company that sells business process software to small and medium-sized businesses in the United States. Most of the agents worked from offices in the Philippines, either for the firm or for outside contractors. An AI customer-service software company that the authors do not name built the tool on OpenAI's GPT models, and the tool suggested replies to agents in real time. A seven-week randomized pilot with 50 agents came first, and most agents gained access between November 2020 and February 2021. In the 2023 working paper, which covers about 3 million chats by 5,179 agents, the authors report that access raised issues resolved per hour by 14% on average and by 34% for novice and low-skilled agents. The version that the Quarterly Journal of Economics published in 2025 covers 5,172 agents and reports a gain of 15% on average. Customers were more polite and asked less often to speak to a manager, and attrition among agents with less than six months of tenure fell by about 10 percentage points. The most experienced and highest-skilled agents saw small gains in speed and small declines in quality. The study covers one firm, and the authors write that the attrition results call for more caution than the productivity results. The Stanford Digital Economy Lab funded the work.

Sources

    Access to AI assistance increases worker productivity, as measured by issues resolved per hour, by 15% on average, with substantial heterogeneity across workers.

  2. Preprint, by authors with no tie to the system.

    Brynjolfsson E, Li D, Raymond LR. Generative AI at Work. NBER Working Paper 31161, April 2023, revised November 2023.

    Access to the tool increases productivity, as measured by issues resolved per hour, by 14% on average, including a 34% improvement for novice and low-skilled workers but with minimal impact on experienced and highly skilled workers.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Agents received real-time reply suggestions inside live text chats. The model was trained on a large set of past chats labeled with outcomes. Agents stayed free to ignore the suggestions.

Measurement

Measurement

Issues resolved per hour, resolution rate, handle time, net promoter score and attrition from the firm's systems. [2]

Setting and sample

5,179 agents and about 3 million chats in the working paper; 5,172 agents in the published version. [2]

Measures

Resolutions per hour. [2]

Results

14% on average and 34% for novice and low-skilled agents (working paper); 15% on average (published). [2]

Additional value from AI

About 14% to 15% more resolutions per hour. [2]

Limitations

One firm; staggered rollout chosen by the firm; attrition estimates lack agent fixed effects; small quality declines for the most skilled agents. [2]

Denominators

Access to real-time AI suggestions during chats. [2]

Materials for replication

Firm data not public. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The work must consist of repeated text conversations on recurring problems. The firm must hold enough past conversations to train the model. Gains depend on a supply of less experienced agents.

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.
