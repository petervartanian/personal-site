UNDP hands Mines Eye to SESU after a pilot scanning over 360 ha

scan over 360 hectares of confirmed and suspected hazardous areas

Mines Eye (magnetometry, visual/infrared imaging, AI detection of explosive remnants) scanned over 360 ha in a five-month SESU pilot; handed over 21 Jun 2025. No detection or land-release counts.

What happened

UNDP Ukraine's press release of 21 June 2025, datelined Mykolaiv, says that UNDP, with financial support from the Government of Luxembourg, 'officially handed over the Mines Eye aerial detection system to the State Emergency Service of Ukraine (SESU)' at the base of SESU's Main Department in Mykolaiv oblast, in the presence of UNDP Acting Administrator Haoliang Xu. The release describes the system, 'Developed in Ukraine' by a developer it does not name, as combining magnetometry with visual and infrared aerial imaging and states that 'Using artificial intelligence, it detects explosive remnants of war (ERW), including anti-tank mines, even beneath vegetation', producing maps and anomaly reports for operators before manual or mechanical clearance. Over a five-month pilot, UNDP-trained SESU teams 'used the system to scan over 360 hectares of confirmed and suspected hazardous areas in Kharkiv and Mykolaiv oblasts'. SESU First Deputy Head Vitalii Myroniuk is quoted that the technology 'enables us to detect threats in areas that are too dangerous for manual inspection'. The release gives no number of items detected or confirmed, no hectares released, and no accuracy figure; the earlier verifier noted that UNDP's 26 June 2025 release concerned a testing exhibition with mock devices. No source independent of UNDP or SESU was found.

What it was built to do. Built to survey contaminated land remotely and flag explosive remnants of war, including anti-tank mines under vegetation, so that clearance teams can plan safely; the pilot was that intended use.

The system's part

UNDP's release attributes the detection of explosive remnants beneath vegetation to the system's artificial intelligence working on magnetometry and visual and infrared imagery, but reports only area scanned; no detection, false-alarm or confirmation figure is given, and no source independent of UNDP and SESU was found. The developer is unnamed, so the AI component cannot be characterised further.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • UNDP-trained SESU teams flying the system over confirmed and suspected hazardous areas during a five-month pilot
  • Donor funding (Government of Luxembourg) for procurement and training

What must hold again

  • Anomaly reports are followed by manual or mechanical clearance that confirms the detections; the release documents no such confirmation
  • Terrain and vegetation resemble the Kharkiv and Mykolaiv pilot areas
