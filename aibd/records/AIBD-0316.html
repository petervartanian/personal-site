Ukraine's emergency service scans over 360 hectares with Mines Eye

Mines Eye, an aerial system whose AI detects explosive remnants of war, scanned over 360 hectares in a five-month pilot by Ukraine's emergency service; the release gives no detection counts.

What happened

Mines Eye is an aerial detection system that combines magnetometry with visual and infrared aerial imaging. A press release of 21 June 2025 from the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in Ukraine calls the system “Developed in Ukraine” but does not name the developer. The release states that “Using artificial intelligence, it detects explosive remnants of war (ERW), including anti-tank mines, even beneath vegetation”. The system produces maps and anomaly reports for operators before manual or mechanical clearance. UNDP-trained teams of the State Emergency Service of Ukraine (SESU) ran a five-month pilot. They “used the system to scan over 360 hectares of confirmed and suspected hazardous areas in Kharkiv and Mykolaiv oblasts”. The release is datelined Mykolaiv. It says that UNDP “officially handed over the Mines Eye aerial detection system to the State Emergency Service of Ukraine (SESU)”. Financial support came from the Government of Luxembourg. The handover took place at the base of SESU's Main Department in Mykolaiv oblast, with UNDP Acting Administrator Haoliang Xu present. SESU First Deputy Head Vitalii Myroniuk is quoted as saying that the technology “enables us to detect threats in areas that are too dangerous for manual inspection”. The release gives no number of items detected or confirmed, no hectares released and no accuracy figure. UNDP's release of 26 June 2025 concerned a testing exhibition with mock devices. No cited source is independent of UNDP or SESU.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Magnetic, visible-light and infrared measurements produced maps and anomaly reports for demining operators.

Autonomy

The system supported aerial survey; operators still needed to verify anomalies and perform manual or mechanical clearance.

Workflow

UNDP-trained SESU teams flying the system over confirmed and suspected hazardous areas during a five-month pilot. Donor funding (Government of Luxembourg) for procurement and training.

Human oversight

The system produces maps and anomaly reports for operators, and manual or mechanical clearance follows. The release documents no clearance that confirmed a detection. [1]

Benefits

Duration

The pilot lasted five months before the handover on 21 June 2025. No source dated after that day reports that the State Emergency Service of Ukraine used Mines Eye. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Anomaly reports are followed by manual or mechanical clearance that confirms the detections. The release documents no such confirmation. Terrain and vegetation resemble the Kharkiv and Mykolaiv pilot areas.

Evidence

Original evidence

Both citations come from the press release of 21 June 2025 by the United Nations Development Programme, which procured the system and trained the teams. No cited source is independent of that agency or the State Emergency Service of Ukraine. [1]
