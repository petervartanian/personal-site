ChatGPT suggests tethered cord syndrome; neurosurgeon confirms

depth 3 for a person

Mother entered son's MRI notes and symptoms into ChatGPT; it suggested tethered cord syndrome, confirmed and operated on by a neurosurgeon.

What happened

In 2023 Courtney, mother of Alex, a boy whose chronic pain began at age 4 during the COVID-19 lockdown and who went on to grind his teeth, stop growing taller and drag his left foot, entered his MRI notes and symptom observations line by line into ChatGPT after 17 physicians over three years had not produced a diagnosis. ChatGPT suggested tethered cord syndrome. She booked a new neurosurgeon, Dr Holly Gilmer of the Michigan Head & Spine Institute, who reviewed the MRI, identified spina bifida occulta with a tethered cord, and operated to release the cord a few weeks before the TODAY.com report (first published 11 September 2023, updated 12 September 2023); Alex was still recovering. The month of the ChatGPT query and the surgery date are not given.

What it was built to do. ChatGPT is a general-purpose conversational assistant built to answer user prompts; producing a diagnostic hypothesis from entered clinical notes is an instance of that general function, not a designed or validated medical capability.

The system's part

ChatGPT produced the hypothesis (tethered cord syndrome) that the mother took to a new neurosurgeon; the diagnosis itself was made by the neurosurgeon from the existing MRI and the treatment was surgical. ChatGPT's contribution was to end the diagnostic search; the source is a news report quoting the mother and the treating neurosurgeon, not a peer-reviewed case report.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A caregiver with detailed clinical records (MRI report text, symptom observations) entering them into a general-purpose LLM. A clinician willing to re-review existing imaging against the proposed diagnosis.

What must hold again. The LLM hypothesis is verified by a qualified clinician against imaging or testing before any treatment. The caregiver has access to the patient's own records to supply to the model.
