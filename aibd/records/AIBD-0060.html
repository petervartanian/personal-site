ChatGPT suggests tethered cord syndrome; neurosurgeon confirms

What happened

In 2023 Courtney, mother of Alex, entered his magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) notes and symptom observations line by line into ChatGPT. Alex's chronic pain began at age 4 during the COVID-19 lockdown. He went on to grind his teeth, stop growing taller and drag his left foot. 17 physicians over three years had found no diagnosis. ChatGPT suggested tethered cord syndrome. She booked a new neurosurgeon, Dr Holly Gilmer of the Michigan Head & Spine Institute. Gilmer reviewed the MRI, found spina bifida occulta with a tethered cord and operated to release it. That was a few weeks before the TODAY.com report (11 September 2023, updated 12 September 2023). Alex was still recovering. The month of the ChatGPT query and the surgery date are not given.

What it was built to do. ChatGPT is a general-purpose conversational assistant built to answer user prompts. Producing a diagnostic hypothesis from entered clinical notes is an instance of that general function, not a designed or validated medical capability.

The system's part

ChatGPT produced the hypothesis (tethered cord syndrome) that the mother took to a new neurosurgeon. The diagnosis itself was made by the neurosurgeon from the existing MRI, and the treatment was surgical. ChatGPT's contribution was ending the diagnostic search. The source is a news report quoting the mother and treating neurosurgeon, not a peer-reviewed case report.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A caregiver entering detailed clinical records (MRI report text, symptom observations) in a general-purpose language model. A clinician willing to re-review existing imaging against the proposed diagnosis.

What must hold again. The model's hypothesis is verified by a qualified clinician against imaging or testing before treatment. The caregiver has access to the patient's own records to supply to the model.
