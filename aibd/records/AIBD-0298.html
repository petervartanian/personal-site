Corsano CardioWatch detects a cardiac arrest in post-processing

In post-processing, the CardioWatch photoplethysmography algorithm detected cardiac arrest at 09:50:17 after loss of pulse in a cyclist; his implanted defibrillator ended it, and no alert was sent.

  • Not rated a person

    The patient was saved by his ICD. The wristband's detection was retrospective and produced no alert or treatment.

  • Not rated society

    The authors argue that a live system could act as a first witness for unwitnessed arrests. No deployment outcome exists yet.

The system’s part
The pulse-loss detector was run afterwards on exported data by a blinded Corsano data scientist. It flagged the loss of pulse, and the fall detector flagged the collapse. Neither ran live or sent an alert, and the ICD shock restored circulation. The group describes its pulse detector as rule-based. Its DETECT-2 paper describes a machine-learning fall detector, though the case report does not name the fall model it used. The developers' own papers are the only full sources; an independent editorial restates the case without new data.
Limits
The wristband's algorithm classified the arrest only after the event and triggered no treatment. The implantable defibrillator ended the arrhythmia. The group describes its pulse detector as rule-based, and the case report does not name the fall model that it used. DETECT-3 is still measuring false-positive rates for a future live system.
Costs and harms
The result establishes feasibility for one event, while false-alarm performance during daily activity remained a study objective.
Later
Persisted, 24 July 2026. The same group's later prospective validation in ICU patients with non-shockable arrest after withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment reported 100% sensitivity in the 23-patient test set with one false positive, and DETECT-1b (April 2026) externally validated the algorithm in 50 induced shockable arrests; no live alerting has been reported.

What happened

The arrest took place before 16 December 2025, when Resuscitation Plus received the case report; the paper gives no calendar date for the event. A 64-year-old man with heart failure, permanent atrial fibrillation and a secondary-prevention Biotronik implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) was wearing the CE-certified CardioWatch 287–2 (Corsano Health, The Hague). He wore it in the DETECT-3 study. After three weeks, while cycling alone, he developed monomorphic ventricular tachycardia (VT). It accelerated to ventricular fibrillation (VF) after an antitachycardia pacing burst. The ICD delivered a 40 J shock. He regained consciousness, cycled home, and was admitted for rhythm observation with a good recovery. The wristband's 32 Hz photoplethysmography and accelerometry data were exported afterwards. A Corsano Health data scientist blinded to the event ran them through the DETECT photoplethysmography algorithm. It detected cardiac arrest at 09:50:17 after loss of pulse. It ended the alert at 09:50:57 when pulsations returned. The group's fall-detection algorithm flagged the fall about 8–10 seconds after pulselessness. Edgar, Jansen, Pol, Pisters, van Royen and Bonnes of Radboud University Medical Center (with Rijnstate Hospital) reported the case in Resuscitation Plus on 29 January 2026. They report that it is the first real-life cardiac arrest captured by a wristband with cardiac-arrest detection. The authors state that the detection was retrospective. They state that the study collects data to measure false-positive rates for a future live system. The patient's survival is attributable to the ICD. The wristband changed nothing for him. In an accompanying editorial, Veigl and Billig, clinicians in St. Poelten and Aachen with no declared tie to the device, wrote that the case is the first real-world proof of concept; they add no data on the event. In its earlier DETECT-1a study the group reported 98% sensitivity in induced circulatory arrests. In the DETECT-2 study, published on 7 March 2026, the group describes a machine-learning fall detector built from healthy volunteers' simulated falls. The case report does not name the fall model that it used. In April 2026 the group reported external validation in 50 patients with induced VT or VF (DETECT-1b). In July 2026, in The Lancet Regional Health - Europe, the group reported 100% sensitivity (23 of 23) with one false positive in true non-shockable arrests after withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment. That paper describes the algorithm as rule-based. No main results of DETECT-3 or live alerting had been published by October 2026.

Details

AI system

Model

DETECT, a rule-based algorithm that detects absent pulsations.

Configuration

Researchers applied DETECT after the event to data from a CardioWatch 287-2. The algorithm did not run live or send an alert.

Inputs and outputs

Stored pulse and movement signals were analyzed after the event to detect circulatory arrest and collapse.

Autonomy

The wristband sent no live alert. The patient’s implanted defibrillator terminated the arrhythmia.

Workflow

Continuous wristband wear with 32 Hz photoplethysmography and accelerometry uploaded to a study cloud. A shockable arrest with clear loss of pulsations during cycling, in a patient whose ICD provided the reference and the rescue. Blinded after-the-fact processing by the developers' data scientist.

Human oversight

A Corsano Health data scientist who was blinded to the event ran the exported data through the algorithm after the arrest. The algorithms did not run live and sent no alert. The patient's implantable defibrillator ended the arrest. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

People and places

Dependencies

The wristband continuously sent sensor data to a protected study portal for later analysis.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Adequate photoplethysmography quality during motion (the authors note it is typically disturbed by motion). A live implementation with acceptable false-positive rates, which DETECT-3 is still measuring. An alerting chain to emergency services, which does not yet exist for this device.

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2.
