What happened

The ACCESS trial (NCT05131451) at the Johns Hopkins Pediatric Diabetes Center, Baltimore, randomized 164 people aged 8–21 with type 1 or type 2 diabetes. Enrollment ran from 24 November 2021 to 6 June 2022. One arm had an autonomous AI diabetic eye exam at the point of care with IDx-DR/LumineticsCore (Digital Diagnostics). The other had scripted referral and education. Exam completion within six months was 100% (81 of 81) in the AI arm versus 22% in controls. 25 AI-arm participants had an abnormal result, and 64% completed follow-through with an eye-care provider versus 22% of controls. Follow-up ended 6 December 2022. The paper appeared in Nature Communications in 2024. No individual participant's diagnosis or treatment is described.

What it was built to do. IDx-DR/LumineticsCore is built to autonomously detect more-than-mild diabetic retinopathy from fundus images at the point of care. Increasing screening completion is its intended benefit.

The system's part

The randomized design supports a causal effect of the autonomous AI exam on screening completion and follow-through. The trial did not report whether any participant's vision outcome changed. Senior author M.D. Abramoff is an investor, director and consultant of Digital Diagnostics (disclosed). The first author and most co-authors (nine of eleven) are Johns Hopkins investigators. Co-author R. Channa is at the University of Wisconsin. The author-contribution statement says Abramoff had no access to the underlying data at any time. The paper is thus not vendor-authored.
