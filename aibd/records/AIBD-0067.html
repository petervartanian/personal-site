An AI eye exam increased screening among young people with diabetes

In the ACCESS trial, all 81 young people offered IDx-DR completed an eye exam, compared with 18 of 82 controls referred to an eye-care provider.

  • 2 a person

    The trial measured a substantial increase in completed diabetic eye exams. It did not establish improved vision or prevention of blindness.

The system’s part
IDx-DR assessed retinal photographs and returned a screening result during the diabetes visit. The trial compared this on-site service with referral elsewhere. A retina specialist also reviewed the images for safety. A coauthor had financial interests in the manufacturer, although he did not have access to the underlying data.
Limits
The paper does not establish later vision outcomes, the device’s exact software release, or a separate causal effect of the algorithm.
Costs and harms
The AI produced positive screening results that required further assessment. Its reported specificity was 78.9% against specialist review. A positive screen was not a confirmed diagnosis.
Later
Persisted, 14 February 2025. In February 2025 two Johns Hopkins clinicians reported that the health system was still using the system for youth and adults. They also cited preliminary data from a trial of almost 400 youths on screening disparities. A Johns Hopkins follow-on trial, ACCESS 2 (NCT05463289), was still recruiting in September 2026, with completion expected on 30 December 2026. No source reports vision outcomes for the ACCESS participants.

What happened

The ACCESS trial enrolled young people with diabetes at two Johns Hopkins clinics in Baltimore. Of 164 participants randomized, one control was excluded after entering another screening study. All 81 participants assigned to an IDx-DR eye exam completed screening, compared with 18 of 82 controls referred to an eye-care provider. The AI marked 25 results as positive, and 16 of those participants attended an eye-care visit within six months. A retina specialist reviewed all AI images for safety because the device was not cleared for this age group. The study measured screening and follow-up. No source reports vision outcomes for the ACCESS participants. In February 2025 two Johns Hopkins clinicians reported that the system was still in systematic use for youth and adults. In April 2026 reviewers at the University of Oxford and the University of Western Australia, who have no tie to the maker or to Johns Hopkins, rated the ACCESS trial at low risk of bias. They found that its gain in follow-up was the largest of six studies of AI-assisted eye screening. A follow-on Johns Hopkins trial, ACCESS 2, is due to finish at the end of 2026.

Details

AI system

Model

The locked diagnostic algorithms within IDx-DR. The trial does not name a separate model family or release. [1]

Provider

Digital Diagnostics, United States. [1]

Role

The software checked image quality and assessed photographs for diabetic eye disease. [1]

Configuration

The device used two retinal views and allowed up to three attempts to obtain suitable images. The trial does not name the camera model. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Two color retinal photographs centered on the fovea and optic disc were assessed. The output indicated disease, no disease, or insufficient image quality. [1]

Autonomy

The software produced its result without a clinician interpreting the photographs first. The research team nevertheless obtained specialist overreads for safety. [1]

Workflow

Clinic staff photographed the retina during the diabetes visit. Participants received the result immediately and were referred for an eye-care visit when indicated. [1]

Human oversight

A board-certified retina specialist reviewed every AI image for safety. An independent monitoring board oversaw the study. [1]

AI contribution

IDx-DR made an eye exam available at the diabetes visit. The trial tests this entire on-site service against referral elsewhere. [1]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Young people with type 1 or type 2 diabetes who were due for a diabetic eye exam. [1]

Reach

All 81 AI participants and 18 of 82 controls completed screening. The 25 positive AI screens were not all confirmed diagnoses. [1]

Distribution and access

The trial included a racially diverse population, and 47% of participants had Medicaid coverage. It was not large enough to establish equal effects across every subgroup. [1]

Duration

The trial followed screening and attendance at eye-care visits for six months. In February 2025 two Johns Hopkins clinicians reported that the health system still used IDx-DR for youth and adults. No source reports vision outcomes for the ACCESS participants. [1] [2] [3]

Measurement

Measurement

The primary outcome was documented completion of a diabetic eye exam within six months. Researchers also tracked referral attendance and participant satisfaction. [1]

Setting and sample

The trial randomized 164 participants at two Baltimore clinics. After one control was excluded, the analysis included 81 AI participants and 82 controls. [1]

Measures

Completion of a diabetic eye exam within six months, with referral attendance as a secondary outcome. [1]

Results

Screening completion was 100% with AI and 22% with referral, a difference of 78 percentage points (95% CI, 69–87). [1]

Additional value from AI

Randomization supports the effect of offering an immediate AI exam rather than a referral. It does not separate the algorithm from convenience and access at the clinic. [1]

Limitations

The trial measured access to screening, not later vision. The referral comparison used 25 AI-positive participants versus the entire control group, so those secondary denominators are not equivalent. [1]

Denominators

Participants enrolled from 24 November 2021 to 6 June 2022. Follow-up ended on 6 December 2022. An AI exam generally took five to ten minutes. [1]

Materials for replication

The protocol and supplement describe the procedure. De-identified data require an approved proposal and data-use agreement. The commercial diagnostic code is not published. [1]

Money

Kind of value

A gift card for study participation. This was a transfer, not value that AI created. [1]

Amount

US$25 per participant, plus a parking pass whose value was not reported. [1]

Currency and prices

U.S. Dollars during the 2021–2022 study. No inflation adjustment was reported. [1]

Period and unit

Per participant for study participation. [1]

Realization

The authors report that participants received the gift card and parking pass. [1]

Recipients and payers

Study participants received compensation. The article does not identify the precise paying account. [1]

Gross or net

The US$25 is the face value of a gift card. It is not net benefit from screening. [1]

Included costs

The article does not report the full cost of the service, specialist review, staff time, or parking passes. [1]

Comparison

No comparison of financial outcomes between the randomized groups was reported. [1]

Method

The source states the gift card’s face value. It does not value health outcomes. [1]

Return and payback

No financial return or payback period was estimated. [1]

People and places

Participants

Johns Hopkins conducted the trial at Johns Hopkins Hospital and Mount Washington Pediatric Hospital. Digital Diagnostics supplied the diagnostic product. [1]

Dependencies

The service required a retinal camera, trained clinic staff, IDx-DR, specialist safety review, and a referral route. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The tested service combined an immediate eye exam, clinic staff, follow-up referrals, and specialist safety review for young participants. [1]

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.

Availability

LumineticsCore is commercially available. The trial’s pediatric use was investigational because the FDA indication covered adults aged 22 or older. [1]

Institutional decision

Johns Hopkins evaluated AI screening in young people below the device’s FDA-indicated age. [1]

Grounds

The study received IRB approval, an investigational device exemption, and independent safety monitoring. [1]

Action and result

Staff used the device in the trial and arranged retina-specialist review of every AI image. [1]

Success criterion

The prespecified primary outcome was completion of an eye exam within six months. [1]

Evidence

Language

English. No translation was used. [1]

Original evidence

The benefit rests on the ACCESS randomized trial, which its investigators published in Nature Communications on 11 January 2024. Independent reviewers rated that trial at low risk of bias in a systematic review published on 17 April 2026. Two Johns Hopkins clinicians described the health system's continued use of the system in February 2025. [1] [2] [3]

Evidence links

Entry details

Sector

Healthcare - diabetic retinopathy screening. Autonomous AI screening in pediatric diabetes care.

Purpose

IDx-DR/LumineticsCore is built to autonomously detect more-than-mild diabetic retinopathy from fundus images at the point of care. Increasing screening completion is its intended benefit.
