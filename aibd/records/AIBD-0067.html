What happened

The ACCESS trial (NCT05131451) randomized 164 young people aged 8 to 21 with type 1 or type 2 diabetes at the Johns Hopkins Pediatric Diabetes Center, Baltimore, enrolled between 24 November 2021 and 6 June 2022, to an autonomous AI diabetic eye exam at the point of care using IDx-DR/LumineticsCore (Digital Diagnostics) or to scripted referral and education. Exam completion within six months was 100% (81 of 81) in the AI arm versus 22% in the control arm; 25 AI-arm participants had an abnormal result, of whom 64% completed follow-through with an eye-care provider versus 22% of controls. Follow-up ended 6 December 2022 and the paper appeared in Nature Communications in 2024. No individual participant's diagnosis or treatment is described.

What it was built to do. IDx-DR/LumineticsCore is built to autonomously detect more-than-mild diabetic retinopathy from fundus images at the point of care; increasing screening completion is its intended benefit.

The system's part

The randomized design supports a causal effect of the autonomous AI exam on screening completion and follow-through; the trial did not report whether any participant's vision outcome changed. Senior author M.D. Abramoff is an investor, director and consultant of Digital Diagnostics (disclosed); the first author and most co-authors (nine of eleven) are Johns Hopkins investigators (co-author R. Channa is at the University of Wisconsin), and the author-contribution statement says Abramoff had no access to the underlying data at any time, so the paper is not vendor-authored.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Autonomous AI camera and software located in the diabetes clinic. Immediate result at the visit with referral only for abnormal findings.

What must hold again. Gradable images obtainable in the clinic. A referral route for abnormal results.
