What happened

During 2023–24, as part of the DIII-D National Fusion Facility Program, Next Step Fusion applied reinforcement learning to control plasma parameters on the DIII-D tokamak. In its submission to the ITU AI for Good report of 2025, the company reports that this work also improved controller training. Next Step Fusion, General Atomics and UC San Diego authors published the DIII-D experiments in Nuclear Fusion on 21 January 2026. They report that four controllers trained in the NSFsim simulator ran in the DIII-D Plasma Control System and replaced its standard shape control, which relies on real-time equilibrium reconstruction. They report that the controllers kept shape errors within 1.5 to 2.0 cm and radial errors within 1 cm, with X-point errors of up to about 4 cm. The authors attribute the X-point error to mismatches between their simulator and the experiments. No independent source verifies these research results. Energy benefits remain prospective.

Details

AI system

Model

The control system uses Soft Actor-Critic reinforcement learning, with a critic that receives additional simulation state during training.

Reported configuration

Four Soft Actor-Critic controllers (RL24#1, RL24#2, RL25#1, RL25#2) trained in the NSFsim simulator

Autonomy

The reinforcement-learning controller is reported to have controlled plasma parameters during DIII-D research experiments in 2023–24.

Workflow

The account does not separately establish the conditions responsible for the benefit.

Human oversight

The controllers ran inside the DIII-D Plasma Control System. In some discharges the facility's fast vertical stability loop stayed on alongside the reinforcement-learning controller. [1] [2]

Measurement

Measurement

Experimental discharges on the DIII-D tokamak with the RL controller in the Plasma Control System at 4 kHz; errors computed against target shape parameters. [2]

Setting and sample

Four controllers tested at DIII-D across heating power scans in L-mode and H-mode plasmas, including L-H transitions and pellet injections. [2]

Measures

Control error in plasma shape, radial position and vertical X-point position. [2]

Results

Shape errors within 1.5 to 2.0 cm and radial errors within 1 cm; X-point errors of up to about 4 cm. [2]

Limitations

All authors work for the developer or the facility or receive support from the developer. The X-point mismatch shows limits of the simulator used for training. [2]

Denominators

Experimental discharges at DIII-D during 2023 and 2024 by the developer's account. [2]

People and places

Dependencies

The system depends on a plasma simulator for training and a real-time C/C++ interface to the tokamak’s plasma control system for experimental operation.
