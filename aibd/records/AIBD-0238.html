AIxCC systems find 54 of 63 synthetic vulnerabilities, patch 43

off the scale

found 54 synthetic vulnerabilities, patched 43, and found 18 real bugs for disclosure

At DEF CON 33 (8 Aug 2025) DARPA reported seven teams' systems found 54 synthetic vulnerabilities, patched 43, and found 18 real bugs for disclosure; Team Atlanta won $4 million.

What happened

DARPA's results release states that in the Final Competition teams' cyber reasoning systems attempted to find and patch synthetic vulnerabilities across 54 million lines of real-world open-source code, discovering 54 unique synthetic vulnerabilities in 63 challenges and patching 43, while also discovering 18 real, non-synthetic vulnerabilities (six in C codebases, 12 in Java) now being responsibly disclosed, with 11 patches for real bugs and an average cost of about $152 per task. Team Atlanta (Georgia Tech, Samsung Research, KAIST, POSTECH) won $4 million, Trail of Bits $3 million and Theori $1.5 million, and four of seven systems were released open source. The Record reported the same figures from Las Vegas with program manager Andrew Carney's remarks. curl maintainer Daniel Stenberg, whose project was among the targets, examined the injected curl flaws and judged them straightforward and not deeply hidden.

What it was built to do. Autonomous AI systems that find and patch vulnerabilities in open-source software underlying critical infrastructure.

The system's part

DARPA, the organizer, reports the figures; The Record corroborates from the event. Stenberg's independent look at the curl tasks suggests the injected flaws were easy, which bounds how much the synthetic scores show.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Injected flaws in real open-source projects with scoring for proofs and patches. Two years of funded development and frontier-lab model credits.

What must hold again. Real-world codebases with harder, layered flaws than the injected ones. Maintainer acceptance of machine-generated patches.
