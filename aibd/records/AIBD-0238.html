AIxCC systems find 54 of 63 synthetic vulnerabilities, patch 43

At DEF CON 33 (8 August 2025) DARPA reported seven teams' systems found 54 synthetic vulnerabilities, patched 43, and found 18 real bugs for disclosure; Team Atlanta won $4 million.

ⓘ

What happened

DARPA's results release describes the Final Competition. Teams' cyber reasoning systems tried to find and patch synthetic vulnerabilities across 54 million lines of real-world open-source code. They found 54 unique synthetic vulnerabilities in 63 challenges and patched 43. They also found 18 real, non-synthetic vulnerabilities (six in C codebases, 12 in Java), now being responsibly disclosed. There were 11 patches for real bugs. The average cost was about $152 per task. Team Atlanta (Georgia Tech, Samsung Research, KAIST, POSTECH) won $4 million, Trail of Bits $3 million and Theori $1.5 million. Four of seven systems were released open source. The Record reported the same figures from Las Vegas with program manager Andrew Carney's remarks. curl maintainer Daniel Stenberg, whose project was among the targets, examined the injected curl flaws.

He judged them straightforward and not deeply hidden.

Purpose. Autonomous AI systems that find and patch vulnerabilities in open-source software underlying critical infrastructure.

AI’s contribution

DARPA, the organizer, reports the figures. The Record corroborates from the event. Stenberg's independent look at the curl tasks suggests the injected flaws were easy. That bounds how much the synthetic scores show.
