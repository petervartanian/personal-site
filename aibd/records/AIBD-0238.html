What happened

DARPA's results release describes the Final Competition. Teams' cyber reasoning systems tried to find and patch synthetic vulnerabilities across 54 million lines of real-world open-source code. They found 54 unique synthetic vulnerabilities in 63 challenges and patched 43. They also found 18 real, non-synthetic vulnerabilities (six in C codebases, 12 in Java). DARPA said that these were being responsibly disclosed. There were 11 patches for real bugs. The average cost was about $152 per task. Team Atlanta (Georgia Tech, Samsung Research, KAIST, POSTECH) won $4 million, Trail of Bits $3 million and Theori $1.5 million. Four of seven systems were released open source. The Record reported the same figures from Las Vegas with program manager Andrew Carney's remarks. On 22 October 2025 curl maintainer Daniel Stenberg, whose project was among the targets, examined the injected curl flaws. He judged that they were straightforward and not deeply hidden. In March 2026 Team Atlanta reported that the released systems were hard to run outside their teams. It also reported that a ported Atlantis found 10 new bugs, three of high severity, in eight OSS-Fuzz projects. In September 2026 researchers at the University of Maryland tested the top three AIxCC systems on 213 transplanted vulnerabilities. Across 11 agents, they found that checks based only on the original crash input overstate patching success by 1.83 times on average. Atlantis passed 92.0% of the original crash checks and solved 48.4% of tasks under full validation. The researchers also found that the AIxCC systems solved fewer tasks than general-purpose coding agents on the same model.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

The finalist systems autonomously searched for vulnerabilities and generated patches during the competition; maintainers still controlled adoption outside it.

Workflow

Injected flaws in real open-source projects with scoring for proofs and patches. Two years of funded development and frontier-lab model credits.

Measurement

Measurement

Benchmark run of 11 patching agents on 213 C/C++ vulnerabilities transplanted or mutated into new code, with patches checked by extra fuzzing PoCs, sanitizer regression, program output state and unit tests.

Setting and sample

213 tasks across 32 open-source projects; the AIxCC systems Atlantis (Team Atlanta), Buttercup (Trail of Bits) and RoboDuck (Theori), and eight general-purpose agents.

Measures

Share of tasks solved under full validation, compared with the share of original PoCs that no longer crash.

Results

Atlantis passed 92.0% of PoCs and solved 48.4% of tasks; Buttercup and RoboDuck solved 42.7% and 29.6%; PoC-only validation inflated solve rates by 1.83 times on average.

Limitations

C/C++ tasks only. The authors ran the AIxCC systems with models of their choice (GPT-5.6 Sol, and also Claude Opus 4.8 for Atlantis). Atlantis had a larger budget and is not directly comparable. The benchmark measures patching and does not measure discovery.
