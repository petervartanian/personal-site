Galaxy Fit records a racing pulse, and doctors treat a rhythm disorder

A Samsung Galaxy Fit band recorded a pulse of 188 beats per minute in a woman in South Korea, and with a routine electrocardiogram the record supported her referral for a successful ablation in 2020.

  • 2 a person

    A young worker's band documented a tachycardia of 188 beats per minute. With her symptoms and a routine electrocardiogram showing Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome the next day, the band's record supported her referral for an electrophysiology study. Successful radiofrequency catheter ablation came 11 days after the recorded episode. The benefit to her was material, although the diagnosis itself came from the routine electrocardiogram.

What happened

A 26-year-old woman worked 12-hour days in a multinational company's quality control department in South Korea. Before she bought a Samsung Galaxy Fit (SM-R370) band, she had had palpitations for four years, and an emergency visit on 9 June 2020 ended with symptomatic treatment only. The band measures pulse by photoplethysmography. On 18 June 2020 she experienced severe palpitations for hours, and her band recorded a peak resting pulse rate of 188 beats per minute. The next day, 19 June 2020, a routine health-examination 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) was taken while she was asymptomatic. It showed a shortened PR interval and delta waves, establishing Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome. The authors of the case report are from the Department of Occupational and Environmental Medicine at Kangbuk Samsung Hospital. They recommended an electrophysiology study based on the ECG, her symptoms and the pulse records from the band. Electrophysiology study and radiofrequency catheter ablation of the bypass tract followed on 29 June 2020 at the authors' hospital. After discharge she monitored for recurrence with a smartwatch. The case was reported in Annals of Occupational and Environmental Medicine on 24 March 2021. A 2024 report describes a Galaxy Watch 4 ECG capturing polymorphic ventricular tachycardia that led to a diagnosis of vasospastic angina in another patient. In a prospective study published on 19 April 2026, Italian cardiologists found that 30 of 59 patients with palpitations recorded an arrhythmia on a smartwatch ECG within three months. Of the 59 patients, 15 used Samsung watches, and the authors do not report results by brand.

Details

AI system

Role

The wearable recorded the rapid heart-rate episode for later clinical assessment.

Configuration

The initial event involved a Galaxy Fit SM-R370. A later report involved a Galaxy Watch Series 4.

Inputs and outputs

The Galaxy Fit recorded pulse optically, including a peak resting rate of 188 beats per minute during the woman's symptoms.

Workflow

The wearer uses a Samsung Galaxy band or watch with heart-rate tracking or the ECG app. A symptomatic arrhythmia occurs while the device is worn and the reading or trace is shown to a clinician. A clinician confirms the rhythm and treats it.

Human oversight

Clinicians diagnosed Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome from a 12-lead ECG, considered the symptoms and band readings, and performed electrophysiology testing and catheter ablation.
