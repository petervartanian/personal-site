Galaxy Fit pulse of 188 bpm leads to WPW diagnosis and ablation

recorded a peak resting pulse rate of 188 beats per minute

Galaxy Fit recorded a resting pulse of 188 bpm; a routine ECG next day showed WPW and the band's records supported referral for successful ablation (South Korea, 2020).

What happened

On 18 June 2020 a 26-year-old woman working 12-hour days in a multinational company's quality control department in South Korea experienced severe palpitations for hours; her Samsung Galaxy Fit (SM-R370), which measures pulse by photoplethysmography, recorded a peak resting pulse rate of 188 beats per minute. The next day, 19 June 2020, a routine health-examination 12-lead ECG taken while she was asymptomatic showed a shortened PR interval and delta waves, establishing Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome. The authors, from the Department of Occupational and Environmental Medicine at Kangbuk Samsung Hospital, recommended electrophysiology study on the basis of the ECG, her symptoms and the pulse records from the band; electrophysiology study and radiofrequency catheter ablation of the bypass tract followed on 29 June 2020 at the authors' hospital and, after discharge, she continued to monitor for recurrence with a smartwatch. The case was reported in Annals of Occupational and Environmental Medicine on 24 March 2021. A 2024 report describes a Galaxy Watch 4 ECG capturing polymorphic ventricular tachycardia that led to a vasospastic angina diagnosis.

What it was built to do. The Galaxy Fit's optical sensor is built to track heart rate, and the Galaxy Watch ECG app to record single-lead ECGs for rhythm assessment; the benefit came from those functions.

The system's part

The band's heart-rate algorithm recorded the 188 bpm episode that documented the tachycardia; clinicians diagnosed WPW on a 12-lead ECG and performed the ablation. The echo relied on the Galaxy Watch ECG app's trace, read by cardiologists.

Replication

What produced it
  • The wearer uses a Samsung Galaxy band or watch with heart-rate tracking or the ECG app
  • A symptomatic arrhythmia occurs while the device is worn and the reading or trace is shown to a clinician
  • A clinician confirms the rhythm and treats it
What must hold again
  • The device recording is reviewed promptly
  • A 12-lead ECG or inpatient monitoring is available for confirmation
  • Definitive therapy (ablation, drugs, device) is accessible
