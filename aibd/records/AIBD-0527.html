Researchers build a laboratory test of safer autonomous wheelchairs

SUPSI and IDSIA built a laboratory demonstrator of the REXASI-PRO autonomous-wheelchair safety system; no source reports a result for wheelchair users.

What happened

In a preprint of 13 March 2024, the SUPSI team reported that combining several sensors can improve the accuracy of road-crossing decisions by an autonomous wheelchair in a laboratory test. By June 2025 SUPSI and IDSIA had developed a laboratory demonstrator and a dataset of outdoor scenarios such as road crossing. The REXASI-PRO project closed on 30 September 2025. In its final report on CORDIS, the consortium states that test campaigns ran in Madrid and Genoa and that final tests involved 52 outside participants. The report gives no result for wheelchair users. Safer and more independent mobility for people with reduced mobility remains an intended benefit.

Details

AI system

Role

Wheelchair and drone views are combined with distance sensors to estimate whether crossing is safe. Dynamic Bayesian networks support uncertainty assessment and changes to the sensing configuration.

Autonomy

Autonomous movement and safety functions have been demonstrated in laboratory experiments, including robot road-crossing tests with simulated disturbances.

Workflow

Conditions that could produce the benefit are unspecified.

Benefits

Reach

No source counts people who benefited. The consortium reports that 52 outside participants took part in the final tests, of whom 16 were trained operators and 36 were environmental agents. [1] [2] [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Redundant sensors and run-time danger estimation. Laboratory evaluation of road-crossing scenarios.
