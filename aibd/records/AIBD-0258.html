Apple Crash Detection alerts firefighters to a Mount Aso crash

Apple Crash Detection on a passenger's iPhone called the fire service about 46 minutes before the operator reported a sightseeing helicopter missing near Mount Aso; all three aboard died.

What happened

On 20 January 2026 a sightseeing helicopter operated by Aso Cadre Dominion took off at 10:52 Japan time and lost contact at 11:04. It carried a pilot in his sixties and two Taiwanese passengers (a 41-year-old man and a 36-year-old woman). Police told Jiji Press that the automatic call reached the fire service at 11:04 on 20 January 2026. They said that the operator's agent reported the helicopter missing at about 11:50. The Asahi Shimbun cited the fire service as saying that the call came from near the Nakadake crater. Jiji reported that the helicopter's GPS track also ended near the crater. Storm Media Japan, drawing on fire-service information, wrote that the phone function gave the search its first important lead. That afternoon a police helicopter found an object believed to be the helicopter around the first crater of Mount Aso's Nakadake. Taiwan's SETN reported on 21 January 2026, citing Japanese media, that the local fire department had received a “strong impact” notification from a passenger's phone before contact was lost. The notification apparently came from the iPhone Crash Detection feature. Callbacks to the phone failed. The SETN article does not cite a named Japanese agency. It quotes a firefighter and a nurse on crash detection in general. On 18 February 2026 the fire department said that bodies believed to be those of the three occupants had been found inside the crater. Apple's feature, on iPhone 14 and later, uses motion sensors and an on-device algorithm to call emergency services after a 10-second countdown. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor lists the case as a protective response. The link to transport safety is indirect. The device alerted responders to an aviation crash that it did not prevent.
