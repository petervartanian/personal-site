Brief from Peter

H. Inada of the University of Tokyo and M. Ichikawa of the University of Tsukuba used national police-reported data from Japan for 2016 to 2019 on pedestrians and cyclists struck by cars of six best-selling models. Those models offered automatic emergency braking (AEB) as an option, and the authors compared injury severity in crashes with and without AEB on the striking car. Among 4,131 pedestrians, AEB was associated with 20% lower odds of a fatal or serious injury.1 Among 6,659 cyclists, the authors found no significant association.2 The study is observational and covers only crashes that happened, so it measures injury severity and cannot count crashes that AEB avoided.

  1. 1 The adjusted odds ratio was 0.80 (95% confidence interval 0.64 to 0.996), and the upper bound of that interval is close to no effect. H. Inada and M. Ichikawa, Accident Analysis & Prevention, May 10, 2025. pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
  2. 2 The adjusted odds ratio for cyclists was 0.91, and the authors call for studies of newer systems that detect cyclists. H. Inada and M. Ichikawa, Accident Analysis & Prevention, May 10, 2025. pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Details

AI system

Workflow

Six popular car models in Japan offered AEB as an option, which allowed a comparison within the same models. The benefit showed up as milder injuries in crashes that still happened.

Measurement

Measurement

Multivariable logistic regression of injury severity (fatal or serious against minor or none). [1]

Setting and sample

4,131 pedestrians and 6,659 cyclists in police-reported crashes with six best-selling car models, Japan, 2016 to 2019. [1]

Measures

Odds of fatal or serious injury. [1]

Results

Pedestrians: adjusted odds ratio 0.80 (0.64 to 0.996); cyclists: 0.91 (0.74 to 1.14), a nonsignificant result. [1]

Additional value from AI

20% lower odds of fatal or serious pedestrian injury. [1]

Limitations

Cross-sectional; counts only crashes that occurred; the upper confidence bound is near 1. [1]

Denominators

AEB fitted to the striking car. [1]

Materials for replication

National police crash data; code not public. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The system must detect the pedestrian early enough to reduce impact speed. Systems that do not detect cyclists are unlikely to help cyclists.
