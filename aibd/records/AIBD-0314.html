Delhi Police FRS matches 2,930 children; no case cracked

MWCD affidavit: FRS run on 45,000 children in homes matched 2,930 identities on 6–10 April 2018; on 22 January 2019 the Delhi High Court recorded that the software had not cracked any case.

ⓘ

What happened

PTI reported on 22 April 2018 (Business Standard) on an affidavit from the Ministry of Women and Child Development to the Delhi High Court. It said the Delhi Police, “on a trial basis, used the FRS on 45,000 children living in different children's homes”. Also, 2,930 children “could be recognized between April 6 and April 10”. The ministry's secretary Rakesh Srivastava said that the police shared the outcome on 10 April. In Sadhan Haldar v NCT of Delhi, the court had pressed the police to test-run facial recognition. The ministry had then supplied data on about seven lakh missing children from the Track Child portal. The same report says identities “have been established and efforts are on to help them reunite with their families”. No reunification is documented in any cited source.

The Wire (republished by the Pulitzer Center, 2–3 July 2025) cites the Bureau of Police Research and Development's Compendium of Equipment. It says the police bought two facial recognition systems on 28 March 2018, manufactured by Innefu Labs and supplied by Pelorus Technologies. It quotes an Innefu blog claiming its AI Vision product “helped in identifying 3000 missing children” in its first four days. On 23 August 2018 Delhi Police counsel Rahul Mehra told the High Court: “The software is not good. There is only two percent match” (NDTV). That was in a habeas corpus matter about women missing from a placement agency. On 22 January 2019 the High Court (Hima Kohli and Manoj Kumar Ohri JJ) recorded in Sadhan Haldar what it was told. The software “has not helped in cracking any case of missing children so far”, against 5,160 children untraced out of 19,916 reported missing in 2016–2018.

A Centre for Internet and Society (CIS India) report gives 10,617 matches, of which 3,202 identities were verified, and no reunification count; the report itself is not among the sources.

Purpose. Procured under the Delhi High Court's direction in Sadhan Haldar to match photographs of missing and found children against the Track Child database. The April 2018 trial run was that intended use.

AI’s contribution

The software produced the 2,930 matches reported in the ministry's affidavit. The claim rests on the police's report of raw output, relayed by PTI, and on the vendor's blog. No cited source verifies a single match to a reunification. The police counsel described the match rate as two percent in August 2018. The Delhi High Court recorded in January 2019 that the software had not helped crack any case. The CIS India figures (10,617 matches, 3,202 identities verified) come from a report that is not among the sources.

Further evidence

Setting. A court-directed 1:many facial recognition trial on photographs of 45,000 children in children's homes, against Track Child portal data on about seven lakh missing children. Police reporting of raw match counts to the ministry, which relayed them to the court by affidavit.

Conditions. Matches are verified against identity records and followed by reunification, which no cited source shows. The software's match rate is adequate. The police counsel's statement of a two percent match in August 2018 concerned the same software.
