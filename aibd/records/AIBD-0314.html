What happened

PTI reported on 22 April 2018 (Business Standard) that the Ministry of Women and Child Development told the Delhi High Court in an affidavit that the Delhi Police, “on a trial basis, used the FRS on 45,000 children living in different children's homes”, and that 2,930 children “could be recognized between April 6 and April 10”; the ministry's secretary Rakesh Srivastava said the police shared the outcome on 10 April, which is the date used here. The ministry had supplied data on about seven lakh missing children from the Track Child portal after the court, in Sadhan Haldar v NCT of Delhi, pressed the police to test-run facial recognition. The same report says identities “have been established and efforts are on to help them reunite with their families”; no reunification is documented in any source on file.

The Wire (republished by the Pulitzer Center, 2–3 July 2025), citing the Bureau of Police Research and Development's Compendium of Equipment, says the police bought two facial recognition systems on 28 March 2018, manufactured by Innefu Labs and supplied by Pelorus Technologies, and quotes an Innefu blog claiming its AI Vision product “helped in identifying 3000 missing children” in its first four days. On 23 August 2018 Delhi Police counsel Rahul Mehra told the High Court, in a habeas corpus matter about women missing from a placement agency, “The software is not good. There is only two percent match” (NDTV). On 22 January 2019 the High Court (Hima Kohli and Manoj Kumar Ohri JJ) recorded in Sadhan Haldar that it was told the software “has not helped in cracking any case of missing children so far”, against 5,160 children untraced out of 19,916 reported missing in 2016–2018.

The earlier reviewer's reason cites a Centre for Internet and Society (CIS India) report giving 10,617 matches of which 3,202 identities were verified and no reunification count; that PDF was not located in this pass. Existing record AIBD-0126 concerns a different Delhi Police facial recognition case.

What it was built to do. Procured under the Delhi High Court's direction in Sadhan Haldar to match photographs of missing and found children against the Track Child database; the April 2018 trial run was that intended use.

The system's part

The software produced the 2,930 matches reported in the ministry's affidavit; the claim rests on the police's report of raw output, relayed by PTI, and on the vendor's blog. No source on file verifies a single match to a reunification, the police counsel described the match rate as two percent in August 2018, and the Delhi High Court recorded in January 2019 that the software had not helped crack any case. The earlier reviewer's CIS India figures (10,617 matches, 3,202 identities verified) were not re-read.

Replication

What produced it. A court-directed trial run of a 1:many facial recognition system on photographs of 45,000 children in children's homes against Track Child portal data on about seven lakh missing children. Police reporting of raw match counts to the ministry, which relayed them to the court by affidavit.

What must hold again. Matches are verified against identity records and followed by reunification, which no source on file shows. The software's match rate is adequate; the police counsel's statement of a two percent match in August 2018 concerned the same software.
