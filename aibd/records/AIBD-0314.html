Face recognition matches 2,930 children; court hears that it solved none

Delhi Police's facial recognition, which The Wire identifies as Innefu Labs' AI Vision, produced 2,930 matches among children in care, and the High Court later recorded that it cracked no case.

  • Not rated a person

    No cited source documents a child reunited with a family as a result of the 2,930 matches. The High Court recorded on 22 January 2019 that the software had not cracked any missing-child case.

  • Not rated an organization

    The police reported 2,930 identities “established” to the ministry. The same police told the court four months later that the software produced only a two percent match.

  • Not rated society

    The court's own figures (5,160 children untraced of 19,916 reported in 2016–2018) are the context. No reduction attributable to the software is documented.

What happened

In Sadhan Haldar v NCT of Delhi, the Delhi High Court had pressed the police to test-run facial recognition. The Ministry of Women and Child Development then supplied data on about seven lakh missing children from the Track Child portal. The Wire cites the Bureau of Police Research and Development's Compendium of Equipment, which records that the police bought two facial recognition systems on 28 March 2018. Innefu Labs manufactured them, and Pelorus Technologies supplied them. On 22 April 2018 the Press Trust of India (PTI) reported in Business Standard on an affidavit from the ministry to the Delhi High Court. It said that the Delhi Police, “on a trial basis, used the FRS on 45,000 children living in different children's homes”. It added that 2,930 children “could be recognised between April 6 and April 10”. The ministry's secretary Rakesh Srivastava said that the police shared the outcome on 10 April. The same report says that identities “have been established and efforts are on to help them reunite with their families”. On 23 August 2018 Delhi Police counsel Rahul Mehra told the High Court: “The software is not good. There is only two per cent match” (NDTV). That was in a habeas corpus matter about women missing from a placement agency. On 22 January 2019 the High Court (Hima Kohli and Manoj Kumar Ohri JJ) recorded in Sadhan Haldar what it was told. The software “has not helped in cracking any case of missing children so far”, against 5,160 children untraced out of 19,916 reported missing in 2016–2018. On 23 August 2019 the ministry's counsel told the Delhi High Court that the software sometimes matched photographs of missing boys with girls. The court had asked why fewer than 1 percent of children were matched. It ordered the ministry to chart each identified child and the status of the child's return home (PTI, 24 August 2019). A Centre for Internet and Society (CIS India) report gives 10,617 matches, of which 3,202 identities were verified, and no reunification count. On 2 and 3 July 2025 the Pulitzer Center republished The Wire's investigations. They quote an Innefu blog that claims that its AI Vision product “helped in identifying 3000 missing children” in its first four days. No reunification is documented in any cited source.

Details

AI system

Role

The software compared facial images to propose possible matches.

Configuration

The Wire identified the facial-recognition software as Innefu Labs’ AI Vision.

Inputs and outputs

Police compared photographs of children in care with missing-child records from Track Child and received candidate face matches.

Workflow

A court-directed 1:many facial recognition trial on photographs of 45,000 children in children's homes, against Track Child portal data on about seven lakh missing children. Police reporting of raw match counts to the ministry, which relayed them to the court by affidavit.
