Face matches reported in a search for missing children

The ministry reported 2,930 facial matches among children in care. The Delhi High Court later recorded that the software had not helped solve any missing-child case.

ⓘ

AI system

System

Delhi Police Facial Recognition System (reported by The Wire as Innefu Labs' AI Vision)

Model

Not assessed

Release

Exact release not established.

Application developer

Innefu Labs Pvt Ltd (manufacturer, per The Wire citing the BPR&D Compendium of Equipment); supplied by Pelorus Technologies Pvt Ltd

Role

The software compared facial images to propose possible matches.

Configuration

The Wire identified the facial-recognition software as Innefu Labs’ AI Vision.

Inputs and outputs

Not assessed

Autonomy

Not assessed

Workflow

A court-directed 1:many facial recognition trial on photographs of 45,000 children in children's homes, against Track Child portal data on about seven lakh missing children. Police reporting of raw match counts to the ministry, which relayed them to the court by affidavit.

Human oversight

Not assessed

Relation to AI

The software produced the 2,930 matches reported in the ministry's affidavit. The claim rests on the police's report of raw output, relayed by PTI, and on the vendor's blog. No cited source verifies a single match to a reunification. The police counsel described the match rate as two percent in August 2018. The Delhi High Court recorded in January 2019 that the software had not helped crack any case. The CIS India figures (10,617 matches, 3,202 identities verified) come from a report that is not among the sources.

Conditions for replication

Matches are verified against identity records and followed by reunification, which no cited source shows. The software's match rate is adequate. The police counsel's statement of a two percent match in August 2018 concerned the same software.

Replication attempts

Later findings

22 January 2019. On 22 January 2019 the Delhi High Court recorded in Sadhan Haldar v. NCT of Delhi that the facial recognition software had not helped crack any missing-child case, after police counsel had reported a two percent match rate in August 2018.

Reasons

People: No cited source documents a child reunited with a family as a result of the 2,930 matches. The High Court recorded on 22 January 2019 that the software had not cracked any missing-child case. Organizations: The police reported 2,930 identities “established” to the ministry. The same police told the court four months later that the software produced only a two percent match. Society: The court's own figures (5,160 children untraced of 19,916 reported in 2016–2018) are the context. No reduction attributable to the software is documented.
