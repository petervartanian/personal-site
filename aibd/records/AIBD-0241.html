Waymo's driverless cars draw fewer insurance claims than human drivers

Over 3,868,506 rider-only miles, the Waymo Driver had no bodily-injury claims and 76% fewer property-damage claims than a Swiss Re human baseline; a later independent study found 68% fewer crashes.

Brief from Peter

As driverless ride-hailing expands, insurance claims are one way to compare it with human driving. In a peer-reviewed paper of 14 July 2024, Swiss Re and Waymo authors reported zero bodily-injury claims over 3,868,506 driverless miles, against a human baseline of 1.11 per million miles. Waymo's property-damage claims were 0.78 per million miles against 3.26. Waymo authors co-wrote that paper and its 25-million-mile follow-up. The comparison is observational, and the authors note that the baseline cannot exclude freeway miles.1 On 23 July 2026, the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) published an independent study with no Waymo authors. IIHS found that Waymo's driverless vehicles were involved in 68% fewer police-reportable crashes per mile than human drivers in the same areas. It also called for better federal data on crashes and mileage to monitor a larger rollout.2
