Waymo has zero bodily-injury, 76% fewer property-damage claims

off the scale

0.78 property-damage claims per million miles against 3.26, a 76% reduction

Heliyon (14 Jul 2024, Swiss Re and Waymo authors): Waymo liability claims benchmarked against 600,000+ Swiss Re claims; a Dec 2024 25.3M-mile update reports 88%/92% reductions.

What happened

The peer-reviewed Heliyon paper (vol. 10, issue 14, e34379; received 25 Apr 2024, accepted 9 Jul 2024, published 14 Jul 2024) by Luigi Di Lillo, Xilin Zhou and Margherita Atzei of Swiss Reinsurance Company and Tilia Gode, Ruoshu Chen and Trent Victor of Waymo LLC built a human-driver baseline from over 600,000 Swiss Re property-damage and bodily-injury liability claims and 125 billion miles of exposure (claims 2016-2021), calibrated to the zip codes of Waymo One's service areas in San Francisco and Phoenix, and compared it with Waymo claims from 1 Jan 2018 to 1 Aug 2023. Over 3,868,506 rider-only miles (no human behind the wheel) Waymo had zero bodily-injury claims against a baseline of 1.11 per million miles and 0.78 property-damage claims per million miles against 3.26, a 76% reduction; both differences had non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals. A follow-up paper posted on Waymo's site on 19 Dec 2024 (Di Lillo, Gode, Zhou, Scanlon, Chen, Victor; not peer-reviewed) extended the data to 25.3 million fully autonomous miles from 1 Jan 2018 to 31 Jul 2024 across San Francisco, Phoenix, Los Angeles and Austin, reporting an 88% reduction in property-damage and a 92% reduction in bodily-injury claims against the overall driving population (two bodily-injury claims observed against 26 expected) and 86%/90% against 2018-2021 model-year vehicles. The study authors note the baseline cannot exclude freeway miles and uses the insured's registered zip code rather than the collision location. The date given is the Heliyon publication date; the data cover the periods stated above.

What it was built to do. The Waymo Driver is built to provide driverless ride-hailing; the studies were designed to measure its liability-claim frequency against human drivers.

The system's part

The ADS drove all rider-only miles; claims were adjudicated by Waymo's claims administrator and the anonymized data were reviewed by Swiss Re, which built the human baseline from its own portfolio. The comparison is observational (different drivers, roads and reporting pathways), the rider-only sample in the peer-reviewed paper is 3.9 million miles, and Waymo authors co-wrote both papers.

Replication

What produced it. Geofenced, mostly non-freeway urban service areas in Phoenix and San Francisco. Liability claims adjudicated under state negligence law and reported for any incident regardless of automation level. A human baseline calibrated by zip code of the insured and by mileage exposure. Rider-only operation with no human in the driver's seat.

What must hold again. Access to an insurer's claims portfolio for the same zip codes and years. Comparable claim-reporting thresholds for ADS and human drivers. Sufficient autonomous mileage for non-overlapping confidence intervals. Operational design domain similar to the baseline's road mix.
