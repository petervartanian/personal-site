ADIAT spots a red helmet and locates Nicola Ivaldo on Monviso

AI analysis of 2,600 drone frames flagged a red helmet in the Perotti couloir at 3,150 m; Ivaldo's body was recovered by 31 July 2025.

AI system

System

AI drone-image analysis software used by CNSAS Piemonte (identified as ADIAT by its developer, TEXSAR; CAI and CNSAS sources do not name it)

Application developer

TEXSAR (Texas Search and Rescue) volunteers, per the developer's own account

Workflow

Close-range high-resolution drone photography of steep faces (about 2,600 frames over 183 hectares). A target object (red helmet) with strong color contrast against rock and ice. Search area narrowed by mobile-phone cell data to the Monviso and Visolotto faces.

Relation to AI

The AI flagged a few red pixels, the helmet, a detail CAI describes as invisible to the naked eye. Ten months of helicopters, specialist teams and advanced technology (Quadricottero) had not resolved the search. Drone pilot Saverio Isola is quoted that it was the AI software that identified the differently colored pixels. CAI/CNSAS sources do not name the software. The ADIAT attribution comes from the developer TEXSAR's own page. So the tool identity rests on a vendor-authored source.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Weather and terrain allow drones to fly close to the walls. The subject or equipment remains visible and not buried by snow. Rescuers verify AI flags, which CNSAS notes can also fire on plastic or unusual rock color.

Later findings

18 January 2026. By January 2026 the CNSAS Piemonte drone lead was working with Politecnico di Torino's geomatics team on a successor tool that adds precise georeferenced coordinates to the color-anomaly detection.

Depth

Reasons

People: Ivaldo's family obtained recovery of his body and certainty about his fate after ten months of searching. A material benefit, not life-saving, since he had died in September 2024. Organizations: CNSAS Piemonte ended a search that had involved ground teams, Guardia di Finanza and fire-service helicopters for ten months. Manual analysis of the drone photos would have taken weeks (CAI) to months (CNSAS, per bluewin).

Field

Mountain search and rescue. Recovery of a missing person in high-alpine terrain.

Purpose

ADIAT was built to scan drone photographs and flag color and shape anomalies for search-and-rescue teams. Locating the missing man's helmet was that intended function.
