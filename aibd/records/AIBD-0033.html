ADIAT spots a red helmet and locates Nicola Ivaldo on Monviso

depth 2 for a person, depth 2 for an organization

located at about 3,150 m and recovered by 31 July 2025

AI analysis of 2,600 drone frames flagged a red helmet in the Perotti couloir at 3,150 m; Ivaldo's body was recovered by 31 July 2025.

What happened

Nicola Ivaldo, a surgeon aged 64 according to the Club Alpino Italiano's Lo Scarpone (another report says 66), disappeared on 14 September 2024 per CAI (Quadricottero dates his departure, when he parked his car at the Pontechianale dam, to 15 September) during a solo outing on Monviso in Piedmont, Italy; searches over ten months found nothing. On 29 July 2025 the Soccorso Alpino e Speleologico Piemontese (CNSAS) flew two drones close to the north face, collecting about 2,600 high-resolution frames over 183 hectares in five hours. Analyzed pixel by pixel with AI image-analysis software, identified by its developer TEXSAR as the open-source Automated Drone Image Analysis Tool (ADIAT), the frames yielded a tiny red anomaly in the Perotti couloir: his helmet. His body was located at about 3,150 m and recovered by 31 July 2025; that date comes from TEXSAR's page (which cites Wired) and is consistent with bluewin's report of under 72 hours from the 29 July flight to the recovery, while CAI gives only July 2025 and Quadricottero no date.

What it was built to do. ADIAT was built to scan drone photographs and flag color and shape anomalies for search-and-rescue teams; locating the missing man's helmet was that intended function.

Who benefited

  • 2
    personal

    Ivaldo's family obtained recovery of his body and certainty about his fate after ten months of searching; a material benefit, not life-saving, since he had died in September 2024.

  • 2
    organizational

    CNSAS Piemonte ended a search that had involved ground teams, Guardia di Finanza and fire-service helicopters for ten months; manual analysis of the drone photos would have taken weeks (CAI) to months (CNSAS, per bluewin).

The system's part

The AI flagged a few red pixels, the helmet, a detail CAI describes as invisible to the naked eye, in a search that ten months of helicopters, specialist teams and advanced technology (Quadricottero) had not resolved; drone pilot Saverio Isola is quoted that it was the AI software that identified the differently colored pixels. CAI/CNSAS sources do not name the software; the ADIAT attribution comes from the developer TEXSAR's own page, so the tool identity rests on a vendor-authored source.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Close-range high-resolution drone photography of steep faces (about 2,600 frames over 183 hectares). A target object (red helmet) with strong color contrast against rock and ice. Search area narrowed by mobile-phone cell data to the Monviso and Visolotto faces.

What must hold again. Weather and terrain allow drones to fly close to the walls. The subject or equipment remains visible and not buried by snow. Rescuers verify AI flags, which CNSAS notes can also fire on plastic or unusual rock color.
