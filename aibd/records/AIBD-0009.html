What happened

On 16 October 2024 at about 8:37 pm, train 15959 Kamrup Express, running from Guwahati toward Lumding, approached a herd of about 60 wild elephants crossing the track between Habaipur and Lamsakhang stations in Assam. Northeast Frontier Railway said loco pilot J.D. Das and assistant loco pilot Umesh Kumar noticed the herd and applied the emergency brake, and that the drivers were also alerted by the AI-based Intrusion Detection System operating on that section, which uses existing optical fibers as sensors to identify animal movements and alert control offices, station masters, gatemen and loco pilots. The train stopped and no collision occurred. NFR reported that it saved 414 elephants in 2023 and 383 up to 16 October 2024.

What it was built to do. Built to detect elephants near the track and alert loco pilots, station masters and control rooms; this benefit is that purpose.

The system's part

The operator's release says the drivers noticed the herd and applied the emergency brake and were “also alerted” by the IDS; one local report says the braking followed the IDS warning. The AI alert is documented, but its share of the causal credit is not separable from the crew's own sighting, hence protective response with prevention unproven.

Replication

What produced it. Fibre-optic acoustic sensing with AI classification installed along an elephant corridor. Alert routed to loco pilots and station masters in time to brake.

What must hold again. Train speed and sighting distance allow a stop after the alert. Crew act on the alert; the operator attributes this stop partly to the crew's own sighting.
