OpenAI flag leads Brazilian police to hold a father a day early

held him a day before his stated date

OpenAI flagged a user's plan to kill his 8-year-old son; relayed via the FBI, Brazilian police held him a day before his stated date.

What happened

In 2026 a 36-year-old farmer in São Gabriel da Palha, Espírito Santo, Brazil, told ChatGPT he had offered R$50,000 to a hitman to kill his 8-year-old son and set 20 June as the date. OpenAI's safety systems flagged the account and the company referred it to the FBI, which passed it to the Ministry of Justice's CyberLab; the Polícia Civil received it on 16 June and, under warrants, arrested him on 19 June, seizing his phone, four bottles of an unidentified substance and a knotted rope. He admitted the searches but denied intent, and was freed on habeas corpus on 13 August 2026 after 54 days, under an ankle monitor and a no-contact order; the inquiry continues.

What it was built to do. ChatGPT is a general-purpose conversational assistant. OpenAI's moderation layer is built to route conversations that indicate plans to harm others to human reviewers, who may refer an imminent threat to law enforcement (policy published 26 August 2025). The referral that triggered the arrest was that layer doing what it was built to do, not an emergent use of the assistant.

Who benefited

  • -3
    personal

    The 8-year-old, and his mother who was placed under a no-contact order, were shielded from a plan the father had dated to 20 June; police say the platform's information was corroborated when they arrived, but he denies intent and no charge has been filed, so a life-saving (-4) rating is not yet supportable.

  • -2
    organizational

    The Polícia Civil do Espírito Santo obtained, through the FBI and the Ministry of Justice CyberLab, a lead it says it had no other way to get; its cybercrime chief called the alert another path for police work and the Ministry rated it the gravest of three such alerts in Brazil.

The system's part

OpenAI's automated classifiers and human reviewers flagged the user's messages and the company chose to refer the account to the FBI; the system did not predict a crime or act on its own, and the warrants, the arrest and the evidence-gathering were police and court actions. OpenAI sent only the user's prompts, not ChatGPT's replies, so what the assistant said to the user, and whether it helped or hindered him, is unknown.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • The user typed explicit, dated threats against an identifiable child into ChatGPT over about two months, naming means and a hired killer
  • OpenAI's moderation pipeline (automated classifiers plus human review) flagged the account and the company elected to refer it to the FBI under its policy on imminent threats to others
  • A standing FBI-to-Brazil channel through the Ministry of Justice CyberLab relayed the file to the state police within days
  • The Polícia Civil do Espírito Santo prioritized the case, identified the user, confirmed the child existed and obtained warrants before the stated date
What must hold again
  • The user discloses the plan to the assistant rather than keeping it offline
  • The vendor's reviewers judge the threat credible and imminent and choose referral rather than only an account ban
  • A cross-border law-enforcement channel exists and acts within days
  • Local police can identify the user from the vendor's data and secure judicial warrants before the stated date
