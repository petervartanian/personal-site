What happened

On 20 March 2024 Thomas Cazenave, minister for public accounts, presented the first bilan of the government's anti-fraud plan at Bercy. He said the DGFiP's algorithmic tool, Foncier innovant, introduced in October 2021, had detected 140,000 undeclared swimming pools. The tool detects pools and buildings on IGN aerial photographs and compares them with declarations. An agent then checks each detection. That would let local authorities collect about €40 million in property tax. Owners received DGFiP letters asking them to regularize (RMC, Ouest-France, L'Indépendant, 21 March 2024). CNews (11 June 2024) cites the DGFiP annual activity report as reported by Ouest-France. It gives 140,000 pools identified in 2023 and €40 million that should have been paid in 2023. That compares with 20,000 pools in the 2022 experiment in nine départements (20 Minutes).

L'Indépendant instead states 120,000 for 2022, so the press accounts of the yearly split disagree. At département level, ici (France Bleu) reported on 24 March 2024 on the Dordogne public-finance office. It had found about 1,500 undeclared pools in 2023 among some 30,000 in the département. It would send property-tax catch-up notices by the end of March. No official DGFiP or ministry document is among the sources, as the ministry's website was not available. No commune-level result was found.

What it was built to do. Detect undeclared pools and buildings from aerial imagery so that they are added to the cadastral base and taxed. This was the tool's intended purpose.

The system's part

The AI detects pool outlines on aerial photographs and compares them with declarations. An agent verifies each detection before the construction is added to the cadastral value (Ouest-France). The 140,000 pools and €40 million are the minister's own attribution to the tool, relayed by several news outlets. CNews cites the DGFiP annual report via Ouest-France. No official document is among the sources. A CGT Finances publiques official told Ouest-France that field colleagues put the algorithm's error threshold at 55–60%. So the detections required substantial human checking. No commune-level outcome is on record. The Dordogne figure is the most local one read.

Replication

What produced it. IGN aerial photographs covering the whole territory. A detection model built with Capgemini and Google, generalized nationally after the 2022 nine-département experiment. Comparison of detected outlines with cadastral declarations. Agent verification of each detection and letters inviting owners to regularize.

What must hold again. Recent, high-resolution aerial coverage. A false-detection rate low enough for agents to verify (a union official cites 55–60% errors). Legal basis to add undeclared constructions to the cadastral value. Local offices with capacity to send and handle catch-up notices.
