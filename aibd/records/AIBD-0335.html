Foncier innovant finds 140,000 undeclared pools

Minister Cazenave (20 March 2024): DGFiP's AI tool detected 140,000 undeclared pools, c. €40m extra property tax for local authorities; Dordogne found 1,500 in 2023. No commune-level figure found.

AI system

System

Foncier innovant (DGFiP deep-learning detection of pools and buildings on IGN aerial photographs, built with Capgemini and Google)

Application developer

Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP) with Capgemini and Google

Workflow

IGN aerial photographs covering the whole territory. A detection model built with Capgemini and Google, generalized nationally after the 2022 nine-département experiment. Comparison of detected outlines with cadastral declarations. Agent verification of each detection and letters inviting owners to regularize.

Relation to AI

The AI detects pool outlines on aerial photographs and compares them with declarations. An agent verifies each detection before the construction is added to the cadastral value (Ouest-France). The 140,000 pools and €40 million are the minister's own attribution to the tool, relayed by several news outlets. CNews cites the DGFiP annual report via Ouest-France. No official document is among the sources. A CGT Finances publiques official told Ouest-France that field colleagues put the algorithm's error threshold at 55–60%. So the detections required substantial human checking. No commune-level outcome is on record. The Dordogne figure is the most local one read.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Recent, high-resolution aerial coverage. A false-detection rate low enough for agents to verify (a union official cites 55–60% errors). Legal basis to add undeclared constructions to the cadastral value. Local offices with capacity to send and handle catch-up notices.

Replication attempts

3 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2. Outcome unresolved: 1.

Later findings

12 June 2024. In June 2024 the DGFiP's 2023 activity report, as relayed by 20 Minutes and CNews, restated the 140,000 pools and the 40 million euros of property tax, against 20,000 pools in 2022, and the plan to extend detection to undeclared buildings.
