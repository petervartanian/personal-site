Foncier innovant finds 140,000 undeclared pools in French aerial photos

Foncier innovant, a tool of France's tax administration that detects pools in aerial photographs, found 140,000 taxable pools in 2023, which the administration values at €40 million in local tax.

What happened

In October 2021 the Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP), France's tax administration, introduced Foncier innovant, an algorithmic tool. The tool detects pools and buildings on IGN aerial photographs and compares them with declarations. An agent then checks each detection. In 2022 an experiment in nine départements found 20,000 pools (20 Minutes, 12 June 2024). On 2 June 2023 the DGFiP announced that Foncier innovant would cover all of metropolitan France in 2023. On 20 March 2024 Thomas Cazenave, minister for public accounts, presented the first assessment of the government's anti-fraud plan at Bercy. He said that the tool had detected 140,000 undeclared swimming pools. He said that this would let local authorities collect about €40 million in property tax. Owners received DGFiP letters asking them to regularize their declarations (RMC, Ouest-France, L'Indépendant, 21 March 2024). In Ouest-France on 21 March 2024, Olivier Vilois of the CGT Finances publiques union said that, according to colleagues in the field, the algorithms' error rate stood at 55 to 60% at the time. L'Indépendant gave a figure of 120,000 for 2022, so the press accounts of the yearly split disagree. On 24 March 2024 ici (France Bleu) reported on the Dordogne public-finance office. It had found about 1,500 undeclared pools in 2023 among some 30,000 in the département. The office would send notices to catch up on property tax by the end of March. CNews (11 June 2024) cites the DGFiP annual activity report as reported by Ouest-France. It gives 140,000 pools identified in 2023 and €40 million that should have been paid in 2023. In its section on pool controls, the DGFiP's 2023 annual report gives 140,000 taxable pools in 2023, compared with 20,000 in 2022, and €40 million in additional local property-tax revenue. The report's separate cadastral overview gives more than 120,000 pools, and the report leaves the difference unresolved. The sources give no result for individual communes. In October 2024 the Cour des comptes reported that Foncier innovant raised the number of taxed pools in the nine pilot départements by 5.7%, or 20,356 pools. The Cour reported that the tool brought in €4.4 million in recurring revenue and €5.7 million in back-tax in 2022. The Cour added that the €40 million for 2023 is a DGFiP estimate, and it found that the tool produces many false positives that agents must clear. In April 2026 the DGFiP extended the campaign to verandas and garages with a €100 million goal (France 2).

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Aerial photographs were checked for pools and buildings, then compared with property declarations to produce candidates for review.

Workflow

IGN aerial photographs covering the whole territory. A detection model built with Capgemini and Google, generalized nationally after the 2022 nine-département experiment. Comparison of detected outlines with cadastral declarations. Agent verification of each detection and letters inviting owners to regularize.

Human oversight

A tax officer checked each detection before cadastral values were changed and owners were asked to regularize their declarations.

AI contribution

Foncier innovant extracted pool outlines from IGN aerial images and compared them with tax declarations, and tax officers verified each detection before contacting owners. The DGFiP's 2023 annual report states that 140,000 taxable pools were detected in 2023, compared with 20,000 in 2022. The Cour des comptes reported the 2022 pilot results from DGFiP data, called the €40 million for 2023 a DGFiP estimate and found that the tool produces many false positives that agents must clear. Benefits

Beneficiaries

Local authorities were the intended revenue beneficiaries, while affected owners faced property-tax catch-up notices.

Reach

The beneficiaries are the local authorities that receive the property tax, and the sources do not count them. The pilot covered nine départements in 2022, and the DGFiP then extended the tool nationally. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]

Duration

The tool has run since October 2021, and the Cour des comptes reports €4.4 million in recurring revenue from the 2022 pilot. The DGFiP reports 140,000 taxable pools for 2023. In April 2026 the DGFiP extended the campaign to verandas and garages with a goal of €100 million, according to France 2. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]

Measurement

Measurement

The Cour des comptes reports figures that the DGFiP sent on pools taxed and revenue collected in the pilot départements. [8]

Setting and sample

Nine pilot départements, 2022; national rollout in 2023 covered only by the DGFiP estimate. [8]

Measures

Number of pools taxed under property tax; recurring revenue and back-tax collected. [8]

Results

+20,356 taxed pools (+5.7%); €4.4 million recurring revenue and €5.7 million in back-tax in 2022. [8]

Limitations

The Cour reports that the tool produces many false positives and has a larger error margin than detection by cadastral surveyors. It notes that the DITP received no figures on staff savings for the tool and that the DGFiP never explained its first target of €130 million a year. [8]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Recent, high-resolution aerial coverage. A false-detection rate low enough for agents to verify (a union official cites 55–60% errors). Legal basis to add undeclared constructions to the cadastral value. Local offices with capacity to send and handle catch-up notices.
