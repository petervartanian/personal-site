Cazenave says Foncier innovant finds 140,000 undeclared pools

detected 140,000 undeclared pools, c. €40m extra property tax

Minister Cazenave (20 Mar 2024): DGFiP's AI tool detected 140,000 undeclared pools, c. €40m extra property tax for local authorities; Dordogne found 1,500 in 2023. No commune-level figure found.

What happened

On 20 March 2024 Thomas Cazenave, minister for public accounts, presented the first bilan of the government's anti-fraud plan at Bercy and said that the algorithmic tool introduced by the DGFiP in October 2021 (Foncier innovant, which detects pools and buildings on IGN aerial photographs and compares them with declarations, each detection then being checked by an agent) had detected 140,000 undeclared swimming pools, which would let local authorities collect about €40 million in property tax; owners received DGFiP letters asking them to regularise (RMC, Ouest-France, L'Indépendant, 21 Mar 2024). CNews (11 Jun 2024), citing the DGFiP annual activity report as reported by Ouest-France, gives 140,000 pools identified in 2023 and €40 million that should have been paid in 2023, against 20,000 pools in the 2022 experiment in nine départements (20 Minutes); L'Indépendant instead states 120,000 for 2022, so the press accounts of the yearly split disagree. At département level, ici (France Bleu) reported on 24 Mar 2024 that the Dordogne public-finance office had found about 1,500 undeclared pools in 2023 among some 30,000 in the département and would send property-tax catch-up notices by the end of March. No official DGFiP or ministry document was fetched (economie.gouv.fr returned 403) and no commune-level result was found; the date used is the minister's statement.

What it was built to do. Detect undeclared pools and buildings from aerial imagery so that they are added to the cadastral base and taxed; this was the tool's intended purpose.

The system's part

The AI detects pool outlines on aerial photographs and compares them with declarations; an agent verifies each detection before the construction is added to the cadastral value (Ouest-France). The 140,000 pools and €40 million are the minister's own attribution to the tool, relayed by several news outlets, and CNews cites the DGFiP annual report via Ouest-France; no official document was fetched. A CGT Finances publiques official told Ouest-France that field colleagues put the algorithm's error threshold at 55–60%, so the detections required substantial human checking. The commune-level outcome sought by the earlier verifier was not found; the Dordogne figure is the most local one fetched.

Replication

What produced it

  • IGN aerial photographs covering the whole territory
  • A detection model built with Capgemini and Google, generalised nationally after the 2022 nine-département experiment
  • Comparison of detected outlines with cadastral declarations
  • Agent verification of each detection and letters inviting owners to regularise

What must hold again

  • Recent, high-resolution aerial coverage
  • A false-detection rate low enough for agents to verify (a union official cites 55–60% errors)
  • Legal basis to add undeclared constructions to the cadastral value
  • Local offices with capacity to send and handle catch-up notices
