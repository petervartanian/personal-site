SUVAS translates 36,271 Supreme Court judgments into Hindi

SUVAS, the Supreme Court of India's translation software, translated 36,271 Supreme Court judgments into Hindi and 17,142 into 16 languages by 5 August 2024

What happened

SUVAS (Supreme Court Vidhik Anuvaad Software) is the Supreme Court of India's artificial-intelligence-assisted tool for translating English judgments into Indian languages. The Supreme Court Observer had counted only 31 translated judgments from March 2020 to October 2021. The Hindu reported on 31 December 2023 that the e-SCR portal held 2,238 translated judgments in January 2023 and over 31,000 by the year's end. Officials credited the rise largely to retired High Court judges and law clerks who corrected the software's errors. A retired Madras High Court judge said that the Tamil translations he had seen could not be read. A 2025 peer-reviewed article cites Press Information Bureau figures as on 5 August 2024. By then 36,271 Supreme Court judgments were translated into Hindi and 17,142 into 16 other regional languages. The Supreme Court's Annual Report 2024–25 summarizes a judge's address that credits SUVAS with translating over 100,000 judgments into 18 regional languages. The report also records High Court translation work. The High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh, which the report says uses SUVAS, translated 4,426 Supreme Court judgments into Urdu. Manipur's AI-Assisted Legal Translation Committee translated 433 Supreme Court judgments into Meetei Mayek, of which 259 were uploaded and 174 were under review. The report calls this “AI-assisted translation” without naming the tool. These figures are aggregates. The sources give no single translated judgment with a dated delivery.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Machine translation of English judgments by SUVAS. Review by High Court AI-assisted translation committees before upload. Publication on the e-SCR portal and High Court websites.

Benefits

Duration

Published translations fell to 31 between March 2020 and October 2021. They rose from 2,238 in January 2023 to over 31,000 by the end of 2023 and to 53,413 by 5 August 2024. On 6 June 2025, according to the Supreme Court's annual report, Justice Surya Kant credited SUVAS with over 100,000 judgments in 18 regional languages. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]
