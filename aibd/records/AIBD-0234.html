Asilla's camera AI may have prompted two suicide interventions

Asilla's behavior-analysis AI for security cameras alerted guards, who then reached two people at apparent risk of suicide, the company says; the facilities and dates are undisclosed.

What happened

Tokyo-based Asilla's system assesses the likelihood that a person will jump by analyzing behavior on security-camera footage. Asilla says that it has trained the system since 2022 with around 200 facilities on about seven million pieces of footage. Its product release of 26 January 2024 identifies a jump-risk detection function in AI Security asilla version 2.4, using movement trajectories, position, and body-motion patterns. The system looks for behavior such as pacing or lingering near a platform edge or rooftop. It alerts security guards and station staff, in some cases with loudspeaker warnings. Kyodo reported on 1 June 2026 that Asilla described two interventions, both undated. In one case the system detected a man loitering in a non-customer area of a commercial facility. A guard found him, and he later said that he had intended to kill himself by jumping. In another, a child lingering near an upper-floor railing was found to have been writing a suicide note. The product release does not identify the version used in these two interventions. All details come from the company via Kyodo, and no operator, police or academic confirmation is among the sources. In Stockholm, Karolinska Institutet researchers reported in July 2026 that suicide-related incidents on the tracks fell at 14 metro stations after an AI camera system was installed in late 2021, compared with 86 other stations. They studied a different system and did not test Asilla's.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Camera footage is analyzed for movement, position, and body-motion patterns associated with a possible jump risk. Alerts direct staff toward the person and location.

Autonomy

The system flagged concerning behavior and sometimes issued warnings. Staff carried out the personal intervention.

Workflow

Existing security cameras in areas with jump risk. A behavior model trained on large volumes of site footage. Guards or staff positioned to respond within minutes.

Human oversight

Guards approached the flagged people and learned information that the video could not establish, including stated intent or a suicide note.

AI contribution

Asilla says that its camera system generated alerts before two interventions. Its product documentation describes how alerts work, but the company supplies no dates, locations, or alert logs for these incidents. [1] [2]

Benefits

Reach

Asilla reports two people whom guards reached after alerts: a man in a commercial facility and a child near an upper-floor railing. The system runs at about 40 stations and commercial buildings, and the sources give no count of the people monitored. [1] [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Staff presence to act on alerts. Acceptable false-alert rates for operators. Camera coverage of the risk zones.
