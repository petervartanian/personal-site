Asilla credits its camera AI with two suicide interventions

alerted guards in two cases where people later said they meant to jump

Kyodo (1 Jun 2026) reports Asilla's camera-analysis system at about 40 stations and buildings alerted guards in two cases where people later said they meant to jump; the claims are the developer's.

What happened

The date is the Kyodo report's publication as carried by The Straits Times; the two interventions are undated. Tokyo-based Asilla's system assesses the likelihood that a person will jump by analysing behaviour on security-camera footage, such as pacing or lingering near a platform edge or rooftop, and alerts security guards and station staff, in some cases with loudspeaker warnings. In one case it detected a man loitering in a non-customer area of a commercial facility; a guard found him and he later said he had intended to kill himself by jumping. In another, a child lingering near an upper-floor railing was found to have been writing a suicide note. Asilla says it trained the system since 2022 with around 200 facilities on about seven million pieces of footage. All details come from the company via Kyodo; no operator, police or academic confirmation was fetched. OECD AIM records the case (2026-06-01-db16).

What it was built to do. Detect behaviour indicating suicide risk (and illness, immobility or violence) in security-camera footage and alert staff.

The system's part

The system's alert prompted the guard's approach in both described cases; the intervention and the person's later statement are human. The sole source chain is the developer speaking to Kyodo, so system accuracy, alert volume and the operators' views are undocumented.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • Existing security cameras in areas with jump risk
  • A behaviour model trained on large volumes of site footage
  • Guards or staff positioned to respond within minutes
What must hold again
  • Staff presence to act on alerts
  • Acceptable false-alert rates for operators
  • Camera coverage of the risk zones
