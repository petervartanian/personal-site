Asilla's camera AI may have prompted two suicide interventions

Asilla told Kyodo that camera alerts prompted guards to intervene in two apparent suicide risks. The facilities and dates were not identified, and the company is the sole source of those accounts.

ⓘ

What happened

The two interventions are undated. Tokyo-based Asilla's system assesses the likelihood that a person will jump by analyzing behavior on security-camera footage. It looks for behavior such as pacing or lingering near a platform edge or rooftop. It alerts security guards and station staff, in some cases with loudspeaker warnings. In one case it detected a man loitering in a non-customer area of a commercial facility. A guard found him, and he later said he had intended to kill himself by jumping. In another, a child lingering near an upper-floor railing was found to have been writing a suicide note. Asilla says it trained the system since 2022 with around 200 facilities on about seven million pieces of footage. All details come from the company via Kyodo, and no operator, police or academic confirmation is among the sources.

Asilla’s earlier product release identifies a jump-risk detection function in AI Security asilla version 2.4, using movement trajectories, position, and body-motion patterns. It does not identify the version used in these two interventions.

Purpose. Detect behavior indicating suicide risk (and illness, immobility or violence) in security-camera footage and alert staff.

AI’s contribution

Kyodo attributes both interventions to Asilla’s account. The company’s original product documentation supports the existence and intended mechanism of jump-risk alerts, but does not independently verify either intervention. The facilities, dates, alert logs, and subsequent outcomes are not supplied.
