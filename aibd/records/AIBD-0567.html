West Berkshire council uses GPT to help managers draft job adverts

West Berkshire Council reports that its job-advert generator, built on OpenAI GPT models, reduced managers' drafting time and made adverts more consistent.

  • 1 an organization

    The council reports that the job-advert tool saves drafting time and makes adverts uniform.

  • 1 a person

    Managers report that they spend less time drafting job postings. The sources do not measure what happened to applicants or to hiring.

What happened

West Berkshire used GPT-based tools to generate job advertisements and explored internal and public-information assistants. Managers report less drafting time and more consistent formatting. The source reports roughly £300 in setup staff time and £2 in monthly running costs. Recycling and policy assistants were planned, and their benefits had not been established. On 19 February 2024 the chief executive of GOSS, the council's platform supplier, wrote that the council's digital services manager had described the job-advert tool at a UKA Live panel. According to that account, the manager said that the cost per use was small but that the results were variable, ranked around B-, and needed human checks.

Sources

  1. Report, by the organization that used it.

    Council or project account published in the Local Government Association AI case-study bank. The account provides no independent outcome evaluation.

    West Berkshire supplies job details to an OpenAI API and has managers test draft advertisements. The article names GPT-3.5 for assistants but leaves the advert model unspecified. Overview and implementation challenges.

    Managers reported a substantial reduction in the time it takes to draft job postings. The use of AI has also ensured consistent formatting and the inclusion of all essential information, leading to a more uniform presentation of job adverts across the council.

  2. Descriptive report, by the makers. Context or contrary evidence.

    Rob McCarthy, A starting point for AI in citizen services, UKAuthority (Industry Voice), 19 February 2024. The author is chief executive of GOSS, whose digital platform the council used with ChatGPT; he reports remarks by the council's digital services manager at a UKA Live panel that the output quality was variable and needed human checks.

    Rumens described West Berkshire's use of ChatGPT to create job ads and LinkedIn posts from recruitment data. He made the point that the cost per transaction of such efforts is relatively small - commodity AI platforms are rapidly reducing the cost and open source platforms are available - but said the results are variable, ranked around B-, and need some human quality assurance.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

OpenAI GPT models through the ChatGPT API (version not stated for the job-advert tool; the council links its assistant prototypes to GPT-3.5)

Inputs and outputs

A form supplies job details that the API converts into a draft advertisement. [1]

Workflow

Forms collect job details and feed them to the OpenAI API through the council’s existing digital platform. Manager feedback informed evaluation of generated content.

Human oversight

Recruiting managers test and refine the generated advertisements. According to an article of 19 February 2024 by the chief executive of GOSS, the council's digital services manager said that the results were variable and needed some human quality assurance. [1] [2]

Measurement

Measurement

Survey of senior managers who reviewed AI-generated job adverts.

Setting and sample

Senior and recruiting managers at West Berkshire Council; sample size not stated.

Measures

Average rating of the AI-generated content out of 10.

Results

Average rating of 8 out of 10.

Limitations

Sample size, survey date and question wording are not reported. No measured drafting time is given.

Evidence

Original evidence

The Local Government Association case study is the council's own account. The second source is an article by the chief executive of GOSS, the council's platform supplier, who reports remarks by the council's digital services manager. Neither source evaluates the results independently. [1] [2]
