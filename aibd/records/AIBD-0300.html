Apple Watch breathing alerts send a 79-year-old to CPAP therapy

Apple Watch sleep apnea notifications brought a 79-year-old man in Fukuoka to his cardiologists; his watch ECGs showed atrial fibrillation after nights rated “elevated”, and he began CPAP therapy.

  • 1 a person

    A man whose atrial fibrillation was thought quiescent learned that paroxysms recur after nights of disturbed breathing, and he started CPAP therapy. No confirmed sleep apnea diagnosis and no treatment effect are reported.

What happened

Maruyama (Haradoi Hospital, Fukuoka), Hieda and Fukata reported a case in Cureus on 26 May 2026. The consultation is undated. The patient is a 79-year-old Japanese man with hypertension, dyslipidemia, type 2 diabetes and symptomatic paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (AF). A 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) had documented it a decade earlier. After retirement no paroxysms were documented by ambulatory or standard ECG, and pulsed-field ablation was deferred. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA) cleared Apple's Sleep Apnea Notification Feature under 510(k) K240929 on 13 September 2024. Apple's 510(k) submission says that it uses a deep learning algorithm on Apple Watch accelerometer data, analyzed in 30-day windows. It was trained on over 11,000 nights with polysomnography or home sleep-test reference. Some months after he began wearing an Apple Watch Series 11 paired with an iPhone 15 Pro, he consulted the authors. He asked about notifications of nocturnal “respiratory disturbance”. He activated the watch ECG every morning and evening. AF paroxysms were detected repeatedly on mornings after nights on which the watch rated breathing disturbance “elevated”. They were not detected after “not elevated” nights, and they terminated by evening. He is undergoing continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy, and pulsed-field ablation will be considered depending on its effect on the paroxysms. No polysomnography was performed, and no treatment outcome is reported. The authors state that watch-detected breathing disturbance is not equivalent to the apnea-hypopnea index. They state that the watch ECG cannot fully substitute for conventional ECG, and that the notification algorithm “was reportedly developed using advanced machine learning”.

Details

AI system

Role

The model detected elevated breathing disturbance and generated a notification.

Configuration

The participant used an Apple Watch Series 11 with an iPhone 15 Pro.

Inputs and outputs

The watch used movement data during sleep to estimate breathing disturbance. The patient separately initiated ECG recordings, which clinicians related to the preceding night’s breathing pattern.

Autonomy

Breathing analysis ran in the background, but ECG recordings were patient-activated and treatment decisions remained with clinicians.

Workflow

Nightly watch wear with the sleep-apnea notification feature active, plus the owner's habit of recording a watch ECG every morning and evening. A known history of paroxysmal AF that made the morning traces interpretable to his cardiologists. Access to a clinic willing to act on watch data (CPAP started without polysomnography).

Human oversight

The patient brought the notifications and ECG recordings to clinicians, who selected CPAP and deferred ablation while considering its effect.

AI contribution

Sleep-apnea notifications prompted the patient to seek care. Clinicians interpreted his morning ECG recordings and chose CPAP treatment. The report does not separately identify an ECG classifier’s contribution or provide a treatment outcome. [1] [2]

Benefits

Reach

One patient benefited: a 79-year-old man in Fukuoka. [1] [2]

Duration

The report of 26 May 2026 says that CPAP therapy is under way and that ablation will depend on its effect. No later outcome is among the sources. [1] [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The user completes onboarding and wears the watch during sleep, since the feature evaluates 30-day windows opportunistically. Clinicians confirm with conventional ECG and polysomnography, which the authors say the watch cannot replace and which was not done here.

Evidence

Original evidence

Treating clinicians report the consultation and ECG findings. Apple’s FDA submission describes the notification algorithm and its validation. FDA’s clearance is a regulatory decision, not confirmation of this patient’s benefit. [1] [2]
