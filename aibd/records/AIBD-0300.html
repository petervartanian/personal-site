Apple Watch breathing alerts send a 79-year-old to CPAP therapy

Fukuoka case (Cureus, 26 May 2026): sleep-apnoea notifications prompted consultation; watch ECGs showed AF only after 'elevated' breathing-disturbance nights; CPAP under way, no polysomnography.

What happened

Maruyama (Haradoi Hospital, Fukuoka), Hieda and Fukata reported in Cureus on 26 May 2026 (publication date used; the consultation is undated) a 79-year-old Japanese man with hypertension, dyslipidaemia, type 2 diabetes and symptomatic paroxysmal atrial fibrillation documented by 12-lead ECG a decade earlier. After retirement no paroxysms were documented by ambulatory or standard ECG and pulsed-field ablation was deferred. Some months after he began wearing an Apple Watch Series 11 paired with an iPhone 15 Pro, he consulted the authors about notifications of nocturnal 'respiratory disturbance'. He activated the watch ECG every morning and evening; AF paroxysms were detected repeatedly on mornings after nights the watch rated breathing disturbance 'elevated' but not after 'not elevated' nights, and they terminated by evening. He is undergoing CPAP therapy, and pulsed-field ablation will be considered depending on its effect on the paroxysms. No polysomnography was performed; the authors state that watch-detected breathing disturbance is not equivalent to the apnoea-hypopnoea index, that the watch ECG cannot fully substitute for conventional ECG, and that the notification algorithm 'was reportedly developed using advanced machine learning'. The FDA cleared Apple's Sleep Apnea Notification Feature under 510(k) K240929 on 13 September 2024; the 510(k) summary says it uses a deep learning algorithm on Apple Watch accelerometer data, analysed in 30-day windows, trained on over 11,000 nights with polysomnography or home sleep-test reference. No treatment outcome is reported.

What it was built to do. The notification feature is built to identify patterns of breathing disturbance suggestive of moderate-to-severe sleep apnoea and tell the user; the ECG app is built to record and classify single-lead rhythm; both were used as designed.

The system's part

The notification feature's deep-learning model (per the FDA summary) flagged elevated breathing disturbance and brought the patient to the clinic; his own morning ECG recordings, read by the authors, showed the AF paroxysms, and the paper does not state separately what the ECG app's classifier reported. Clinicians chose CPAP and deferred ablation. The maker's account was not used; the FDA 510(k) summary and one independent case report are the sources.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • Nightly watch wear with the sleep-apnoea notification feature active, plus the owner's habit of recording a watch ECG every morning and evening
  • A known history of paroxysmal AF that made the morning traces interpretable to his cardiologists
  • Access to a clinic willing to act on watch data (CPAP started without polysomnography)

What must hold again

  • The user completes onboarding and wears the watch during sleep, since the feature evaluates 30-day windows opportunistically
  • Clinicians confirm with conventional ECG and polysomnography, which the authors say the watch cannot replace and which was not done here
