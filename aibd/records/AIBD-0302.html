Apple Watch Crash Detection calls 911 after a truck flips into a river

On 22 February 2026 near Batavia, Ohio, a 19-year-old's truck flipped into the Little Miami River; Clermont County officials say that her Apple Watch Crash Detection called 911, and crews freed her.

  • 5 a person

    Burns was trapped upside down in a submerged cab in the river at night with an air pocket of about four inches. Crews pulled her out with bruises only, which is a life preserved. Burns told WKRC that a Life360 service on her mother's phone also called police, so the sources do not establish that the watch's call alone brought the crews.

  • Not rated an organization

    Clermont County dispatch received the automated call and stayed on the line with Burns. No source reports time or resources saved for the sheriff's office or the fire district.

The system’s part
ABC News, citing Clermont County officials, and WLWT say the Apple Watch's Crash Detection placed the 911 call. Apple's support page says the feature detects severe crashes from on-device sensor data. It calls automatically after a countdown if the wearer does not respond. Burns also told WKRC that a Life360 crash-detection service on her mother's phone called police and family. The watch may not have been the only alert. Only news reports are among the sources, and no dispatch log or agency statement is on record.
Limits
Agency dispatch logs are needed to distinguish Apple Watch from Life360 alerts and determine their timing. Rescue and limited injuries are supported by reporting, but the watch’s necessity cannot be isolated from other alerts, dispatchers, and responders.
Costs and harms
The watch contributed an alert, and firefighters and deputies pulled her out. The reports do not establish whether its call was the only or the earliest alert. Okayama University physicians found that 35 of 49 crash-detection calls to the Okayama City Fire Bureau from October 2022 to March 2023 were false alarms.
Later
Persisted, 11 March 2026. WKRC reported on 11 March 2026 that Burns was alive and well and had thanked the Clermont County responders; ABC News (4 March) said the accident report recorded only bruises.

What happened

Andi Burns, 19, was driving home from work on State Route 222 in Batavia Township, Clermont County, Ohio, on 22 February 2026. The Ohio State Highway Patrol accident report cited by ABC News says that she lost control on black ice. She hit a tree and went off an embankment into the Little Miami River. WLWT says that the truck dropped down a 15-foot ravine and flipped. ABC News (4 March 2026) reports that “According to Clermont County officials, Burns was wearing an Apple Watch that automatically called 911 using its crash detection feature”. Body-camera footage released by the Clermont County Sheriff's Office shows a dispatcher keeping her on the line. Central Joint Fire-EMS District firefighters and sheriff's deputies smashed a window and pulled her out. First-year firefighter Tommy Jetter was first into the water. Burns had found a pocket of air of about four inches at the floorboard. The accident report says that she walked away with bruises. Burns told WKRC (11 March 2026) that there was also “a crash detection on the Live 360 that my mom had on her phone”. She said that it called the police and her family, so a second crash-detection service may have placed calls. WLWT and ABC attribute the 911 call to the Apple Watch. Okayama University emergency physicians studied Okayama City Fire Bureau records. They found that 13 of 49 crash-detection calls from October 2022 to March 2023 came from real crashes or falls and that 35 were false alarms. They did not examine outcomes for patients.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

The watch reportedly placed the emergency call automatically. Dispatch and physical rescue were performed by people.

Workflow

A supported Apple Watch (Series 8 or later, SE 2nd generation, Ultra) with Crash Detection on and a cellular or Wi-Fi path to emergency services. A severe crash (rollover into water) with the wearer unable to reach a phone. A dispatcher able to act on the automated call and location.

Human oversight

A dispatcher stayed on the line with Burns, while deputies and firefighters located the overturned truck, broke a window, and pulled her out.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The on-device crash classifier recognizes the impact as a severe car crash. The wearer does not dismiss the alert within the countdown. Responders can reach the transmitted location in time.
