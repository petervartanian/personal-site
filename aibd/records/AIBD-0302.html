Apple Watch Crash Detection calls 911 for Andi Burns in a river

On 22 Feb 2026 near Batavia, Ohio, a 19-year-old's truck flipped into the Little Miami River; Clermont County officials say her Apple Watch called 911 by Crash Detection and crews freed her.

What happened

Andi Burns, 19, was driving home from work on State Route 222 in Batavia Township, Clermont County, Ohio, on 22 February 2026 when, according to the Ohio State Highway Patrol accident report cited by ABC News, she lost control on black ice, hit a tree and went off an embankment into the Little Miami River; WLWT says the truck dropped down a 15-foot ravine and flipped. ABC News (4 March 2026) reports that 'According to Clermont County officials, Burns was wearing an Apple Watch that automatically called 911 using its crash detection feature', and body-camera footage released by the Clermont County Sheriff's Office shows a dispatcher keeping her on the line while Central Joint Fire-EMS District firefighters and sheriff's deputies smashed a window and pulled her out; first-year firefighter Tommy Jetter was first into the water. Burns had found a pocket of air of about four inches at the floorboard. The accident report says she walked away with bruises. Burns told WKRC (11 March 2026) that there was also 'a crash detection on the Live 360 that my mom had on her phone' and that it called the police and her family, so a second crash-detection service may have placed calls; WLWT and ABC attribute the 911 call to the Apple Watch.

What it was built to do. Crash Detection is built to recognise a severe car crash from sensor data and call emergency services with the device's location if the wearer does not respond; this call was that purpose.

The system's part

ABC News, citing Clermont County officials, and WLWT say the Apple Watch's Crash Detection placed the 911 call; Apple's support page says the feature detects severe crashes from on-device sensor data and calls automatically after a countdown if the wearer does not respond. Burns also told WKRC that a Life360 crash-detection service on her mother's phone called police and family, so the watch may not have been the only alert. Only news reports were fetched; no dispatch log or agency statement was obtained.

Replication

What produced it
  • A supported Apple Watch (Series 8 or later, SE 2nd generation, Ultra) with Crash Detection on and a cellular or Wi-Fi path to emergency services
  • A severe crash (rollover into water) with the wearer unable to reach a phone
  • A dispatcher able to act on the automated call and location
What must hold again
  • The on-device crash classifier recognises the impact as a severe car crash
  • The wearer does not dismiss the alert within the countdown
  • Responders can reach the transmitted location in time
