What happened

The OECD AI Incidents entry concerns Operation Sindoor in May 2025. It says the Indian Air Force (IAF) used “the AI-powered X-Guard decoy system on Rafale jets”. It says this “prevented enemy missiles from hitting real aircraft”. The claim originates in an idrw.org post of 6 July 2025, sourced to “AFI”, an outsourced content partner. The post quotes Ryan Bodenheimer, a former US Air Force F-15E and F-16 pilot. He calls the IAF's tactics “the best spoofing and deception we've ever seen” and credits “the Rafale's X-Guard AI-enhanced jamming decoy system”. idrw itself writes that the system “likely played a pivotal role”. It writes that Pakistani claims of downed Rafales were “likely X-Guard decoys” according to Jane's Defence Weekly. The Economic Times (ET) writes on 8 July 2025 that the IAF “reportedly used artificial intelligence-powered technology”.

LatestLY (9 Jul) says it “allegedly used” it. India TV (9 Jul) attributes a finding that Pakistani kills were hits on towed decoys to “a recent Jane's Defence Weekly investigation”. No other outlet on record corroborates it, and the Jane's Defence Weekly investigation itself is not among the sources. Oneindia (9 Jul) says false claims of downed Rafales “may have been decoys”. TFIPOST (9 Jul) says the IAF “reportedly deployed” the system and names the maker correctly as Rafael Advanced Defense Systems. ET, India TV, LatestLY, Oneindia and idrw write “Rafale Advanced Defense Systems”. The Week and Business Today are quoted from an earlier reading; their pages were not available afterward. The Week's headline says the IAF “may have used” X-Guard. It describes Bodenheimer as “a military tech digital creator”. Business Today writes “reportedly misdirected”.

No IAF, Ministry of Defence (MoD), Dassault or Rafael statement confirms that X-Guard was carried or deployed. Rafael's product page was not available. The claimed outcome (no losses) is contradicted by India's Chief of Defence Staff (CDS), General Anil Chauhan. At the Shangri-La Dialogue on 31 May 2025 he admitted that an unspecified number of Indian jets were shot down. He rejected Pakistan's claim of six (Al Jazeera, 1 June 2025; Wikipedia citing Bloomberg). Operation Sindoor began on 7 May 2025 and lasted four days (India TV). The Monitor's 7 July date is the coverage date.

What it was built to do. A towed decoy is built to present a false target to enemy radars and radar-guided missiles. They then engage the decoy rather than the aircraft. The outlets describe a 30 kg body on a 100 m fiber-optic cable emitting a 500 W jamming signal.

The system's part

Not assessed. No source independent of the commentary chain describes X-Guard as an AI system. idrw writes “AI-enhanced jamming decoy” and “AI-enhanced algorithms dynamically adjust signals” without attribution. ET, India TV, LatestLY, Oneindia and TFIPOST repeat the label. The Week gives no source for “AI-enabled”. Rafael's own page was unreadable. Deployment itself is unconfirmed by the IAF, MoD, Dassault or Rafael. The no-loss outcome is contradicted by the CDS. Neither the system's AI character nor any contribution to the engagements can therefore be assessed.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A towed decoy on a fiber-optic cable emitting a jamming signal that mimics the aircraft's radar return and Doppler shift (as the outlets describe it). Radar-guided missiles (PL-15E, per the outlets) engaging the decoy rather than the aircraft.

What must hold again. Official confirmation that X-Guard was carried and deployed by the IAF in the 7–10 May 2025 engagements. Evidence that missiles struck decoys rather than aircraft. India TV attributes this to Jane's Defence Weekly, but no other outlet corroborates it. It is at odds with the CDS's admission of losses.
