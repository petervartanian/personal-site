Indian outlets say X-Guard diverted missiles; CDS admits losses

Every account traces to ex-USAF pilot Ryan Bodenheimer via idrw.org (6 Jul 2025). No IAF, MoD, Dassault or Rafael confirmation; CDS Gen Anil Chauhan admitted on 31 May 2025 that India lost jets.

What happened

The OECD AI Incidents Monitor record 2025-07-07-7754 says that during Operation Sindoor in May 2025 the Indian Air Force used 'the AI-powered X-Guard decoy system on Rafale jets' and that this 'prevented enemy missiles from hitting real aircraft'. The claim originates in an idrw.org post of 6 July 2025 (sourced to 'AFI', an outsourced content partner) that quotes Ryan Bodenheimer, a former US Air Force F-15E and F-16 pilot, calling the IAF's tactics 'the best spoofing and deception we've ever seen' and crediting 'the Rafale's X-Guard AI-enhanced jamming decoy system'; idrw itself writes that the system 'likely played a pivotal role' and that Pakistani claims of downed Rafales were 'likely X-Guard decoys' according to Jane's Defence Weekly. The Economic Times (8 Jul 2025) writes that the IAF 'reportedly used artificial intelligence-powered technology'; LatestLY (9 Jul) that it 'allegedly used' it; India TV (9 Jul) attributes a finding that Pakistani kills were hits on towed decoys to 'a recent Jane's Defence Weekly investigation', which no other fetched outlet corroborates and which could not be checked; Oneindia (9 Jul) says that false claims of downed Rafales 'may have been decoys'; TFIPOST (9 Jul) says the IAF 'reportedly deployed' the system and names the maker correctly as Rafael Advanced Defense Systems, while ET, India TV, LatestLY, Oneindia and idrw write 'Rafale Advanced Defense Systems'. The earlier verifier read The Week, whose headline says the IAF 'may have used' X-Guard and which describes Bodenheimer as 'a military tech digital creator', and Business Today, which writes 'reportedly misdirected'; neither could be fetched here. No Indian Air Force, Ministry of Defence, Dassault or Rafael statement confirms that X-Guard was carried or deployed; Rafael's product page returned nothing to this fetch and a bot check to the earlier one. The claimed outcome (no losses) is contradicted by India's Chief of Defence Staff, General Anil Chauhan, who at the Shangri-La Dialogue on 31 May 2025 admitted that an unspecified number of Indian jets were shot down while rejecting Pakistan's claim of six (Al Jazeera, 1 Jun 2025; Wikipedia citing Bloomberg). Operation Sindoor began on 7 May 2025 and lasted four days (India TV); the Monitor's 7 July date is the coverage date.

What it was built to do. A towed decoy is built to present a false target to enemy radars and radar-guided missiles so that they engage the decoy rather than the aircraft; the outlets describe a 30 kg body on a 100 m fibre-optic cable emitting a 500 W jamming signal.
