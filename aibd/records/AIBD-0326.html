X-Guard decoy may have diverted Pakistani missiles from Indian jets

Commentary credits Rafael's X-Guard towed decoy, labeled “AI-powered”, with diverting Pakistani missiles from Indian Rafales; no official source confirms its use, and India admitted jet losses.

  • Not rated a person

    No pilot or aircraft is documented as saved by the decoy. India's Chief of Defence Staff admitted jet losses. The claim rests on one commentator's inference.

  • Not rated an organization

    No official account of the engagements names X-Guard. India's later move to expedite X-Guard deliveries (idrw search listing, 13 July 2025) concerns procurement and reports no outcome of this operation.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

What happened

Rafael’s March 2024 product brochure describes a reusable fiber-optic towed decoy. It does not identify an AI model or confirm deployment in Operation Sindoor. Operation Sindoor began on 7 May 2025 and lasted four days (India TV). At the Shangri-La Dialogue on 31 May 2025, India's Chief of Defence Staff, General Anil Chauhan, admitted that an unspecified number of Indian jets were shot down. He rejected Pakistan's claim of six (Al Jazeera, 1 June 2025; Wikipedia citing Bloomberg). He placed India's air losses on the first day of fighting. The claim for the decoy originates in an idrw.org post of 6 July 2025, sourced to “AFI”, an outsourced content partner. The post quotes Ryan Bodenheimer, a former US Air Force F-15E and F-16 pilot. He calls the Indian Air Force's tactics “the best spoofing and deception we've ever seen” and credits “the Rafale's X-Guard AI-enhanced jamming decoy system”. idrw itself writes that the system “likely played a pivotal role”. It writes that Pakistani claims of downed Rafales were “likely X-Guard decoys” according to Jane's Defence Weekly. On the same day the Associated Press quoted General Jérôme Bellanger, chief of the French air force. He said that he had seen evidence of three Indian aircraft losses, a Rafale among them. The OECD AI Incidents entry concerns Operation Sindoor and carries the coverage date of 7 July. It says that the Indian Air Force used “the AI-powered X-Guard decoy system on Rafale jets”. It says that this “prevented enemy missiles from hitting real aircraft”. The Economic Times writes on 8 July 2025 that the Indian Air Force “reportedly used artificial intelligence-powered technology”. On 9 July LatestLY says that it “allegedly used” it. India TV, also on 9 July, attributes a finding that Pakistani kills were hits on towed decoys to “a recent Jane's Defence Weekly investigation”. No other outlet on record corroborates it, and the Jane's Defence Weekly investigation itself is not among the sources. Oneindia (9 July) says that false claims of downed Rafales “may have been decoys”. TFIPOST (9 July) says that the Indian Air Force “reportedly deployed” the system, and it names the maker correctly as Rafael Advanced Defense Systems. The Economic Times, India TV, LatestLY, Oneindia and idrw write “Rafale Advanced Defense Systems”. The Week's headline says that the Indian Air Force “may have used” X-Guard. It describes Bodenheimer as “a military tech digital creator”. Business Today writes “reportedly misdirected”. The pages of The Week and Business Today are no longer available. No Indian Air Force, Ministry of Defence, Dassault or Rafael statement confirms that X-Guard was carried or deployed. The claimed outcome of no losses is contradicted by India's Chief of Defence Staff.

Details

AI system

Role

The claimed role was to draw radar-guided missiles toward a towed electronic decoy rather than the aircraft, but use in these engagements is unconfirmed.

Workflow

A towed decoy on a fiber-optic cable emitting a jamming signal that mimics the aircraft's radar return and Doppler shift (as the outlets describe it). Radar-guided missiles (PL-15E, per the outlets) engaging the decoy rather than the aircraft.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Aircrew and aircraft were the intended protected parties. No specific aircraft survival can be attributed to X-Guard from the evidence.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Official confirmation that X-Guard was carried and deployed by the IAF in the 7–10 May 2025 engagements. Evidence that missiles struck decoys rather than aircraft. India TV attributes this to Jane's Defence Weekly, but no other outlet corroborates it. It is at odds with the CDS's admission of losses.

Entry details

Sector

Defense: electronic warfare. Airborne towed radar decoys.

Purpose

A towed decoy is built to present a false target to enemy radars and radar-guided missiles, which then engage the decoy and spare the aircraft. The outlets describe a 30 kg body on a 100 m fiber-optic cable emitting a 500 W jamming signal.
