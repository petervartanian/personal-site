Refuge cuts near misses at a Coventry junction that AI sensors flagged

Transport for West Midlands reports that VivaCity sensors detected 61,000 near misses and that near misses at one Coventry junction fell by more than 88 percent after a refuge was built.

  • 2 society

The system’s part
The sensors detected near misses and supplied the data that TfWM used to choose the Grange Road site and work out how to reduce them. Coventry City Council built the refuge with TfWM funding, and TfWM credits the refuge with slowing turning traffic. The same sensors measured the result, so the 88 percent figure depends on the detector that chose the site.
Limits
Detected near misses need validation, exposure denominators and event definitions. The before-and-after count at Grange Road covers one junction over two equal two-month periods, and the same sensors that flagged the site made the count. Collision and injury data are needed to assess the safety benefit and to account for other changes.

Persisted, 18 November 2025. TfWM reports that the Grange Road refuge cut recorded near misses by more than 88 percent and that it is looking at more sites with VivaCity and local councils. The follow-on trial was funded for 30 months from August 2024.

What happened

Transport for West Midlands enabled VivaCity near-miss detection in 40 sensors and used the observations to select and implement road-layout changes. The source reports 61,000 detected near misses in six months and the installation of measures including central islands and narrower junction mouths. The interventions are documented, but the source does not establish a reduction in collisions or injuries. On 5 February 2025 Coventry City Council was still set to install a pedestrian refuge at Grange Road after the sensors recorded near misses there. On 18 November 2025 TfWM reported that near misses at the junction fell from 17 to 2 over equal two-month periods after the refuge was built, a cut of more than 88 percent. TfWM gave no data on collisions or injuries.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Roadside video becomes movement paths and near-miss clips that road-safety staff inspect. [1]

Workflow

The January–August 2024 pilot enabled near-miss functions on 40 existing sensors, with later continuation funding. Practitioners used dashboards and video clips to identify hotspots and implement measures such as islands and narrower junction mouths.

Human oversight

Transport practitioners used the near-miss observations to select road-layout changes, which were then implemented. The account does not specify checks on every detected event. [1]

Benefits

Duration

The fall in near misses at Grange Road was measured over one two-month period after the refuge was built. On 18 November 2025 Transport for West Midlands said that it was investigating more sites with VivaCity and local councils. No source reports later counts at the Grange Road junction. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Measurement

Measurement

Near misses counted by the VivaCity sensor at the junction over two-month windows before and after the refuge was built.

Setting and sample

One T-junction, Grange Road and Anderton Road, Longford, Coventry.

Measures

Number of detected near misses; average vehicle speed at recorded near misses.

Results

17 near misses before and 2 after (more than 88 percent fewer); speeds at near misses fell from 14 to 16 mph to 10.5 to 12.5 mph.

Additional value from AI

Not isolated; the change follows a physical road measure that the sensor data helped to select.

Limitations

Small counts over short windows at a single site, chosen because near misses were high, so regression to the mean is possible. No control site, no collision or injury data, and the figures come from TfWM.

Denominators

Two months before and two months after the refuge was built.

Evidence

Original evidence

Three sources are the deployer's own accounts: the Local Government Association case study and two West Midlands Combined Authority press releases. The Traffic Technology Today feature is independent and gives no outcome data. The same sensors that led to the choice of the Grange Road site measured the fall of more than 88 percent. The BBC report cited for the counts of 17 and 2 near misses attributes them to Transport for West Midlands. [1] [2] [3] [4]
