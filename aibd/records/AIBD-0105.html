GLAD alerts cut deforestation probability in Africa by 18%

Independent evaluation: subscriptions to GLAD Landsat alerts (bagged decision trees) cut deforestation probability in Africa by 18%.

What happened

Global Forest Watch's GLAD deforestation alerts are produced by the University of Maryland from Landsat imagery using a set of bagged decision trees that estimate per-pixel likelihood of forest loss; the method was published on 2 March 2016. Moffette, Alix-Garcia, Shea and Pickens evaluated the alerts' effect in Nature Climate Change on 4 January 2021, analyzing subscriptions to alerts across 22 tropical countries. They found that subscriptions decreased the probability of deforestation in Africa by 18 percent relative to 2011–2016 baseline levels, with stronger effects in protected areas and concessions, no detectable effect on other continents, and little effect where alerts existed without subscribers; they valued the avoided emissions at USD 149–696 million. The date given is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Detect humid tropical forest disturbance in near real time from Landsat imagery to support land management and enforcement.

The system's part

The alerts are a machine-learning product (bagged decision trees) and the evaluation attributes the 18 percent reduction to subscriptions to those alerts rather than to their mere availability; the evaluation's lead authors are independent economists, but co-authors are from WRI (operator) and the University of Maryland (producer).

Replication

What produced it. Weekly Landsat coverage and a conservative classifier. Free public platform with subscription-based alert delivery. Subscribers with enforcement or management capacity.

What must hold again. Alerts reach actors who can respond. Cloud cover does not suppress detections for long periods. Protected-area or concession managers retain enforcement authority.
