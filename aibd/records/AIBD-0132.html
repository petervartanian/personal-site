What happened

In January 2025 Metropolitan Police Live Facial Recognition cameras on a van at Denmark Hill identified David Cheneler, 73, under a 2019 Sexual Offences Prevention Order barring him from being alone with children under 14 after a 2010 conviction for 15 counts of indecent assault on a female under 16 and five of gross indecency with a child. Officers found him with a six-year-old girl he had collected from school for her mother, who did not know his history, and a lock knife hidden in his belt buckle. He pleaded guilty at Wimbledon Magistrates' Court on 13 January 2025 to breaching the order and possessing an offensive weapon; on 20 May 2025 Kingston Crown Court jailed him for two years.

What it was built to do. The Met deploys Live Facial Recognition cameras on police vans to identify people of interest and alert officers; its LFR lead, Lindsey Chiswick, describes the aim as using data and technology to identify offenders that pose a risk to our communities, and identifying a sex offender in breach of his order was that purpose.

The system's part

The LFR system did the identification: cameras on the back of a van at Denmark Hill matched Cheneler's face and alerted officers, Southwark News reports. Finding the child and the knife, the arrest, the charges and the sentence were human and judicial acts, and the offense itself existed only because of his 2019 order. The counterfactual is the Met's own hedge: DC Adam Pearce said it is possible that, had he not been identified, Cheneler could have gone on to abuse the child, and no allegation about this occasion is reported. The readable source does not name the vendor or algorithm, and the Met's press release could not be read.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A van-mounted LFR deployment at Denmark Hill in January 2025 and a face the system was able to identify, so the match alerted officers on the spot. Officers who stopped him at once, found the child and the lock knife hidden in his belt buckle, and knew the terms of his 2019 Sexual Offences Prevention Order. A court record of the order and his 2010 convictions, which turned the stop into a guilty plea for breaching the order and possessing an offensive weapon.

What must hold again. The person must be known to the system and subject to conditions that make his presence with a child an offense; the benefit depends on the prior order, not on the camera alone. Officers must verify the match and intervene immediately; the child was found only because he was stopped on the spot. The deployment must be lawful and proportionate: the OECD Monitor files this same event under human-rights, psychological, reputational and public-interest harm types, so the protective benefit sits beside a contested surveillance practice.
