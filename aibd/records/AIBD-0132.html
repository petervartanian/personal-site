Met LFR flags David Cheneler, a sex offender, alone with a child

Met LFR flagged David Cheneler, 73, with a six-year-old and a lock knife at Denmark Hill, Jan 2025, breaching his SOPO; jailed 20 May 2025.

ⓘ

What happened

In January 2025 Metropolitan Police Live Facial Recognition (LFR) cameras on a van at Denmark Hill identified David Cheneler, 73. He was under a 2019 Sexual Offences Prevention Order barring him from being alone with children under 14. The order followed a 2010 conviction for 15 counts of indecent assault on a female under 16 and five of gross indecency with a child. Officers found him with a six-year-old girl he had collected from school for her mother. The mother did not know his history. A lock knife was hidden in his belt buckle. He pleaded guilty at Wimbledon Magistrates' Court on 13 January 2025 to breaching the order and possessing an offensive weapon. On 20 May 2025 Kingston Crown Court jailed him for two years.

Purpose. The Met deploys Live Facial Recognition cameras on police vans to identify people of interest and alert officers. Its LFR lead, Lindsey Chiswick, describes the aim as using data and technology to identify offenders that pose a risk to our communities. Identifying a sex offender in breach of his order was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The LFR system did the identification. Cameras on the back of a van at Denmark Hill matched Cheneler's face and alerted officers, Southwark News reports. Finding the child and the knife, the arrest, the charges and the sentence were human and judicial acts. The offense itself existed only because of his 2019 order. The counterfactual is the Met's own hedge. DC Adam Pearce said it is possible that, had he not been identified, Cheneler could have gone on to abuse the child. No allegation about this occasion is reported. The readable source does not name the vendor or algorithm. The Met's press release is not among the sources.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    A six-year-old girl was taken out of unsupervised contact with a man convicted of 15 counts of indecent assault on a female under 16. He had collected her from school twice before and was carrying a lock knife. Material for one child, though the Met itself puts the averted abuse as possible rather than established.

  • society

    The public: a registered sex offender in breach of his order was jailed for two years. The Met presents this as removing a dangerous offender from the streets. The OECD Monitor tags the same event with human-rights, psychological, reputational and public-interest harm types. The wider benefit is therefore contested.

Further evidence

Setting. A van-mounted LFR deployment at Denmark Hill in January 2025 and a face the system was able to identify. The match alerted officers on the spot. Officers who stopped him at once and found the child and the lock knife hidden in his belt buckle. They knew the terms of his 2019 Sexual Offences Prevention Order. A court record of the order and his 2010 convictions. This turned the stop into a guilty plea for breaching the order and possessing an offensive weapon.

Conditions. The person must be known to the system and subject to conditions that make his presence with a child an offense. The benefit depends on the prior order, not on the camera alone. Officers must verify the match and intervene immediately. The child was found only because he was stopped on the spot. The deployment must be lawful and proportionate. The OECD Monitor files this same event under human-rights, psychological, reputational and public-interest harm types. The protective benefit therefore sits beside a contested surveillance practice.
