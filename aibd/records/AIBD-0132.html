Police cameras flag a sex offender alone with a six-year-old girl

The Metropolitan Police's Live Facial Recognition cameras identified David Cheneler, a sex offender, alone with a six-year-old girl on 10 January 2025, and he was jailed for two years.

What happened

In January 2025 Metropolitan Police Live Facial Recognition cameras on a van at Denmark Hill identified David Cheneler, 73. BBC News reports that officers stopped him on 10 January 2025. He was under a 2019 Sexual Offences Prevention Order that barred him from being alone with children under 14. The order followed a 2010 conviction for 15 counts of indecent assault on a female under 16 and five of gross indecency with a child. Officers found him with a six-year-old girl that he had collected from school for her mother. The mother did not know his history. A lock knife was hidden in his belt buckle. He pleaded guilty at Wimbledon Magistrates' Court on 13 January 2025 to breaching the order and possessing an offensive weapon. On 20 May 2025 Kingston Crown Court jailed him for two years. On 4 July 2025 the Met announced more than 1,000 arrests through live facial recognition and published Cheneler's case as one of its examples. On 21 April 2026 the High Court dismissed a challenge to the Met's policy on live facial recognition. It held that the policy is lawful, including its watchlists of offenders under court orders. The judges said that they had not been asked to consider whether the technology is effective. The claimants were Silkie Carlo of Big Brother Watch and Shaun Thompson, who had been misidentified by the system at London Bridge. In May 2026 William Page of Birmingham Law School wrote that Thompson intends to appeal.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Van-mounted cameras compared faces with a watchlist and alerted officers when Cheneler was matched.

Workflow

A van-mounted LFR deployment at Denmark Hill in January 2025 and a face the system was able to identify. The match alerted officers on the spot. Officers who stopped him at once and found the child and the lock knife hidden in his belt buckle. They knew the terms of his 2019 Sexual Offences Prevention Order. A court record of the order and his 2010 convictions. This turned the stop into a guilty plea for breaching the order and possessing an offensive weapon.

Human oversight

Officers stopped him, checked the restrictions in his court order, and found the child and concealed knife.

Benefits

One child benefited directly: the six-year-old girl that officers found with Cheneler. No source counts the wider benefit to the public from his two-year sentence. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The person must be known to the system and subject to conditions that make his presence with a child an offense. The benefit depends on the prior order, not on the camera alone. Officers must verify the match and intervene immediately. The child was found only because he was stopped on the spot. The deployment must be lawful and proportionate. The OECD Monitor files this same event under human-rights, psychological, reputational and public-interest harm types. The protective benefit therefore sits beside a contested surveillance practice.
