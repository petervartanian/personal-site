ALERTCalifornia AI flags night smoke, and crews keep a forest fire small

ALERTCalifornia's AI flagged smoke on a Cleveland National Forest camera at about 3 am on 5 August 2023, and Cal Fire held the verified fire under a quarter acre.

  • 2 an organization

    Cal Fire's Mount Laguna station received a verified alert at about 3 am. Its crews extinguished the fire at under a quarter acre. Cal Fire intelligence specialist Suzann Leininger said: “It's getting us time to react in a faster manner”.

  • 2 society

    The area around Mount Laguna was spared a larger fire. Captain Yeary judged that it “could have been a devastating fire”. Battalion Chief Slumpff judged that it would probably have reached hundreds of acres. No source measures what would have happened without the alert.

The system’s part
The model's contribution was the detection. Its algorithm flagged a smoke column in a Cleveland National Forest camera feed at about 3 am. Human operators verified it before Cal Fire was alerted. The containment was done by Captain Yeary's crews. No source records whether a 911 call would have come later. The time gained is documented only for the program as a whole. TIME reports 77 fires identified before any 911 call in the first two months. UC San Diego's Neal Driscoll says the alerts beat 911 calls about 40 percent of the time. AFP also notes the system sometimes misreads dust, insects or weather as smoke. That is why the human verification step sits between the model and the dispatch.
Limits
The alert and containment are reported, but the size of the fire without the warning is unknowable from this event. Program-wide comparisons with 911 calls do not establish time gained for this fire, and false alarms require human validation.
Costs and harms
AFP reports that the system sometimes misreads dust, insects or weather as smoke. Operators therefore verify each detection before dispatch. A RAND researcher told the Associated Press that false alarms can be costly in terms of time and attention.
Later
Scaled up, 19 September 2025. After the September 2023 rollout to all 21 Cal Fire dispatch centers, Cal Fire's figures for 2024 show that the cameras picked up 1,668 of 7,553 wildfires in its jurisdiction, and 636 of them before any 911 call.

What happened

The ALERTCalifornia AI, a machine-learned model run by the University of California San Diego, watches live feeds from more than 1,000 cameras. Its watch over the feeds began at the end of June 2023 under a Cal Fire trial. At about 3 am on 5 August 2023, the AI flagged a column of smoke in the Cleveland National Forest near Mount Laguna. That is about 40 miles east of San Diego. Human operators verified the detection and alerted Cal Fire. The Los Angeles Times reported on 24 August 2023 that Cal Fire Battalion Chief Scott Slumpff got the confirmed alert just before 3 am and that crews held the fire to a 10-foot-by-10-foot spot. Slumpff said that without the alert nobody would have known about the fire until the next morning, and he judged that it would probably have reached hundreds of acres. On 12 September 2023 AFP reported that the operators had alerted Cal Fire Captain Kris Yeary and that his crews put the fire out at less than a quarter acre. Yeary told AFP that it could have become a devastating fire had the AI not alerted them. By September 2023 the tool reached all 21 Cal Fire dispatch centers. Cal Fire's figures for 2024 show that the cameras picked up 1,668 of 7,553 wildfires in its jurisdiction, 636 of them before any 911 call.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The model inspected live camera feeds for smoke and flagged the column near Mount Laguna at about 3 a.m.

Workflow

A camera network with machine-learned smoke detection watching remote forest at night, when human spotting is weakest. A verification step by trained operators that turns a model flag into a confirmed alert to the local Cal Fire station. A fire still small at detection, with a crew close enough to reach it before it grows.

Human oversight

Operators checked the smoke alert before notifying Cal Fire. Captain Yeary's crews then contained the fire below a quarter acre.

AI contribution

ALERTCalifornia flagged smoke in a camera feed at about 3 a.m. Operators verified the detection and notified Cal Fire, whose crews contained the fire.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The detection must precede any human report for the time gained to be attributable to the model. For this fire no source documents the absence of a 911 call. Human verification must be fast enough that the alert still arrives in the incipient phase. False positives from dust, insects or weather must be screened out so that crews keep trusting the alerts.
