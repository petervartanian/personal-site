ALERTCalifornia AI flags smoke; fire held under a quarter acre

AI on Cleveland National Forest cameras flagged smoke at about 3 am on 5 August 2023; Cal Fire held the verified fire under a quarter acre.

ⓘ

What happened

At about 3 am on 5 August 2023, the ALERTCalifornia AI flagged a column of smoke in the Cleveland National Forest near Mount Laguna. That is about 40 miles east of San Diego. The AI, a machine-learned model run by UC San Diego, watches live feeds from more than 1,000 cameras. Human operators verified the detection and alerted Cal Fire Captain Kris Yeary. His crews put it out at less than a quarter acre. Yeary told AFP that it could have become a devastating fire had the AI not alerted them. The AI watch over the feeds began at the end of June 2023 under a Cal Fire trial. By September 2023 the tool reached all 21 Cal Fire dispatch centers.

Purpose. The tool was built to spot smoke in ALERTCalifornia camera feeds and notify Cal Fire emergency command centers. Crews can then reach a fire in its incipient phase. This detection was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The model's contribution was the detection. Its algorithm flagged a smoke column in a Cleveland National Forest camera feed at about 3 am. Human operators verified it before Cal Fire was alerted. The containment was done by Captain Yeary's crews. No source records whether a 911 call would have come later. The time gained is documented only for the program as a whole. TIME reports 77 fires identified before any 911 call in the first two months. UC San Diego's Neal Driscoll says the alerts beat 911 calls about 40 percent of the time. AFP also notes the system sometimes misreads dust, insects or weather as smoke. That is why the human verification step sits between the model and the dispatch.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    Cal Fire's Mount Laguna station received a verified alert at about 3 am. Its crews extinguished the fire at under a quarter acre. Cal Fire intelligence specialist Suzann Leininger says the system “is getting us time to react in a faster manner”.

  • society

    Residents, property and forest around Mount Laguna were spared a larger fire. Captain Yeary's statement that it “could have been a devastating fire” is a judgment, not a proven counterfactual.

Further evidence

Setting. A camera network with machine-learned smoke detection watching remote forest at night, when human spotting is weakest. A verification step by trained operators that turns a model flag into a confirmed alert to the local Cal Fire station. A fire still small at detection, with a crew close enough to reach it before it grows.

Conditions. The detection must precede any human report for the time gained to be attributable to the model. For this fire no source documents the absence of a 911 call. Human verification must be fast enough that the alert still arrives in the incipient phase. False positives from dust, insects or weather must be screened out so that crews keep trusting the alerts.
