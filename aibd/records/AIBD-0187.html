Brief from Peter

The Department of Justice reported on 30 June 2025 that its analysts and their partners had detected anomalous Medicare billing in Operation Gold Rush. According to the department, the Office of Inspector General of the Department of Health and Human Services and the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services stopped all but about $41 million of about $4.45 billion scheduled for payment. The $4.41 billion went into escrow, and the Justice Department said that the health agencies intended to seek its return to the Medicare trust fund. Later Justice Department announcements do not say what finally became of that money.1 Medicare supplemental insurers still paid about $900 million. The Justice Department credits the detection to proactive data analytics, and in the same release it announced plans for an AI-enabled Data Fusion Center. Accountable care organizations had also reported the suspect billing for intermittent urinary catheters, according to the American Hospital Association, and in June 2024 the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services called the 2023 volume of that billing highly suspect.2 No source shows that AI flagged these claims or says what share of the hold the analytics produced.3

Details

AI system

Provider

The Department of Justice identifies its Health Care Fraud Unit Data Analytics Team and partners; it names no software supplier. [1]

Role

The analysts detected unusual billing. HHS-OIG and CMS then stopped most scheduled payments. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Analysts examined health care billing and identified anomalous patterns. The model inputs and alert format are not specified. [1]

Autonomy

HHS-OIG and CMS controlled payment suspensions. The sources do not describe an autonomous system that could withhold funds. [1]

Workflow

Analysts detected anomalous billing; investigators examined the scheme, and HHS-OIG and CMS withheld scheduled payments. [1]

Human oversight

Federal analysts, investigators, prosecutors, and payment administrators performed distinct roles. The sources do not describe a specific review protocol for software alerts. [1]

AI contribution

The Department of Justice credits proactive data analytics by its Data Analytics Team and partners, and it names no AI component in the detection. The American Hospital Association reports that accountable care organizations had reported the suspect billing. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services had called the 2023 catheter billing highly suspect in June 2024. No source measures what share of the hold the analytics produced. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
