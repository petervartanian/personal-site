DOJ data analytics flag a $10.6 billion catheter billing scheme

HHS-OIG and CMS prevented all but $41 million of $4.45 billion

DOJ's 2025 Health Care Fraud Takedown says its Data Analytics Team detected anomalous billing by a transnational organization and that HHS-OIG and CMS prevented all but $41 million of $4.45 billion...

What happened

The Department of Justice's press release of 30 June 2025 announcing the 2025 National Health Care Fraud Takedown (324 defendants, over $14.6 billion in intended loss) describes Operation Gold Rush, in which an organization allegedly used foreign straw owners to buy dozens of medical supply companies and submitted $10.6 billion in fraudulent Medicare claims for urinary catheters and other durable medical equipment using the stolen identities of over one million Americans. The release states that the Health Care Fraud Unit's Data Analytics Team and its partners detected the anomalous billing through proactive data analytics, that HHS-OIG and CMS prevented the organization from receiving all but approximately $41 million of the approximately $4.45 billion scheduled to be paid by Medicare, that HHS and CMS intend to return the $4.41 billion in escrow to the Medicare trust fund, and that Medicare supplemental insurers nonetheless paid approximately $900 million. Nineteen defendants were charged and twelve arrested, four of them in Estonia. The release says that the Data Analytics Team was established in 2018 and announces a Health Care Fraud Data Fusion Center to leverage cloud computing, artificial intelligence and advanced analytics; it does not say that AI or machine learning was used in the Gold Rush detection.

What it was built to do. Detect, investigate and prosecute complex health care fraud schemes.

The system's part

not assessed The detection is attributed to the Data Analytics Team's proactive data analytics, with no statement that AI or machine learning was involved; the Fusion Center that will use artificial intelligence is announced as future work. Developer and deployer of the analytics are the same public body (DOJ's Health Care Fraud Unit), acting with HHS-OIG and CMS.

Replication

What produced it

  • Centralised Medicare claims data in which rapid, anomalous billing for durable medical equipment could be detected before payment
  • Coordination that let CMS and HHS-OIG hold scheduled payments in escrow

What must hold again

  • Payment holds triggered before funds leave Medicare
  • Interagency data sharing and analytic capacity (the Fusion Center is meant to pool DOJ, HHS-OIG and FBI analysts)
  • Prosecutorial follow-through converting holds into charges and seizures
