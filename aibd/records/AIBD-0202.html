AI weighing lifts income per pig 18% on six Gyeonggi farms

In a 2025 provincial pilot, a system that weighs pigs by AI without contact cut shipping age on six Gyeonggi farms from 182.3 to 176.7 days and raised income per head 18%.

What happened

Gyeonggi-do Agricultural Research and Extension Services announced the results of its 2025 livestock AI pilot in a press release of 6 March 2026. The Fact reported the results that day with a Suwon dateline, and Joongboo Ilbo reported them on 8 March. Both newspaper stories restate the press release and add no reporting of their own. The figures therefore rest on the agency's own before-and-after comparison. The pilot is listed in the OECD AI Incidents Monitor. A system for selecting fattening pigs for shipping uses AI to measure their weight without contact and to advise the optimal shipping date. It was introduced on six farms in Anseong, Pocheon and Yeoju. Shipping age fell from 182.3 to 176.7 days, a drop of 5.6 days. The share shipped at grade 1+ rose from 35.3% to 37.7%, up 2.4 percentage points. Income per head rose from 100,000 to 118,000 won, up 18%. A sow farrowing management system that uses information and communication technology equipment for real-time monitoring was introduced on Pocheon farms. It cut the sow accident rate from 3.1% to 2.7%. Those accidents are mastitis, fever, loss of appetite and crushing of piglets. Piglets shipped per sow per year rose from 18.3 to 19.2, up 4.9%. Real-time alerts reduced the need for farmers to stay in the barn. Lee Jun-bae, head of the agency's technology dissemination division, said that the results confirmed AI as a way to raise productivity and income. The vendor of the systems is not named. The agency plans to extend the weighing system to Yeoncheon in 2026. No report of that rollout was found.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The AI system estimated pig weight without contact and recommended when to send the animals to market. The separate farrowing system monitored sow condition and issued alerts.

Workflow

A provincial extension pilot funded in 2025. Six commercial pig farms adopting non-contact AI weighing. A grading system that pays a premium for grade 1+ pigs.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The reported financial beneficiaries were the six farms using the shipping-selection system. The sow-monitoring results came from a separate Pocheon deployment.

Reach

Six pig farms in Anseong, Pocheon and Yeoju used the weighing system, and farms in Pocheon used the sow system. The sources give no count of farmers or animals. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The reported gains came from the 2025 pilot and were announced in March 2026. The account does not establish that they persisted across later production cycles.

Measurement

Measurement

The agency's comparison of farm figures before and after the systems were introduced; the method, period and data are not published. [4]

Setting and sample

Six pig farms in Anseong, Pocheon and Yeoju (weighing system); farms in Pocheon (sow system), 2025. [4]

Measures

Shipping age, share shipped at grade 1+, income per head; sow accident rate, piglets shipped per sow per year. [4]

Results

Shipping age 182.3 to 176.7 days; grade 1+ share 35.3% to 37.7%; income per head 100,000 to 118,000 won; sow accidents 3.1% to 2.7%; piglets per sow per year 18.3 to 19.2. [4]

Limitations

No control farms, no statistical test and no published data. The agency that ran the pilot reports the results, and the vendor is not named. [4]
