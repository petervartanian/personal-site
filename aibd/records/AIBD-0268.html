Mayo authors: AI-ECG flags low EF, 41-year-old recovers to 55%

depth 3 for a person

at four months his ejection fraction had improved to 55%

At Mayo Clinic two ECGs scored 97.88% and 98.67% for reduced ejection fraction in a 41-year-old with no cardiac history; echo confirmed HFrEF, therapy followed, EF 55% at 4 months. Developer-authored.

What happened

Harmon, Witt, Friedman and Attia (Cardiovascular Digital Health Journal, 24 August 2021) report a 41-year-old man with class III obesity and sleep apnoea and no cardiac history whose two ECGs, run through Mayo's AI-ECG dashboard, gave probabilities of reduced ejection fraction of 97.88% and 98.67%; echocardiography the next day confirmed reduced function, guideline-directed therapy for new HFrEF was started, and at four months his ejection fraction had improved to 55%, which the authors attribute to therapy, recovery from post-viral sequelae and weight loss. All four authors are at Mayo Clinic, which developed the algorithm and licensed it to Eko and Anumana; Friedman, Attia and other inventors may receive financial benefit. The same developer group published further cases: Dhawan et al. (PACE, 2024) on AI-ECGs predicting left-bundle-branch-block cardiomyopathy six years before presentation, Kenyon et al. (J Pers Med, 2024) on AI-ECG prompting an early AL amyloidosis diagnosis, and Liu et al. (HeartRhythm Case Reports, 2025) on defibrillator decision-making. No source outside Mayo was found for these cases, which is why the earlier verifier set them aside. The date is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Screen 12-lead ECGs for a low left-ventricular ejection fraction with a convolutional neural network

The system's part

The AI-ECG probabilities (97.88% and 98.67%) prompted the echocardiogram and treatment, according to the authors. The source is the developer's own case report; the authors disclose that Mayo licensed the technology and that inventors may receive financial benefit. No independent account of the case exists.

Replication

What produced it. Routine 12-lead ECGs scored by the low-EF network. A dashboard that clinicians consult during evaluation. Confirmatory echocardiography and guideline-directed therapy.

What must hold again. The algorithm is deployed in the clinical workflow. Clinicians act on a high probability score.
