What happened

Harmon, Witt, Friedman and Attia (Cardiovascular Digital Health Journal, 24 August 2021) report a 41-year-old man with class III obesity, sleep apnea, no cardiac history. Two electrocardiograms (ECGs) on Mayo's AI-ECG dashboard gave reduced-ejection-fraction probabilities of 97.88% and 98.67%. Echocardiography next day confirmed reduced function. Guideline-directed therapy for new heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) began. At four months his ejection fraction had risen to 55%, attributed by the authors to therapy, recovery from post-viral sequelae and weight loss. All four authors are at Mayo Clinic, which developed the algorithm and licensed it to Eko and Anumana. Friedman, Attia and other inventors may receive financial benefit. The same group published further cases. Dhawan et al.

(PACE, 2024): AI-ECGs predicting left-bundle-branch-block cardiomyopathy six years before presentation. Kenyon et al. (J Pers Med, 2024): AI-ECG prompting an early AL amyloidosis diagnosis. Liu et al. (HeartRhythm Case Reports, 2025): defibrillator decision-making. No source outside Mayo was found for these cases.

What it was built to do. Screen 12-lead ECGs for a low left-ventricular ejection fraction with a convolutional neural network

The system's part

The AI-ECG probabilities (97.88% and 98.67%) prompted the echocardiogram and treatment, according to the authors. The source is the developer's own case report. The authors disclose that Mayo licensed the technology and that inventors may receive financial benefit. No independent account of the case exists.

Replication

What produced it. Routine 12-lead ECGs scored by the low-EF network. A dashboard that clinicians consult during evaluation. Confirmatory echocardiography and guideline-directed therapy.

What must hold again. The algorithm is deployed in the clinical workflow. Clinicians act on a high probability score.
