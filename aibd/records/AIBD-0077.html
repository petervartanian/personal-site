What happened

A male infant was delivered at an outside hospital at 34+2 weeks and had bilateral kidney enlargement with innumerable tiny cysts consistent with autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease; he was cared for in the Mount Sinai neonatal intensive care unit, New York, and died at two days of life. Whole-genome sequencing found a known paternal pathogenic missense variant, and SpliceAI scores in the NICUnet Rareservoir identified a maternally inherited deep intronic PKHD1 variant scoring 0.98, which RNA studies on maternal urine-derived renal epithelial cells confirmed creates a novel exon with a premature stop codon; GeneDx classified it likely pathogenic. After genetic counseling the parents proceeded with IVF and preimplantation genetic testing for monogenic disorders within one year of the death.

What it was built to do. SpliceAI is a deep-learning model built to predict the effect of DNA variants on RNA splicing. Nominating a splice-altering deep intronic variant is exactly its intended use.

The system's part

SpliceAI's score of 0.98 nominated the maternally inherited deep intronic variant as the second allele. Among 78,575 rare intronic PKHD1 variants in gnomAD only one scored higher. Pathogenicity was then established by RT-PCR and Sanger sequencing of maternal urine-derived cells and GeneDx's likely-pathogenic classification. The AI's contribution was candidate selection. Without it the second allele would have remained unexplained. Authors declare no relevant financial interests.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Whole-genome sequencing with SpliceAI scoring of rare intronic variants in a disease gene already implicated by one pathogenic allele. Access to parental samples for RNA-level validation.

What must hold again. Functional or RNA confirmation before clinical use of the variant. Genetic counseling and access to IVF with PGT-M.
