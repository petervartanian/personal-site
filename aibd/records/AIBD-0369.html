Rekor plate-pattern analysis leads to a Scarsdale drug stop

depth 2 for society

Westchester County PD's Rekor system flagged David Zayas's driving pattern across 480 cameras; a March 2022 stop in Scarsdale found crack cocaine, cash and a pistol; he was convicted (Carscoops).

What happened

Carscoops reports that the Westchester County Police Department, New York, used technology provided by Rekor to examine the driving patterns of anyone passing its 480 license-plate-reader cameras over a two-year period, and determined that David Zayas had made several trips along routes typically used by drug traffickers and turned around quickly to go home. In March 2022, while Zayas was driving a gray Chevrolet in Scarsdale, officers pulled him over, searched the car and found crack cocaine, cash and a pistol; the case ended in his conviction (Carscoops, 18 July 2023, drawing on Forbes). The Star's AFP Relaxnews item adds that police took an interest in a vehicle registered to Zayas in March 2022.

The OECD Monitor summary says the system analyzed billions of plate records and lists rights and public-interest harms; Carscoops quotes ACLU lawyer Brett Max Kaufman and defense attorney Ben Gold criticizing the surveillance. Only the month is given, so 1 March 2022 is used.

What it was built to do. Carscoops describes Rekor's Public Safety Network as aggregating vehicle location data from its customers so police can examine driving patterns; flagging a pattern associated with drug trafficking for a stop is that function, not a side effect.

The system's part

Per Carscoops, the Rekor-supplied analysis over 480 cameras and two years of reads produced the ground for the stop: several trips along routes typically used by drug traffickers with quick turnarounds. Everything after the flag was police work, and the conviction rested on what the search found. The original Forbes report, which cited court filings, returned 403; the fetched pages give no accuracy figure, no count of other drivers flagged, and no court ruling on the stop. Carscoops quotes Ben Gold calling avoidance of the system unfeasible and Brett Max Kaufman calling the scale of the surveillance massive.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A county network of 480 license-plate-reader cameras feeding Rekor's aggregated vehicle-location data. Pattern analysis over two years of reads that flags routes and quick turnarounds associated with drug trafficking. A traffic stop and vehicle search by officers acting on the flag.

What must hold again. The flagged pattern must be a lawful basis for a stop and search, which defense counsel contested. Officers must corroborate the pattern with evidence found at the stop. Retention and analysis of plate data over years must be permitted.
