Rekor software flags a driver's trips; police find drugs in Scarsdale

Rekor Scout software flagged David Zayas's trips past Westchester County's 480 plate-reader cameras; officers who stopped him in Scarsdale on 10 March 2022 found crack cocaine, a pistol and cash.

  • 2 society

    The stop removed 112 grams of crack cocaine and a semiautomatic pistol from a driver who, per a prosecutor filing that Forbes cites, had made nine trafficking-pattern trips. He pleaded guilty, received 48 months and agreed to a $34,000 forfeiture, per the court docket. The sentence was later commuted, and no source measures any effect on drug supply or violence.

The system’s part
Per the defense motion, Westchester's Real Time Crime Center ran an Interdiction Analysis report in Rekor Scout over more than 1.6 billion plate records. Per a prosecutor filing cited by Forbes, it showed nine trips from Massachusetts between October 2020 and August 2021 on known trafficking routes with short stays. That flag opened the investigation and marked the car for interdiction. Officers then made the stop, questioned him and used a dog before the search, and the conviction rested on what they found. No source gives an accuracy figure or a count of other drivers flagged, and the court never decided the March 2023 motion that challenged the search of plate data.

What happened

Carscoops reports that the Westchester County Police Department, New York, used technology provided by Rekor. It examined the driving patterns of anyone passing its 480 license-plate-reader cameras over a two-year period. It determined that David Zayas had made several trips along routes typically used by drug traffickers and turned around quickly to go home. The Star's AFP Relaxnews item adds that police took an interest in a vehicle registered to Zayas in March 2022. On 10 March 2022 Zayas was driving a gray Chevrolet in Scarsdale. Officers pulled him over and searched the car. Carscoops reports that they found crack cocaine, cash and a pistol. Forbes reported that the search found 112 grams of crack cocaine, a semiautomatic pistol and $34,000 in cash. On 10 March 2023 the defense filed a motion to suppress the evidence from the location-data searches. The court never decided it. The federal docket shows that Zayas pleaded guilty on 28 June 2023. Forbes reported the case on 17 July 2023, and Carscoops followed on 18 July 2023, drawing on Forbes. Carscoops quotes ACLU lawyer Brett Max Kaufman and defense attorney Ben Gold, who criticized the surveillance. The OECD Monitor summary says that the system analyzed billions of plate records, and it lists harms to rights and to the public interest. On 24 January 2024 Zayas received 48 months, with a $34,000 forfeiture. A presidential commutation filed on 17 January 2025 ended his prison term on 16 May 2025. The conviction and three years of supervised release stand.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

Rekor Scout (Interdiction Analysis report)

Inputs and outputs

Police queried stored license-plate observations for travel patterns and then placed an alert on Zayas’s vehicle. [1] [2]

Autonomy

The analysis prompted police attention, while officers made the stop and search that produced the physical evidence.

Workflow

A county network of 480 license-plate-reader cameras feeding Rekor's aggregated vehicle-location data. Pattern analysis over two years of reads that flags routes and quick turnarounds associated with drug trafficking. A traffic stop and vehicle search by officers acting on the flag.

Human oversight

The defense motion states that Westchester's Real Time Crime Center ran the Interdiction Analysis report in Rekor Scout. Officers then stopped the car, questioned the driver and used a dog before they searched it. The defense says that the search of plate data ran without judicial supervision. The court never decided that motion. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The flagged pattern must be a lawful basis for a stop and search, which defense counsel contested. Officers must corroborate the pattern with evidence found at the stop. Retention and analysis of plate data over years must be permitted.
