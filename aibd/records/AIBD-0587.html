AI drilling planner helps KoBold appraise a copper deposit in Zambia

KoBold Metals used the Intelligent Prospector drilling planner to help appraise the high-grade zone of the Mingomba copper deposit in about eight months; no source isolates the effect of AI.

What happened

Jef Caers of Stanford University co-developed the Intelligent Prospector drilling planner. His 2023 paper with a KoBold author tested the planner only on synthetic problems. In May 2024, Caers wrote that the AI-planned campaign at Mingomba appraised the high-grade zone in about eight months. KoBold Africa chief executive Mfikeyi Makayi described the work at Mingomba in a first-person account in PwC’s October 2024 Zambia mining report. She reported that data science helped the team target areas with substantial copper grade and thickness. The work combined historical exploration, drilling, geoscience, and computing. She also described decades of exploration before KoBold entered the project. The sources therefore do not support a claim that AI first discovered the deposit. In April 2025, the International Energy Agency characterized the work as AI-assisted resource assessment. None of these accounts provides a controlled comparison or a quantified saving that is specific to AI. By March 2026 the project had moved into development, and Zambia's president launched shaft sinking on 29 April 2026.

Details

AI system

Role

The reported AI helped geologists select boreholes that would distinguish competing geological explanations. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Workflow

Historical information, new drilling, data science, and geologists’ decisions were combined.

Human oversight

The sources describe a workflow in which geologists made the decisions, using historical data, new drilling and the planner’s models. No source describes specific safeguards. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Benefits

Reach

The recorded benefit goes to the two organizations that carried out the work, KoBold Metals and Mingomba Mining Limited. No source counts other beneficiaries. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Duration

The project moved into development by March 2026, with first copper set for the early 2030s. Shaft sinking began on 29 April 2026. Any lasting mining benefit still depends on that development. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Measurement

Measurement

Monte Carlo trials of the sequential planner and of baseline drilling patterns on synthetic ore bodies. [5]

Setting and sample

Synthetic two-dimensional exploration problems with one or two ore bodies; no field data. [5]

Measures

Error in estimated ore volume and remaining uncertainty after each measurement. [5]

Results

The authors report that the sequential planner reduced uncertainty more than the conventional patterns. [5]

Limitations

One author works for KoBold. The tests are synthetic and do not show the size of any gain at Mingomba. [5]

Materials for replication

The authors deposited code on Zenodo (doi 10.5281/zenodo.6727378). [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The reviewed sources do not establish causal conditions or an effect size for reproducing the benefit.

Evidence

Original evidence

Caers co-developed the planner, and Makayi leads KoBold Africa, so both firsthand accounts come from people tied to the project. The 2023 paper and the project page also come from the makers. The International Energy Agency and African Mining Market have no stake in the project. The shaft-sinking report says nothing about the role of AI. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]
