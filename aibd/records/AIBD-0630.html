What happened

DermaSensor of Miami makes a handheld device that shines light on a skin lesion and uses a machine learning algorithm to classify the reflected spectrum. In the DERM-SUCCESS pivotal study, 30 primary care physicians at 22 sites in the United States and Australia used it on 1,579 lesions in 1,005 patients. FDA reviewers report a device sensitivity of 95.5% for melanoma, basal cell carcinoma and squamous cell carcinoma, against 83.0% for the physicians. Device specificity was 20.7%, against 54.2% for the physicians. In a companion reader study, 108 primary care physicians reviewed 50 lesion images with and without the device output. Their sensitivity rose from 82.0% to 91.4%, and their specificity fell from 44.2% to 32.4%. FDA granted the De Novo request on 12 January 2024. The reviewers note that the device will raise referrals of benign lesions and that patients with darker skin had limited representation.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Physicians applied the device to lesions they already found suspicious. Physicians used the output as a second read after their own assessment.

Measurement

Measurement

Study 1: Multi-reader study; each lesion image viewed with and without device output in random order; refer or do not refer decision. Study 2: Prospective blinded standalone accuracy study against biopsy, with physician assessment on the same lesions. [1]

Setting and sample

Study 1: 108 internal medicine or family practice physicians; 25 malignant and 25 benign lesions. Study 2: 1,005 patients, 1,579 lesions, 22 sites in the US and Australia. [1]

Measures

Study 1: Referral sensitivity and specificity. Study 2: Sensitivity and specificity. [1]

Results

Study 1: Sensitivity 82.0% to 91.4%; specificity 44.2% to 32.4%. Study 2: Sensitivity 95.5% against 83.0%; specificity 20.7% against 54.2%. [1]

Additional value from AI

Study 1: AUROC 0.708 to 0.762. Study 2: Not a with-and-without comparison. [1]

Limitations

Study 1: Image-based reads; lower specificity; limited data for Fitzpatrick IV to VI. Study 2: Low specificity; under-40 sensitivity below 90%. [1]

Denominators

Study 1: Two reads per lesion. Study 2: One device reading per lesion. [1]

Materials for replication

Study 1: None public. Study 2: None public. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The patient is aged 40 or older. A dermatologist makes the final management decision after referral.
