AlphaTensor cuts multiplications for 4x4 binary matrices from 49 to 47

DeepMind's AlphaTensor found ways to multiply 4x4 matrices over the field with two elements in 47 multiplications and 5x5 matrices in 96; two mathematicians then cut the 5x5 count to 95.

  • 2 society

    Researchers in algebraic complexity gained new explicit decompositions, and Kauers and Moosbauer improved the 5x5 one within a week. The gains hold only over particular fields. Nature reports the 4x4 improvement for matrices “in a finite field”, and Kauers and Moosbauer give the 5x5 record of 96 multiplications over the field with two elements.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The reinforcement-learning agent found the 47-multiplication 4x4 scheme and the 96-multiplication 5x5 scheme. Kauers and Moosbauer report that their flip-graph search, which uses no learning, reached only 97 for 5x5 from the standard algorithm and reached 95 when it started from AlphaTensor's scheme. The same search found a 4x4 by 4x5 scheme of rank 60 from the standard algorithm, valid modulo 2, where AlphaTensor had found 63.
Limits
The improvements apply to specific matrix sizes over the field with two elements. They do not by themselves establish savings in computing time. Kauers and Moosbauer found more than 100,000 further 4x4 schemes with 47 multiplications by a search that uses no learning, and none of them could be lifted to the integers modulo 4.

What happened

On 5 October 2022, Nature published AlphaTensor, a reinforcement-learning system from DeepMind (Fawzi, Balog, Huang, Hubert, Romera-Paredes and others). The system treats the search for matrix multiplication algorithms as a one-player game called TensorGame. A deep neural network guides a planning procedure derived from AlphaZero. AlphaTensor found an algorithm multiplying two 4x4 matrices over the field with two elements in 47 multiplications. That beat Strassen's two-level algorithm, which needs 49, for the first time in about 50 years. AlphaTensor also found a 96-multiplication scheme for 5x5 matrices over that field. On 8 October 2022, Manuel Kauers and Jakob Moosbauer of Johannes Kepler University Linz posted a preprint, which they revised on 13 October. They started from the published 5x5 scheme and improved it to 95 multiplications. On 2 December 2022 they posted a further preprint. Its peer-reviewed version appeared on 24 July 2023 in the proceedings of the International Symposium on Symbolic and Algebraic Computation. With a search method that uses no learning, they found more than 100,000 further 4x4 schemes with 47 multiplications over the field with two elements. None of these schemes could be lifted to the integers modulo 4. Their search reached 95 multiplications for 5x5 matrices only when it started from AlphaTensor's scheme. From the standard algorithm it reached 97. In February 2025, Moosbauer and Poole reported 93 multiplications for 5x5 matrices over any field, below AlphaTensor's 96.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

AlphaTensor searched tensor decompositions representing matrix-multiplication algorithms and returned explicit multiplication schemes.

Workflow

Formulation of algorithm discovery as a tensor-decomposition game. Neural-network-guided search with exact verification of each decomposition. Publication of explicit decompositions that others can check and extend.

Human oversight

The resulting schemes were checked algebraically over the stated field. Independent researchers then used the published 5-by-5 scheme as a starting point for an improvement.

Benefits

Reach

The sources give no count of people who benefit. The benefit is a set of new explicit decompositions for researchers in algebraic complexity. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

On 8 October 2022, three days after publication, Kauers and Moosbauer cut the 5x5 count from AlphaTensor's 96 to 95. In their peer-reviewed paper of 24 July 2023, they reported more than 100,000 further 4x4 schemes with 47 multiplications. In February 2025, Moosbauer and Poole reported 93 multiplications for 5x5 matrices over any field. [1] [2] [3]
