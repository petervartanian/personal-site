What happened

On 5 October 2022 Nature published AlphaTensor, a reinforcement-learning system from DeepMind (Fawzi, Balog, Huang, Hubert, Romera-Paredes and others) that treats the search for matrix multiplication algorithms as a single-player game, TensorGame, in which a deep neural network guides a planning procedure derived from AlphaZero. It found an algorithm multiplying two 4x4 matrices over the field with two elements in 47 multiplications, beating Strassen's two-level algorithm for the first time in about 50 years, and a 96-multiplication scheme for 5x5 matrices over the same field. On 8 October 2022 Manuel Kauers and Jakob Moosbauer posted a preprint, revised on 13 October, that took the published 5x5 scheme as its starting point and improved it to 95 multiplications.

What it was built to do. Built to discover matrix multiplication algorithms by searching for low-rank tensor decompositions; the new schemes were that purpose.

The system's part

The reinforcement-learning agent found the decompositions, which are explicit and checkable; the independent preprint treats the 96-multiplication 5x5 result as established and improves it, which corroborates the result's existence. The 47-multiplication 4x4 scheme rests on the vendor paper's published decomposition.

Replication

What produced it. Formulation of algorithm discovery as a tensor-decomposition game. Neural-network-guided search with exact verification of each decomposition. Publication of explicit decompositions that others can check and extend.

What must hold again. Decompositions must be verified exactly over the stated ring or field. Independent reproduction or extension by researchers outside the vendor.
