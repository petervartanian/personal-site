Garmin Autoland lands depressurized King Air B200; pilots unhurt

Garmin Autoland engaged when a King Air B200 lost cabin pressure on 20 December 2025 and landed it at Rocky Mountain Metropolitan Airport, with the two pilots unhurt.

  • 2 a person

    Two pilots who had lost cabin pressure at 23,000 feet were on the ground in Broomfield within about 35 minutes of departure, untreated and uninjured. They were conscious and could have flown the descent themselves. The benefit is a safe automatic recovery, and no source documents a life saved.

  • 1 an organization

    Buffalo River Aviation's aircraft “landed safely without incident”, in the airport spokeswoman's words. No damage is reported, and the operator said that the aircraft returned to service the next day.

The system’s part
Autoland engaged itself when cabin altitude exceeded the limit. It declared the emergency to controllers with automated “pilot incapacitation” broadcasts. It flew the descent, approach and landing. The pilots elected to leave it engaged rather than take over. So the landing is the system's work. But the counterfactual, that they could not have landed, is not established. Garmin, the FAA and the operator all call it an automated system. None calls it AI. It is here because the OECD Monitor lists it as an AI system. The Register describes it choosing landing sites from runway length, distance and fuel range.
Limits
The safe landing does not establish that the pilots were incapacitated or that a manual landing would have failed. No finding by the Federal Aviation Administration or the National Transportation Safety Board on the flight had been reported by 14 October 2026, and the safety board's case database lists no case for the aircraft. The automated landing ended a pressurization emergency without injury. It does not show that a manual landing would have failed. AvBrief reported that with Autoland engaged the crew could transmit only on the guard frequency and could not switch to the controllers' frequencies.

What happened

On Saturday 20 December 2025 Buffalo River Aviation's Beechcraft Super King Air B200, N479BR, left Aspen-Pitkin County Airport at 1:43 p.m. It carried two pilots and no passengers. At about 23,000 feet it suffered a rapid, uncommanded loss of pressurization. When cabin altitude passed the safe limit, Garmin Autoland engaged automatically. It sent automated emergency broadcasts to air traffic control. The crew chose to leave it engaged. It flew the aircraft to a landing on Runway 30 at Rocky Mountain Metropolitan Airport, Broomfield, Colorado, shortly after 2 p.m. Nobody was treated. The operator said that the aircraft returned to service the next day. The Federal Aviation Administration said that it was investigating. The National Transportation Safety Board said that it was gathering information. Garmin called it the first use of Autoland from start to finish in an actual emergency. On 27 December 2025 AvBrief reported audio that Max Trescott, a host of the NTSB NewsTalk podcast, recorded while flying nearby. The audio shows that with Autoland engaged the crew could transmit only on the guard frequency and could not switch to the controllers' frequencies. The case database of the National Transportation Safety Board lists no case for the aircraft. On 14 October 2026 Garmin reported that more than 2,500 aircraft carry the system. In 2026 Autoland was certified on four more models, among them the first twin-engine jets. No second real-world activation and no finding by either agency on the Colorado flight had been reported by then.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

Autoland activated after cabin pressure became unsafe and flew the diversion, approach, and landing. The pilots retained the option to take over and deliberately left it engaged.

Workflow

An aircraft fitted with Garmin Autoland. Garmin says more than 1,700 are equipped. A rapid, uncommanded loss of pressurization that pushed cabin altitude past the system's automatic trigger. A crew that decided to let the automatic sequence run to touchdown rather than take over.

Human oversight

The two pilots, both on oxygen, chose to leave Autoland engaged until touchdown. AvBrief reported that with Autoland engaged the crew could not switch from the guard frequency to the controllers' frequencies. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Benefits

Duration

The landing was a single event on 20 December 2025, and the operator said that the aircraft returned to service the next day. On 14 October 2026 Garmin reported that more than 2,500 aircraft carry Autoland, and no second real-world activation had been reported by then. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

People and places

Dependencies

The aircraft needed an installed and functioning Autoland system, usable flight controls, adequate fuel, and a suitable airport approach.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The aircraft remains airworthy. Autoland is a fail-safe for a working aircraft whose pilot cannot fly it, in the words of the journalist CBS quoted. A suitable airport is in range. The Register describes selection by runway length, distance and fuel range. Controllers and ground services act on the automated broadcasts, as Broomfield's two fire departments did.

Entry details

Sector

Aviation safety / automatic emergency landing. Charter turboprop operations.

Purpose

Autoland exists to take complete control of an aircraft and land it when the pilot cannot fly. It engages on its own when cabin altitude exceeds a set level. That is what happened here. The two pilots turned out not to be incapacitated, though, and chose to let it finish.
