Garmin Autoland lands depressurised King Air B200; pilots unhurt

depth 2 for a person, depth 1 for an organization

Independently reported, 6 sources.

On 20 December 2025 Garmin Autoland flew a depressurised King Air B200 to a landing at Rocky Mountain Metropolitan Airport; two pilots unhurt.

What happened

On Saturday 20 December 2025 Buffalo River Aviation's Beechcraft Super King Air B200, N479BR, left Aspen-Pitkin County Airport at 1:43 p.m. with two pilots and no passengers. At about 23,000 ft it suffered a rapid, uncommanded loss of pressurization; when cabin altitude passed the safe limit, Garmin Autoland engaged automatically, sent automated emergency broadcasts to air traffic control and flew the aircraft to a landing on Runway 30 at Rocky Mountain Metropolitan Airport, Broomfield, Colorado, shortly after 2 p.m. The crew chose to leave it engaged. Nobody was treated; the FAA said it was investigating and the NTSB that it was gathering information. Garmin called it the first use of Autoland from start to finish in an actual emergency.

What it was built to do. Autoland exists to take complete control of an aircraft and land it when the pilot cannot fly, engaging on its own when cabin altitude exceeds a set level; that is what happened here, although the two pilots turned out not to be incapacitated and chose to let it finish.

The system's part

Autoland engaged itself when cabin altitude exceeded the limit, declared the emergency to controllers with automated “pilot incapacitation” broadcasts, and flew the descent, approach and landing; the pilots elected to leave it engaged rather than take over, so the landing is the system's work but the counterfactual, that they could not have landed, is not established. Garmin, the FAA and the operator all call it an automated system; none calls it AI. It is here because the OECD Monitor lists it as an AI system, and The Register describes it choosing landing sites from runway length, distance and fuel range.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. An aircraft fitted with Garmin Autoland; Garmin says more than 1,700 are equipped. A rapid, uncommanded loss of pressurization that pushed cabin altitude past the system's automatic trigger. A crew that decided to let the automatic sequence run to touchdown rather than take over.

What must hold again. The aircraft remains airworthy: Autoland is a fail-safe for a working aircraft whose pilot cannot fly it, in the words of the journalist CBS quoted. A suitable airport is in range; The Register describes selection by runway length, distance and fuel range. Controllers and ground services act on the automated broadcasts, as Broomfield's two fire departments did.
