Garmin Autoland lands depressurised King Air B200; pilots unhurt

On 20 December 2025 Garmin Autoland flew a depressurised King Air B200 to a landing at Rocky Mountain Metropolitan Airport; two pilots unhurt.

ⓘ

What happened

On Saturday 20 December 2025 Buffalo River Aviation's Beechcraft Super King Air B200, N479BR, left Aspen-Pitkin County Airport at 1:43 p.m. It carried two pilots and no passengers. At about 23,000 ft it suffered a rapid, uncommanded loss of pressurization. When cabin altitude passed the safe limit, Garmin Autoland engaged automatically. It sent automated emergency broadcasts to air traffic control. It flew the aircraft to a landing on Runway 30 at Rocky Mountain Metropolitan Airport, Broomfield, Colorado, shortly after 2 p.m. The crew chose to leave it engaged. Nobody was treated. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) said it was investigating and the NTSB that it was gathering information. Garmin called it the first use of Autoland from start to finish in an actual emergency.

Purpose. Autoland exists to take complete control of an aircraft and land it when the pilot cannot fly. It engages on its own when cabin altitude exceeds a set level. That is what happened here. The two pilots turned out not to be incapacitated, though, and chose to let it finish.

AI’s contribution

Autoland engaged itself when cabin altitude exceeded the limit. It declared the emergency to controllers with automated “pilot incapacitation” broadcasts. It flew the descent, approach and landing. The pilots elected to leave it engaged rather than take over. So the landing is the system's work. But the counterfactual, that they could not have landed, is not established. Garmin, the FAA and the operator all call it an automated system. None calls it AI. It is here because the OECD Monitor lists it as an AI system. The Register describes it choosing landing sites from runway length, distance and fuel range.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Two pilots who had lost cabin pressure at 23,000 ft were on the ground in Broomfield within about 35 minutes of departure, untreated and uninjured. They were conscious and could have flown the descent themselves. So the benefit is a safe automatic recovery, not a documented life saved.

  • an organization

    Buffalo River Aviation's aircraft “landed safely without incident”, in the airport spokeswoman's words. No damage is reported, and the FAA investigation was still open.

Further evidence

Setting. An aircraft fitted with Garmin Autoland. Garmin says more than 1,700 are equipped. A rapid, uncommanded loss of pressurization that pushed cabin altitude past the system's automatic trigger. A crew that decided to let the automatic sequence run to touchdown rather than take over.

Conditions. The aircraft remains airworthy. Autoland is a fail-safe for a working aircraft whose pilot cannot fly it, in the words of the journalist CBS quoted. A suitable airport is in range. The Register describes selection by runway length, distance and fuel range. Controllers and ground services act on the automated broadcasts, as Broomfield's two fire departments did.
