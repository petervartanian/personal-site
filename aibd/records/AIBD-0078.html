What happened

A 47-year-old man presented to the transferring hospital of the authors' large urban healthcare system (the paper names neither the hospitals nor the city; all authors are at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, New York) with severe headache, nausea and vomiting but no speech or motor deficits. Viz ICH Plus (Viz.ai) analyzed his CT, revealing a substantial right parieto-occipital haematoma with ventricular extension, and automatically alerted the Neuroemergencies Management and Transfer (NEMAT) team at the receiving specialized stroke center. With an ICH score of 2 he was transferred within 101 minutes across 13.7 miles, against an average interhospital transfer time of 199.7 minutes, and underwent supratentorial craniotomy with endoscopic haematoma evacuation and ventriculoperitoneal shunt placement.

His scores were mRS 4 and NIHSS 6 at one month, 3 and 5 at three months, and mRS 1 and NIHSS 2 at one year.

What it was built to do. Viz ICH Plus is built to detect intracranial hemorrhage on non-contrast CT and automatically notify care and transfer teams; faster interhospital transfer is its intended function.

The system's part

The software detected the haematoma and auto-alerted the receiving team, which coordinated stabilization advice and transfer; a CT ordered for severe headache would have shown the bleed regardless, so the AI's contribution is the speed of notification and transfer. Dr Kellner discloses research grant support from Viz.AI among other companies; no author is a Viz.ai employee.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Viz ICH Plus running on CT scans at the transferring hospital with automatic notification to a centralized neuro-emergency transfer team. A command-center workflow that coordinates stabilization advice and ambulance transfer.

What must hold again. A receiving center able to operate promptly. Clinicians act on the automated alert rather than waiting for a formal radiology read.
