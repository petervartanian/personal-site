Viz ICH Plus flags a haematoma; transfer in 101 minutes, mRS 1

Viz ICH Plus flagged a 47-year-old's haematoma and alerted the transfer team; transfer in 101 min vs 199.7 average; mRS 1 at one year.

ⓘ

What happened

A 47-year-old man came to the transferring hospital of the authors' large urban health system. He had severe headache, nausea and vomiting but no speech or motor deficits. The paper names neither the hospitals nor the city. All authors are at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, New York. Viz ICH Plus (Viz.ai) analyzed his computed tomography (CT), revealing a substantial right parieto-occipital haematoma with ventricular extension. It auto-alerted the Neuroemergencies Management and Transfer (NEMAT) team at the receiving specialized stroke center. With an ICH score of 2 he was transferred within 101 minutes across 13.7 miles, against an average interhospital transfer time of 199.7 minutes. He had supratentorial craniotomy with endoscopic haematoma evacuation and ventriculoperitoneal shunt placement. His scores were mRS 4 and NIHSS 6 at one month, 3 and 5 at three months.

At one year: mRS 1 and NIHSS 2.

Purpose. Viz ICH Plus is built to detect intracranial hemorrhage on non-contrast CT and automatically notify care and transfer teams. Faster interhospital transfer is its intended function.

AI’s contribution

The software detected the haematoma and auto-alerted the receiving team, which coordinated stabilization advice and transfer. A CT ordered for severe headache would have shown the bleed regardless, so the AI's contribution is the speed of notification and transfer. Dr Kellner discloses research grant support from Viz.AI among other companies. No author is a Viz.ai employee.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    After a hemorrhage with ventricular extension the patient improved from mRS 4 at one month to mRS 1 at one year. The AI's share is the time saved in transfer, the surgery being the treatment.

  • an organization

    Interhospital transfer took 101 minutes against the system's 199.7-minute average, with the receiving team engaged before the patient left the first hospital.

Further evidence

Setting. Viz ICH Plus running on CT scans at the transferring hospital with automatic notification to a centralized neuro-emergency transfer team. A command-center workflow that coordinates stabilization advice and ambulance transfer.

Conditions. A receiving center able to operate promptly. Clinicians act on the automated alert rather than waiting for a formal radiology read.
