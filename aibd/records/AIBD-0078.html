Viz ICH Plus flags a brain bleed and a man is transferred in 101 minutes

Viz ICH Plus flagged a brain hemorrhage on a 47-year-old man's scan and alerted a transfer team, which moved him in 101 minutes against a 199.7-minute average.

  • 3 a person

    After a hemorrhage with ventricular extension, the patient improved from a modified Rankin Scale score of 4 at one month to 1 at one year. The software's share is the time saved in transfer, and surgery was the treatment.

  • 2 an organization

    Interhospital transfer took 101 minutes against the system's 199.7-minute average, and the receiving team was engaged before the patient left the first hospital.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The software detected the hematoma and alerted the receiving transfer team, which advised on stabilization and arranged the transfer. A CT ordered for severe headache would have shown the bleed in any case, so the AI's contribution is the speed of notification and transfer. The authors write that, without it, care would have waited for a radiologist's read and a chain of calls. The comparison is one patient against a historical average with no matched control. The case report declares no financial relationships for any author, while Dr Kellner discloses research grant support from Viz.AI in a 2025 paper on the same software.
Limits
The 101-minute transfer is compared with a different cohort’s average, not a matched counterfactual. Surgery and subsequent care also contributed to recovery. Three excerpts from one report do not establish that AI caused the one-year functional outcome.
Costs and harms
The authors do not measure costs or harms for this patient. They report that scanner compatibility and limited bandwidth for exporting scans caused operational problems for the software.
Later
Nothing later is known. The report ends with the one-year outcome. No later report on the patient or on transfer times under the program was found.

What happened

A 47-year-old man came to the transferring hospital of the authors' large urban health system. He had severe headache, nausea and vomiting but no speech or motor deficits. The paper names neither the hospitals nor the city. All authors are at the Icahn School of Medicine at Mount Sinai, New York. An institutional review board had approved a study named ADVANCE, which was evaluating the software's accuracy and usability in the health system at the time. Viz ICH Plus (Viz.ai) analyzed his computed tomography (CT) scan and revealed a substantial right parieto-occipital hematoma with ventricular extension. It automatically alerted the Neuroemergencies Management and Transfer team at the receiving specialized stroke center. With an intracerebral hemorrhage score of 2, he was transferred within 101 minutes across 13.7 miles, against an average interhospital transfer time of 199.7 minutes. The 199.7-minute comparator comes from a 2021 series of 89 transferred patients in a New York City hospital system, whose authors include two authors of the case report. Those patients traveled an average of 13.6 kilometers. He had supratentorial craniotomy with endoscopic evacuation of the hematoma and placement of a ventriculoperitoneal shunt. His modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score was 4 and his National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score was 6 at one month. At three months the scores were 3 and 5. At one year they were mRS 1 and NIHSS 2. Follow-up CT scans at six and 12 months showed no recurrent hemorrhage or infarct.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

Viz ICH Plus automatically notified the transfer team after detecting hemorrhage on the CT scan.

Workflow

Viz ICH Plus running on CT scans at the transferring hospital with automatic notification to a centralized neuro-emergency transfer team. A command-center workflow that coordinates stabilization advice and ambulance transfer.

Human oversight

The receiving team reviewed the scans, advised stabilization, and coordinated transfer before clinicians performed surgery.

Benefits

Reach

One patient, a 47-year-old man, is reported to have benefited from the faster transfer. No source reports transfer times for other patients under the program. [1]

Duration

Follow-up documented functional improvement through one year.

People and places

Dependencies

Different CT formats required configuration, and limited bandwidth sometimes delayed image transmission to Viz ICH Plus.
