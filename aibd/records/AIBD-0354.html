What happened

OCHA's Anticipatory Action Framework for Philippine typhoons (posted on ReliefWeb on 15 September 2021, the date used here) set up a US$7.5 million CERF pilot for about 270,500 people in Regions 5 and 8 with FAO, IOM, UNFPA, UNICEF and WFP, with a two-stage trigger: a readiness trigger on PAGASA and 510 forecasts of a category 4 or stronger cyclone, and an activation trigger within 72 hours of landfall based on the IFRC/Netherlands Red Cross 510 probabilistic typhoon impact prediction model's predicted number of totally damaged houses, which the framework describes as partially using a machine learning model relating wind speed to housing damage. 510's 2024 NHESS paper (vendor-authored) states the XGBoost model served in 2021 as the trigger model for the UN OCHA AA pilot.

OCHA's After Action Review of 31 March 2022 records that Typhoon Rai (Odette) in December 2021 rapidly intensified and did not meet the activation criteria, so there was a near activation with staff pre-deployed. The 2023 revision retained the 510 model, and the framework endorsed on 19 October 2025 (US$6 million CERF, about 955,507 people in Regions 5, 8 and 13) has OCHA and its Centre for Humanitarian Data compute predicted totally damaged buildings with the 510 model every 6–12 hours, plus an observational trigger for super typhoons of 185 km/h or more reported by PAGASA. OCHA's June 2025 report says the readiness trigger was met for Super Typhoon Pepito (Man-yi), within its November 2024 to April 2025 reporting period, but activation thresholds were not reached and CERF AA resources were reallocated to the response.

On 6 November 2025 CERF AA was activated for Super Typhoon Fung-Wong (Uwan) after the observational wind-speed threshold was triggered; OCHA reports nearly US$6 million released and WFP alone reaching 42,100 families. No read document records an activation produced by the 510 model's output.

What it was built to do. Forecast the number of totally damaged houses from typhoon track, wind and rainfall forecasts so that CERF-funded early actions can be triggered before landfall; the benefit claimed (AI-triggered anticipatory payouts) is that purpose, but no activation on the model's output is documented.

The system's part

OCHA's 2021, 2023 and 2025 frameworks name the IFRC/510 impact model as the activation-trigger model and the 2021 framework describes it as partially a machine learning model; this is documented by the deployer, not only the maker. The model's forecasts were monitored during the December 2021 near activation (Typhoon Rai) without meeting criteria, and the 2024 readiness phase for Pepito ended with CERF AA resources reallocated to response. The 6 November 2025 activation for Uwan is attributed by OCHA's flash update to the observational threshold (forecast peak 195 km/h against the 185 km/h trigger), with no statement that the 510 model's estimates reached the activation matrix. The AI's contribution to any payout is therefore not established; the maker's NHESS paper is the only source describing the algorithm (XGBoost).

Replication

What produced it. A pre-arranged CERF allocation with agency action plans ready before the typhoon season. ECMWF ensemble track, wind and rainfall forecasts fed to the 510 impact model every 6–12 hours. Historical house-damage data linking hazard, vulnerability and exposure to train the model. A threshold matrix of predicted totally damaged houses per region with a 1-in-3.5-year return period (2025).

What must hold again. Forecast lead time of at least 72 hours; rapid intensification (Rai 2021) shortened lead time below the trigger window. Model impact estimates reaching the regional threshold matrix, which no read activation record documents. Agency delivery capacity (financial service providers, data-sharing agreements) in the 72 hours before landfall. PAGASA and ECMWF data availability.
