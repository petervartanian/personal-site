Data mining grows to half of French tax controls, with efficiency gains

The Cour des comptes found that data mining, including machine learning in the French tax administration's CFVR program, came to originate half of tax controls and made choosing them more efficient.

  • 1 an organization

    The Cour des comptes states in December 2025 that efficiency gains in control programming are established. It gives no measured gain in results attributable to data mining, and it reports that these controls accounted for 13.8% of the tax and penalties recovered in 2023.

  • Not rated society

    The Cour des comptes attributes 13.8% of the tax and penalties recovered in 2023 to controls from mass data cross-matching. It judged that the method's efficacy still needs support. It reports no gain for the public from data mining.

What happened

The Cour des comptes published its report “La détection de la fraude fiscale des particuliers” on 15 November 2023. It says that nearly a third of tax controls now rest on risk analyses fed by mass data processing. A 2023–2027 plan targeted 50%. The report says that the efficacy of these analyses remains difficult to measure. It states that mass data cross-matching may assist controls and that it can never automatically characterize fraud. The full report identifies the CFVR program and explicitly describes machine learning. Its property-valuation example uses a random forest with rental value, location, floor area and room count to compare estimated values with declared sale prices. The report says that about 16% of files assessed by this model led to revised tax notices, totaling €49.3 million in assessed tax and penalties. The separate “foncier innovant” scheme analyzes aerial images. The report gives €14.61 billion in assessed taxes and penalties in 2022, with about one-fifth attributed to individuals in its synthesis. It attributes no share to data mining. About 55% of audited files produced a fraud finding or tax adjustment, a share that had barely changed since 2018. On 16 December 2025 the Cour des comptes published a report on the fight against tax fraud at the end of a cycle of audits. It found that mass data cross-matching originated half of tax controls but accounted for 13.8% of the tax and penalties recovered in 2023. The Cour judged that the method brings efficiency gains in the programming of controls and that its efficacy still needs support. La Revue du Digital (22 July 2026) reported a divergence at the parliamentary finance commission on 21 July 2026. Mathilde Feld, a rapporteur from La France insoumise, said that data-mining controls were 44% of Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP) controls in 2023. She said that they produced less than 14% of droits et pénalités, less than a seventh of the total. La Revue du Digital reported that in 2023 the average yield of controls started by agents was nearly five times that of controls from data mining. Feld told the commission that the share of controls from data mining was already more than 57% for businesses and 63% for individuals. DGFiP Director General Amélie Verdier defended data mining as an efficient and far less staff-intensive way to detect declarative inconsistencies. She said that the 50% level had been reached and was no longer a uniform target.

Details

AI system

Model

CFVR uses several statistical methods. The report identifies a random forest for property valuation and describes supervised and unsupervised models for detecting tax risks.

Configuration

CFVR centralized data processing and a team of 31 data scientists within the tax administration’s SCJF-1D office. Its algorithms produced lists of cases for the teams that scheduled audits.

Inputs and outputs

The administration cross-matched declarations and third-party data to identify cases for tax controls.

Workflow

Mass cross-matching of declarative and third-party data to program a growing share of controls.

Human oversight

Tax officials had to investigate and establish any tax adjustment or fraud. Cross-matching could assist selection but could not itself characterize fraud.

AI contribution

CFVR combines data cross-matching with machine learning to flag tax risks, and tax officials select and investigate the cases. The Cour des comptes describes a random-forest model that compares estimated property values with declared sale prices, plus supervised and unsupervised methods for detecting suspicious patterns. In December 2025 it attributed 13.8% of the tax and penalties recovered in 2023 to controls from mass data cross-matching, and it gives no measured gain attributable to data mining. [1] [2] [3] [4]
