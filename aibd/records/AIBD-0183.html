Nearly a third of DGFiP tax controls rest on mass-data analysis

The Cour des comptes (November 2023) found that nearly a third of DGFiP tax controls rest on mass-data risk analyzes (target 50% by 2027); a July 2026 parliamentary exchange put data mining at 44% ...

ⓘ

What happened

The Cour des comptes published its report “La détection de la fraude fiscale des particuliers” on 15 November 2023. It says nearly a third of tax controls now rest on risk analyses fed by mass data processing. The administration aims at 50% by 2027. Their efficacy remains difficult to measure. Mass data cross-matching may assist controls but can never automatically characterize fraud. The “foncier innovant” scheme illustrates the growing use of artificial intelligence. It gives 14.6 billion euro of droits et pénalités from controls in 2022, slightly more than a third on individuals. It attributes no share to data mining. It gives a 55% recovery rate that has barely improved since 2018. La Revue du Digital (22 July 2026) reported a divergence at the parliamentary finance commission on 21 July.

LFI rapporteur Mathilde Feld said that data-mining controls were 44% of Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP) controls in 2023. She said they produced less than 14% of droits et pénalités, less than a seventh of the total. DGFiP Director General Amélie Verdier defended data mining as an efficient and far less staff-intensive way to detect declarative inconsistencies.

Purpose. Target tax audits by cross-matching mass data to detect declarative inconsistencies.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. The Cour describes risk analyses fed by mass data processing. It reserves the term artificial intelligence for the separate foncier innovant scheme. Whether the targeting uses machine learning is thus not established. DGFiP builds and runs the targeting itself. The Cour des comptes, an independent audit court, wrote the report.

Since then

Further evidence

Setting. Mass cross-matching of declarative and third-party data to program a growing share of controls.

Conditions. The legal limit that cross-matching assists but never automatically characterizes fraud. Audit staff capacity to convert targeting into droits et pénalités, which the 2026 debate questioned.
