Nearly a third of DGFiP tax controls rest on mass-data analysis

off the scale

nearly a third of DGFiP tax controls rest on mass-data risk analyzes (target 50% by 2027)

The Cour des comptes (November 2023) found that nearly a third of DGFiP tax controls rest on mass-data risk analyzes (target 50% by 2027); a July 2026 parliamentary exchange put data mining at 44% ...

What happened

In its report 'La détection de la fraude fiscale des particuliers' published on 15 November 2023, the Cour des comptes states that nearly a third of tax controls now rest on risk analyzes fed by mass data processing, that the administration aims at 50% by 2027, that their efficacy remains difficult to measure, that mass data cross-matching may assist controls but can never automatically characterize fraud, and that the 'foncier innovant' scheme illustrates the growing use of artificial intelligence; it reports 14.6 billion euro of droits et pénalités from controls in 2022, slightly more than a third on individuals, without attributing any share to data mining, and a 55% recovery rate that has barely improved since 2018. La Revue du Digital (22 July 2026) reported a divergence at the parliamentary finance commission on 21 July between LFI rapporteur Mathilde Feld, who said that data-mining controls were 44% of DGFiP controls in 2023 but produced less than 14% of droits et pénalités, less than a seventh of the total, and DGFiP Director General Amélie Verdier, who defended data mining as an efficient and far less staff-intensive way to detect declarative inconsistencies. The date used is the Cour's publication date.

What it was built to do. Target tax audits by cross-matching mass data to detect declarative inconsistencies.

The system's part

not assessed The Cour describes risk analyzes fed by mass data processing and reserves the term artificial intelligence for the separate foncier innovant scheme, so whether the targeting uses machine learning is not established; DGFiP builds and runs the targeting itself, and the Cour des comptes, an independent audit court, wrote the report.

Replication

What produced it. Mass cross-matching of declarative and third-party data to program a growing share of controls.

What must hold again. The legal limit that cross-matching assists but never automatically characterizes fraud. Audit staff capacity to convert targeting into droits et pénalités, which the 2026 debate questioned.
