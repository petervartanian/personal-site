Nelli flags 247 nocturnal seizure events missed by carers

Cornwall NHS evaluation (15 people who could not tolerate EEG): Nelli flagged 707 possible seizure events, 247 unheard or unrecognised by carers; Danish phase-3 validation sensitivity 93.7%.

ⓘ

What happened

Nelli, made by Neuro Event Labs of Tampere, Finland, is a camera-and-microphone unit. Computer vision and what its validators call an artificial-intelligence-based algorithm flag nocturnal motor seizures from its signals. A phase-3 validation at the Danish Epilepsy Center and Aarhus University Hospital had 191 participants, recruited 14 October 2019 to 18 June 2021. It had 4,183 night-time hours, analyzed in real time against video electroencephalography (EEG). Armand Larsen, Beniczky and colleagues, with two Neuro Event Labs co-authors, reported 93.7% sensitivity for major motor seizures (Brain and Behavior, 8 August 2022). All 11 tonic-clonic and four of five hypermotor seizures were detected. False detections were 0.16 per hour, device deficiency 10.5% of the time, and sensitivity for minor motor seizures only 8.3%.

Lennard, Shankar and colleagues of the National Health Service (NHS) trust Cornwall Partnership NHS Foundation Trust then evaluated Nelli in the community. The 15 participants had intellectual disability and drug-resistant epilepsy and could not tolerate routine or ambulatory EEG. The report appeared in Epilepsy & Behavior Reports on 23 April 2023. The source gives no study dates. Nelli recorded 707 possible seizure events in 14 people, 247 of them unheard or unrecognized by carers. One person had half of possible events missed by night staff. Carers logged 165 “restless” or “seizure behavior” episodes that Nelli did not deem seizures. Acceptability was 93%. The authors conclude Nelli “allowed for improved communication and care planning”.

A single-center feasibility study in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes (Wilms et al., Epilepsia, online 9 October 2025; abstract read) ran four weeks of home monitoring. It detected 368 seizures with 97.8% accuracy, while only 26.1% were logged in diaries. No individual's changed outcome is documented in any cited source.

Purpose. Built to detect nocturnal major motor seizures from audio-video so that staff can intervene and clinicians can see events patients cannot report. The unrecognized events it surfaced are that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The system flagged motor events that night staff and carers had not noticed (247 of 707 in Cornwall). In the Danish validation it detected every tonic-clonic seizure in real time. Clinicians still reviewed sampled clips to confirm events. Sensitivity for minor motor seizures was 8.3%. One Cornwall participant's unit was removed to prevent injury and equipment damage. The validation paper is co-authored by two vendor employees. The Cornwall evaluation is independent of the maker.

Further evidence

Setting. A camera and microphone unit streaming to a server where computer-vision and AI algorithms flag motor events. Night-time use for people with suspected nocturnal seizures who could not tolerate EEG, with family or carer consent. Clinician review of flagged clips (the Cornwall paper says 8–9% of the recording is sampled by an epilepsy expert).

Conditions. Device deficiency kept low (10.5% of monitored time in the Danish validation). Staff available at night to act on alarms, the setting the validators say the system is suited to. The target is major motor seizures; minor motor seizures were detected at 8.3% sensitivity.
