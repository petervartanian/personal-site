Nelli flags 247 nocturnal seizure events missed by carers

In a Cornwall NHS evaluation of 15 people who could not tolerate EEG, Nelli flagged 707 possible seizure events, 247 unheard or unrecognized by carers; Danish validators reported 93.7% sensitivity.

  • Not rated a person

    Carers reported peace of mind (13 of 14). Clinicians gained event counts for people who cannot report seizures. Linköping clinicians report that the recordings were clinically useful in 8 sessions. No cited source documents a treatment change or an injury avoided for an individual.

The system’s part
The system flagged motor events that night staff and carers had not noticed (247 of 707 in Cornwall). In the Danish validation it detected every tonic-clonic seizure in real time. Clinicians still reviewed sampled clips to confirm events. Sensitivity for minor motor seizures was 8.3%. One Cornwall participant's unit was removed to prevent injury and equipment damage. The validation paper is co-authored by two vendor employees. The Cornwall evaluation is independent of the maker.
Limits
Possible events in Cornwall are not all confirmed seizures, and improved communication is not a measured health outcome. Danish major-motor sensitivity cannot be generalized to minor seizures or the Cornwall cohort; device downtime and false alerts matter. The later feasibility study’s accuracy denominator needs checking.
Costs and harms
The Danish validation detected major motor seizures much better than minor events and recorded false alarms and downtime. In Cornwall, one participant became distressed by the camera and had it removed.
Later
Persisted, 9 October 2025. A later single-center feasibility study in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes, with vendor co-authors, reported that Nelli detected 368 seizures at 97.8% accuracy over four weeks of home monitoring while diaries documented only 26.1% of seizures.

What happened

Nelli, made by Neuro Event Labs of Tampere, Finland, is a camera-and-microphone unit. Computer vision and what its validators call an artificial-intelligence-based algorithm flag nocturnal motor seizures from its signals. A phase-3 validation at the Danish Epilepsy Center and Aarhus University Hospital had 191 participants, recruited 14 October 2019 to 18 June 2021. It had 4,183 nighttime hours, analyzed in real time against video electroencephalography (EEG). Armand Larsen, Beniczky and colleagues, with two Neuro Event Labs co-authors, reported 93.7% sensitivity for major motor seizures (Brain and Behavior, 8 August 2022). All 11 tonic-clonic and four of five hypermotor seizures were detected. False detections were 0.16 per hour, device deficiency 10.5% of the time, and sensitivity for minor motor seizures only 8.3%. Lennard, Shankar and colleagues of the National Health Service (NHS) trust Cornwall Partnership NHS Foundation Trust then evaluated Nelli in the community. The 15 participants had intellectual disability and drug-resistant epilepsy and could not tolerate routine or ambulatory EEG. The report appeared in Epilepsy & Behavior Reports on 23 April 2023. The methods identify patients with suspicious nighttime events during the autumn and winter of 2020–2021. Individual recording dates are not given, but monitoring generally lasted 14 nights. Nelli recorded 707 possible seizure events in 14 people, 247 of them unheard or unrecognized by carers. One person had half of possible events missed by night staff. Carers logged 165 “restless” or “seizure behaviour” episodes that Nelli did not deem seizures. Acceptability was 93%. The authors conclude that Nelli “allowed for improved communication and care planning”. The Cornwall evaluation was funded by an investigator-initiated grant from Neuro Event Labs, which also paid salary support to the first author; the paper states that the company had no input to the design or analysis. Clinicians at Linköping University, who declare no competing interests, monitored 20 patients at home with Nelli. On 13 November 2024 they reported that the recordings were diagnostic in 11 sessions and clinically useful in 8. A single-center feasibility study in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes, with Neuro Event Labs co-authors (Wilms et al., Epilepsia, online 9 October 2025), ran four weeks of home monitoring. The system detected 368 seizures with 97.8% accuracy, while only 26.1% were logged in diaries. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) cleared Nelli (Version 7.11) on 21 November 2025 as an adjunct to seizure monitoring of adults in healthcare facilities. No individual's changed outcome is documented in any cited source.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

A camera and microphone supplied overnight recordings that the system used to flag possible motor seizures for review.

Workflow

A camera and microphone unit streaming to a server where computer-vision and AI algorithms flag motor events. Night-time use for people with suspected nocturnal seizures who could not tolerate EEG, with family or carer consent. Clinician review of flagged clips (the Cornwall paper says 8–9% of the recording is sampled by an epilepsy expert).

Human oversight

Nurse specialists helped install the equipment, and clinicians reviewed selected video and event descriptions. Reports went to the treating professionals, who used them to revise seizure descriptions and care plans.

Benefits

Duration

Most Cornwall participants were monitored for 14 nights. One continued for 20 nights, while another stopped after four days because the camera caused distress. Later studies monitored patients at home for a median of 15.5 nights in Linköping and for four weeks in the Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut study. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Clinical experts reviewed the captured video recordings and classified each registration session as diagnostic or not; clinical utility was judged against pre-specified measures. [4]

Setting and sample

20 patients with focal pharmacoresistant epilepsy referred for inpatient video-EEG monitoring at Linköping University Hospital, monitored at home for a median of 15.5 nights. [4]

Measures

Diagnostic yield and clinical utility per registration session; agreement between the algorithm and expert consensus. [4]

Results

Diagnostic yield in 11 of 20 sessions and clinical utility in 8 of 20; positive predictive value of 81.8% for sessions classified as epileptic; no significant difference between the algorithm and the experts. [4]

Limitations

Small exploratory sample of 20 patients; yield and utility were much lower for subtle focal motor seizures. [4]

Denominators

One home registration session per patient. [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Device deficiency kept low (10.5% of monitored time in the Danish validation). Staff available at night to act on alarms, the setting the validators say the system is suited to. The target is major motor seizures; minor motor seizures were detected at 8.3% sensitivity.
