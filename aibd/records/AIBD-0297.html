Nelli flags 247 nocturnal seizure events missed by carers

off the scale

Nelli flagged 707 possible seizure events, 247 unheard or unrecognised by carers

Cornwall NHS evaluation (15 people who could not tolerate EEG): Nelli flagged 707 possible seizure events, 247 unheard or unrecognised by carers; Danish phase-3 validation sensitivity 93.7%.

What happened

Nelli, made by Neuro Event Labs of Tampere, Finland, is a camera-and-microphone unit whose signals are analyzed by computer vision and what its validators call an artificial-intelligence-based algorithm to flag nocturnal motor seizures. In a phase-3 validation at the Danish Epilepsy Center and Aarhus University Hospital (191 participants recruited 14 October 2019 to 18 June 2021, 4,183 night-time hours, analysis in real time against video-EEG), Armand Larsen, Beniczky and colleagues, with two Neuro Event Labs employees as co-authors, reported sensitivity of 93.7% for major motor seizures, detection of all 11 tonic-clonic and four of five hypermotor seizures, a false detection rate of 0.16 per hour, device deficiency 10.5% of the time and sensitivity of only 8.3% for minor motor seizures (Brain and Behavior, 8 August 2022). Lennard, Shankar and colleagues of Cornwall Partnership NHS Foundation Trust then evaluated Nelli in the community for 15 people with intellectual disability and drug-resistant epilepsy who had not tolerated routine or ambulatory EEG (Epilepsy & Behavior Reports, 23 April 2023, the date used here because the study dates are not given in the fetched text): Nelli recorded 707 possible seizure events across 14 people, of which 247 were not heard or not recognized by carers, one person had half of possible events missed by night staff, carers logged 165 'restless' or 'seizure behavior' episodes that Nelli did not deem seizures, acceptability was 93%, and the authors conclude that Nelli 'allowed for improved communication and care planning'. A later single-center feasibility study in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes (Wilms et al., Epilepsia, online 9 October 2025; abstract read) reported 368 seizures detected with 97.8% accuracy over four weeks of home monitoring while only 26.1% of seizures were documented in diaries. No individual's changed outcome is documented in any fetched source.

What it was built to do. Built to detect nocturnal major motor seizures from audio-video so that staff can intervene and clinicians can see events patients cannot report; the unrecognised events it surfaced are that purpose.

The system's part

The system flagged motor events that night staff and carers had not noticed (247 of 707 in Cornwall), and in the Danish validation detected every tonic-clonic seizure in real time; clinicians still reviewed sampled clips to confirm events. Sensitivity for minor motor seizures was 8.3%, and one Cornwall participant's unit was removed to prevent injury and equipment damage. The validation paper is co-authored by two vendor employees; the Cornwall evaluation is independent of the maker.

Replication

What produced it. A camera and microphone unit streaming to a server where computer-vision and AI algorithms flag motor events. Night-time use for people with suspected nocturnal seizures who could not tolerate EEG, with family or carer consent. Clinician review of flagged clips (the Cornwall paper says 8-9% of the recording is sampled by an epilepsy expert).

What must hold again. Device deficiency kept low (10.5% of monitored time in the Danish validation). Staff available at night to act on alarms, the setting the validators say the system is suited to. The target is major motor seizures; minor motor seizures were detected at 8.3% sensitivity.
