What happened

Artera, Inc. of Los Altos, California, developed ArteraAI Prostate, deep learning software that reads digitized H&E slides from prostate needle biopsies and estimates the 10-year risk of distant metastasis and of death from prostate cancer. The model was trained on slides from randomized trials and other clinical studies with recorded outcomes. FDA reviewers describe a clinical validation in 886 patients at three US sites. The 10-year risk of distant metastasis was 28.1% for patients that the software placed in its high risk category and 3.3% for those in its low risk category, against 8.1% overall. FDA reviewers write that both differences are statistically and clinically significant. FDA granted the De Novo request on 31 July 2025 and found that the probable benefits outweigh the probable risks. The study measured how well the categories separate risk. It did not test whether treatment guided by the software changed outcomes, and only 72 patients were African American.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Slides with the highest Gleason score were scanned on a Philips Ultra Fast scanner at 40x. A pathologist confirmed cancer on the slide and released the report.

Measurement

Measurement

Prognostic validation of risk categories against observed 10-year distant metastasis. [1]

Setting and sample

886 patients at three US sites. [1]

Measures

10-year risk of distant metastasis by risk category. [1]

Results

High 28.1%, low 3.3%, overall 8.1%. [1]

Limitations

No comparison of decisions with and without the software; few African American patients (72). [1]

Denominators

One biopsy slide set per patient. [1]

Materials for replication

None reported. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The patient is a man aged 55 or older with non-metastatic prostate cancer who is a candidate for curative intent management. Physicians use the risk estimate together with other clinical and pathological factors.
