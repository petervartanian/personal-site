Quartz Solar goes live at NESO; OCF claims £30m a year avoided

OCF claims at least £30m a year in avoided imbalance costs

Open Climate Fix's machine-learning solar forecast is live in NESO's control room; OCF claims at least £30m a year in avoided imbalance costs, a figure NESO's quoted statement does not give.

What happened

On 6 November 2025 Open Climate Fix (OCF), a not-for-profit, announced that Quartz Solar, its AI-powered solar PV forecasting tool combining machine learning, live satellite imagery and weather data, was live in the National Energy System Operator's (NESO) control room, fully integrated from day-ahead scheduling to real-time control after starting as a Network Innovation Allowance project (The Energyst, 6 November 2025). The announcement says the tool helps avoid at least £30 million in imbalance costs each year and cuts around 300,000 tonnes of CO2. NESO's Carolina Tortora, Head of AI, Digitalisation and Innovation, is quoted saying the tool can accurately predict solar generation up to 36 hours in advance, revolutionizing solar forecasts in the control room; she does not state the £30m figure. pv magazine (7 November 2025) attributes the £30 million (US$39 million) per year saving to Quartz Solar itself and reports OCF's claim of halving forecasting errors across about 300 grid supply points. OCF's website states the forecast is used daily by NESO. The savings are therefore the maker's estimate, not an operator-reported or audited figure.

What it was built to do. Quartz Solar is built to forecast national and regional solar PV output for grid balancing; operational use for reserve setting is the intended purpose.

The system's part

not assessed The machine-learning forecast is the system in use, but both figures (£30m, 300,000 t CO2) come from Open Climate Fix's announcement reproduced by trade press; NESO's own quoted statement confirms deployment and 36-hour accuracy only. No NESO-authored page or regulator document with a savings figure was found.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • Satellite imagery, numerical weather data and historical generation data for a national PV fleet
  • A system operator innovation program (NIA) that funded testing before operational integration
  • A forecast refresh cycle faster than the operator's existing six-hourly weather updates
What must hold again
  • Access to live satellite and generation data
  • An operator willing to integrate a third-party forecast into reserve setting
  • Independent measurement of imbalance-cost changes, which is absent here
