What happened

On 6 November 2025 Open Climate Fix (OCF), a not-for-profit, announced that Quartz Solar was live in the National Energy System Operator's (NESO) control room. OCF's AI-powered solar PV forecasting tool combines machine learning, live satellite imagery and weather data. It is fully integrated from day-ahead scheduling to real-time control after starting as a Network Innovation Allowance project (The Energyst, 6 November 2025). The announcement says the tool helps avoid at least £30 million in imbalance costs a year and cuts around 300,000 tonnes of CO2. NESO's Carolina Tortora, Head of AI, Digitalisation and Innovation, is quoted saying it can accurately predict solar generation up to 36 hours in advance. She says it is revolutionizing solar forecasts in the control room.

She does not state the £30m figure. pv magazine (7 November 2025) attributes the £30 million (US$39 million) per year saving to Quartz Solar itself. It reports OCF's claim of halving forecasting errors across about 300 grid supply points. OCF's website says the forecast is used daily by NESO. The savings are the maker's estimate, not an operator-reported or audited figure.

What it was built to do. Quartz Solar is built to forecast national and regional solar PV output for grid balancing. Operational use for reserve setting is the intended purpose.

The system's part

Not assessed. The machine-learning forecast is the system in use. But both figures (£30m, 300,000 t CO2) come from Open Climate Fix's announcement reproduced by trade press. NESO's own quoted statement confirms deployment and 36-hour accuracy only. No NESO-authored page or regulator document with a savings figure was found.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Satellite imagery, numerical weather data and historical generation data for a national PV fleet. A system operator innovation program (NIA) that funded testing before operational integration. A forecast refresh cycle faster than the operator's existing six-hourly weather updates.

What must hold again. Access to live satellite and generation data. An operator willing to integrate a third-party forecast into reserve setting. Independent measurement of imbalance-cost changes, which is absent here.
