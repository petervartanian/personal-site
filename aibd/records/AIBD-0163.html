Britain's grid operator uses Quartz Solar, which may save £30m a year

Open Climate Fix's machine-learning solar forecast, Quartz Solar, is live in the control room of Great Britain's grid operator; the maker claims at least £30m a year in avoided imbalance costs.

The £30 million saving is an estimate and is not rated.

What happened

On 24 April 2025 Open Climate Fix, a not-for-profit, published a case study in which it said that its forecast service, Quartz Solar, had halved the solar forecast errors of the National Energy System Operator (NESO). The case study already described control-room use and the same estimated savings. On 6 November 2025 Open Climate Fix announced that Quartz Solar was live in NESO's control room. Its AI-powered solar photovoltaic forecasting tool combines machine learning, live satellite imagery and weather data. The Energyst reproduced the announcement on 6 November 2025. It says that the tool started as a Network Innovation Allowance project and is now fully integrated from day-ahead scheduling to real-time control. The announcement says that the tool helps avoid at least £30 million in imbalance costs a year and cuts around 300,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide (CO2). It also says that Quartz Solar has halved large errors and is 2.8 times more accurate than the solar generation forecasting tools that NESO used before. The announcement quotes Carolina Tortora, NESO's Head of AI, Digitalisation and Innovation, as saying that the tool can accurately predict solar generation up to 36 hours in advance. She says that it is revolutionizing solar forecasts in the control room. She does not state the £30m figure. On 7 November 2025 pv magazine attributed the £30 million (US$39 million) per year saving to Quartz Solar itself. It reports that Open Climate Fix claims to have halved forecasting errors across about 300 grid supply points. Open Climate Fix's website says that NESO uses the forecast daily. The savings are the maker's estimate, and no source gives a figure reported by the operator or an audited figure. Because the April 2025 case study already described control-room use, the November announcement does not establish the first deployment date. On 21 September 2026 the International Energy Agency listed NESO's solar forecasting among early AI deployments. The agency gives estimated savings of £30 million a year in reserve costs as the reported outcome and makes no measurement of its own.

Details

AI system

Model

PVNet.

Role

The model forecast electricity generation from solar panels.

Inputs and outputs

Quartz Solar combined live satellite images and weather data to forecast solar generation. NESO used the forecasts in control-room balancing, scheduling, and reserve planning.

Workflow

Satellite imagery, numerical weather data and historical generation data for a national PV fleet. A system operator innovation program (NIA) that funded testing before operational integration. A forecast refresh cycle faster than the operator's existing six-hourly weather updates.

Human oversight

No source describes how control-room staff at the National Energy System Operator review or override the forecast. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

AI contribution

Quartz Solar, a machine-learning forecast, supplies solar forecasts in the National Energy System Operator's control room. The £30m saving and the figure of 300,000 tonnes of carbon dioxide both come from Open Climate Fix's announcement, which trade press reproduced and the International Energy Agency repeated. The operator's quoted statement confirms deployment and 36-hour accuracy only. No source written by the operator or a regulator gives a savings figure. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]
