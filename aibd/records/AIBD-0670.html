Machine translation lifts cross-border sales on eBay

eBay's in-house machine translation, introduced for Latin America in May 2014, raised exports on eBay by 10.9% in the published study and 17.5% to 20.9% in the working paper.

What happened

In 2014 eBay replaced Bing Translator with its own machine translation system, eBay Machine Translation (eMT), in three regions: Russia in January, Latin America in May and the European Union in July. For buyers in Latin America, eMT translated Spanish search queries into English and showed English listing titles in Spanish. Human raters accepted 91.4% of eMT translations of frequent queries, against 84.4% for Bing, a moderate gain of 7 percentage points. Erik Brynjolfsson of MIT and Xiang Hui and Meng Liu of Washington University in St. Louis used eBay data to compare U.S. exports on eBay to Spanish-speaking Latin American countries before and after the change with exports to other destinations. The 2018 working paper estimates that eMT raised those exports by 17.5% to 20.9%, depending on the time window, and export revenue by 13.1% to 17%. The version that Management Science published in December 2019 reports a rise in exports of 10.9%. Effects were larger for differentiated and cheaper products, for listings with longer titles and for less experienced buyers. The study is a natural experiment on one platform, and eBay supplied the data. Hui disclosed a paid summer internship at eBay from June to August 2015 and no tie to eBay after that.

Details

AI system

Workflow

EBay translated buyers' search queries and listing titles automatically, replacing an older machine translation service. The platform already connected sellers and buyers across borders.

Measurement

Measurement

Export quantity and revenue from eBay transaction data. [2]

Setting and sample

U.S. Exports on eBay to Latin America and other countries, six and twelve months around the change. [2]

Measures

Export quantity and revenue. [2]

Results

10.9% more exports (published); 17.5% to 20.9% in quantity and 13.1% to 17% in revenue (working paper). [2]

Additional value from AI

About a tenth to a fifth more exports over the older machine translation. [2]

Limitations

One platform; nonrandom rollout timing; eBay supplied the data; one author interned at eBay in 2015; the published estimate is smaller than the working paper estimate. [2]

Denominators

Machine-translated listing titles. [2]

Materials for replication

Data not public. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Language must be a real barrier for the buyers. Translation quality must clear what buyers find usable.

Entry details

Sector

Purpose

EMT was built to translate listings for buyers in other languages. More cross-border trade on the platform was that purpose.
