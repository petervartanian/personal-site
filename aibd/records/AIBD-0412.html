GeoGPT identifies deep-sea species in images faster than people do

Zhejiang Lab reports that its GeoGPT platform identifies deep-sea species with 81.3% accuracy and takes 40 milliseconds per image, compared with 40 seconds manually.

What happened

In its submission to the International Telecommunication Union, current as of June 2025, Zhejiang Lab describes GeoGPT as a research and product platform for geoscience analysis. The lab reports that GeoGPT identifies deep-sea species with 81.3% accuracy. It also reports that recognition time fell from 40 seconds to 40 milliseconds per image. The intended applications include marine monitoring and less disruptive exploration. The marine figures rest on the makers' submission alone. No source establishes that ecosystem losses were avoided. Cleverley and colleagues reported on 25 July 2026 that the International Union of Geological Sciences had suspended Deep-time Digital Earth, the program behind GeoGPT, in early 2025. They also reported that core GeoGPT software remains proprietary.

Details

AI system

Model

GeoGPT uses a portfolio that includes Llama, Qwen, Mixtral, and DeepSeek R1, adapted for geoscience. The submission does not assign the marine-image result to a particular one.

Inputs and outputs

GeoGPT classifies deep-sea species in marine images and returns image-level identification results.
