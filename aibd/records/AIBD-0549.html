Hertfordshire trials an AI robot that repairs road cracks

Hertfordshire County Council reports that the Robotiz3d ARRES Prevent robot, which uses AI to detect road defects, filled a small number of cracks in a public-road pilot.

What happened

Hertfordshire and Robotiz3d trialed the ARRES Prevent robot, which uses AI to detect road defects and fill cracks. The first public-road test took place in Potters Bar on 6 March 2024. The pilot reportedly repaired a small number of cracks. The project reports that detection accuracy increased from 66% to 100% in testing. In February 2025 the BBC reported that the council had moved the ARRES Eye scanner into a months-long test on a trial route. That report gives no results on the durability of the robot's repairs. Long-term repair performance, pothole prevention, cost savings, and population safety effects remain unestablished.

Details

AI system

Role

AI detects road defects, and the ARRES Prevent robot physically fills selected cracks.

Workflow

The development partnership used Hertfordshire road data and highway-engineering feedback. The reported pilot involved a prototype on a public road, with further tests and long-term repair review planned.

Human oversight

The council's account says that contractor risk assessments for highway work cover the road trials. It says that Hertfordshire engineers will review the long-term benefits of the repairs. No source describes who supervises the robot on the road. [1] [2] [3]

Benefits

Reach

The council reports that the pilot repaired a small number of cracks and gives no exact count. No source counts the road users who benefited. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

The robot filled the cracks in the 2024 pilot. In February 2025 the BBC reported that the scanner had moved into a long-term test. No source reports whether the repaired cracks lasted. [1] [2] [3]
