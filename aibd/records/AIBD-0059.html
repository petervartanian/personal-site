CapisciAMe CNN outperforms familiar listeners on impaired speech

A CNN trained only on one patient's voice recognized 72.65% of her words versus 56.75% for 12 familiar professionals; lab result only.

ⓘ

What happened

A 34-year-old right-handed woman took part in a single-case proof of concept at the University of Messina and IRCCS Centro Neurolesi Bonino-Pulejo. It appears in Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences (2026). She has right hemiparesis, global aphasia and dysarthria from a massive left-hemisphere hemorrhage at age 19. A compact convolutional neural network in the CapisciAMe voice-input communication app was trained only on her voice. Training used 1,120 utterances of 13 Italian target words from 33 sessions in one month in a therapy room. Utterances from November 2022 (day not given, so 1 November is used) were the held-out test set. The tailored recognizer identified 680 of 936 test utterances (72.65%). 12 rehabilitation professionals who knew her averaged 56.75%. No real-world communication use is documented.

Purpose. Recognize the impaired speech of a person with aphasia and dysarthria so a communication aid can voice her intended words

AI’s contribution

The personalized network is the entire intervention. Its 72.65% word accuracy exceeded the 56.75% mean of familiar listeners. The comparison used a 13-word closed set. The authors state the single-case design and limited vocabulary constrain generalization.

Further evidence

Setting. Speaker-dependent training on hundreds of the patient's own utterances. Closed, small target vocabulary. Multiple prompting modalities during recording.

Conditions. Patient can produce consistent vocalizations for each target word. Quiet recording environment with a headset microphone. Clinician time to collect and label training data.
