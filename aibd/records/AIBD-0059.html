CapisciAMe CNN outperforms familiar listeners on impaired speech

off the scale

recognized 72.65% of her words versus 56.75% for 12 familiar professionals

A CNN trained only on one patient's voice recognized 72.65% of her words versus 56.75% for 12 familiar professionals; lab result only.

What happened

A 34-year-old right-handed woman with right hemiparesis, global aphasia and dysarthria from a massive left-hemisphere hemorrhage at age 19 took part in a single-case proof of concept at the University of Messina and IRCCS Centro Neurolesi Bonino-Pulejo (Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences, 2026). A compact convolutional neural network inside the CapisciAMe voice-input communication app was trained only on her voice, using 1,120 utterances of 13 Italian target words recorded in 33 sessions over one month in a therapy room; utterances from November 2022 (day not given, so 1 November is used) formed the held-out test set. The tailored recognizer identified 680 of 936 test utterances (72.65%), against a mean of 56.75% for 12 rehabilitation professionals who knew her. No real-world communication use is documented.

What it was built to do. Recognise the impaired speech of a person with aphasia and dysarthria so a communication aid can voice her intended words

The system's part

The personalized CNN is the entire intervention; its 72.65% word accuracy exceeded the 56.75% mean of familiar listeners, but the comparison used a 13-word closed set and the authors state the single-case design and limited vocabulary constrain generalization.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Speaker-dependent training on hundreds of the patient's own utterances. Closed, small target vocabulary. Multiple prompting modalities during recording.

What must hold again. Patient can produce consistent vocalizations for each target word. Quiet recording environment with a headset microphone. Clinician time to collect and label training data.

Harm on record

AI Incident Database 732, Whisper Speech-to-Text AI Reportedly Found to Create Violent Hallucinations. Both decode speech for people with impaired speech; Whisper fabricated words and violent phrases when transcribing impaired speech.
