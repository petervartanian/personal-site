What happened

Warwickshire County Council tested a locally hosted Llama 2 model that classified businesses by size and food availability. The council aimed to identify areas with limited access to affordable, nutritious food. Accuracy was 63%, compared with 95% for a simpler method. Processing took up to 1.5 hours per output. In its case study, the Local Government Association reports that the model added irrelevant text to 3.8 percent of its answers and that eight answers included business names. The association describes those names as a potential security risk. Inconsistent model labels in the case study leave the exact configuration unclear. The council anticipated that further fine-tuning could save staff time on manual searches. Its evaluation demonstrates neither time savings nor improved food access. According to a separate cost analysis, dedicated hardware for large language models would be expensive, with a used graphics card costing about £41,000. The association closes with the note that the council continued to explore cheaper ways to run large language models in-house.

Details

AI system

Model

Llama 2. The exact variant remains uncertain. [1]

Role

Llama 2 classified businesses by size and food availability. [1]

Configuration

The council ran a downloaded, quantized model on an available local laptop and used prompt engineering. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Business-property descriptions went into prompts that asked for classifications. [1]

Workflow

A locally hosted LLM classified businesses but underperformed a simpler method.

Human oversight

The council compared the model's classifications with a method without AI on the same task. That method was more accurate. No source reports that the model's output informed a decision about food access. [1]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Council analysts were the immediate intended beneficiaries. Residents in areas with poor food access were the intended eventual beneficiaries. [1]

Reach

No source counts beneficiaries. The intended beneficiaries are council staff and residents with limited access to affordable, nutritious food. No source reports that any of them benefited. [1]

Duration

No source reports a time saving, so no benefit is shown to persist. The case study says that the council continued to explore cheaper ways to run large language models in-house. No later report shows that the food-access model was fine-tuned, adopted or replaced. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

Accuracy of business classifications from a prompted LLM compared with a rule-based string-concatenation method on council business-property data.

Setting and sample

Warwickshire County Council business properties and types; sample size not stated.

Measures

Classification accuracy; share of outputs with irrelevant content; processing time.

Results

63 percent accuracy for the LLM against 95 percent for the simpler method; irrelevant output in 3.8 percent of cases; up to 1.5 hours per output.

Additional value from AI

Negative: the LLM was less accurate than the method without AI.

Limitations

The council evaluated its own proof of concept, and the case study gives no sample size or labeling method. Model labels differ within the text.

Denominators

Prompt engineering only, with no fine-tuning, on a quantized model run on a laptop.

People and places

Ownership and control

Warwickshire County Council controlled the prototype’s local deployment. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Further development and comparative testing would be needed. Conditions sufficient for a beneficial outcome remain unknown.
