AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 reach IMO silver-medal standard

DeepMind AI scored 28/42 on IMO 2024 problems, graded by Gowers and Myers; Gemini Deep Think's 35/42 in 2025 was IMO-certified gold

ⓘ

What happened

Google DeepMind's AlphaProof (reinforcement learning over the Lean proof language) and AlphaGeometry 2 produced solutions to four of the six 2024 International Mathematical Olympiad problems. Because the systems work in formal language, the problems were first translated manually into Lean. One problem was solved within minutes and others took up to three days, longer than the contest allows. Two former IMO gold medalists, Sir Timothy Gowers and Joseph Myers, graded the solutions under IMO rules. They awarded 28 of 42 points, a silver-medal score. DeepMind announced the result on 25 July 2024 (grading date not given). In July 2025 a Gemini Deep Think model working in natural language within the time limit scored 35 of 42. IMO president Gregor Dolinar confirmed this as a gold-medal score. Nature reported that OpenAI also claimed a gold-level result.

Purpose. Solve competition mathematics problems with machine-checkable formal proofs

AI’s contribution

The AI systems produced the proofs. Humans manually formalized the 2024 problem statements into Lean (Scientific American) and graded the output. In 2024 the solutions were graded by two mathematicians at DeepMind's request (one of them, Myers, chaired the IMO 2024 Problem Selection Committee). The result was not officially certified by the IMO. DeepMind states the 2025 result was graded and certified by IMO coordinators, with the IMO president's confirmation appearing in the vendor blog. The cited sources do not state whether OpenAI's 2025 claim was certified by the IMO.

Further evidence

Setting. Manual translation of the 2024 problem statements into Lean. AlphaProof reinforcement-learning proof search and AlphaGeometry 2 for the geometry problem. Grading under IMO rules by two former IMO gold medalists.

Conditions. Each proof is machine-checked in Lean or graded by qualified IMO graders. Problems are unseen by the system before the contest. Grading criteria identical to those for human contestants.
