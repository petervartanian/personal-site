AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry 2 reach IMO silver-medal standard

awarded 28 of 42 points, a silver-medal score

DeepMind AI scored 28/42 on IMO 2024 problems, graded by Gowers and Myers; Gemini Deep Think's 35/42 in 2025 was IMO-certified gold

What happened

At the 2024 International Mathematical Olympiad, Google DeepMind's AlphaProof (reinforcement learning over the Lean proof language) and AlphaGeometry 2 produced solutions to four of the six problems. Because the systems work in formal language, the problems were first translated manually into Lean; one problem was solved within minutes and others took up to three days, longer than the contest allows. Two former IMO gold medallists, Sir Timothy Gowers and Joseph Myers, graded the solutions under IMO rules and awarded 28 of 42 points, a silver-medal score; DeepMind announced the result on 25 July 2024 (grading date not given). In July 2025 a Gemini Deep Think model working in natural language within the time limit scored 35 of 42, which IMO president Gregor Dolinar confirmed as a gold-medal score; Nature reported that OpenAI also claimed a gold-level result.

What it was built to do. Solve competition mathematics problems with machine-checkable formal proofs

The system's part

The AI systems produced the proofs; humans manually formalized the 2024 problem statements into Lean (Scientific American) and graded the output. In 2024 the solutions were graded by two mathematicians at DeepMind's request (one of them, Myers, chaired the IMO 2024 Problem Selection Committee); the result was not officially certified by the IMO, unlike the 2025 result, which DeepMind states was graded and certified by IMO coordinators, with the IMO president's confirmation appearing in the vendor blog. The fetched sources do not state whether OpenAI's 2025 claim was certified by the IMO.

Replication

What produced it
  • Manual translation of the 2024 problem statements into Lean
  • AlphaProof reinforcement-learning proof search and AlphaGeometry 2 for the geometry problem
  • Grading under IMO rules by two former IMO gold medallists
What must hold again
  • Each proof is machine-checked in Lean or graded by qualified IMO graders
  • Problems are unseen by the system before the contest
  • Grading criteria identical to those for human contestants
