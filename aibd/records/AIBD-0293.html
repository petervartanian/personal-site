Oura ring trends feed into a Holter showing 2,078 PVCs in 24 h

Dubai clinic case: Oura ring heart-rate and HRV summaries, read by clinicians, fed into a stress test and Holter showing 2,078 PVCs in 24 h; the ring detects no arrhythmia and issued no alert.

System

ŌURA ring (heart-rate and heart-rate-variability summaries) used within GluCare.Health's hybrid care model

Application developer

Maker of the ŌURA ring (company not named in the paper)

Workflow

Continuous ring wear under a clinic hybrid care model in which staff review daily synced heart-rate and HRV summaries. Risk factors (type 2 diabetes, smoking) that led to a treadmill stress test. Access to ECG, 24-hour Holter and echocardiography to confirm and characterize the PVCs.

Relation to AI

The ring generated no alert and, per the paper, cannot detect PVCs. Clinicians read its heart-rate and HRV summaries twice. Once was before the stress test (an elevated resting heart rate seen in remote monitoring is listed among the reasons for testing). Once was retrospectively afterwards. The ECG, stress test and Holter made the diagnosis. The paper describes no machine-learning component, so whether an AI system was involved is not shown by the source text. The only source is the treating clinic's case report.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Clinicians act on wearable trend deviations. The clinic later wrote thresholds into its workflow (an RMSSD drop of more than 30% or a resting heart rate above 110 bpm prompts review). The patient keeps wearing and syncing the ring so that trends can be compared with a baseline.

Later findings

The paper's own two-month and four-month follow-up (resting heart rate 83 to 74 bpm, RMSSD 15.2 to 32.8 ms) belongs to the first report; nothing published after it was found.
