Skylight alerts may have led PNA to intercept XINRUN 579

PNA intercepted XINRUN 579 fishing illegally in Argentina's EEZ on 5 January 2025; Skylight says its speed-range and ML fishing alerts prompted the response and a first electronic fine was paid.

ⓘ

What happened

On Sunday 5 January 2025 the Prefectura Naval Argentina (PNA) detected the Vanuatu-flagged trawler XinRun 579. It was in Argentina's exclusive economic zone off Caleta Olivia, Santa Cruz, sailing at under 6 knots. Local rules treat that as indicating trawling. Coastguard vessel GC-25 Azopardo intercepted it. The captain was formally notified of an infraction under Law 24.922, opening administrative proceedings (Agenda Malvinas, 8 January 2025, citing official information). Skylight, the free AI maritime-monitoring platform of the Allen Institute for AI, published its own account on 31 March 2025. A rule-based Speed Range Event first alerted the authorities. A Fishing Event alert from its machine-learning model came 40 minutes later. PNA analysts redirected a nearby coastguard vessel.

It led to Argentina's first international electronic fine using AI-powered evidence, with the fine and operational costs paid. It quotes the EICEMAR coordinator and the PNA GIS division lead crediting Skylight. The independent news report attributes the detection to the PNA's Sistema Guardacostas and does not mention Skylight or AI. The PNA release Skylight links to is not found live or in the Internet Archive. An NVIDIA Developer blog (3 March 2025) also reports the interdiction and fine.

Purpose. Real-time detection of suspicious vessel behavior and fishing activity to support maritime enforcement against illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing. The interception was the intended use.

AI’s contribution

Skylight's own account describes a two-stage AI contribution. A rule-based Speed Range Event flagged the vessel at under 6 knots inside an area of interest. 40 minutes later a Fishing Event from its machine-learning model, which detects longlining, trawling and squid jigging, reinforced the suspicion. PNA analysts then redirected a coastguard vessel. A retired PNA captain is quoted saying Skylight contributed directly to the decision. This is the maker's account, with the deployer's officers quoted inside it. The independent report (Agenda Malvinas, citing Pescare and official information) attributes the detection to the PNA's Sistema Guardacostas and does not name Skylight or AI. So the attribution to the AI output is not independently confirmed. The PNA release Skylight links to is not among the sources.

Further evidence

Setting. A vessel transmitting AIS inside a monitored area of interest. A speed-range rule tuned to the local legal definition of trawling speed. A machine-learning fishing-behavior model producing a second alert within 40 minutes. PNA analysts integrating Skylight with the Sistema Guardacostas and a coastguard vessel within reach.

Conditions. AIS transmissions not switched off or spoofed. A patrol asset close enough to intercept before the vessel leaves the EEZ. A legal route to sanction (Law 24.922 and the electronic fine procedure). Analyst confirmation of each alert before dispatch.
