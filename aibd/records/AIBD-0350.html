Skylight alerts may have led Argentina's coast guard to stop a trawler

Skylight reports that its speed alert and its machine-learning fishing alert led Argentina's coast guard to intercept a trawler on 5 January 2025, and that the vessel paid a fine.

  • Not rated a person

    No individual beneficiary is identified.

  • 2 an organization

    The PNA intercepted the vessel within hours and, per Skylight, issued its first international electronic fine with the fine and costs paid. The independent report confirms the interception and administrative proceedings.

  • Not rated society

    The interception concerns one vessel among hundreds that operate at the edge of Argentina's exclusive economic zone each season, and no effect on fish stocks or on illegal fishing at large is reported.

The system’s part
Skylight's own account describes a two-stage AI contribution. A rule-based Speed Range Event flagged the vessel at under 6 knots inside an area of interest. 40 minutes later a Fishing Event from its machine-learning model, which detects longlining, trawling and squid jigging, reinforced the suspicion. PNA analysts then redirected a coastguard vessel. A retired PNA captain is quoted saying Skylight contributed directly to the decision. This is the maker's account, with the deployer's officers quoted inside it. The independent report (Agenda Malvinas, citing Pescare and official information) attributes the detection to the PNA's Sistema Guardacostas and does not name Skylight or AI. So the attribution to the AI output is not independently confirmed. The PNA release Skylight links to is not among the sources.
Limits
The rule-based speed alert preceded the machine-learning alert by 40 minutes. Independent reporting does not identify Skylight, so neither the decision's reliance on the later model output nor the claimed paid fine is independently established in the retained chain.

Taken to court, 31 March 2025. The administrative proceeding ended with the vessel paying the fine and the authorities' operational costs, as Skylight reported on 31 March 2025 (the payment date itself is not given; NVIDIA's 3 March 2025 blog already said the vessel was later fined).

What happened

On Sunday 5 January 2025 the Prefectura Naval Argentina (PNA), Argentina's coast guard, detected the Vanuatu-flagged trawler XinRun 579. It was in Argentina's exclusive economic zone off Caleta Olivia, Santa Cruz, sailing at under 6 knots. Local rules treat that speed as a sign of trawling. The coast guard vessel GC-25 Azopardo intercepted it. The captain was formally notified of an infraction under Law 24.922, which opened administrative proceedings (Agenda Malvinas, 8 January 2025, citing official information). This independent news report attributes the detection to the PNA's Sistema Guardacostas and does not mention Skylight or AI. On 3 March 2025 an NVIDIA Developer blog post reported that the coast guard interdicted the vessel and later fined it after Skylight alerted it. Skylight, the free AI maritime-monitoring platform of the Allen Institute for AI, published its own account on 31 March 2025. A rule-based Speed Range Event first alerted the authorities. A Fishing Event alert from its machine-learning model came 40 minutes later. PNA analysts redirected a nearby coast guard vessel. Skylight says that the case led to Argentina's first international electronic fine using AI-powered evidence. It says that the vessel paid the fine and the operational costs. The account quotes a retired PNA captain who coordinates EICEMAR, an interdisciplinary team for the control of maritime spaces and their resources. It also quotes the lead of the PNA's geographic information systems division. Both credit Skylight. The PNA release that Skylight links to is not found live or in the Internet Archive.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

AIS vessel behavior first produced a low-speed rule alert and later a machine-learning fishing alert, 40 minutes apart.

Autonomy

The platform supplied alerts, while analysts redirected a patrol vessel and the coast guard handled interception and proceedings.

Workflow

A vessel transmitting AIS inside a monitored area of interest. A speed-range rule tuned to the local legal definition of trawling speed. A machine-learning fishing-behavior model producing a second alert within 40 minutes. PNA analysts integrating Skylight with the Sistema Guardacostas and a coastguard vessel within reach.

Human oversight

Coast guard analysts assessed the alerts and decided to redirect the patrol vessel.

AI contribution

A speed rule in Skylight first flagged the vessel. Its machine-learning model then detected a pattern consistent with fishing. According to Skylight, coast guard analysts used those alerts when redirecting a patrol vessel.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

AIS transmissions not switched off or spoofed. A patrol asset close enough to intercept before the vessel leaves the EEZ. A legal route to sanction (Law 24.922 and the electronic fine procedure). Analyst confirmation of each alert before dispatch.
