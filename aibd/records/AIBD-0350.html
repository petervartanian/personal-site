Skylight says its alerts led PNA to intercept XINRUN 579

PNA intercepted XINRUN 579 fishing illegally in Argentina's EEZ on 5 Jan 2025; Skylight says its speed-range and ML fishing alerts prompted the response and a first electronic fine was paid.

What happened

On Sunday 5 January 2025 the Prefectura Naval Argentina (PNA) detected the Vanuatu-flagged trawler XinRun 579 inside Argentina's exclusive economic zone off Caleta Olivia, Santa Cruz, sailing at under 6 knots, which local rules treat as an indication of trawling; the coastguard vessel GC-25 Azopardo intercepted it and the captain was formally notified of an infraction under Law 24.922, opening administrative proceedings (Agenda Malvinas, 8 January 2025, citing official information). Skylight, the free AI maritime-monitoring platform of the Allen Institute for AI, published its own account on 31 March 2025: a rule-based Speed Range Event first alerted the authorities, a Fishing Event alert from its machine-learning model followed 40 minutes later, PNA analysts redirected a nearby coastguard vessel, and the case led to Argentina's first international electronic fine using AI-powered evidence, with the fine and operational costs paid; it quotes the EICEMAR coordinator and the PNA GIS division lead crediting Skylight. The independent news report attributes the detection to the PNA's Sistema Guardacostas and does not mention Skylight or AI; the PNA press release that Skylight links to returns 404 live and in the Internet Archive. An NVIDIA Developer blog (3 March 2025) also reports the interdiction and fine.

What it was built to do. Real-time detection of suspicious vessel behavior and fishing activity to support maritime enforcement against illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing; the interception was the intended use.

The system's part

Skylight's own account describes a two-stage AI contribution: a rule-based Speed Range Event flagged the vessel at under 6 knots inside an area of interest, and 40 minutes later a Fishing Event from its machine-learning model, which detects longlining, trawling and squid jigging, reinforced the suspicion before PNA analysts redirected a coastguard vessel; a retired PNA captain is quoted saying Skylight contributed directly to the decision. This is the maker's account, with the deployer's officers quoted inside it. The independent report (Agenda Malvinas, citing Pescare and official information) attributes the detection to the PNA's Sistema Guardacostas and does not name Skylight or AI, so the attribution to the AI output is not independently confirmed; the PNA release Skylight links to could not be fetched.

Replication

What produced it
  • A vessel transmitting AIS inside a monitored area of interest
  • A speed-range rule tuned to the local legal definition of trawling speed
  • A machine-learning fishing-behavior model producing a second alert within 40 minutes
  • PNA analysts integrating Skylight with the Sistema Guardacostas and a coastguard vessel within reach
What must hold again
  • AIS transmissions not switched off or spoofed
  • A patrol asset close enough to intercept before the vessel leaves the EEZ
  • A legal route to sanction (Law 24.922 and the electronic fine procedure)
  • Analyst confirmation of each alert before dispatch
