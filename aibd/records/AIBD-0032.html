Darpan matches a missing boy to a child home in Assam after five years

Darpan, a Telangana State Police facial-recognition tool, matched Som Soni, missing from Allahabad since 14 July 2015, to a boy in a child home in Goalpara, Assam, where his parents identified him.

  • 3 a person

    A child separated from his parents for five years, living in an institution in another state, was identified and returned to his family. The sources describe the match as having “changed the lives of the child and his parents”.

What happened

Som Soni went missing from his home in Allahabad (Uttar Pradesh) on 14 July 2015, aged eight. ETV Bharat describes him as a resident of Handia in Allahabad, and the case lay with Handia police station. On 23 July 2015, Goalpara police found him and took him to a child welfare center at Goalpara, Assam (spelled “Golapara” in the reports). Darpan, a facial-recognition tool built by Telangana State Police, matches photographs of missing children against photographs of children lodged in child homes across the country. A routine matching run matched his photograph to the missing-child record from Uttar Pradesh. Telangana Police notified Handia police, and his parents traveled to Goalpara and identified him. Swati Lakra, Additional Director General of Police (ADGP) of the Telangana Police Women Safety Wing, announced the case on 9 October 2020. The reports do not give the exact day of the reunion. ANI reported on 12 October 2020 that Lakra had told it that Telangana State Police built Darpan in 2018. She said that the police draw data from child care institutions, the central Track the Missing Child portal and the Crime and Criminal Tracking Network and System (CCTNS), and that Darpan had helped reunite about 33 children by then. In July 2023, Deccan Chronicle reported that Hyderabad police were still using Darpan and that it had helped reunite 36 children.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Darpan compared photographs in missing-child records with photographs from child-care institutions and returned a possible match for Som Soni.

Workflow

A pool of missing-child photographs (the reports do not say how these are collected). Photographs of children in child-care institutions supplied by welfare departments. Routine matching runs by Telangana State Police.

Human oversight

Telangana Police contacted Handia police, and Soni's parents traveled to Goalpara and identified him in person before the reunion.

Benefits

Reach

One boy and his parents benefited in this case. Swati Lakra told ANI on 12 October 2020 that Darpan had helped reunite about 33 children since 2018, and Deccan Chronicle reported 36 by July 2023. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The reunion is a one-time event, and no cited source reports on Som Soni after October 2020. Hyderabad police were still using Darpan in July 2023, when Deccan Chronicle reported that it had helped reunite 36 missing children. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Institutions keep supplying current photographs of found children. Matches are verified in person by family and local police before handover. Child's appearance five or ten years after disappearance remains matchable.
