Darpan reunites Som Soni with his family after five years

depth 3 for a person

Independently reported, 4 sources.

Darpan matched Som Soni, missing from Allahabad since 14 July 2015, to a boy in a Goalpara, Assam child home; reunited October 2020.

What happened

Darpan, a facial-recognition tool built by Telangana State Police, matches photographs of missing children against photographs of children lodged in child homes across the country. A routine matching run matched Som Soni to a boy police had found on 23 July 2015. He had been admitted to a child welfare center at Goalpara, Assam (spelled “Golapara” in the reports). Soni had gone missing from his home in Allahabad (Uttar Pradesh) on 14 July 2015 aged eight. ETV Bharat describes him as a resident of Handia in Allahabad. The case lay with Handia police station. Telangana Police notified Handia police. His parents traveled to Goalpara and identified him. The case was announced on 9 October 2020 by Swati Lakra, ADGP of the Telangana Police Women Safety Wing. The exact day of the reunion is not given, so the announcement date is used.

What it was built to do. Darpan was built to match missing-child photographs with children found in institutions so they can be returned to their families. This reunification is that intended benefit.

The system's part

The face match was the sole lead. The boy had been in an Assam institution for five years with no identification until Darpan matched his photograph to the Uttar Pradesh missing-child record. Police then contacted the parents. Verification rested on the parents identifying him in person. The Telangana CID news page was not available. So the record relies on news reports quoting the ADGP.

Replication

What produced it. A pool of missing-child photographs (the reports do not say how these are collected). Photographs of children in child-care institutions supplied by welfare departments. Routine matching runs by Telangana State Police.

What must hold again. Institutions keep supplying current photographs of found children. Matches are verified in person by family and local police before handover. Child's appearance five or ten years after disappearance remains matchable.
