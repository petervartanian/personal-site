LMU's eBL platform finds manuscripts of a lost Hymn to Babylon

LMU's AI-supported eBL platform identified further manuscripts of a lost Hymn to Babylon; edition published in Iraq (online 17 March 2025)

ⓘ

What happened

Assyriologists Anmar A. Fadhil (University of Baghdad) and Enrique Jiménez (LMU Munich) published an edition of a previously unknown Babylonian hymn. It praises Babylon and its people and is in the journal Iraq (volume 86, online 17 March 2025). The composition may have been about 250 lines long. It survives in no fewer than 20 manuscripts dating from the 7th to the 2nd/1st centuries BCE. They come from the Sippar Library tablets. Per LMU's press release of 1 July 2025, the team's AI-supported Electronic Babylonian Library platform identified 30 further manuscripts belonging to the hymn. That would formerly have taken decades. The article itself states that without the eBL platform the edition could not have used 21 manuscripts in 31 fragments.

Purpose. Match and join cuneiform fragments across collections to reconstruct Babylonian literary texts

AI’s contribution

The platform's matching identified additional manuscripts of the hymn (30 per the LMU release; the edition uses 21 manuscripts in 31 fragments per the article). The edition, translation and interpretation were done by the two authors. The AI attribution comes from the LMU release, in which Jiménez speaks of “our AI-supported platform”. The article credits the eBL platform and team without mentioning artificial intelligence. The LMU release is vendor-authored, since LMU is the platform's developer, and ScienceAlert mainly relays that release and the paper. The peer-reviewed edition appeared in Iraq. Its first author Anmar A. Fadhil is at the University of Baghdad, with one developer co-author (Jiménez).

Further evidence

Setting. Digitized transliterations of cuneiform fragments in the eBL Fragmentarium. Algorithmic matching of sign sequences across fragments and collections. Philological edition and translation by Fadhil and Jiménez.

Conditions. Fragment transliterations exist in the database. Overlapping text between manuscripts allows joins. Each proposed join is verified by Assyriologists.
