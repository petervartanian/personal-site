AI platform helps scholars recover a lost hymn to Babylon

The AI-supported Electronic Babylonian Library platform at LMU Munich matched tablet fragments, which let two scholars edit a lost Hymn to Babylon from 21 manuscripts in 31 fragments.

Relation to AI

The platform's matching identified additional manuscripts of the hymn (30 per the LMU release; the edition uses 21 manuscripts in 31 fragments per the article). The edition, translation and interpretation were done by the two authors. The AI attribution comes from the LMU release, in which Jiménez speaks of “our AI-supported platform”. The article credits the eBL platform and team without mentioning artificial intelligence. The LMU release is vendor-authored, since LMU is the platform's developer, and ScienceAlert mainly relays that release and the paper. The peer-reviewed edition appeared in Iraq. Its first author Anmar A. Fadhil is at the University of Baghdad, with one developer co-author (Jiménez).

  4. News report Independent author

    Leonard, B., Hymn to Babylon, Top 10 Discoveries of 2025, Archaeology Magazine (Archaeological Institute of America), January/February 2026, online December 2025. The magazine has no stake in the platform; its editors chose the hymn as one of the year's ten main discoveries, and the writer interviewed Jiménez, who gave new figures on fragment joins.

    After transliterating the text, they used artificial intelligence models to search for overlaps with other tablets. The researchers identified 30 fragments from 20 different tablets containing sections of the hymn.

  5. Peer-reviewed study Independent author Context or contrary evidence

    Zaia, S., Gods in the Classroom: Religion and Education in 1st Millennium BCE Babylonia, Iraq, published online 24 September 2026. She uses the edited hymn as evidence for Babylonian scribal education, which shows that the recovered text has entered other scholarship, but she does not assess the platform's part in its recovery.
