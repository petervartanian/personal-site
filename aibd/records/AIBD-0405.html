What happened

The contact for the project is Teophiler Daud Mage of the Department of Electronics and Telecommunication Engineering at Mbeya University of Science and Technology in Tanzania. The university describes a monitoring and advisory system that is intended to improve access to preventive healthcare. The device reads heart rate, blood oxygen and body temperature, applies simple machine learning and anomaly detection, and sends alerts and advice when readings are abnormal. The university plans further biosensors, more advanced models and partnerships with telecommunications companies. The account names local health centers as partners but lists no testbed. The account reports no measurements of detection, treatment or access outcomes. No later report of a test or a patient result had been published by October 2026.

Details

AI system

Role

The proposed AI component interprets biosensor measurements to support early detection and personalized preventive-health advice.

Inputs and outputs

The proposed wearable system would measure heart rate, blood oxygen saturation, and body temperature and return alerts or advice when values appear abnormal.
