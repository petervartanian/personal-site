What happened

Europa Press reported on 12 February 2026, from a statement by the Diputació de València, on a set of pilot tests. The provincial council had begun them on the CV-600 road, in the area of Simat de la Valldigna and Benifairó de la Valldigna. The intelligent signaling system is designed to detect wild boar near the carriageway and automatically activate luminous warning signs. The Valencian road-safety company Metalesa carried out the work, with civil works by Grupo Bertolín. The system combines cameras with computer vision and automatic detection algorithms that identify animals passing. It activates dynamic luminous signs only when a real risk exists, and it is powered by photovoltaic energy. The council said that the system records crossing patterns, times and how often the signs light up. Council president Vicent Mompó visited the site with roads deputy Reme Mazzolari. He said that after some weeks the council would evaluate the results. If the system proved effective, the council would extend the model to the most sensitive stretches of the provincial network. No detection counts or accident figures are given. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor lists the pilot as an aggregate benefit. Metalesa lists the Simat installation as a 2026 project for its PlugSmart Pro device. No evaluation or extension had been reported by late October 2026.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

PlugSmart Pro (Metalesa)

Inputs and outputs

Cameras identify animals near the carriageway and trigger illuminated warnings. An event log records crossing patterns, times, and activation frequency for evaluation.

Autonomy

The pilot automatically activates the warning signs when it detects a hazard. Drivers remain responsible for slowing or stopping.

Workflow

A road stretch with frequent wildlife crossings. Camera detection tied to dynamic signs powered by solar panels. A road authority committed to evaluating before extending.
