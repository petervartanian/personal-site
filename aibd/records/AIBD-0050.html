Two amputees regain daily activities with Coapt-controlled prostheses

Two transulnar amputees given Coapt pattern-recognition prostheses on osseointegration implants regained activities of daily living.

  • 2 a person

    The authors report that both patients gained enough prosthetic control for activities of daily living, with the most recent follow-ups at 18 months and 2 years. The abstract gives no validated functional scores, so a lasting change is not established.

  • Not rated society

    The report covers two patients at one hospital, and no source reports a realized outcome beyond them, so no societal depth can be established.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
Not isolated. The Coapt software is a machine-learning pattern-recognition controller that maps electromyographic patterns to hand and wrist motions. The report attributes improved control to training with this software on an osseointegrated attachment. So the AI contribution is entangled with the surgical attachment and cannot be isolated.
Limits
Osseointegration, the prosthesis, pattern-recognition control and training were combined, so the two-patient case report cannot isolate the contribution of the Coapt software. Surgery dates are not given in the abstract.
Costs and harms
The second patient had two soft-tissue infections of the stoma that cleared with oral antibiotics. The reported gains combine surgery, pattern-recognition control and training.
Later
Persisted, 31 January 2023. A later randomized home-use study of transradial amputees confirmed broader grip use and better myoelectric control with Coapt pattern recognition than with direct control.

What happened

In an independent study funded by the Department of Veterans Affairs and published 24 December 2018, 11 users of a pattern recognition prototype that Coapt built for the DEKA Arm rated skill, comfort and weight worse than 25 users of foot-worn sensors. A JBJS Case Connector report published 15 December 2021 (Jaime, Reif, Kafedzic, Garrison, Dhawan and Rozbruch) describes two people with traumatic transulnar amputations. Both underwent osseointegration implantation. Surgery dates are not given in the abstract. After surgery a myoelectric prosthesis fitted with Coapt pattern-recognition software was attached directly to the implant. The software classifies electromyographic (EMG) signal patterns to drive a multiarticulating hand and wrist. The full case report adds that the hand fitted was a TASKA hand and that the custom implants came through a humanitarian device exemption. Both patients trained with the software. At the most recent follow-ups of 18 months and 2 years the implants showed no loosening. The authors state that prosthetic control gradually improved to allow activities of daily living. The report describes the return of activities of daily living for the first patient. For the second patient it reports two soft-tissue infections of the stoma that cleared with oral antibiotics and a flexion contracture that improved from 75 to 45 degrees.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Coapt classified each participant's residual-muscle signals and used the predicted movement to control the prosthetic hand and wrist.

Workflow

Stable implanted attachment delivering consistent electrode contact. Pattern-recognition classifier trained on the patient's own EMG patterns. Structured training with the software after surgery.

Human oversight

Surgeons and prosthetists coordinated the implanted attachments and myoelectric prostheses. Both participants trained with pattern recognition software to control the hand and wrist.

Benefits

Reach

The case report describes two people. It describes the return of activities of daily living for the first patient, and for the second it reports two stoma infections and a flexion contracture that improved from 75 to 45 degrees. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The most recent follow-ups were at 18 months and 2 years, when the implants showed no loosening. The 2023 home-use study concerns other users with transradial amputations, so it does not show that the benefit persisted for these two patients. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Sufficient residual muscle signals to separate grip and wrist classes. Osseointegration without loosening over the follow-up period. Clinician-supervised calibration and training.

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.

Evidence

Original evidence

The case report is the only source on the two patients. The 2023 home-use study comes from authors who include the makers. The 2018 Veterans Affairs study and the 2024 eye-tracking study come from independent groups that studied other users of Coapt pattern recognition. [1] [2] [3] [4]
