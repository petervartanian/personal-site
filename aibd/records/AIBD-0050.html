Coapt pattern recognition restores daily living for two amputees

Two transulnar amputees given Coapt pattern-recognition prostheses on osseointegration implants regained activities of daily living.

ⓘ

What happened

A JBJS Case Connector report published 15 December 2021 (Jaime, Reif, Kafedzic, Garrison, Dhawan and Rozbruch) describes two people with traumatic transulnar amputations. Both underwent osseointegration implantation. After surgery a myoelectric prosthesis fitted with Coapt pattern-recognition software was attached directly to the implant. The software classifies electromyographic (EMG) signal patterns to drive a multiarticulating hand and wrist. Both trained with the software. At the most recent follow-ups of 18 months and 2 years the implants showed no loosening. The authors state that prosthetic control gradually improved to allow activities of daily living. Surgery dates are not given in the abstract.

Purpose. Pattern-recognition control of a multiarticulating myoelectric hand and wrist for people with upper-limb amputation

AI’s contribution

The Coapt software is a machine-learning pattern-recognition controller that maps electromyographic patterns to hand and wrist motions. The report attributes improved control to training with this software on an osseointegrated attachment. So the AI contribution is entangled with the surgical attachment and cannot be isolated.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Two identifiable patients gained prosthetic control enough for daily living activities, documented at 18-month and 2-year follow-up. The abstract has no validated functional scores, so life-changing is unsupported.

  • society

    A two-patient case report demonstrating feasibility of combining osseointegration with pattern-recognition control; no population-level effect shown.

Since then
A later randomized home-use study of transradial amputees confirmed broader grip use and better myoelectric control with Coapt pattern recognition than with direct control.While at home, participants demonstrated broader usage of grips in pattern recognition compared to direct control. source

Further evidence

Setting. Stable implanted attachment delivering consistent electrode contact. Pattern-recognition classifier trained on the patient's own EMG patterns. Structured training with the software after surgery.

Conditions. Sufficient residual muscle signals to separate grip and wrist classes. Osseointegration without loosening over the follow-up period. Clinician-supervised calibration and training.
