Coapt pattern recognition restores daily living for two amputees

depth 2 for a person, depth 1 for society

Two transulnar amputees given Coapt pattern-recognition prostheses on osseointegration implants regained activities of daily living.

What happened

A JBJS Case Connector report published 15 December 2021 (Jaime, Reif, Kafedzic, Garrison, Dhawan and Rozbruch) describes two people with traumatic transulnar amputations who underwent osseointegration implantation. After surgery a myoelectric prosthesis fitted with Coapt pattern-recognition software, which classifies electromyographic signal patterns to drive a multiarticulating hand and wrist, was attached directly to the implant, and both patients trained with the software. At the most recent follow-ups of 18 months and 2 years the implants showed no loosening, and the authors state that prosthetic control gradually improved to allow activities of daily living. Surgery dates are not given in the fetched abstract, so the publication date is used.

What it was built to do. Pattern-recognition control of a multiarticulating myoelectric hand and wrist for people with upper-limb amputation

The system's part

The Coapt software is a machine-learning pattern-recognition controller that maps electromyographic patterns to hand and wrist motions; the report attributes improved control to training with this software on an osseointegrated attachment, so the AI contribution is entangled with the surgical attachment and cannot be isolated.

Replication

What produced it. Stable implanted attachment delivering consistent electrode contact. Pattern-recognition classifier trained on the patient's own EMG patterns. Structured training with the software after surgery.

What must hold again. Sufficient residual muscle signals to separate grip and wrist classes. Osseointegration without loosening over the follow-up period. Clinician-supervised calibration and training.
