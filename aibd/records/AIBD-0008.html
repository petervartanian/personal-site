IIHS finds FCW and AEB cut rear-end striking crash rates 27–50%

reduced rear-end striking crash involvement rates by 27%, 43% and 50%

IIHS: FCW alone, low-speed AEB and FCW+AEB cut rear-end striking crash rates 27%, 43%, 50% in 22 US states, 2010–2014 (cohort)

What happened

In Accident Analysis and Prevention volume 99 (February 2017; the first day of that month is used as the date), Jessica Cicchino of the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety compared police-reported crash involvements per insured vehicle year in 22 US states during 2010–2014 between passenger vehicle models with optional forward collision warning (FCW) alone or FCW with autonomous emergency braking (AEB) and the same models without the option, and between Volvo 2011–2012 S60 and 2010–2012 XC60 vehicles with standard low-speed AEB and other luxury midsize cars and SUVs without it. FCW alone, low-speed AEB and FCW with AEB reduced rear-end striking crash involvement rates by 27%, 43% and 50%, and injury crash rates by 20%, 45% and 56%; FCW with AEB increased rear-end struck involvements by 20%. No individual avoided crash is documented.

What it was built to do. Built to warn drivers and brake automatically when a frontal collision is imminent; the measured reduction in rear-end striking crashes is that purpose.

The system's part

Front crash prevention systems are automated perception-and-braking systems; the within-model comparisons (FCW alone, FCW with AEB) and the comparison of Volvo S60/XC60 with other luxury midsize cars and SUVs (low-speed AEB) control for other factors but shows effectiveness as a rate, not any single avoided crash. Whether such systems count as AI is a definitional choice the sources do not settle.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • Vehicle fitted with FCW and/or AEB
  • Rear-end conflict within the system's operating speed range

What must hold again

  • FCW and FCW+AEB comparisons use the same vehicle models with and without the optional system, controlling for other crash-risk factors; the low-speed AEB comparison uses Volvo S60/XC60 models against other luxury midsize cars and SUVs without the system
  • Results are rates per insured vehicle year, not individual events
