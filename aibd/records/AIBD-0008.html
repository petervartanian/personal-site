Crash warnings and automatic braking cut rear-end crash rates up to 50%

In an Insurance Institute for Highway Safety study, forward collision warning alone, low-speed emergency braking and warning with braking cut rear-end striking crash rates by 27%, 43% and 50%.

  • 5 society

    Across insured vehicles in 22 states, rates of rear-end striking crashes fell 27%, 43% and 50% for the three system types, and rates of those crashes with injuries fell 20%, 45% and 56%. This is a change documented across a vehicle population. The study does not report the severity of the injuries.

What happened

Jessica Cicchino of the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety measured police-reported crash involvements per insured vehicle year in 22 U.S. states during 2010–2014. She compared passenger vehicle models with optional forward collision warning alone, or with forward collision warning and autonomous emergency braking, to the same models without these systems. She also compared Volvo 2011–2012 S60 and 2010–2012 XC60 vehicles, which had standard low-speed autonomous emergency braking, to other luxury midsize cars and sport utility vehicles without it. She published the study in Accident Analysis and Prevention volume 99 in February 2017. Forward collision warning alone, low-speed autonomous emergency braking and warning with braking reduced rear-end striking crash involvement rates by 27%, 43% and 50%. Injury crash rates fell 20%, 45% and 56%. Warning with braking increased rear-end struck involvements by 20%. No individual avoided crash is documented. In its proposed rule of 13 June 2023, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration cited this study and adopted its finding that forward collision warning cut rear-end crashes by 27 percent. On 9 May 2024 the agency adopted a final rule that makes autonomous emergency braking mandatory on new light vehicles from 1 September 2029. On 17 January 2025 Auto Innovators, an industry group, asked the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit to review the rule. The case has been on hold since February 2025. On 23 March 2026 the Department of Transportation told the court that it is preparing a proposal to amend the rule. On 22 April 2026 the National Law Review reported that the proposal would extend the compliance lead time by two years.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

Forward collision warning alerted the driver. Versions with automatic emergency braking could apply the brakes within their operating conditions.

Workflow

Vehicle fitted with FCW and/or AEB. Rear-end conflict within the system's operating speed range.

Human oversight

Forward collision warning alerts the driver, who decides how to respond. Autonomous emergency braking brakes the car automatically when a frontal collision is imminent. The sources describe no further safeguards. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Reach

The author reports rates per insured vehicle year and gives no count of the people or crashes spared. No individual avoided crash is documented. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The crash rates cover 2010–2014 in 22 states. The federal rule whose proposal cited the study makes autonomous emergency braking mandatory on new light vehicles from 1 September 2029. The rule is under challenge in court. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Measurement

Measurement

Poisson regression of police-reported crash involvements per insured vehicle year, controlling for other factors that affect crash risk.

Setting and sample

Passenger vehicles in 22 U.S. States, 2010 to 2014; models with optional FCW or FCW with AEB, and Volvo 2011-2012 S60 and 2010-2012 XC60 with standard low-speed AEB.

Measures

Rear-end striking crash involvement rates, with and without injuries.

Results

Rear-end striking crash rates fell 27% (FCW alone), 43% (low-speed AEB) and 50% (FCW with AEB); injury crash rates fell 20%, 45% and 56%.

Limitations

Owners chose optional systems, so buyers may differ from other drivers; the low-speed AEB comparison uses different models; FCW with AEB raised rates of being struck from behind by 20%.

Denominators

Insured vehicle years in the study states.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

FCW and FCW+AEB comparisons use the same vehicle models with and without the optional system, controlling for other crash-risk factors. The low-speed AEB comparison uses Volvo S60/XC60 models against other luxury midsize cars and SUVs without the system. Results are rates per insured vehicle year, not individual events.
