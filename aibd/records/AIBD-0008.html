What happened

Jessica Cicchino (Insurance Institute for Highway Safety) published in Accident Analysis and Prevention volume 99 (February 2017; dated to the first of the month). Her measure was police-reported crash involvements per insured vehicle year in 22 US states during 2010–2014. She compared passenger vehicle models with optional forward collision warning (FCW) alone or FCW with autonomous emergency braking (AEB) to the same models without it. She also compared Volvo 2011–2012 S60 and 2010–2012 XC60 vehicles with standard low-speed AEB to other luxury midsize cars and SUVs without it. FCW alone, low-speed AEB and FCW with AEB reduced rear-end striking crash involvement rates by 27%, 43% and 50%. Injury crash rates fell 20%, 45% and 56%. FCW with AEB increased rear-end struck involvements by 20%. No individual avoided crash is documented.

What it was built to do. Built to warn drivers and brake automatically when a frontal collision is imminent. The measured reduction in rear-end striking crashes is that purpose.

The system's part

Front crash prevention systems are automated perception-and-braking systems. The within-model comparisons (FCW alone, FCW with AEB) and Volvo S60/XC60 comparison with other luxury midsize cars and SUVs (low-speed AEB) control for other factors. They show effectiveness as a rate, not any single avoided crash. Whether such systems count as AI is a definitional choice the sources do not settle.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Vehicle fitted with FCW and/or AEB. Rear-end conflict within the system's operating speed range.

What must hold again. FCW and FCW+AEB comparisons use the same vehicle models with and without the optional system, controlling for other crash-risk factors. The low-speed AEB comparison uses Volvo S60/XC60 models against other luxury midsize cars and SUVs without the system. Results are rates per insured vehicle year, not individual events.

Harm on record

AI Incident Database 341, Nissan's "Automatic Emergency Braking" False Positives Posed Traffic Risks to Drivers. Automatic emergency braking on passenger cars: Nissan's AEB false positives caused abrupt braking that endangered its drivers and other traffic participants, the road users whose rear-end crash rates FCW and AEB reduced in the IIHS data.
