FCW and AEB cut rear-end striking crash rates 27–50%

IIHS: FCW alone, low-speed AEB and FCW+AEB cut rear-end striking crash rates 27%, 43%, 50% in 22 US states, 2010–2014 (cohort)

Front crash prevention systems (FCW, low-speed AEB, FCW with AEB) on passenger vehicles from multiple automakers, including Volvo's standard low-speed AEB on 2011–2012 S60 and 2010–2012 XC60

Relation to AI

Front crash prevention systems are automated perception-and-braking systems. The within-model comparisons (FCW alone, FCW with AEB) and Volvo S60/XC60 comparison with other luxury midsize cars and SUVs (low-speed AEB) control for other factors. They show effectiveness as a rate, not any single avoided crash. Whether such systems count as AI is a definitional choice the sources do not settle.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

FCW and FCW+AEB comparisons use the same vehicle models with and without the optional system, controlling for other crash-risk factors. The low-speed AEB comparison uses Volvo S60/XC60 models against other luxury midsize cars and SUVs without the system. Results are rates per insured vehicle year, not individual events.
