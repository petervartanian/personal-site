Sanyo Electric Railway uses AI cameras to spot people left in crossings

In July 2021 Sanyo Electric Railway began to use Optage's AI analysis of crossing camera video to detect people left inside two crossings and warn train drivers.

What happened

On 11 August 2020 Optage announced a field trial of AI image analysis of crossing surveillance cameras. Mynavi News reported on 12 August 2020, from the press release, that Optage, Sanyo Electric Railway and the Sanyo Electric Railway Information Center had begun the trial. It ran from early August to mid-October 2020 at three crossings on Sanyo Electric Railway lines. Edge devices analyze the video in real time to detect people, including those with prams or wheelchairs. The aim is to stop trains safely by signaling the driver through a special signal lamp and notifying the operations center over an IP-VPN. Accuracy in rain and at night was to be verified with K4Digital. Full deployment was targeted for spring 2021. The trial announcement reported no results, and the OECD AI Incidents Monitor entry notes that no incidents had been reported. Optage reported on 29 June 2021 that Sanyo had adopted the system after the trial. Optage reports that the system offers comparable function at lower cost than laser radar. It supplies no numerical detection results or measured reduction in accidents. Kobe Shimbun reported on 3 August 2021 that Sanyo had put the system into use from July 2021 at two busy crossings, the Shikama depot crossing in Himeji and the Tsukimiyama crossing in Suma, Kobe. The paper notes that 137 of Sanyo's 165 crossings already had automatic detectors that have difficulty detecting people. Sanyo's electrical systems section manager told the paper that the company hoped to add sites after reviewing the results. The paper reports no detections or measured safety results after the rollout.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Edge devices analyzed crossing-camera video for people and sent warnings through a signal lamp and an IP-VPN connection to operations staff.

Workflow

Existing crossing surveillance cameras. Edge-device inference and a special signal lamp link. A telecom partner supplying the analysis.

Human oversight

The system signals the train driver through a special signal lamp and notifies the operations center over a private network (IP-VPN). No source describes how staff review the alerts. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

AI contribution

Software on edge devices analyzes crossing camera video to detect people and signals the driver through a special signal lamp. Kobe Shimbun reports that 137 of Sanyo's 165 crossings already had automatic detectors that have difficulty detecting people.
