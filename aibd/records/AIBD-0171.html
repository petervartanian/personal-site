What happened

ENEA, Politecnico di Bari and Università Roma Tre tested machine-learning forecasts of heatwave-related failures in a large distribution grid in southern Italy. ENEA's announcement of 18 May 2023 says that training used failures from 2015 to 2020, temperature, humidity and energy flows, followed by tests on unseen inputs. The researchers proposed that operators use the forecasts for corrective maintenance. The announcement reports neither a named utility deployment nor an avoided outage. ENEA's Maria Valenti said that most failures in the studied network occurred at cable joints. The study appeared in IEEE Transactions on Industry Applications in 2023 under the title "A Machine Learning Approach to Fault Prediction of Power Distribution Grids under Heatwaves." In 2025 a Politecnico di Milano team tested similar forecasts on the Milan distribution network. The authors of the Italian study posted a copy of the paper on TechRxiv on 7 October 2025. No source reports that the grid operator or any other utility adopted the model.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The model used grid failures recorded from 2015 to 2020, ambient temperature, humidity, and energy flows to predict failures in a large distribution network in southern Italy.

Workflow

Historical grid-failure and temperature data for Italian urban networks. A research consortium of a public agency and two universities.

Human oversight

The researchers proposed that distribution operators use the forecasts to plan corrective work, especially during the May-to-September period when heat-related failures were concentrated.
