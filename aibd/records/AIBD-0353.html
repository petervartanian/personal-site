Türkiye quake: Mappers using AI-assisted RapiD were up to 12.4% slower

Researchers found that contributors using the AI-assisted RapiD editor mapped up to 12.4% slower after the 2023 Türkiye earthquake; the OpenStreetMap wiki reports that it was then disabled.

What happened

After the M7.8 earthquake in Türkiye on 6 February 2023, the Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT) activated, led by the Turkish community Yer Çizenler. It ran Tasking Manager projects for roads and buildings in Türkiye and Syria. HOT's own activation and size-up pages (Internet Archive captures of 7 February and 29 May 2023) mention no AI tool and list no partner requests. Tasking Manager records for the 11 Türkiye projects, which Yer Çizenler ran on HOT's platform, show that the projects were set up with MapWithAI suggestions in the Rapid editor, which Meta maintains. The OpenStreetMap (OSM) wiki page for the response is community-authored and was last modified 5 June 2026. It states that, because of the large project areas, AI-assisted mapping with the RapiD editor was originally encouraged. It proved counter-productive and was then disabled. Research from the University of Colorado Boulder and Indiana University, shared with HOT in August 2023, compared 1,650 Tasking Manager contributors before and during the response. The wiki's only citation for this study is a HOT meeting recording of 9 August 2023. Contributors using RapiD mapped tasks up to 12.4% slower than those not using AI-assisted mapping. Validators reported many data-quality issues from RapiD contributors, which led to RapiD being disabled on all activation projects. The archived settings of all 11 Türkiye projects still list Rapid as an editor. The same wiki page records that OSM data supported rescue coordination by the AKUT Search and Rescue Association and AFAD. It also supported public-health programming in the Kahramanmaraş tent city and the World Bank's GRADE damage report. No cited source documents Microsoft building footprints or HOT's fAIr here, nor any AI role in HOT's 2022 Pakistan floods activation. No cited source covers Cyclone Idai.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

MapWithAI AI-predicted buildings and roads, offered in the Rapid editor (maintained by Meta) and in the JOSM MapWithAI plugin

Workflow

A HOT activation with large Tasking Manager project areas and Maxar imagery. RapiD AI-assisted editing offered to volunteer contributors. Validators reviewing contributions and feeding back on quality. Independent researchers comparing 1,650 contributors before and during the response.

Human oversight

Human validators reported data-quality problems from RapiD contributors. The OpenStreetMap wiki reports that this feedback led to RapiD being disabled on all activation projects. The archived settings of the 11 Türkiye projects still list Rapid as an editor. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Rescue and recovery organizations used the resulting OSM data, but their benefit cannot be assigned to the AI-assisted portion that was later disabled.

Reach

No source counts beneficiaries. The researchers compared 1,650 Tasking Manager contributors and found that contributors using RapiD mapped tasks up to 12.4% slower. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The OpenStreetMap wiki reports that RapiD was disabled on all activation projects after validators' complaints, without giving a date. The archived settings of the 11 Türkiye projects still list Rapid as an editor, so the extent of the withdrawal is uncertain. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Volunteer contributors of mixed experience using AI suggestions without extra validation. AI-suggested features (for example roads defaulting to “residential”) not corrected before upload. Validator capacity to detect and report quality problems.
