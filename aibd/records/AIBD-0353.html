What happened

After the M7.8 earthquake in Türkiye on 6 February 2023, the Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT) activated, led by the Turkish community Yer Çizenler. It ran Tasking Manager projects for roads and buildings in Türkiye and Syria. The OpenStreetMap (OSM) wiki page for the response is community-authored and was last modified 5 June 2026. It states that, because of the large project areas, AI-assisted mapping with the RapiD editor was originally encouraged. It proved counter-productive and was then disabled. Research from the University of Colorado Boulder and Indiana University, shared with HOT in August 2023, compared 1,650 Tasking Manager contributors before and during it. Contributors using RapiD mapped tasks up to 12.4% slower than those not using AI-assisted mapping.

Validators reported many data-quality issues from RapiD contributors, which led to RapiD being disabled on all activation projects. The same page records that OSM data supported rescue coordination by the AKUT Search and Rescue Association and AFAD. It also supported public-health programming in the Kahramanmaraş tent city and the World Bank's GRADE damage report. HOT's own activation and size-up pages (Internet Archive captures of 7 February and 29 May 2023) mention no AI tool and list no partner requests. No source on file documents Microsoft building footprints, HOT's fAIr or Meta maps here, nor any AI role in HOT's 2022 Pakistan floods activation. Cyclone Idai material was not found.

What it was built to do. AI-assisted mapping was encouraged to speed building and road mapping over large areas for responders. The evidence shows it slowed mapping and lowered quality in this activation. The claimed benefit was not delivered.

The system's part

The AI component (RapiD AI-assisted mapping) is documented by the community-authored OSM wiki as counter-productive. It was up to 12.4% slower at task mapping, with validator-reported quality problems, and was then disabled on all activation projects. Use of OSM data by AKUT, AFAD, a tent-city health team and the World Bank is documented on the same page. It is not tied to AI-derived data. HOT's activation and size-up pages, from the Internet Archive, contain no AI mention. No source on file documents Microsoft building footprints, fAIr or Meta maps in the 2023 Türkiye, 2022 Pakistan or 2019 Idai responses.

Replication

What produced it. A HOT activation with large Tasking Manager project areas and Maxar imagery. RapiD AI-assisted editing offered to volunteer contributors. Validators reviewing contributions and feeding back on quality. Independent researchers comparing 1,650 contributors before and during the response.

What must hold again. Volunteer contributors of mixed experience using AI suggestions without extra validation. AI-suggested features (for example roads defaulting to “residential”) not corrected before upload. Validator capacity to detect and report quality problems.
