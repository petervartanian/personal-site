Participants in the UN Women AI School build custom GPTs for advocacy

UN Women’s AI School has participants use ChatGPT, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot and build custom GPTs for research and advocacy; no source measures what the AI changed.

  • Not rated a person

    Participants use generative AI in practical tasks and custom GPT development. The extent of any learning improvement caused by AI is not measured; attendance and satisfaction describe the overall program.

  • Not rated an organization

    Custom GPTs and AI-assisted research or knowledge products are intended to improve UN and civil-society work. No separate workplace productivity or quality improvement is measured.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

Not isolated. Generative AI contributes directly to participants’ research, drafting and custom-GPT exercises. Human teaching also contributes; program attendance and satisfaction do not isolate AI’s learning benefit.
Limits
Training attendance and a stated 100% satisfaction rate do not establish skill gains, better workplace outputs, employment, or gender equality. The survey denominator and response rate are absent, and the contribution of AI tools is not separated from human teaching. The 1,200-person target concerns a later planned cohort.
Costs and harms
No source reports costs or harms from the AI School. In the linked AI Incident Database incident 1193, a Deloitte report for the Australian Government allegedly contained AI-generated citations and a fabricated legal quote. In both cases staff use generative AI to research and draft knowledge products for an organization. No source reports that such an error occurred in the AI School.

What happened

Participants in the UN Women AI School use ChatGPT, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot for research, knowledge products, content creation and advocacy. Activities include developing custom GPTs for policy briefs, proposals and other work. The program links to custom GPTs for humanitarian work, technology-facilitated gender-based violence and women’s empowerment principles. UN Women reports that young leaders from the 30 for 2030 network built a custom GPT for the Beijing+30 review with the AI School and showed it at the ministerial conference in Bangkok on 19 November 2024. UN Women opened the 2025 edition as an online program on 5 March 2025. In the ITU account, current as of June 2025, the project team reports attendance and satisfaction for the overall program. The team does not measure the effect of AI on learning or workplace performance. No evaluation of what participants learned or produced with AI had been published by October 2026.
