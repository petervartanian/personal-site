ChatGPT shortens science teachers' lesson preparation

In a 2024 trial with 259 science teachers in 68 schools, teachers told to use ChatGPT spent 56.2 minutes a week on lesson preparation against 81.5, with no evidence of lower quality.

What happened

The Education Endowment Foundation and the Hg Foundation commissioned the National Foundation for Educational Research (NFER) in December 2023 to test whether ChatGPT reduces teachers' workload. In 2024, 259 teachers of Year 7 and Year 8 science from 68 schools took part in a school-randomized trial that lasted ten weeks of the summer term. Teachers in the ChatGPT group were asked to use ChatGPT to prepare lessons and resources and received an online guide. Teachers in the other group were asked not to use any generative AI tool for this work. In weeks six to ten, a typical teacher in the comparison group spent 81.5 minutes a week preparing these lessons and a teacher in the ChatGPT group spent about 56.2 minutes, a saving of 25.3 minutes a week, or 31 percent. An expert panel that did not know which group made each resource found no evidence that the quality of the resources differed. Teachers used ChatGPT mostly for one or two activities per lesson, such as quizzes or new activity ideas. The trial measured teacher time and resource quality and did not measure student learning. NFER published the results on 12 December 2024.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Teachers received an online guide to using ChatGPT. Teachers used ChatGPT for parts of a lesson, most often questions, quizzes and activity ideas.

Measurement

Measurement

Teacher-reported weekly lesson and resource preparation time; blind expert panel review of sampled resources. [1]

Setting and sample

259 Year 7 and Year 8 science teachers in 68 schools, ten weeks in the 2024 summer term. [1]

Measures

Minutes per week of lesson and resource preparation. [1]

Results

56.2 against 81.5 minutes a week (69 percent of the comparison group's time). [1]

Additional value from AI

The time saving is attributed to ChatGPT use with the guide. [1]

Limitations

Time was self-reported; teachers used the approach for part of their timetable; student outcomes were not measured. [1]

Denominators

Ten weeks of lesson preparation for Year 7 and Year 8 science. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Teachers need access to ChatGPT and time to learn it. Teachers must check the resources that ChatGPT produces.

Entry details

Sector

Education.

Purpose

ChatGPT is a general-purpose chatbot. In this trial it was used to help teachers prepare lessons, and the time saving was the purpose of the trial.
