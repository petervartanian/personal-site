Milton Keynes reports faster planning validation with Valon

Milton Keynes reports faster validation and decisions during its Valon pilot, with receipt-to-validation time falling from 15.8 to 7.6 days.

ⓘ

What happened

Milton Keynes piloted Valon for planning work from October to December 2025. The council reports that officers validated applications 60–80% faster. Its analysis of the planning register put average receipt-to-validation time at 15.8 days before and 7.6 days during the pilot, and validation-to-decision time at 53.1 and 43.2 days. The estimated 1,360 officer hours per year is an extrapolation from an assumed 80% saving. Valon’s own account describes major-application checks falling from about 65 to 10 minutes and non-major checks from 15 to 5 minutes. It reports 7.7 days for early-November validation turnaround and a separate 18% decision-time difference against a non-pilot group. The two accounts do not publish the underlying timesheets, selected application records, sample sizes, or rules for constructing the comparisons.

These are reported efficiency gains, with uncertainty about how much AI caused. The council purchased a one-year license after the pilot and reports continued use.

Purpose. Support planning officers from application validation through decision-making.

AI’s contribution

The council used Valon to read and check planning documents and present information for officer review. The council and developer describe shorter processing times, but provide no reproducible analysis that isolates AI from case mix, staffing, or other workflow changes. Planning decisions remain officers’ responsibility.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    The council and developer report shorter validation and decision-processing times. The underlying samples and comparison methods are not published, so the gain remains a first-party estimate.

  • a person

    Officers report less processing work, and the council reports shorter waits for applicants. No improvement in decision quality or realized housing delivery is demonstrated.

Comparison

Compared with
No comparison cited.
Since then
The council reports that it purchased a one-year license after the pilot and that officers continue using Valon. Integration with Arcus is being scoped, and rollout to all planning officers remains an ambition.

Further evidence

Setting. A nominated council pilot group using Valon on live planning applications from October to December 2025. Document retrieval from the public planning register, officer training, and regular feedback to the developer.

Conditions. Officers must review the information that the system supplies and retain responsibility for planning decisions. The system must reflect the authority’s validation requirements and have access to the necessary application documents. Comparable timings and case characteristics are needed to determine whether similar savings recur.
