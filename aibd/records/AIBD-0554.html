Milton Keynes council checks planning applications faster with Valon

Milton Keynes City Council reports that receipt-to-validation time for planning applications fell from 15.8 to 7.6 days while its officers piloted the Valon AI planning platform.

  • 1 an organization

    The council and developer report shorter validation and decision-processing times. The underlying samples and comparison methods are not published, so the gain remains a first-party estimate.

  • 1 a person

    Officers report less processing work, and the council reports shorter waits for applicants. No improvement in decision quality or realized housing delivery is demonstrated.

What happened

Milton Keynes piloted Valon for planning work from October to December 2025. The council reports that officers validated applications 60–80% faster. Its analysis of the planning register put average receipt-to-validation time at 15.8 days before and 7.6 days during the pilot, and validation-to-decision time at 53.1 and 43.2 days. The council writes that it built its evidence base from officer timesheets and data from the public planning register. The estimated 1,360 officer hours per year is an extrapolation from an assumed 80% saving. Valon’s own account describes major-application checks falling from about 65 to 10 minutes and non-major checks from 15 to 5 minutes. It reports 7.7 days for early-November validation turnaround and a separate 18% decision-time difference against a non-pilot group. Neither account publishes the underlying timesheets, selected application records, sample sizes, or rules for constructing the comparisons. The gains are reported, and how much of them the AI caused is uncertain. The council purchased a one-year license after the pilot and reports continued use (Local Government Association case study, 8 May 2026). Integration with Arcus is being scoped, and rollout to all planning officers remains an ambition.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Submitted planning documents become validation suggestions with links back to the relevant evidence. [1]

Workflow

A nominated council pilot group using Valon on live planning applications from October to December 2025. Document retrieval from the public planning register, officer training, and regular feedback to the developer.

Human oversight

Planning officers verify Valon’s findings before taking action. [1]

AI contribution

Valon read planning documents, checked them, and presented information to officers. The council and developer report faster processing but do not isolate AI from differences in applications, staffing, or workflow. Officers retained responsibility for decisions. [1] [2]

Benefits

Duration

The pilot ran from October to December 2025. The council then bought a one-year license and reports that officers continue to use Valon, but no source reports timings after the pilot. [1] [2]

Measurement

Measurement

The council analyzed its public planning register and officer timesheets.

Setting and sample

Milton Keynes City Council planning service, October to December 2025; the number of applications is not stated.

Measures

Average days from receipt to validation and from validation to decision.

Results

Receipt to validation fell from 15.8 to 7.6 days; validation to decision fell from 53.1 to 43.2 days.

Additional value from AI

Not isolated. The council reports no control for case mix or staffing.

Limitations

The council publishes no sample sizes and no rules for selecting the applications. The two accounts give different validation figures (7.6 and 7.7 days).

Denominators

A nominated pilot group of validation and development management officers.

Materials for replication

The planning register is public. The council's timesheets and analysis are not published.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Officers must review the information that the system supplies and retain responsibility for planning decisions. The system must reflect the authority’s validation requirements and have access to the necessary application documents. Comparable timings and case characteristics are needed to determine whether similar savings recur. [1] [2]

Evidence

Original evidence

The council and developer report the results but publish no underlying timesheets or analysis. Their measures of officer time and elapsed processing time differ. The developer reports 7.7 days and the council 7.6 days, without explaining the difference. [1] [2]
