Raleigh police use Traffic Jam to link a trafficker's movements

CBN (18 Oct 2017) shows Raleigh PD detectives using Traffic Jam, a CMU-born tool with facial recognition over escort ads, to corroborate a trafficker's movements; outcomes stated only as aggregates.

ⓘ

What happened

The case described was ongoing. Major Richard Hoffman of the Raleigh Police Department described Traffic Jam to CBN. It lets investigators link a potential victim to another victim and then to a suspect. Its image search returns historical images tied to the same phone number or similar image. Detective Rob Pereira described pulling a phone number from a Colorado police report into Traffic Jam to see where and when it had been used. This corroborated several victims' accounts of where the trafficker was taking them. Marinus co-founder Cara Jones described the tool as applying artificial intelligence to publicly available ad data. CBN states that Traffic Jam has helped recover hundreds of victims. Futurism (2018) repeats a similar claim from CEO Emily Kennedy with an 88 percent success rate. No individually attributable rescue is documented.

Purpose. Mine online sex advertisements (text, phone numbers, facial recognition on images) to help law enforcement find trafficking victims and link cases.

AI’s contribution

Named Raleigh officers describe what the tool does for them (phone-number and image linkage across cities). The claims that it has led to hundreds of recoveries come from the vendor, relayed by CBN and Futurism without a documented case.

Further evidence

Setting. Continuous scraping of escort ads since 2011. Phone-number and image-similarity indexing across cities. Detectives with an existing lead (phone number or photo).

Conditions. Ads must remain publicly scrapable (Backpage was shut in 2018). Agency access and training.
