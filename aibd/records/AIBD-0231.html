Raleigh police use Traffic Jam to link a trafficker's movements

Raleigh police detectives used Traffic Jam, a Marinus Analytics tool that searches escort ads by phone number and face, to corroborate victims' accounts of a trafficker's movements.

What happened

The case described was ongoing. Major Richard Hoffman of the Raleigh Police Department described Traffic Jam to CBN. It lets investigators link a potential victim to another victim and then to a suspect. Its image search returns historical images tied to the same phone number or similar image. Detective Rob Pereira described pulling a phone number from a Colorado police report into Traffic Jam to see where and when it had been used. This corroborated several victims' accounts of where the trafficker was taking them. Marinus co-founder Cara Jones described the tool as applying artificial intelligence to publicly available ad data. CBN states that Traffic Jam has helped recover hundreds of victims. Futurism (2018) repeats a similar claim from chief executive Emily Kennedy with an 88 percent success rate. No individually attributable rescue is documented.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

Traffic Jam generated investigative links; detectives interpreted those links and built the case.

Workflow

Detective Pereira searched a phone number from a Colorado police report, reviewed its locations and dates in advertisements, and used the matches to corroborate victims’ accounts.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Raleigh detectives obtained corroborating leads, while trafficking victims were the intended downstream beneficiaries. The reported hundreds of recoveries cannot be attributed to this investigation.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Ads must remain publicly scrapable (Backpage was shut in 2018). Agency access and training.

Evidence

Original evidence

Futurism attributes its recovery claim to chief executive Emily Kennedy of Marinus Analytics. CBN states that Traffic Jam has helped recover hundreds of victims. CBN names no source or case for that figure. The Raleigh officers' description of their own use is the only account from outside the company. Pereira's corroboration rests on accounts that the victims had already given. [1] [2]
