Raleigh police use Traffic Jam to link a trafficker's movements

off the scale

CBN states that Traffic Jam has helped recover hundreds of victims

CBN (18 Oct 2017) shows Raleigh PD detectives using Traffic Jam, a CMU-born tool with facial recognition over escort ads, to corroborate a trafficker's movements; outcomes stated only as aggregates.

What happened

The date is CBN's publication; the case described was ongoing. Major Richard Hoffman of the Raleigh Police Department told CBN that Traffic Jam lets investigators link a potential victim to another victim and then to a suspect, and that its image search returns historical images tied to the same phone number or similar image. Detective Rob Pereira described pulling a phone number from a Colorado police report into Traffic Jam to see where and when it had been used, corroborating accounts from several victims about where the trafficker was taking them. Marinus co-founder Cara Jones described the tool as applying artificial intelligence to publicly available ad data. CBN states that Traffic Jam has helped recover hundreds of victims; Futurism (2018) repeats a similar claim from CEO Emily Kennedy with an 88 percent success rate. No individually attributable rescue is documented.

What it was built to do. Mine online sex advertisements (text, phone numbers, facial recognition on images) to help law enforcement find trafficking victims and link cases.

The system's part

Named Raleigh officers describe what the tool does for them (phone-number and image linkage across cities); the claims that it has led to hundreds of recoveries come from the vendor, relayed by CBN and Futurism without a documented case.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Continuous scraping of escort ads since 2011. Phone-number and image-similarity indexing across cities. Detectives with an existing lead (phone number or photo).

What must hold again. Ads must remain publicly scrapable (Backpage was shut in 2018). Agency access and training.
