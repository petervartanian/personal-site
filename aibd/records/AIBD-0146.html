HelioLinc3D finds asteroid 2022 SF289 in sparse ATLAS detections

depth 2 for an organization, depth 2 for society

about 600 feet across with an Earth MOID of 0.0015 AU

HelioLinc3D linked sparse ATLAS detections into PHA 2022 SF289, published by the Minor Planet Center in MPEC 2023-O26 (Jul 2023).

What happened

On 18 July 2023, during a test run on archival data from the NASA-funded ATLAS survey operated by the University of Hawaii, the HelioLinc3D linking algorithm developed by Ari Heinze and colleagues at the University of Washington's DiRAC Institute for the Vera C. Rubin Observatory identified a previously unrecognised near-Earth asteroid, 2022 SF289. ATLAS had first imaged the object on 19 September 2022 and observed it three times across four nights, fewer than the same-night detections conventional discovery requires. The Minor Planet Center published the object in MPEC 2023-O26, issued 18 July 2023, as an Apollo-type potentially hazardous asteroid about 600 feet across with an Earth MOID of 0.0015 AU; it poses no foreseeable risk to Earth. The sources describe HelioLinc3D as an algorithm, not a learned model.

What it was built to do. Built as Rubin Observatory's linking algorithm to discover moving objects, including near-Earth asteroids, from sparse multi-night detections; this benefit was that purpose.

The system's part

HelioLinc3D linked detections from separate nights that conventional pipelines could not combine, producing the orbit that the Minor Planet Center published. Caveat: UW, UH, Rubin and MPC sources call it a 'next-generation algorithm'; no fetched source describes it as machine learning or AI, so its status as an 'AI system' is a scope decision for the database.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Survey detections too sparse per night for conventional linking. Heliocentric linking across multiple nights. Submission to and designation by the Minor Planet Center.

What must hold again. Astrometry from several nights must be precise enough to constrain a heliocentric orbit. The Minor Planet Center must confirm the linkage as a new object.
