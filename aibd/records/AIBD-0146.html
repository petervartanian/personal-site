What happened

On 18 July 2023 the HelioLinc3D linking algorithm identified an unrecognized near-Earth asteroid, 2022 SF289. It was a test on archival data from the NASA-funded ATLAS survey operated by the University of Hawaii (UH). HelioLinc3D was developed by Ari Heinze and colleagues at the University of Washington's (UW) DiRAC Institute for the Vera C. Rubin Observatory. ATLAS had first imaged the object on 19 September 2022 and observed it three times across four nights. Conventional discovery requires more same-night detections. The Minor Planet Center (MPC) published the object in MPEC 2023-O26, issued 18 July 2023. It is an Apollo-type potentially hazardous asteroid about 600 feet across with an Earth MOID of 0.0015 AU and no foreseeable risk to Earth. The sources call HelioLinc3D an algorithm, not a learned model.

What it was built to do. Built as Rubin Observatory's linking algorithm to discover moving objects, including near-Earth asteroids, from sparse multi-night detections. This benefit was that purpose.

The system's part

HelioLinc3D linked detections from separate nights that conventional pipelines could not combine, producing the orbit that the MPC published. Caveat: UW, UH, Rubin and MPC sources call it a “next-generation algorithm”. No source on file describes it as machine learning or AI, so its status as an “AI system” is a scope decision for the database.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Survey detections too sparse per night for conventional linking. Heliocentric linking across multiple nights. Submission to and designation by the Minor Planet Center.

What must hold again. Astrometry from several nights must be precise enough to constrain a heliocentric orbit. The Minor Planet Center must confirm the linkage as a new object.
