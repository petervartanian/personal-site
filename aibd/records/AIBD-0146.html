HelioLinc3D finds asteroid 2022 SF289 in sparse ATLAS detections

HelioLinc3D linked sparse ATLAS detections into PHA 2022 SF289, published by the Minor Planet Center in MPEC 2023-O26 (Jul 2023).

ⓘ

What happened

On 18 July 2023 the HelioLinc3D linking algorithm identified an unrecognized near-Earth asteroid, 2022 SF289. It was a test on archival data from the NASA-funded ATLAS survey operated by the University of Hawaii (UH). HelioLinc3D was developed by Ari Heinze and colleagues at the University of Washington's (UW) DiRAC Institute for the Vera C. Rubin Observatory. ATLAS had first imaged the object on 19 September 2022 and observed it three times across four nights. Conventional discovery requires more same-night detections. The Minor Planet Center (MPC) published the object in MPEC 2023-O26, issued 18 July 2023. It is an Apollo-type potentially hazardous asteroid about 600 feet across with an Earth MOID of 0.0015 AU and no foreseeable risk to Earth. The sources call HelioLinc3D an algorithm, not a learned model.

Purpose. Built as Rubin Observatory's linking algorithm to discover moving objects, including near-Earth asteroids, from sparse multi-night detections. This benefit was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

HelioLinc3D linked detections from separate nights that conventional pipelines could not combine, producing the orbit that the MPC published. Caveat: UW, UH, Rubin and MPC sources call it a “next-generation algorithm”. No cited source describes it as machine learning or AI.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    The Rubin Observatory and UW DiRAC team obtained a real-data validation of the linking software before the ten-year survey began.

  • society

    One roughly 600-foot potentially hazardous asteroid was added to the catalog. The sources state that it poses no foreseeable risk, so the benefit is knowledge, not averted harm.

Since then
On 2 April 2026 UW and Rubin announced over 11,000 new asteroids, including 33 near-Earth objects, confirmed by the Minor Planet Center and found with DiRAC-developed discovery software built by Ari Heinze and Jacob Kurlander; the release does not use the name HelioLinc3D.The findings were confirmed by the International Astronomical Union's Minor Planet Center (MPC), and include hundreds of distant worlds beyond Neptune as well as 33 previously unknown near-Earth objects. source

Further evidence

Setting. Survey detections too sparse per night for conventional linking. Heliocentric linking across multiple nights. Submission to and designation by the Minor Planet Center.

Conditions. Astrometry from several nights must be precise enough to constrain a heliocentric orbit. The Minor Planet Center must confirm the linkage as a new object.
