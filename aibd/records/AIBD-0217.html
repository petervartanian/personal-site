Rising Academies' WhatsApp tutor Rori lifts math scores 0.36 SD

an effect size of 0.36 (Cohen's d, p < 0.001)

Preprint by Oxford, Rising Academies and J-PAL North America authors: students using Rori one hour a week in study hall gained 0.36 SD more in math (637 baseline, 477 endline) than controls in 2023.

What happened

Rori is an AI-powered conversational math tutor on WhatsApp created by Rising Academies, an educational network based in Ghana. Henkel (University of Oxford), Horne-Robinson (Rising Academies), Kozhakhmetova and Lee (J-PAL North America) report a preliminary evaluation in 11 Rising Academies schools in Ghana: students in grades 3-8 took a 35-question math assessment in early February 2023 (baseline) and late August 2023 (endline); 637 students (336 treatment, 301 control) took the baseline and 477 the endline. Treated students were given a phone for one hour a week during study hall to study math with Rori while controls continued regular instruction. The May 2024 PDF (v2) reports an effect size of 0.36 (Cohen's d, p < 0.001) and a marginal cost of about $5 per student. The arXiv abstract still describes approximately 1,000 students in grades 3-9, two 30-minute sessions a week over 8 months and an effect size of 0.37, which does not match the v2 body. The date observed is the first day of the baseline month, used as a placeholder.

What it was built to do. Rori was built to give extra math practice and feedback through chat; the math gain is that purpose.

The system's part

Rori delivered the extra practice hour itself, so the comparison is Rori time versus a normal study hall; the authors call the result preliminary, year 1 only, and the vendor co-authored the preprint.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • A chat tutor that runs on basic phones over low-bandwidth networks
  • One scheduled hour a week of independent use during study hall with a provided phone
  • A control group kept on the normal timetable in the same school network
What must hold again
  • Phone and WhatsApp access during school time
  • Schools accustomed to routine testing (the authors cite this against a Hawthorne effect)
  • Replication outside the Rising Academies network, which the authors say is still needed
