Rising Academies' WhatsApp tutor Rori lifts math scores 0.36 SD

Preprint by Oxford, Rising Academies and J-PAL North America authors: students using Rori one hour a week in study hall gained 0.36 SD more in math (637 baseline, 477 endline) than controls in 2023.

ⓘ

What happened

Rori is an AI-powered conversational math tutor on WhatsApp created by Rising Academies, an educational network based in Ghana. Henkel (University of Oxford), Horne-Robinson (Rising Academies), Kozhakhmetova and Lee (J-PAL North America) report a preliminary evaluation in 11 Rising Academies schools in Ghana. Students in grades 3–8 took a 35-question math assessment in early February 2023 (baseline) and late August 2023 (endline). 637 students (336 treatment, 301 control) took the baseline and 477 the endline. Treated students were given a phone for one hour a week during study hall to study math with Rori. Controls continued regular instruction. The May 2024 PDF (v2) reports an effect size of 0.36 (Cohen's d, p < 0.001) and a marginal cost of about $5 per student.

The arXiv abstract still describes approximately 1,000 students in grades 3–9, two 30-minute sessions a week over 8 months and an effect size of 0.37. That does not match the v2 body.

Purpose. Rori was built to give extra math practice and feedback through chat. The math gain is that purpose.

AI’s contribution

Rori delivered the extra practice hour itself, so the comparison is Rori time versus a normal study hall. The authors call the result preliminary, year 1 only. The vendor co-authored the preprint.

Further evidence

Setting. A chat tutor that runs on basic phones over low-bandwidth networks. One scheduled hour a week of independent use during study hall with a provided phone. A control group kept on the normal timetable in the same school network.

Conditions. Phone and WhatsApp access during school time. Schools accustomed to routine testing (the authors cite this against a Hawthorne effect). Replication outside the Rising Academies network, which the authors say is still needed.
