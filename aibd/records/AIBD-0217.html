Rising Academies' WhatsApp tutor Rori raises math scores in Ghana

In a preprint coauthored by Rising Academies staff, Ghanaian students who used its WhatsApp tutor Rori one hour a week in 2023 gained 0.36 standard deviations more in math than controls.

  • 2 a person

    Treated students' math growth scores exceeded those of controls by 0.36 standard deviations on the same 35-question test, a measured gain in a test score reported in the second version of the preprint.

  • Not rated an organization

    The paper gives a marginal cost of about $5 per student, which is a cost estimate; no source reports a saving or a changed result for the school network.

What happened

Rori is an AI-powered conversational math tutor on WhatsApp, created by Rising Academies, an education network that runs schools in Ghana. Henkel (University of Oxford), Horne-Robinson (Rising Academies), Kozhakhmetova and Lee (J-PAL North America) report a preliminary evaluation in 11 Rising Academies schools in Ghana. The schools were randomly assigned at the school level, five to treatment and six to control. Students in grades 3–8 took a 35-question math assessment in early February 2023 (baseline) and late August 2023 (endline). Of the 637 students who took the baseline (336 treatment, 301 control), 477 took the endline. Treated students were given a phone for one hour a week during study hall to study math with Rori. Controls continued regular instruction. The first version of the preprint appeared on arXiv on 15 February 2024. In the second version, posted on 5 May 2024, the authors report an effect size of 0.36 (Cohen's d, p < 0.001) and a marginal cost of about $5 per student. The arXiv abstract still describes approximately 1,000 students in grades 3–9, two 30-minute sessions a week over 8 months and an effect size of 0.37. These figures do not match the body of the second version. In December 2024 a University of Ghana professor and two Rising Academies staff registered a 2025 follow-up pilot in government and low-cost private schools. The registry entry posts no results. By May 2026 Rising Academies reported that it was piloting Rori in government schools in Ghana's Eastern Region with World Bank support. It had extended the pilot by six months and was preparing a larger randomized trial in more than 100 public schools from September 2026.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Students sent answers and questions through WhatsApp. Rori interpreted natural-language messages, guided them through math micro-lessons, and supplied hints followed by worked solutions when they struggled.

Workflow

Students used a supplied phone to practice math with Rori during one hour of weekly study hall, while comparison students continued usual instruction.

Human oversight

Teachers stayed in the room to resolve technical problems while students worked independently with Rori during study hall.

Benefits

Reach

The trial covered 637 students at baseline, 336 in treatment and 301 in control, and 477 took the endline. No source counts the students reached by later pilots. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The second version of the preprint measured change from early February to late August 2023, the first year only. By May 2026 Rising Academies was piloting Rori in government schools in Ghana's Eastern Region, and the registered 2025 follow-up pilot posts no results. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Measurement

Measurement

The same 35-question math test (numeracy and algebra, grades 3 to 5 of the Global Proficiency Framework) given at baseline in February 2023 and at endline in August 2023; growth scores compared with a t-test and Cohen's d. [2]

Setting and sample

11 Rising Academies schools in Ghana, five assigned to treatment and six to control at the school level; 637 students in grades 3 to 8 at baseline and 477 at endline. [2]

Measures

Growth in raw test score from baseline to endline. [2]

Results

Mean growth of 5.13 points in treatment against 2.12 in control, a difference of 3.01 points (p < 0.001), Cohen's d of 0.36. [2]

Additional value from AI

The comparison isolates an extra hour a week of Rori practice against a normal study hall; it does not separate the chat tutor from the extra practice time. [2]

Limitations

Assignment by school with only 11 schools; 25% attrition between tests; ceiling effects in higher grades; one year of data; one of the four authors lists Rising Academies, the developer, as affiliation, and a December 2024 trial registration lists the first author at Rising Academies as well. [2]

Denominators

Two 30-minute sessions a week over about eight months. [2]

People and places

Dependencies

The school sessions required supplied phones, WhatsApp and mobile data. The cost model also included access to language-model services, and teachers provided technical help during sessions.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Phone and WhatsApp access during school time. Schools accustomed to routine testing (the authors cite this against a Hawthorne effect). Replication outside the Rising Academies network, which the authors say is still needed.

Evidence

Original evidence

Both versions of the preprint come from authors at the University of Oxford, Rising Academies and J-PAL North America, so the vendor coauthored them. The registry entry of 4 December 2024 lists two Rising Academies staff with a University of Ghana investigator, and the news post of 6 May 2026 is Rising Academies' own. No source independent of the makers reports the result. [1] [2] [3] [4]
