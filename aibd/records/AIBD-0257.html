What happened

Deputy Minister of Transport Konstantinos Kyranakis said that the ministry presented an AI system on Saturday 16 May 2026. SKAI reported his interview about the system on 18 May 2026. In the interview he described a system that transcribes GSM-R communications between stationmasters and train drivers. GSM-R is the digital radio network that railways use. He said that the system would identify departures from railway communication protocols so that the control center could intervene. He called it a new safeguard that works in real time. He contrasted it with investigation after an accident. The interview supplies no developer, operational start date, transcription accuracy, alert counts, or measured safety result.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The proposed system would transcribe GSM-R dialogue and identify departures from required stationmaster-driver communication protocols.

Autonomy

Transcription and flagging were intended to run automatically. Control-center staff would decide when to intervene.

Workflow

Railway GSM-R communications available for transcription. Speech-to-text and a method for checking communications against railway protocols. A control center able to review departures from those protocols.

Human oversight

Deputy Minister Konstantinos Kyranakis said that the system would flag departures from protocol so that the control center could intervene. No source through 29 October 2026 reports that the center has reviewed a flag or acted on one. [1]

Benefits

Duration

No source through 29 October 2026 reports that the system has operated or produced a result. The duration of any benefit is therefore not established. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The system needs to transcribe Greek railway speech accurately. Control-center staff need to review and act on the conversations that it flags. [1]

Availability

The minister described a recently presented system. The interview did not establish routine operation or a completed rollout.
