What happened

Victor, a partnership between the Supremo Tribunal Federal (STF) and the Universidade de Brasília begun at the end of 2017 under Chief Justice Cármen Lúcia, was designed to read extraordinary appeals and identify the most frequent repercussão geral themes through four tasks: converting images to text, splitting documents, classifying the main procedural documents and identifying repercussão geral themes. On 30 August 2018 Cármen Lúcia announced it was in operation, with tests on 27 themes and a claimed triage precision of 84%, and said work taking a clerk three hours would take five seconds; on 23 October 2018 Chief Justice Dias Toffoli told a CNJ-reported seminar that the software did in five seconds what cost a clerk 40 minutes to an hour, with 85% accuracy.

The STF's own status report of 19 August 2021 (the date used here) states that only the OCR text layer had been effectively implemented, since the end of December 2020 for extraordinary appeals and agravos, with more than 10 million pages processed by May 2021, while the document splitter and classifier were still being put into production with no set date, and that the theme indication is always validated by the ministers. No measured time saving or case outcome was published; the successor tool VitorIA (2023) is recorded separately.

What it was built to do. Speed up admissibility analysis of extraordinary appeals by flagging the repercussão geral theme that applies; the production OCR layer is a precondition for that, not the classification itself.

The system's part

By the STF's own account of 19 August 2021 the AI classifier that would flag repercussão geral themes was not in production; the only live component was automatic OCR of incoming appeals (which the STF frames as part of the Victor project), and any theme indication is validated by the ministers. The time savings announced in 2018 (three hours or 40 minutes to five seconds) were forecasts made by chief justices at launch. All fetched sources are the deployer's news service (STF) and the CNJ, which the same chief justice presided; no independent evaluation was found.

Replication

What produced it. STF–UnB research partnership with about 40 graduate scholarship holders. Extraction of a training base of extraordinary appeals, which took months. Automatic OCR of all incoming appeal PDFs on the day of receipt (from December 2020). Validation of any theme indication by the ministers.

What must hold again. Filings arriving as PDFs that can be converted to text. Splitter and classifier reaching production (not achieved by August 2021). Classifier accuracy acceptable to the Secretaria de Gestão de Precedentes. Continued STF–UnB funding and staff.
