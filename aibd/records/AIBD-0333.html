Victor converts 10 million appeal pages to text at Brazil's top court

Victor, the AI project of Brazil's Supreme Court and the University of Brasília, has converted appeals to text since December 2020, with more than 10 million pages processed by May 2021.

What happened

Victor is a partnership of the Supremo Tribunal Federal (STF) and the Universidade de Brasília (UnB). It began at the end of 2017 under Chief Justice Cármen Lúcia. It was designed to read extraordinary appeals and identify the most frequent repercussão geral themes. It has four tasks: converting images to text, splitting documents, classifying the main procedural documents and identifying repercussão geral themes. On 30 August 2018 Cármen Lúcia announced that it was in operation, with tests on 27 themes and a claimed triage precision of 84%. She said that work taking a clerk three hours would take five seconds. On 23 October 2018 Chief Justice Dias Toffoli spoke at a seminar reported by the Conselho Nacional de Justiça (CNJ). He said that it did in five seconds what cost a clerk 40 minutes to an hour, with 85% accuracy. The STF's own status report of 19 August 2021 states that only the optical character recognition (OCR) layer had been implemented. That layer ran from the end of December 2020 for extraordinary appeals and agravos, with more than 10 million pages processed by May 2021. The splitter and the classifier were still being put into production with no set date. The ministers always validate the theme indication. No measured time saving or case outcome was published. Prado and Andrade of the University of Fortaleza concluded in February 2022 that Victor saved considerable time in identifying repercussão geral themes. Their study rests on CNJ and STF secondary data and reports no measurement of its own. In May 2023 the STF said that it still operated Victor for repercussão geral triage of incoming appeals, alongside Rafa. It was then finishing tests of the successor tool VitorIA, which is recorded separately. In October 2023 Valle, Fuentes i Gasó and Ajus credited Victor with efficiency, and they also reported inadequate summaries of decisions. They proposed that case records show when Victor intervened.

Details

AI system

Configuration

By August 2021, the production service converted appeal images to text. The document splitter and theme classifier were still awaiting deployment.

Inputs and outputs

Incoming appeal PDFs produced searchable text through OCR; classification of legal themes remained a planned later step.

Autonomy

Any eventual theme recommendation required validation by the justices.

Workflow

STF–UnB research partnership with about 40 graduate scholarship holders. Building a training set of extraordinary appeals, which took months. Automatic OCR of all incoming appeal PDFs on the day of receipt (from December 2020). Validation of any theme indication by the ministers.

Human oversight

The STF states that the ministers always validate the theme that Victor indicates. Valle, Fuentes i Gasó and Ajus proposed that case records show when Victor intervened. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Filings arriving as PDFs that can be converted to text. Splitter and classifier reaching production (not achieved by August 2021). Classifier accuracy acceptable to the Secretaria de Gestão de Precedentes. Continued STF–UnB funding and staff.
