Daejeon's Kkumdori robot flags “I want to die”, and police respond

Daejeon's Kkumdori care robot, built on ChatGPT 4.0, flagged a woman in her 70s saying “I want to die” at 2 a.m. on 6 August 2025; police came, and a hospital admitted her the next day.

  • 3 a person

    A woman in her 70s living alone, with schizophrenia and bipolar disorder, was in acute crisis at 2 a.m. She got a check-in call, a police visit that night and protective hospital admission the next afternoon. The city and the headlines call it life-saving. No attempt was reported, so the counterfactual is unproven. Her guardian said that having the robot beside her had been a great support to her.

  • Not rated an organization

    The sources report no time, cost or capability that the city welfare office gained from this case. Its director cited the case as a model and the city kept the program, and neither of these is a realized outcome for the office.

The system’s part
The robot's crisis-detection algorithm recognized repeated danger words (“죽고 싶다”, “살려줘”) in conversation at 2 a.m. It sent an alert to a human control center. People did everything after that: the check-in call, the 112 report with location, the police visit, the family contact and the admission. The city's welfare director says the alert is what made the rapid dispatch and protection possible. Whether A would have come to harm that night is not established. Every account derives from the city's 13 August briefing.
Limits
The accounts share the city's briefing. They do not establish the counterfactual risk of self-harm, the algorithm's false-alert rate, or longer-term outcomes after admission. A timely intervention should not be converted into a verified life saved.
Costs and harms
The service probably reduced the delay before a vulnerable resident received human help overnight. Its speech-based trigger could miss a crisis in which the person could not or did not speak to the device. Daejon Ilbo reported that the city acknowledges a gap in use between men and women.
Later
Persisted, 20 July 2026. Yonhap reported on 1 August 2026 that Daejeon's 1,000 care robots remain in service and that on 20 July 2026 a robot in Seo-gu relayed an 80-something's call for 119 during severe abdominal pain, with paramedics arriving at about 00:30; it recounts the 2025 Kkumdori case as an earlier example.

What happened

The supplier Mr. Mind announced on 14 January 2025 that it would provide the 1,000 robots of the 2025 program. They are its 4.0 model, with conversation based on ChatGPT 4.0. At about 2 a.m. on 6 August 2025, a woman in her 70s identified as “A” was at home in Deogam-dong, Daedeok-gu, Daejeon, South Korea. She lives alone and has schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. She repeatedly told the city-issued AI care robot Kkumdori “I want to die” and “save me”. The robot's crisis-detection algorithm flagged the words and alerted the operator's control center. A staff member phoned her. When she kept repeating them, staff reported to police on 112 with her location. Officers found her emotionally unstable and contacted her family. Police judged hospital treatment urgent. On the afternoon of 7 August she was admitted with her guardian's consent. Daejeon announced the case on 13 August 2025. On 10 September 2025 Daejon Ilbo reported that two users that it interviewed value the robot as company. The paper also reported that the city acknowledges a gap in use between men and women. Yonhap reported on 1 August 2026 that Daejeon's 1,000 care robots remain in service.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

Mr. Mind (미스터마인드) 4.0 model, with two-way conversation based on ChatGPT 4.0, supplied for the 2025 Daejeon program

Role

The robot detected signs of distress in conversation and alerted a human control center.

Configuration

The report describes a robot that used ChatGPT technology for conversation. Its phrase “ChatGPT 4.0” does not identify a verified model release.

Inputs and outputs

The robot detected repeated crisis-related speech and sent an alert to a monitoring center with information that enabled a police response.

Workflow

A municipal program that put conversational robots with a crisis-word detection algorithm into 1,000 homes of people living alone, 200 per district, from January 2025. A 24-hour human control center run by the supplier that receives alerts, phones the resident, and reports to police (112) with the location. A resident who habitually talked, sang and danced with the robot, so her statements at 2 a.m. Were spoken aloud to it. Police able to attend at night, and a family and hospital able to complete a protective admission the next day.

Human oversight

Monitoring personnel escalated the alert, police attended, and the family helped arrange hospital admission with guardian consent the next day.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Alerts go to humans who act at night. The robot itself does nothing beyond detecting words and raising the alarm. The detection catches explicit statements (“죽고 싶다”, “살려줘”). A silent crisis would not trigger it. Residents keep talking to the device. Her guardian said it was a great support to her.

Entry details

Sector

Social welfare (home care for people living alone). Municipal elder-care robots.

Purpose

Daejeon issued 1,000 Kkumdori robots from January 2025, 200 per district, under its community integrated-care project. They went to elderly, disabled and low-income people living alone. They offer companionship, medication and schedule reminders and health monitoring. They detect crisis signs such as suicide risk and depression and automatically notify agencies and guardians. The alert was that function.
