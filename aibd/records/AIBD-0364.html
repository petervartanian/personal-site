Tesla Model 3 steadies a Belgian baker's swerve around a wild boar

Baker Kenneth Van Hoesen swerved around a wild boar at 4 am on 30 May 2020 in Houthalen-Helchteren; he says his Model 3 then took over and straightened the car; Musk replied on Twitter.

ⓘ

What happened

Kenneth Van Hoesen, a Flemish baker, was driving his Tesla Model 3 on the Kazernelaan in Houthalen-Helchteren, Belgium, at 4 am on 30 May 2020. A wild boar walked into his path (Daily Star, 9 June 2020). He told Het Laatste Nieuws he pulled the steering wheel himself and the car then took over and straightened its course. He felt the wheel working against him (Daily Star; DH.be, 11 June 2020). The Daily Star says the boar was barely touched, if at all. Electrek reads the footage as a possible strike on the car's side. No injury is reported. The video, shared on Twitter by Pranay Pathole, drew Musk's reply that Autopilot's “prime directive” is not to crash (Electrek, 8 June 2020). He added that its cameras, sonar and radar make it “superhuman”. Le Figaro (12 June 2020) credited the swerve to Autopilot rather than the driver's reflexes, contrary to his own account.

No Tesla data is reported.

Purpose. Autopilot is a driver-assistance system that steers, accelerates and brakes within a lane and includes collision-avoidance features. Keeping the car under control after an evasive swerve is within that intended use as the driver and Musk describe it.

AI’s contribution

By Van Hoesen's account to Het Laatste Nieuws, relayed by the Daily Star and DH.be, he turned the wheel to avoid the boar. The car then took over, straightening the course and working against his steering. On that account the system's contribution was stabilization after a human evasive input, not the initial avoidance. Le Figaro credits Autopilot with the swerve itself, and Electrek notes Autopilot had been thought incapable of such drastic maneuvers. Musk's reply endorses the Autopilot reading but adds no data. No Tesla telemetry, police report or engineering assessment is cited.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    One driver kept control of his car after swerving around a wild boar at 4 am and arrived unhurt. By his account the car straightened the swerve he had begun. Whether he would have lost control or struck the animal without that correction is unknown. No avoided injury or damage is measured, so a removed risk is not established.

Further evidence

Setting. A Tesla with Autopilot engaged, by the driver's account, on an unlit road at about 4 am, with its cameras recording from four sides. A sudden steering input by the driver to avoid an animal, after which the car's control systems straighten the course.

Conditions. The road is otherwise empty, so the swerve and the correction stay within the carriageway. The driver does not fight the correction. Van Hoesen said he felt the wheel working against him. The animal is small enough, or far enough away, to be missed or only grazed.
