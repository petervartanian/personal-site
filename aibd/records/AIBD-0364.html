Tesla Model 3 steadies a Belgian baker's swerve around a wild boar

Kenneth Van Hoesen says that his Tesla Model 3 straightened its course after he swerved around a wild boar in Belgium on 30 May 2020; press reports credit Tesla's Autopilot.

  • 2 a person

    One driver kept control of his car after swerving around a wild boar at 4 am and arrived unhurt. By his account the car straightened the swerve he had begun. Whether he would have lost control or struck the animal without that correction is unknown. No avoided injury or damage is measured, so a removed risk is not established.

The system’s part
By Van Hoesen's account to Het Laatste Nieuws, relayed by the Daily Star and DH.be, he turned the wheel to avoid the boar. The car then took over, straightening the course and working against his steering. On that account the system's contribution was stabilization after a human evasive input, not the initial avoidance. Le Figaro credits Autopilot with the swerve itself, and Electrek notes Autopilot had been thought incapable of such drastic maneuvers. Musk's reply endorses the Autopilot reading but adds no data. No Tesla telemetry, police report or engineering assessment is cited.
Limits
The driver's account credits his own initial swerve, while some coverage credits Autopilot. Neither telemetry nor an engineering assessment establishes which assistance feature acted. The possible contact with the boar is also disputed, so a collision-prevention claim is stronger than the evidence.

What happened

Kenneth Van Hoesen, a Flemish baker, was driving his Tesla Model 3 on the Kazernelaan in Houthalen-Helchteren, Belgium, at 4 am on 30 May 2020. A wild boar walked into his path (Daily Star, 9 June 2020). He told Het Laatste Nieuws that he pulled the steering wheel himself and that the car then took over and straightened its course. He felt the wheel working against him (Daily Star; DH.be, 11 June 2020). The Daily Star reports that he had once had a bad crash in a normal car without Autopilot. The Daily Star says that the boar was barely touched, if at all. Electrek reads the footage as a possible strike on the car's side. No injury is reported. TV Limburg (10 June 2020) reports that he posted the video to warn local residents about wildlife crossing the road. Pranay Pathole shared the video on Twitter, and it drew Musk's reply that Autopilot's “prime directive” is not to crash (Electrek, 8 June 2020). He added that its cameras, sonar and radar make it “superhuman”. Le Figaro (12 June 2020) credited the swerve to Autopilot. That reading contradicts the driver's own account, in which he steered first. No Tesla data is reported.

AI system

Role

The driver reported that the system stabilized the car after his evasive steering.

Configuration

The report identified a Tesla Model 3, without an installed Autopilot release.

Workflow

Van Hoesen steered around the boar himself and reported that the car then corrected its course.

Human oversight

The driver made the first evasive steering input himself. By his account the car then corrected his course while he felt the wheel working against him. No Tesla data is reported on how the driver and the system shared control. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The road is otherwise empty, so the swerve and the correction stay within the carriageway. The driver does not fight the correction. Van Hoesen said he felt the wheel working against him. The animal is small enough, or far enough away, to be missed or only grazed.
