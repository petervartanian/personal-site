UK tax office's Connect system flags cases yielding about £4.6 billion

HM Revenue & Customs estimates that cases flagged by its Connect data-matching system yielded about £4.6 billion of additional tax in 2024–25.

What happened

HM Revenue & Customs (HMRC) launched Connect in 2010–11. Its annual report calls Connect an information technology system that joins a billion pieces of data across its tax regimes to help identify fraud. In February 2015 the National Audit Office called Connect a good illustration of HMRC's ability to use new technology. It cited HMRC's figures of about £80 million invested since 2008 and £3 billion of extra revenue. Drawing on HMRC's annual report, it also said that Connect let HMRC cut its Risk and Intelligence staff by 40% while it collected more compliance revenue. The Comptroller and Auditor General's report in HMRC's 2024–25 accounts records that HMRC used TRUCE (Transaction Risking Upstream in a Connect Environment). It served to identify and stop value-added tax and income-tax repayments that may require investigation. A system called IDRS replaced it from August 2023. The official sources describe Connect's methods as data analytics and risk rules, and none of them calls Connect AI. In a freedom-of-information response reported by Pinsent Masons on 8 October 2025, HMRC said that Connect cases generated an average £3.4 billion of additional annual yield, about £4.6 billion in 2024–25, with around 4,300 staff using the system. The Comptroller and Auditor General notes that compliance yield, the measure behind the £4.6 billion, is HMRC's own estimate of the revenue that it considers it generated and the losses that it prevented. HMRC's annual report for 2025–26, ordered printed on 9 July 2026, credits AI and advanced analytics with the protection and recovery of £10 billion in tax in 2025–26 and does not name Connect.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Connect linked financial and tax information to identify anomalies for compliance investigations.

Workflow

Linking of bank, marketplace, land-registry and other data to taxpayer records. Risk rules and scorecards feeding compliance cases to about 4,300 staff. Upstream risking of VAT and Self Assessment repayment claims (TRUCE, then IDRS).

Human oversight

HMRC staff investigated the cases, while the audit reviewed controls over risk rules and scorecards.
