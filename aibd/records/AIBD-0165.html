Princeton RL controller cuts DIII-D disruptions to two shots

depth 2 for an organization

Claimed by the parties, 3 sources.

An RL controller trained on a tearing-likelihood model kept DIII-D plasmas below the instability threshold; before RL control all but one shot disrupted, after it only two did (Nature, 21 Feb 2024).

What happened

Seo et al. (Nature, published 21 February 2024), from Princeton University, the Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory and partners, trained a reinforcement-learning (RL) controller. Its training environment was built from a multimodal dynamic model that estimates the likelihood of future tearing instability, the leading cause of disruptions. It was deployed on DIII-D, the largest magnetic fusion facility in the United States, at General Atomics in San Diego. It adjusted total beam power and plasma triangularity to keep the estimated tearability under a threshold while maintaining H-mode performance. It did so even at low safety factor and low torque. In the experimental session, discharges up to 193276 either had no RL control or tore before control started. Of the controlled discharges from 193277, only two (193277 and 193282) were disrupted.

Before RL control all shots except one were disrupted. Average time-integrated normalized beta rose. Princeton Engineering's release (21 February 2024) says the model could forecast tearing mode instabilities up to 300 milliseconds in advance. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor logged the result as a positive outcome. The experiment dates are not given. The date used is the paper's publication date.

What it was built to do. The controller was built to avoid tearing instability while maintaining high-pressure plasma in DIII-D. The avoided disruptions were its intended use.

The system's part

The RL controller set the high-level actuator commands that the plasma control system executed. The disruption outcomes in the controlled shots are therefore attributable to it. The comparison is within one session, and the paper is authored by the system's developers. Independent evaluation is the journal's peer review and the OECD AIM registry entry.

Replication

What produced it. A database of past DIII-D discharges to train the tearability predictor. Real-time profile reconstruction and equilibrium fitting feeding the DNN. A plasma control system that accepts high-level beam-power and shape commands.

What must hold again. A tearability model valid for the operating regime. Actuators (beam power, triangularity) able to move the plasma along the stable path. Session-level comparison shots to judge disruption rates.
