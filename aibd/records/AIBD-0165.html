AI from Princeton steers fusion plasmas clear of tearing instability

A deep reinforcement-learning controller from Princeton kept DIII-D fusion plasmas below a tearing-instability threshold; all but one shot disrupted before it took control, and only two did after.

Citable summary

An RL controller trained on a tearing-likelihood model kept DIII-D plasmas below the instability threshold; before RL control all but one shot disrupted, after it only two did (Nature, 21 Feb 2024). This is a demonstrated experimental control capability, not electricity delivered to users or established commercial fusion performance. The abstract supports instability prevention under specified conditions, while the detailed discharge counts and 300-millisecond claim were not independently reanalyzed. Generalization to ITER remains prospective.
