AI from Princeton steers fusion plasmas clear of tearing instability

A deep reinforcement-learning controller from Princeton kept DIII-D fusion plasmas below a tearing-instability threshold; all but one shot disrupted before it took control, and only two did after.

What happened

Researchers from Princeton University, the Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory and partners trained a reinforcement-learning controller. Its training environment was built from a multimodal dynamic model that estimates the likelihood of future tearing instability, the leading cause of disruptions. They deployed it on DIII-D, the largest magnetic fusion facility in the United States, at General Atomics in San Diego. It adjusted total beam power and plasma triangularity to keep the estimated tearability under a threshold while maintaining high-confinement mode (H-mode) performance. It did so even at low safety factor and low torque. In the experimental session, discharges up to 193276 either had no reinforcement-learning control or tore before control started. Before reinforcement-learning control all shots except one were disrupted. Of the controlled discharges from 193277, only two (193277 and 193282) were disrupted. Average time-integrated normalized beta rose. The authors do not give the experiment dates. Nature received the manuscript on 12 July 2023, so the controlled shots took place before that day. Seo and colleagues published the results in Nature on 21 February 2024. Princeton Engineering's release of the same day says that the model could forecast tearing mode instabilities up to 300 milliseconds in advance. On 24 February 2024 the OECD AI Incidents Monitor logged the result as a positive outcome. No independent group has reported a test of this controller since.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

A learned dynamic model estimated near-future tearing risk from plasma measurements and supplied the risk signal for the control policy.

Autonomy

The reinforcement-learning controller adjusted beam power and plasma shape, and the plasma-control system executed those commands during DIII-D experiments.

Workflow

A database of past DIII-D discharges to train the tearability predictor. Real-time profile reconstruction and equilibrium fitting feeding the DNN. A plasma control system that accepts high-level beam-power and shape commands.

Human oversight

The controller set high-level actuator commands, which the DIII-D plasma control system executed. No source describes how operators supervised the controlled discharges. [1] [2] [3]

Benefits

Reach

No beneficiary count applies. The benefit went to the DIII-D research program at General Atomics. Only two of the controlled discharges were disrupted. [1] [2] [3]

The result comes from controlled discharges in one experimental session before 12 July 2023. Later DIII-D papers from the same group describe successor tearing-mode controllers. No source reports a further result for this controller. [1] [2] [3]
