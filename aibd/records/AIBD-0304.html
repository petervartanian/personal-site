Apple Watch Fall Detection calls 911 for hiker James Prudenciano

On 14 October 2019 a lost hiker fell about 30 ft in Hartshorne Woods Park, NJ; his new Apple Watch's Fall Detection called 911 and texted his family; Middletown police say three agencies responded.

ⓘ

What happened

James Prudenciano, 28, and his hiking companion (named by the Associated Press as Paige Paruso) got lost in Hartshorne Woods Park, Monmouth County, New Jersey. It was 14 October 2019, as darkness fell. They slid down a steep slope onto a ledge. Police said they fell about 30 feet (NBC News, 23 October 2019). He had bought the watch two days earlier and turned on Fall Detection while exploring its settings. He told NBC New York he heard “911, what's your emergency” from the watch. The watch also texted his mother and sister with his location, and his mother drove to the park. “Three agencies responded, according to Middletown police”, and a civilian boater brought the pair to safety. NBC News reports a fractured back and leg and foot injuries. ABC7 New York reports a fracture to his right hand. The Associated Press says undisclosed injuries.

No police report or dispatch record is among the sources.

Purpose. Fall Detection is built to detect hard falls and, after about a minute of immobility, call emergency services and message emergency contacts with a location. This call was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The watch's fall classifier triggered the 911 call and the location message to his family. Apple's support page says the watch calls automatically only if it detects a hard fall and then immobility for about a minute. The hiker was conscious and spoke to the dispatcher once connected. All sources are news reports from October 2019. The response-count claim is attributed to Middletown police by NBC News.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    An injured hiker stranded on a ledge at night was located and brought out the same night. Injuries are reported inconsistently (fractured back per NBC News, fractured hand per ABC7). Whether he could have called 911 himself is not addressed.

  • an organization

    Middletown police say three agencies responded to the automated call. No statement on search time saved is among the sources.

Further evidence

Setting. An Apple Watch Series 4 or later with Fall Detection switched on (off by default for wearers under 55). A hard fall followed by immobility, or a wearer who confirms the call. Cellular or Wi-Fi connectivity from the watch or a nearby iPhone.

Conditions. The fall classifier recognizes the impact as a hard fall. Emergency services can act on the transmitted coordinates.
