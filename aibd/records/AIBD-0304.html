Apple Watch Fall Detection calls 911 after a hiker falls about 30 feet

On 14 October 2019 a lost hiker fell about 30 feet in Hartshorne Woods Park, New Jersey; his new Apple Watch's Fall Detection called 911 and texted his family with his location.

  • 3 a person

    An injured hiker stranded on a ledge at night was located and brought out the same night. Injuries are reported inconsistently (fractured back per NBC News, fractured hand per ABC7). Whether he could have called 911 himself is not addressed.

  • Not rated an organization

    Middletown police say that three agencies responded to the automated call. No source reports time or resources saved for any of them.

The watch's fall classifier triggered the 911 call and the location message to his family. What happened

James Prudenciano, 28, and his hiking companion (named by the Associated Press as Paige Paruso) got lost in Hartshorne Woods Park, Monmouth County, New Jersey. It was 14 October 2019, as darkness fell. They slid down a steep slope onto a ledge. Police said that they fell about 30 feet (NBC News, 23 October 2019). He had bought the watch two days earlier and turned on Fall Detection while exploring its settings. He told NBC New York that he heard “911, what's your emergency” from the watch. The watch also texted his mother and sister with his location, and his mother drove to the park. “Three agencies responded, according to Middletown police”, and a civilian boater brought the pair to safety. NBC News reports a fractured back and leg and foot injuries. ABC7 New York reports a fracture to his right hand. The Associated Press reports undisclosed injuries. No police report or dispatch record is among the sources.
