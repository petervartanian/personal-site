Apple Watch Fall Detection calls 911 for hiker James Prudenciano

Three agencies responded, according to Middletown police

On 14 Oct 2019 a lost hiker fell about 30 ft in Hartshorne Woods Park, NJ; his new Apple Watch's Fall Detection called 911 and texted his family; Middletown police say three agencies responded.

What happened

James Prudenciano, 28, and his hiking companion (named by the Associated Press as Paige Paruso) got lost in Hartshorne Woods Park in Monmouth County, New Jersey, on 14 October 2019 as darkness fell, and slid down a steep slope onto a ledge; police said they fell about 30 feet (NBC News, 23 October 2019). He had bought the watch two days earlier and had turned on Fall Detection while exploring its settings. He told NBC New York he heard '911, what's your emergency' from the watch; the watch also texted his mother and sister with his location, and his mother drove to the park. 'Three agencies responded, according to Middletown police', and a civilian boater brought the pair to safety. NBC News reports a fractured back and leg and foot injuries; ABC7 New York reports a fracture to his right hand; the Associated Press says undisclosed injuries. No police report or dispatch record was fetched.

What it was built to do. Fall Detection is built to detect a hard fall and, if the wearer stays immobile for about a minute, call emergency services and message emergency contacts with a location; this call was that purpose.

The system's part

The watch's fall classifier triggered the 911 call and the location message to his family; Apple's support page says the watch calls automatically only if it detects a hard fall and then immobility for about a minute. The hiker was conscious and spoke to the dispatcher once connected. All sources are news reports from October 2019; the response-count claim is attributed to Middletown police by NBC News.

Replication

What produced it
  • An Apple Watch Series 4 or later with Fall Detection switched on (off by default for wearers under 55)
  • A hard fall followed by immobility, or a wearer who confirms the call
  • Cellular or Wi-Fi connectivity from the watch or a nearby iPhone
What must hold again
  • The fall classifier recognizes the impact as a hard fall
  • Emergency services can act on the transmitted coordinates
