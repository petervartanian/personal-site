ChatGPT appeal letter gets a York student's £60 fine withdrawn

Millie Houlton, 22, asked ChatGPT to draft an appeal against a wrongly issued £60 parking fine; York City Council withdrew it.

ⓘ

What happened

Millie Houlton, a 22-year-old final-year events and business management student at York St John University, received a £60 parking notice from York City Council. It was for parking on her own street, where she holds a permit. Busy with coursework, she was tempted to pay. Instead she typed into ChatGPT “please help me write a letter to the council, they gave me a parking ticket”. She gave the location, reference number and circumstances. The letter came back in minutes. Within days the council told her the appeal was accepted and the fine withdrawn. The BBC and ITV News reported the case on 30 and 31 March 2023, and she shared it on TikTok.

Purpose. ChatGPT is a general-purpose conversational assistant built to answer questions and draft text on request. Writing a formal appeal letter from the user's facts is squarely within that purpose.

AI’s contribution

ChatGPT drafted the appeal from the facts the student entered. The ground for withdrawal was her valid permit, which a human-written appeal could also have stated. The system's contribution was overcoming the effort barrier. She said she would otherwise have paid rather than spend time on a response. The council's reasoning has not been published, so the letter's causal weight is inferred, not documented.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    One identifiable person avoided a £60 fine she considered wrongly issued and the time of drafting an appeal she said she found hard to articulate.

Further evidence

Setting. The appellant had a genuine ground (a valid residents' permit) and the specific facts to hand: location, date, reference number and circumstances. The appeal channel accepted a written letter, so a well-formed draft was the whole task. The user already used ChatGPT for work emails and so trusted it enough to try it rather than pay.

Conditions. The facts entered are true and the appellant checks the draft before sending. The model can produce plausible but incorrect text, as the BBC noted OpenAI admits. The authority judges the appeal on its merits, so the outcome rests on the permit, not on prose quality.
