A York student's appeal drafted by ChatGPT gets a £60 fine withdrawn

Millie Houlton, 22, asked ChatGPT to draft an appeal against a wrongly issued £60 parking fine; York City Council withdrew it.

The system’s part
ChatGPT drafted the appeal from the facts the student entered. The ground for withdrawal was her valid permit, which a human-written appeal could also have stated. The system's contribution was overcoming the effort barrier. She said she would otherwise have paid rather than spend time on a response. The council's reasoning has not been published, so the letter's causal weight is inferred, not documented.
Limits
Withdrawal of the fine is a reported outcome, but the council's reasoning is unavailable in the material on record. A valid parking permit is an alternative explanation for the successful appeal. The claimant's statement supports reduced effort, not proof that AI wording changed the legal decision.
Costs and harms
No source reports a cost or harm in this case. The BBC noted that OpenAI admits that the model can produce plausible but incorrect text, so the appellant has to check the draft before sending it.

What happened

Millie Houlton, a 22-year-old final-year events and business management student at York St John University, received a £60 parking notice from York City Council. It was for parking on her own street, where she holds a permit. Busy with coursework, she was tempted to pay. She typed into ChatGPT “please help me write a letter to the council, they gave me a parking ticket”. She gave the location, reference number and circumstances. The letter came back in minutes. Within days the council told her that the appeal was accepted and the fine withdrawn. The BBC first published the story on 30 March 2023, and ITV News and the Yorkshire Post reported it on 31 March 2023. She also shared it on TikTok. Fox News retold the BBC report on 2 April 2023. No source gives the date of the ticket or of the council's reply. About a month before the BBC report, the RAC reported that Shaun Bosley of Brighton had used ChatGPT to get an NCP fine at Gatwick Airport cut from £100 to £15.

Details

AI system

Configuration

Houlton entered the parking location, ticket reference, and circumstances into ChatGPT, then used its generated text for her appeal. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Houlton supplied the parking notice details and her permit circumstances, and ChatGPT produced a draft appeal letter.

Workflow

The appellant had a genuine ground (a valid residents' permit) and the specific facts to hand: location, date, reference number and circumstances. The appeal channel accepted a written letter, so a well-formed draft was the whole task. The user already used ChatGPT for work emails and so trusted it enough to try it rather than pay.

Human oversight

Houlton submitted the appeal, and York City Council decided to withdraw the charge.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The facts entered are true and the appellant checks the draft before sending. The model can produce plausible but incorrect text, as the BBC noted OpenAI admits. The authority judges the appeal on its merits, so the outcome rests on the permit, not on prose quality.
