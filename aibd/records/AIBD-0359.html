Drivers slowed down when signs warned of wildlife ahead

In a Virginia pilot, Crosstek software detected wildlife in thermal camera feeds with 97 percent accuracy, and drivers slowed by an average of 3 mph at the warning signs that it activated.

What happened

In September 2023 a report of the Virginia Transportation Research Council on wildlife crossings for Corridor Q estimated the societal cost of an elk crash, including monetized pain and suffering, at $80,771.49 in 2020 dollars. The Virginia Department of Transportation (VDOT) later sponsored a one-year pilot by the Virginia Tech Transportation Institute (VTTI) on Route 460 near Grundy in Southwest Virginia, as part of the Corridor Q expansion. VDOT names the pilot the Animal Detection and Driver Warning System, and its project page identifies VTTI as the research partner. The page gave an estimated start of February 2025 and an estimated completion of September 2026 for the study. Eight portable solar-powered trailers with thermal cameras were installed on a 750-meter section of a 14-mile corridor that is home to about 100 elk. AI software from Crosstek analyzed the camera feeds. When the software detected wildlife, dynamic message signs told motorists that wildlife had been detected ahead and to slow down. T-Mobile provided 5G and satellite links. WCYB reported the test on 19 November 2025. In its separate interview, Michelle Earl of VDOT described animal detection and communication with the message boards. The pilot ended in September 2026. Gwen Sheldon reported the results for Virginia Tech on 20 October 2026. The system achieved 97 percent overall accuracy in identifying medium and large wildlife. In the initial analysis the researchers found an average speed reduction of 3 mph as vehicles approached activated signs. Virginia Tech reports that the team collected data on more than 10,000 passing vehicles and that data analysis was continuing after the pilot ended. Virginia Tech reports no result on collisions or fatalities prevented. Its report identifies Crosstek as the supplier of the cameras and the AI software. The measured results come from Virginia Tech's own report, and WCYB's earlier interview describes only the deployment.

Details

AI system

Application developer

Crosstek [1] [2] [3] [4]

Configuration

Thermal cameras watched for animals near Route 460. Detections activated warning signs, while 5G and satellite connections supported remote monitoring. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Inputs and outputs

Inputs: Thermal-camera images near the Route 460 wildlife crossing. Outputs: Roadside warnings when animals approach.

Workflow

Portable solar trailers with thermal cameras on a known elk corridor. AI detection software linked to dynamic message signs. 5G and satellite connectivity. State DOT sponsorship within a highway expansion project.

Human oversight

Researchers monitored the equipment and analyzed detection and speed data. Drivers decided how to respond to the warning signs.

AI contribution

AI detected animals in thermal images and activated roadside warning signs. Virginia Tech reports that approaching drivers slowed by an average of 3 mph. It reports no measured reduction in collisions. [1] [2]

Benefits

Reach

The team collected data on more than 10,000 passing vehicles. The sources do not count the drivers who slowed or any crashes avoided. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The pilot lasted one year and ended in September 2026. Further data analysis was still underway at the October announcement.

Measurement

Measurement

Roadside radar units measured vehicle speeds along the corridor. The report gives an overall detection accuracy for medium and large wildlife and does not say how it was scored. [1]

Setting and sample

More than 10,000 passing vehicles on Route 460 in Southwest Virginia, where eight trailers covered a 750-meter section, during a one-year pilot that ended in September 2026. [1]

Measures

Average change in speed as vehicles approached activated signs; overall detection accuracy for medium and large wildlife. [1]

Results

An average speed reduction of 3 mph at activated signs and 97 percent detection accuracy. [1]
