Drivers slowed down when signs warned of wildlife ahead

AI system

System

Animal Detection and Driver Warning System (ADDWS), with detection software from Crosstek. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Model

Crosstek’s detector for animals in thermal images. The pilot account gives no separate model name. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Release

The pilot’s model and software releases are not disclosed. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Application developer

Crosstek [1] [2] [3] [4]

Configuration

Thermal cameras watched for animals near Route 460. Detections activated warning signs, while 5G and satellite connections supported remote monitoring. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Inputs and outputs

Inputs: Thermal-camera images near the Route 460 wildlife crossing. Outputs: Roadside warnings when animals approach.

Workflow

Solar-powered trailers carried thermal cameras beside an elk corridor. Crosstek’s software detected animals and activated warning signs. VDOT and Virginia Tech monitored the pilot through 5G and satellite connections. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Relation to AI

AI analysis of thermal camera feeds triggered the warning signs. Virginia Tech reports a 3 mph average slowdown as drivers approached activated signs, but it gives no result on collisions prevented. The results come from the evaluating institution's own report. WCYB's separate interview with VDOT describes deployment and adds no independent test of the measured results. [1] [2]

AI contribution

AI detected animals in thermal images and activated roadside warning signs. Virginia Tech reports that approaching drivers slowed by an average of 3 mph. It reports no measured reduction in collisions. [1] [2]

Conditions for replication
