Huawei digital twin's window alert comes from a sensor, not AI

A Huawei-hosted launch on 25 Feb 2021 presented a window-opening alert sent to a guard's wristband. Knews and Jiefang Daily say that a window sensor detected it; the story is retold with three dates.

AI system

System

Nanjing Building 1:1 'living' digital twin on Huawei Cloud: 80-plus IoT sensor sets in 17 categories, 170-plus cameras, and 20-plus (Knews, Jiefang Daily) or 50-plus (Eastday) AI algorithms for smoking, crowd-flow and parking detection

Application developer

Huawei (Huawei Cloud, big data, AI, edge computing) with Huangpu District government under the Shanghai City Operations Management Center

Workflow

Window-mounted contact sensors reporting to the building's digital twin. An alert pushed to a security guard's smart wristband. A guard on site who closes the window.

Relation to AI

For the window alert the sources document a window-mounted sensor whose signal passes through the Huawei Cloud digital twin to a guard's wristband. Knews and Jiefang Daily both say so, and neither attributes the detection to an AI algorithm. The 20- to 50-plus AI algorithms described run on cameras for smoking, crowd flow and bike parking. The one smoking alert shown at the launch was simulated by a Huawei engineer. The Monitor's statement that an AI system “detected a high-risk behavior (open window)” is therefore unsupported by its own sources.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

A real, not visitor-triggered, window opening: Jiefang Daily reports that over 80% of 995 logged door/window alerts were visitors trying the devices. An identifiable risk to pedestrians below, which no source documents.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 1. Outcome unresolved: 1.

Later findings

7 May 2021. Jiefang Daily reported that from January to early May 2021 the digital twin had logged 995 door/window alerts, 439 smoking alerts and 55 fire-lane obstruction alerts, while an official said more than 80% of the handled events were triggered by visitors trying the IoT devices.

Field

Urban governance and building safety. Smart-building monitoring under Shanghai's 'one network to manage all'.

Purpose

The digital twin was built to monitor the building's “vital signs” (vibration, smoke, lifts, crowd flow, open windows) and push alerts to staff. Window opening was named at the launch as a falling-object risk the system flags.
