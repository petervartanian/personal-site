Huawei digital twin's window alert comes from a sensor, not AI

What happened

The OECD AI Incidents entry says Shanghai, with Huawei, launched an AI-powered digital twin in the Nanjing Building. It says the twin “detected a high-risk behavior (open window), alerted security, and enabled immediate intervention”. The anecdote appears in Eastday (reporter Xue Ningwei, 25 Feb 2021, via Sohu). During Spring Festival a customer on the third floor opened a window and held a phone out to photograph Nanjing Road. A guard arrived to stop it. Eastday says how the guard knew was “revealed at today's launch”, the “Shanghai city minimum management unit digital governance” release. It was held at the Huangpu District city operations center, hosted by Huawei Technologies per smartcity.team (1 Mar 2021). Jiefang Daily (7 May 2021, via GMW) retells the same window scene during the May Day holiday, naming guard Fan Tianyu.

Banyuetan/Xinhua (reporter Zhou Lin, 6 Jul 2021, via The Paper) retells it during the Dragon Boat holiday with the same guard and wording. No single date can be fixed. The Cyberspace Administration of China repost (4 Mar 2021) phrases it conditionally (“if a visitor opens a window...”). The only witnessed detection was a Knews reporter's deliberate “open window” test at the launch. Huawei engineer Chen Tao also simulated smoking there for the camera algorithm. Both Knews and Jiefang Daily say a window-mounted sensor detected the opening and pushed an alert to the guard's wristband through the digital twin. The AI algorithms described (20-plus per Knews and Jiefang Daily, 50-plus per Eastday) run on the 170-plus cameras for smoking, crowd flow and bike parking. Jiefang Daily reports 995 door/window alerts from January to May 2021.

It quotes an official that over 80% were triggered by visitors trying the IoT devices. The building is Huawei's flagship store. No harm occurred and no beneficiary is identifiable. The deployment itself is well documented (Huangpu District and Huawei, built January 2021; 17 device categories, 80-plus sensor sets, 170-plus cameras). No English-language report was found, and the China Central Television (CCTV-2) segment is not among the sources. The system's zhiding.cn copy is marked “industry-supplied copy”.

What it was built to do. The digital twin was built to monitor the building's “vital signs” (vibration, smoke, lifts, crowd flow, open windows) and push alerts to staff. Window opening was named at the launch as a falling-object risk the system flags.

The system's part

For the window alert the sources document a window-mounted sensor whose signal passes through the Huawei Cloud digital twin to a guard's wristband. Knews and Jiefang Daily both say so, and neither attributes the detection to an AI algorithm. The 20- to 50-plus AI algorithms described run on cameras for smoking, crowd flow and bike parking. The one smoking alert shown at the launch was simulated by a Huawei engineer. The Monitor's statement that an AI system “detected a high-risk behavior (open window)” is therefore unsupported by its own sources.

Replication

What produced it. Window-mounted contact sensors reporting to the building's digital twin. An alert pushed to a security guard's smart wristband. A guard on site who closes the window.

What must hold again. A real, not visitor-triggered, window opening: Jiefang Daily reports that over 80% of 995 logged door/window alerts were visitors trying the devices. An identifiable risk to pedestrians below, which no source documents.
