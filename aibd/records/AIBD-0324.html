Sensor in a Shanghai digital twin alerts a guard to an opened window

The Nanjing Building's digital twin on Huawei Cloud relayed a window sensor's alert to a guard's wristband, in an anecdote that the sources place on three different holidays.

  • Not rated society

    No pedestrian was shown at risk and no falling object occurred; the only documented trigger was a reporter's test, so no outcome is documented.

  • Not rated an organization

    Jiefang Daily reports 995 door/window alerts handled from January to May 2021. An official said that visitors trying the devices triggered more than 80% of all handled events and that real events were far fewer. No efficiency measure is given.

What happened

Huangpu District and Huawei built the Nanjing Building's digital twin in January 2021, with 17 device categories, 80-plus sensor sets and 170-plus cameras. The building is Huawei's flagship store. During Spring Festival a customer on the third floor opened a window and held a phone out to photograph Nanjing Road. A guard arrived to stop it. Eastday (reporter Xue Ningwei, 25 February 2021, via Sohu) tells this anecdote. It says that the way the guard knew was “revealed at today's launch”, the “Shanghai city minimum management unit digital governance” release. The launch was held at the Huangpu District city operations center, hosted by Huawei Technologies per smartcity.team (1 March 2021). The only witnessed detection was a Knews reporter's deliberate “open window” test at the launch. Huawei engineer Chen Tao also simulated smoking there for the camera algorithm. The system's zhiding.cn copy of the same day is marked “industry-supplied copy”. The OECD AI Incidents entry says that Shanghai, with Huawei, launched an AI-powered digital twin in the Nanjing Building. It says that the twin “detected a high-risk behavior (open window), alerted security, and enabled immediate intervention”. The Cyberspace Administration of China repost (4 March 2021) phrases the anecdote conditionally (“if a visitor opens a window...”). Jiefang Daily (7 May 2021, via GMW) retells the same window scene during the May Day holiday and names the guard as Fan Tianyu. Both Knews and Jiefang Daily say that a window-mounted sensor detected the opening and pushed an alert to the guard's wristband through the digital twin. Both outlets also list window opening among the events that the building's cameras and AI algorithms can sense or anticipate. Neither outlet credits the window alert in the guard's story to those algorithms. The AI algorithms described (20-plus per Knews and Jiefang Daily, 50-plus per Eastday) run on the 170-plus cameras for smoking, crowd flow and bike parking. Jiefang Daily reports 995 door/window alerts from January to May 2021, along with 439 smoking alerts and 55 fire-lane obstruction alerts. An official attributed more than 80% of handled events across the system to visitors testing the devices. The report does not give that percentage specifically for windows. Banyuetan/Xinhua (reporter Zhou Lin, 6 July 2021, via The Paper) retells the anecdote during the Dragon Boat holiday with the same guard and wording. No single date can be fixed. No harm occurred and no beneficiary is identifiable. The sources include no English-language report, and the China Central Television (CCTV-2) segment is not among them.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

A window sensor sent an opening signal through the digital twin to a guard’s wristband.

Autonomy

The alert summoned a guard, who assessed the situation and intervened with the visitor.

Workflow

Window-mounted contact sensors reporting to the building's digital twin. An alert pushed to a security guard's smart wristband. A guard on site who closes the window.

Human oversight

A security guard receives the alert on a smart wristband and goes to stop the visitor. An official said that visitors trying the devices triggered more than 80% of the handled events and that real events were far fewer. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Sector

Urban governance and building safety. Smart-building monitoring under Shanghai's 'one network to manage all'.

Purpose
