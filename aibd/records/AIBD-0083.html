Brazil's audit court credits its Alice robot with R$426m in 2021

Brazil's audit court (TCU) says that Alice, its screening robot, captured R$426m in 2021 (R$194m in 2020); an independent researcher reports that Alice helped auditors stop irregular tenders.

What happened

The Tribunal de Contas da União (TCU) operates Alice, a text-mining robot. It reads federal tender notices and electronic-auction records daily and sends auditors a list of possible irregularities. Trade press in 2017 and 2018 expanded the name as “Análise de Licitações e Editais”. The TCU's own July 2020 release calls the tool the “Sistema de Análise de Licitações e Contratos (Alice)”. Trade press in September 2017 quoted TCU auditor Valéria Nakano as saying that Alice found two DNIT notices worth R$39 million. They were for maintaining stretches of BR-153 already under concession, and a TCU ruling (acórdão) blocked them within two months. That ruling is not available, so the example is unverified. In its 4 March 2022 statement on the benefits of its control work in 2021, the TCU reported the value captured through Alice. It was R$426 million in 2021 against R$194 million in 2020. Fernanda Odilla of the University of Bologna published an independent study of Brazilian anti-corruption bots in March 2023. She reports that the Controladoria-Geral da União (CGU) created Alice in 2015 before sharing it with the TCU. She also reports that Alice helped auditors stop irregular tenders and that its accuracy fell from 88.27% in testing to 73.91% in later auditing. She adds that auditors complained of inboxes overloaded with alerts that the TCU lacked staff to check. A TCU plenary ruling of 6 May 2026 records that the tool, now called Alice 360, has moved to intensive use of AI.

Details

AI system

Application developer

Tribunal de Contas da União (TCU), in-house

Inputs and outputs

ALICE read federal tender and auction notices each day and sent auditors lists of possible irregularities.

Workflow

Daily text-mining of the Diário Oficial and Comprasnet notices by Alice with e-mailed alerts to auditors. Low-cost follow-up by auditors (phone call, e-mail, ofício) or formal acórdão when needed. Agencies withdrawing, annulling or redoing flawed notices after contact.

Human oversight

Auditors followed up alerts through contact with agencies or formal proceedings, and agencies could then withdraw, annul or revise the tender. Fernanda Odilla reports that auditors complained of inboxes overloaded with alerts that the TCU lacked staff to check. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

AI contribution

The TCU attributes the 2021 figure to control actions supported by Alice under its own method for measuring benefits. Fernanda Odilla, an independent researcher, reports that Alice helped auditors stop irregular tenders and that its accuracy fell from 88.27% in testing to 73.91% in later auditing. She does not separate its use at the CGU from its use at the TCU. No source offers a counterfactual for the R$426 million. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]
