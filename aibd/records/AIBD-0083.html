TCU's ALICE tender-analysis robot captures R$426m in 2021

TCU says its ALICE tender-analysis robot captured R$426m in 2021 (R$194m in 2020); specific cases reported only via press

ⓘ

What happened

The Tribunal de Contas da União (TCU) operates ALICE, a text-mining robot. It reads federal tender notices and electronic-auction records daily and sends auditors a list of possible irregularities. Trade press in 2017 and 2018 expanded the name as “Análise de Licitações e Editais”. The TCU's own July 2020 release calls it the “Sistema de Análise de Licitações e Contratos (Alice)”. In its 4 March 2022 statement on 2021 control benefits, the TCU reported the value captured through Alice. It was R$426 million in 2021 against R$194 million in 2020. Only the year is given, so 1 January 2021 is used. Trade press in September 2017 quoted TCU auditor Valéria Nakano that Alice found two DNIT notices worth R$39 million. They were for maintaining BR-153 stretches already under concession, blocked by an acórdão within two months. That acórdão could not be obtained, so the example is unverified.

Purpose. Daily automated screening of federal procurement notices to direct external-control auditors to likely irregularities

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. The TCU attributes the 2021 figure to control actions supported by Alice under its benefit-measurement method. It says “o segundo de fato reflete o benefício calculado, que resultou da ação de controle e foi medido”. No independent evaluation or counterfactual was found. The 2017 DNIT/BR-153 example rests on a trade-press interview with a TCU auditor. The TCU acórdão was not available. Developer and deployer are the same public body.

Since then
A TCU plenary ruling of 6 May 2026 (Acórdão 1.145/2026) records that the tool, now called Alice 360, has moved to intensive use of AI, with LLM-based query screens; no outcome figures are given.As evoluções incrementais já implementadas no Alice 360 incluem a transição das telas de consulta para sistemas baseados em Modelos de Linguagem de Grande Escala (LLM). source

Further evidence

Setting. Daily text-mining of the Diário Oficial and Comprasnet notices by Alice with e-mailed alerts to auditors. Low-cost follow-up by auditors (phone call, e-mail, ofício) or formal acórdão when needed. Agencies withdrawing, annulling or redoing flawed notices after contact.

Conditions. Structured publication of notices on federal systems. Auditor capacity to act on the daily list. A benefit-measurement method that records actual, not potential, benefits.
