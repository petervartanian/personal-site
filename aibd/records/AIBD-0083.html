TCU's ALICE tender-analysis robot captures R$426m in 2021

off the scale

captured R$426m in 2021 (R$194m in 2020)

TCU says its ALICE tender-analysis robot captured R$426m in 2021 (R$194m in 2020); specific cases reported only via press

What happened

The Tribunal de Contas da União (TCU) operates ALICE, a text-mining robot that reads federal tender notices and electronic-auction records daily and sends auditors a list of possible irregularities; trade press in 2017 and 2018 expanded the name as 'Análise de Licitações e Editais', while the TCU's own July 2020 release calls it the 'Sistema de Análise de Licitações e Contratos (Alice)'. In its 4 March 2022 statement on 2021 control benefits, the TCU reported that the value captured through the Alice system was R$426 million in 2021 against R$194 million in 2020; only the year is given, so 1 January 2021 is used. Trade press in September 2017 quoted TCU auditor Valéria Nakano that Alice found two DNIT notices worth R$39 million for maintaining BR-153 stretches already under concession, blocked by an acórdão within two months; that acórdão could not be retrieved, so the example is unverified.

What it was built to do. Daily automated screening of federal procurement notices to direct external-control auditors to likely irregularities

The system's part

not assessed The TCU attributes the 2021 figure to control actions supported by the Alice system under its benefit-measurement method ('o segundo de fato reflete o benefício calculado, que resultou da ação de controle e foi medido'); no independent evaluation or counterfactual was found. The 2017 DNIT/BR-153 example rests on a trade-press interview with a TCU auditor; the TCU acórdão was not located because the court's search application is JavaScript-only and its public API path returned 404. Developer and deployer are the same public body.

Replication

What produced it. Daily text-mining of the Diário Oficial and Comprasnet notices by Alice with e-mailed alerts to auditors. Low-cost follow-up by auditors (phone call, e-mail, ofício) or formal acórdão when needed. Agencies withdrawing, annulling or redoing flawed notices after contact.

What must hold again. Structured publication of notices on federal systems. Auditor capacity to act on the daily list. A benefit-measurement method that records actual, not potential, benefits.
