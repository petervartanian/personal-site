Auditors use an Alice email to stop overlapping road tenders in Brazil

Alice, the federal audit court's tender-reading robot, emailed tender information to auditors, who found road-maintenance notices that overlapped a concession; the court ordered them annulled.

  • Not rated a person

    No individual beneficiary is identified in the sources.

  • 2 an organization

    The outcome is material to DNIT and TCU. Inforchannel and JOTA report that an acórdão blocked or canceled the tenders, and the July 2018 visit report reproduces the order to annul notices 88/2017 and 102/2017. Inforchannel gives R$39 million for two notices, and JOTA gives R$20 million for one.

Alice delivered tender information to auditors. The firsthand visit report states that its email contained no irregularity alert for this case. Auditors identified the concession overlap, and the TCU ordered annulment. The evidence supports an information-delivery contribution, not automated identification of this particular problem.

The visit report reproduces the annulment order and describes two notices totaling R$39 million, while JOTA gives R$20 million for one tender. The full financial effect remains unresolved. Odilla reports that TCU added a random forest classifier to Alice’s rules, and no source says whether it played any part in this case.

What happened

Alice (Análise de Licitações e Editais) is the Tribunal de Contas da União's robot. It reads every tender notice and auction record published on Comprasnet and the Diário Oficial da União. It had been running for about ten months when the Tribunal de Contas da União (TCU) presented it at a joint data-analysis seminar of the TCU and the Controladoria-Geral da União (CGU) on 25–26 September 2017. Inforchannel (27 September 2017), quoting TCU auditor Valéria Nakano, reported that in Goiás the court, via Alice, found that DNIT's state unit had published two notices. They were worth R$39 million for maintenance of the BR-153 on stretches under concession, where maintenance falls to the concessionaire. Within two months an acórdão blocked both tenders. A JOTA article of 22 November 2017 states that Alice was decisive in Acórdão-TCU 1.116/2017 (rapporteur Minister Walton Alencar Rodrigues). It ordered cancellation of the BR-153 tender budgeted at R$20 million. The two sources give different numbers of notices and amounts. The court’s site does not expose the complete acórdão. The decision date is not reported. A firsthand report of a July 2018 visit to TCU states that Alice’s email contained no irregularity alert. It states that auditors identified the concession overlap after receiving the information. It reproduces the order to annul notices 88/2017 and 102/2017, and it describes their combined value as R$39 million. The operative part of Acórdão 1.116/2017-Plenário, reproduced in the visit report, gave DNIT's Goiás and Federal District unit fifteen days to annul pregões 88/2017-12 and 102/2017-12. Fernanda Odilla (Crime, Law and Social Change, 2023) reports, citing an interviewee, that TCU added a random forest classifier to the rules that CGU first wrote for Alice. She lists alert overflow and false positives among the main weaknesses of the procurement tools that include Alice.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Alice sent procurement information by email; auditors identified the maintenance overlap in this case. [1]

Autonomy

Auditors identified the problem, and the court ordered the tenders annulled. [1]

Workflow

Daily collection of all tender notices and auction records from Comprasnet and the Diário Oficial da União. Rule-based text analysis (nine checks on competitiveness restrictions, reading of estimated value) and 23 database cross-checks of suppliers. Auditors in the TCU Goiás unit reading the alerts during the public phase of the tender. A TCU plenary decision (acórdão) within two months of the alert.

Human oversight

Alice’s email reached TCU auditors without an irregularity alert for this case, and the auditors identified the concession overlap themselves. The court then gave DNIT's Goiás and Federal District unit fifteen days to annul the two notices. Odilla lists alert overflow and false positives among the main weaknesses of the procurement tools that include Alice. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Notices published on Comprasnet in machine-readable text. Alerts reaching auditors within the 15–30-day public phase. Auditors with capacity to open a case or contact the contracting body. Contracting bodies or the court willing to block or cancel flawed tenders.
