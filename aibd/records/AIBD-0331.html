What happened

Alice (Análise de Licitações e Editais) is the Tribunal de Contas da União's robot. It reads every tender notice and auction record published on Comprasnet and the Diário Oficial da União. It had been running for about ten months when TCU presented it at a TCU–CGU data-analysis seminar on 25–26 September 2017. Inforchannel (27 September 2017), quoting TCU auditor Valéria Nakano, reported that in Goiás the court, via Alice, found DNIT's state unit had published two notices. They were worth R$39 million for maintenance of the BR-153 on stretches under concession, where maintenance falls to the concessionaire. Within two months an acórdão blocked both tenders. A JOTA article of 22 November 2017 states that Alice was decisive in Acórdão-TCU 1.116/2017 (rapporteur Minister Walton Alencar Rodrigues). It ordered cancellation of the BR-153 tender budgeted at R$20 million.

The two sources give different numbers of notices and amounts. The acórdão itself was not available. So the date observed is the date of the earliest report read, not the decision date.

What it was built to do. Daily automated screening of federal tender notices for signs of irregularity so that auditors can act during the public phase of a tender. This use was its intended purpose.

The system's part

Alice's role was detection. Per Inforchannel it pulls the estimated value from notice text, runs nine text analyses for competitiveness restrictions and 23 database cross-checks. The auditor's statement and the JOTA article attribute the discovery of the BR-153 notices to Alice. The cancellation was a decision of the TCU plenary (Acórdão 1116/2017). Both sources rest on TCU staff statements, the deployer describing its own in-house tool. The source counts as independent because TCU is a public body, not a vendor. The acórdão text was not obtained, so the two accounts' differing figures (R$39 million, two notices; R$20 million, one notice) cannot be reconciled.

Replication

What produced it. Daily collection of all tender notices and auction records from Comprasnet and the Diário Oficial da União. Rule-based text analysis (nine checks on competitiveness restrictions, reading of estimated value) and 23 database cross-checks of suppliers. Auditors in the TCU Goiás unit reading the alerts during the public phase of the tender. A TCU plenary decision (acórdão) within two months of the alert.

What must hold again. Notices published on Comprasnet in machine-readable text. Alerts reaching auditors within the 15–30-day public phase. Auditors with capacity to open a case or contact the contracting body. Contracting bodies or the court willing to block or cancel flawed tenders.
