TCU's Alice flags DNIT's BR-153 notices; TCU cancels the tender

depth 2 for an organization, depth 1 for society

ordered cancellation of the BR-153 tender budgeted at R$20 million

Alice flagged DNIT Goiás notices for BR-153 maintenance on concession stretches (R$39m per Inforchannel); TCU Acórdão 1116/2017 canceled the R$20m tender (JOTA).

What happened

Alice (Análise de Licitações e Editais), the Tribunal de Contas da União's robot that reads every tender notice and auction record published on Comprasnet and the Diário Oficial da União, had been running for about ten months when TCU presented it at a TCU–CGU data-analysis seminar on 25–26 September 2017. Inforchannel (27 Sep 2017), quoting TCU auditor Valéria Nakano, reported that in Goiás the court, via Alice, found that DNIT's state unit had published two notices worth R$39 million for maintenance of the BR-153 on stretches under concession, where maintenance falls to the concessionaire, and that within two months an acórdão blocked both tenders. A JOTA article of 22 November 2017 states that Alice was decisive in Acórdão-TCU 1.116/2017 (rapporteur Minister Walton Alencar Rodrigues), which ordered cancellation of the BR-153 tender budgeted at R$20 million; the two sources give different numbers of notices and amounts. The acórdão itself could not be retrieved (the TCU search application is JavaScript-only and its REST path returned 404 or a firewall block), so the date observed is the date of the earliest fetched report, not the decision date.

What it was built to do. Daily automated screening of federal tender notices for signs of irregularity so that auditors can act during the public phase of a tender; this use was its intended purpose.

The system's part

Alice's role was detection: per Inforchannel it extracts the estimated value from notice text, runs nine text analyzes for competitiveness restrictions and 23 database cross-checks; the auditor's statement and the JOTA article attribute the discovery of the BR-153 notices to Alice, while the cancellation was a decision of the TCU plenary (Acórdão 1116/2017). Both sources rest on TCU staff statements (the deployer describing its own in-house tool; the source is marked independent because TCU is a public body, not a vendor), and the acórdão text was not retrieved, so the two accounts' differing figures (R$39 million, two notices; R$20 million, one notice) cannot be reconciled.

Replication

What produced it. Daily collection of all tender notices and auction records from Comprasnet and the Diário Oficial da União. Rule-based text analysis (nine checks on competitiveness restrictions, extraction of estimated value) and 23 database cross-checks of suppliers. Auditors in the TCU Goiás unit reading the alerts during the public phase of the tender. A TCU plenary decision (acórdão) within two months of the alert.

What must hold again. Notices published on Comprasnet in machine-readable text. Alerts reaching auditors within the 15–30-day public phase. Auditors with capacity to open a case or contact the contracting body. Contracting bodies or the court willing to block or cancel flawed tenders.
