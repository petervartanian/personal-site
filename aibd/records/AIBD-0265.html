Utrecht implanted BCI gives a woman with ALS seven years of use

A fully implanted ECoG interface turned band-power changes from attempted hand movement into clicks; she typed independently from 2016 and used it at home about seven years until ALS atrophy ended it.

ⓘ

What happened

Vansteensel et al. (NEJM, 12 November 2016) described a fully implanted brain-computer interface in a woman with late-stage ALS at UMC Utrecht. It used subdural electrodes over the motor cortex with a subcutaneous transmitter. She controlled a typing program at about two letters per minute 28 weeks after electrode placement. She used it outdoors, where eye tracking failed, as her only means of communication. The authors note that suppressing unintended clicks “required several months of refining the algorithms”. The 2024 NEJM follow-up reports seven years of independent at-home use, starting seven months after implantation. Features from a single electrode pair (low- and high-frequency band power during attempted hand movement) became click commands for spelling and a caregiver call. Use declined close to six years after implantation as click accuracy fell, and ended by March 2023.

The authors attribute this to ALS-related atrophy rather than device failure. Neither text names a machine-learning decoder. The decoder is treated as threshold-based.

Purpose. Provide autonomous communication for a person with late-stage ALS who could no longer rely on eye tracking

AI’s contribution

The interface's decoding is described as converting low- and high-frequency band-power changes from one electrode pair into click commands, with algorithms refined over months. No machine-learning decoder is named in the source text. Whether the system uses AI is not established. The benefit itself (seven years of home communication) is documented in two peer-reviewed reports by the developers.

Conditions. Neural signal amplitude remains sufficient (it declined with ALS-related atrophy after about six years). The participant can attempt hand movement reliably.
