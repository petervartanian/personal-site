Google says Big Sleep isolated SQLite zero-day CVE-2025-6965

depth 2 for an organization

Google says Big Sleep, fed indicators that attackers were staging a zero-day, isolated SQLite CVE-2025-6965; SQLite shipped the fix in 3.50.2 on 28 Jun 2025, announced 15 Jul 2025.

What happened

The date is Google's announcement; the SQLite 3.50.2 release carrying the fix is dated 28 June 2025 and the CVE record was published 15 July 2025. Google's post says that, based on intel from Google Threat Intelligence, the Big Sleep agent discovered an SQLite vulnerability known only to threat actors and at risk of being exploited. A Google spokesperson told The Record that its threat intelligence group found artifacts indicating a zero-day was being staged but could not identify the vulnerability, and that Big Sleep was used to isolate it; Google declined to name the actors or describe the indicators. The CVE record, assigned by Google, credits Vlad Stolyarov of Google's Threat Analysis Group with assistance from Google Big Sleep, and SQLite's release log lists the corresponding fix. Independent confirmation that exploitation was imminent is absent; the claim rests on Google.

What it was built to do. Actively search for and find unknown security vulnerabilities in software using an LLM-based agent (Google DeepMind and Project Zero).

The system's part

Per Google's spokesperson, human threat analysts supplied limited indicators and Big Sleep isolated the specific vulnerability; the CVE credit names a human finder 'with assistance from Google Big Sleep'. Developer and the organization reporting the benefit are the same company; The Record and the SQLite release log are the independent sources, and neither confirms the threat-actor staging.

Replication

What produced it. Threat-intelligence artifacts narrowing the target without identifying the bug. An LLM agent with code navigation, sandboxed execution and debugging tools pointed at a specific codebase. A responsive upstream maintainer shipping a fix before disclosure.

What must hold again. Partial indicators of a staged exploit must exist and be shared with the agent's operators. Target code must be small enough for the agent to reason over. Coordinated disclosure before the actor's exploitation window.
