What happened

Researchers at the International Center of Insect Physiology and Ecology (icipe) used MaxEnt to predict desert locust breeding regions. The paper describes MaxEnt as a machine learning algorithm. Co-authors came from the Desert Locust Control Organization for Eastern Africa (DLCO-EA), Kenya's Plant Protection Services and the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), including Keith Cressman of FAO. The authors accessed 9,134 records from FAO and the Moroccan anti-locust center. They used 5,406 breeding sites from Morocco (367), Mauritania (2,661) and Saudi Arabia (1,703). The records date from 1985 to 2020. The authors used 70% of the records for training and 30% for testing. They measured accuracy as the area under the curve (AUC). Of the three country models, the Mauritania model had the highest mean AUC (0.887), followed by Saudi Arabia (0.884) and Morocco (0.820). The Morocco model, projected to Mauritanian and Saudi sites that it had not used, gave a mean predicted suitability of 0.85 at the Mauritanian sites and 0.84 at the Saudi sites. The authors projected the models to potential oviposition and breeding areas in Kenya, Uganda, South Sudan and Sudan. They thank DLCO-EA and Kenya's Ministry of Agriculture for field data. The paper, published on 20 July 2020, does not report use of the predictions in control operations. Among 30 papers indexed in Europe PMC that cite the study by 29 October 2026, none reports that FAO, DLCO-EA or national teams used the maps.

Details

AI system

Model

The Maxent algorithm.

Release

Maxent 3.4.1.

Role

The model estimated areas suitable for desert locust breeding.

Configuration

Researchers used Maxent software version 3.4.1.

Inputs and outputs

MaxEnt related historical locust breeding locations to temperature, rainfall, and soil sand and moisture. Researchers projected suitable breeding areas in East Africa and overlaid recent vegetation maps.

Workflow

FAO and Moroccan breeding-site time series 1985–2020. Bioclimatic covariates and NOAA CPC soil moisture data. A 70/30 cross-validation split.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Locust surveillance planners were the intended users of the breeding-risk maps. The retained paper does not document farmers receiving an operational benefit.

Duration

Among 30 papers indexed in Europe PMC that cite the study by 29 October 2026, none reports that the Food and Agriculture Organization, the Desert Locust Control Organization for Eastern Africa or national teams used the maps. No citing authors report a field test of the predicted East African breeding areas. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

Cross-validation with a 70/30 split on presence records, plus projection of the Morocco model to presence records in Mauritania and Saudi Arabia that were not used to build it.

Setting and sample

5,406 breeding sites from Morocco (367), Mauritania (2,661) and Saudi Arabia (1,703), 1985 to 2020; 28 Kenyan records from 2020 and 647 Sudanese records from 2013 to 2019 used to check projections.

Measures

Mean area under the ROC curve (AUC) and mean predicted suitability at withheld presence sites.

Results

Mean AUC of 0.887 for Mauritania, 0.884 for Saudi Arabia and 0.820 for Morocco; mean suitability of 0.85 and 0.84 at withheld Mauritanian and Saudi sites.

Limitations

Presence-only data with no absence records; no field survey; the authors give no hit rate for the Kenyan and Sudanese check records.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Environmental drivers in the East African projection area comparable to the training countries.
