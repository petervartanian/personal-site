Peer-reviewed MaxEnt model trained on 5,406 FAO breeding records (1985-2020) projected breeding suitability for Kenya, Uganda, South Sudan and Sudan; no operational outcome reported.

What happened

Researchers at the International Center of Insect Physiology and Ecology (icipe) with co-authors from the Desert Locust Control Organization for Eastern Africa, Kenya's Plant Protection Services and FAO's Keith Cressman used MaxEnt, which the paper describes as a machine learning algorithm, to predict desert locust breeding regions. Of 9,134 records accessed from FAO and the Moroccan anti-locust center, 5,406 breeding sites from Morocco (367), Mauritania (2,661) and Saudi Arabia (1,703) dating from 1985-2020 were used, with a 70/30 training-test split; the Mauritania model had the highest mean AUC (0.887), followed by Saudi Arabia (0.884) and Morocco (0.820). The models were projected to potential oviposition and breeding areas in Kenya, Uganda, South Sudan and Sudan. The authors thank DLCO-EA and Kenya's Ministry of Agriculture for field data. The paper does not report that the predictions were used in control operations; the date is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Predict regions suitable for desert locust breeding so that preventive control can be targeted before hoppers cause damage.

The system's part

The paper itself labels MaxEnt a machine learning algorithm and reports cross-validated AUCs. Nothing in the fetched source shows that a forecast changed a survey or control decision.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. FAO and Moroccan breeding-site time series 1985-2020. Bioclimatic covariates and NOAA CPC soil moisture data. A 70/30 cross-validation split.

What must hold again. Environmental drivers in the East African projection area comparable to the training countries.
