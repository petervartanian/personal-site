Summary
Mr. Joseph Coates received three drugs (i) that a consulting physician had considered and (ii) that Every Cure’s early drug-repurposing system had scored highly.
Account
In January 2024, Joseph Coates, 37, of Renton, Washington, was critically ill with POEMS syndrome. His heart and kidneys were failing, and he was too sick for a stem cell transplant. His partner, Tara Theobald, asked David Fajgenbaum of the University of Pennsylvania and Every Cure for help. Fajgenbaum considered three drugs used for multiple myeloma, a related disease. He later explained that an early Every Cure algorithm also scored those drugs highly for POEMS. He recommended carfilzomib, cyclophosphamide, and dexamethasone to Coates’s treating hematologist and oncologist, Wayne Gao, who agreed to administer them. Coates later reported that he responded within one or two weeks and left intensive care three weeks later. He recovered enough to receive a stem cell transplant and reported full remission five months after that transplant. The New York Times reported improvement within a week and transplant eligibility about four months later. A brief vignette in The Lancet Haematology describes a matching unnamed patient and labels the result unpublished data. The sources support a contribution from AI alongside the physicians’ judgment, but they do not establish the exact model release or isolate its effect on recovery.
