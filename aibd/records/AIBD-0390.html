Dentbird software designs crowns faster but still needs human review

In university tests, Imagoworks' Dentbird software let technicians design crowns about four times faster, and Ohio State researchers concluded that its designs needed human review.

  • 1 an organization

    Researchers at Kanagawa Dental University and Ohio State University measured shorter design time with Dentbird. Their tests took place in laboratories, and the authors did not measure workload in a dental lab's routine work.

  • Not rated a person

    The authors of a clinical study found that Dentbird crowns fitted about as well as conventional crowns, which shows no gain for patients over the usual method.

Researchers isolated the software's part in controlled comparisons. What happened

Dentbird uses deep learning and 3D dental data to detect margins and generate prosthesis designs. University researchers with no stated tie to Imagoworks tested Dentbird in 2025. Kanagawa Dental University researchers reported on 24 February 2025 that five technicians designed crowns about four times faster with Dentbird than with conventional computer-aided design (CAD) software. For one premolar crown, design time was 397.2 ± 80.4 minutes with the 3Shape Dental System and 99.4 ± 17.1 minutes with Dentbird. Ohio State researchers reported in April 2025 that six prosthodontists rated Dentbird single-crown designs as less acceptable than those of technicians and of a rival AI program. Imagoworks' submission to the International Telecommunication Union, current as of June 2025, describes real-world pilots and automated design in minutes. It claims lower technician workload and shorter treatment workflows. The submission gives no standardized before-and-after timing comparison and no patient health outcomes. The authors of a clinical study published in August 2025, funded by the Korea Medical Device Development Fund, found that Dentbird crowns fitted 62 patients about as well as conventional crowns, with larger lingual and occlusal discrepancies. In December 2025 the Ohio State researchers found that Dentbird saved design time on 30 bridges but underperformed on esthetic and functional criteria. They concluded that the designs needed human review.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Dental scans supplied three-dimensional geometry. The software classified jaws, detected preparation margins, and generated prosthesis designs for clinical or laboratory use.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

Human oversight

Ohio State researchers concluded in December 2025 that “human oversight remains essential for occlusal and proximal contact, connector design, and anterior FDPs.” In their tests, prosthodontists rated unedited Dentbird designs below those of technicians. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Benefits

Reach

The clinical study fitted crowns in 62 patients, and the Kanagawa test involved five technicians. No source counts the laboratories that use Dentbird in routine work or the patients who receive its crowns. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

No source follows Dentbird in routine use over time. The studies of 2025 are single tests, in laboratories and in one clinical study. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Measurement

Measurement

Study 1: Each patient received one crown designed by Dentbird and one designed by a technician in conventional CAD for the same prepared tooth; fit was measured by a digital triple-scan technique. Study 2: Five technicians each designed two crowns with conventional CAD and with Dentbird on the same model; design time, occlusal accuracy and marginal fit were measured by one dentist. [2] [3]

Setting and sample

Study 1: 62 patients needing complete crowns on premolars and molars, Korea. Study 2: Five technicians and two crowns each on one master model, Kanagawa Dental University, Japan. [2] [3]

Measures

Study 1: Marginal and internal gaps and occlusal discrepancies. Study 2: Design time and occlusal surface accuracy. [2] [3]

Results

Study 1: Fit was equivalent at the buccal, mesial and distal margins and internally; Dentbird crowns had larger lingual margin gaps and larger occlusal discrepancies. Study 2: Design time fell from 397.2 to 99.4 min for the premolar and from 516.4 to 97.6 min for the molar. The occlusal deviation between technicians' crowns fell from 275.5 to 25.7 µm. [2] [3]

Additional value from AI

Study 2: About four to five times faster design with Dentbird. [2] [3]

Limitations

Study 1: Interim resin crowns; one technician as the comparator; no measure of design time or patient-reported outcomes. Study 2: One model, five technicians and twenty crowns; a single measurer; the reported design times are long for a single crown and the paper does not explain how time was counted. [2] [3]

People and places

Dependencies

Dentbird ran as a cloud service accessed through a web interface, with no local CAD installation required.
