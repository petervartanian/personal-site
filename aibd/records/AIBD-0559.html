Peterborough develops OSCAR to connect council AI services

Peterborough and Datnexa are developing OSCAR to connect council services. The council reports that OSCAR Workforce is live, but has not supplied measured results.

ⓘ

What happened

Peterborough’s May 13, 2026 LGA case study describes OSCAR as a proof of concept for connecting council assistants through a shared service interface. Datnexa identifies itself as the development partner and describes AI that interprets requests and routes them to specialist services. On October 21, the council reported that OSCAR Workforce was live and helped staff find emails and documents and query data faster. It did not publish timings, user counts, or a comparative evaluation. The resident-facing accessibility benefits remain intended. A separate council contract identifies Datnexa as the supplier for Workforce design and scoping. That contract establishes procurement, not a measured benefit.

Purpose. Connect service-specific assistants through a shared, governed digital front door.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. OSCAR is intended to interpret requests, route them among specialist agents, and apply common controls. Datnexa states that the system will not independently diagnose, decide eligibility, or make safeguarding judgments. The council reports internal Workforce use, but no source supplies a model version, task-level test results, or an independent assessment of the AI contribution.

Since then
Peterborough reports that the internal OSCAR Workforce tool is live. No rollout date, usage count, or measured time saving is supplied, and resident-facing benefits remain unverified.

Further evidence

Setting. The framework is designed for web, WhatsApp, and voice channels and for connection to existing services. The reported design includes centralized permissions, redaction, guardrails, and audit logging.

Conditions. Specialist services and information sources must be available for the routing system to use. Human staff must retain responsibility for the decisions that the system is not authorized to make. Access controls and records of system actions must work in the deployment, rather than merely being described in the design.
