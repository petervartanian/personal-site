Brief from Peter

Sophia Chan's recovery at Mayo Clinic in Jacksonville, Florida, is a story that Mayo tells about its use of AI in stroke care. Her brain aneurysm ruptured on 24 February 2022, and her husband was told at first that she was unlikely to survive. She spent 24 days in the hospital, and Mayo reports that two years after the stroke she was getting back to an active family life. Mayo's account of 28 October 2022 does not mention AI.1 On 13 January 2025, Mayo Clinic Magazine credited AI algorithms with letting the care team find her aneurysm in minutes, and it named her short distance from the hospital as another likely key to her recovery.2 No source names the software or compares her care with care without it.

  1. 1 Mayo Clinic News Network is the source for the date of the stroke and the length of her stay. A. Osiadacz, “Florida mom reunites with ICU team after stroke,” Mayo Clinic News Network, October 28, 2022. web.archive.org
  2. 2 The magazine is also the source for what her husband was told and for her recovery two years after the stroke. It contrasts half an hour or longer for manual review of head CT images with seconds using AI, a comparison that describes the process in general and was not measured for her case. A. Caldwell, “Time Is Brain: Mayo Clinic experts are leveraging AI to transform stroke care and outcomes,” Mayo Clinic Magazine, January 13, 2025. mayomagazine.mayoclinic.org
