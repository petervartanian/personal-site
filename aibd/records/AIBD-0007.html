Crews stop a small night fire near Auburn after AI cameras flag it

DigitalPath's smoke-detection software on ALERTCalifornia cameras flagged a fire near Auburn at 2:33 a.m. on 6 July 2024, before any 911 call, and crews kept it to a 30 x 30 foot area.

What happened

The University of California San Diego operates the ALERTCalifornia camera network, which runs AI smoke-detection software from DigitalPath of Chico, California. Cal Fire uses the network. At 2:33 a.m. on 6 July the network posted an alert of a fire near Auburn, in the foothills east of Sacramento. The report, from September 2025, gives the day but not the year. It places the case among the previous year's fires, so the record dates it to 2024. Nobody had called 911. Cal Fire's Grass Valley Emergency Command Center verified the alert and sent engines. Crews put the fire out before it spread beyond a 30 x 30 foot area. Cal Fire Deputy Chief Brian York said that in 2024 the cameras picked up 1,668 of the 7,553 fires in Cal Fire's jurisdiction. The cameras detected 636 of those fires before any person called 911. By October 2026 the network had grown to nearly 1,300 cameras. The program director planned 1,800.
