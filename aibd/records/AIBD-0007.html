AI camera alert at 2:33 a.m. on 6 July 2024 sent Cal Fire engines to a fire near Auburn before any 911 call; held to a 30 x 30 ft area

What happened

The ALERTCalifornia camera network, operated by UC San Diego with AI smoke-detection software from DigitalPath of Chico, California, and used by Cal Fire, posted an alert at 2:33 a.m. on July 6 of a fire near Auburn, in the foothills east of Sacramento; the report, published in September 2025, gives the day but not the year, and places the case among the previous year's fires, so 2024 is used. Nobody had called 911. Cal Fire's Grass Valley Emergency Command Center verified the alert and sent engines, and crews put the fire out before it spread beyond a 30 x 30 foot area. Cal Fire Deputy Chief Brian York said that in 2024 the cameras picked up 1,668 of the 7,553 fires in Cal Fire's jurisdiction, 636 of them before any person called 911.

What it was built to do. Built to detect smoke in camera feeds and alert fire dispatchers automatically; this benefit is that purpose.

The system's part

The AI alert was the only report of the Auburn fire; Cal Fire's command center verified it and dispatched on it. The same source says the system still produces false positives from dust and clouds, so human verification sits between alert and dispatch.

Replication

What produced it
  • A fixed camera network with 24/7 AI smoke detection covering remote terrain
  • A dispatch center that verifies AI alerts and sends crews without waiting for a 911 call
What must hold again
  • A human verifies the alert before dispatch; the same source reports false positives from dust and clouds
  • Crews can reach the fire while it is still small
