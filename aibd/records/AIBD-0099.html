Google Research RMAB cuts mMitra engagement drops by 32%

off the scale

32.0% fewer cumulative engagement drops than standard care

RMAB picked weekly service calls among 23,003 mMitra mothers from 26 Apr 2021; 32% fewer engagement drops than standard care

What happened

ARMMAN's mMitra program sends automated voice messages to low-income pregnant women and new mothers in India, many of whom stop listening. With Google Research India and Harvard, ARMMAN tested a restless multi-armed bandit (RMAB) that selects which beneficiaries receive limited staff service calls each week. In a seven-week field study starting 26 April 2021, 23,003 women were split into RMAB, round-robin and current-standard-of-care groups (7,668/7,668/7,667); 225 RMAB-selected women per week were called. The RMAB group had 32.0% fewer cumulative engagement drops than standard care (p = 0.044) and 28.3% fewer than round robin (p = 0.098, significant only at the 10% level); round robin was not significantly better than standard care. ARMMAN reports the AI reduced drop-off risk by up to 32% and Google Research reports the system was deployed for weekly call scheduling.

What it was built to do. Allocate limited health-worker service calls to the beneficiaries whose program engagement they are most likely to preserve.

The system's part

The RMAB output determined which 225 women per week received ARMMAN-initiated service calls; calls were made by ARMMAN staff blind to group. The peer-reviewed AAAI paper is authored by the developers together with ARMMAN, so it is marked vendor-authored; the non-vendor documentation is ARMMAN's own program page as the implementing NGO.

Replication

What produced it. RMAB Whittle-index policy over per-beneficiary engagement states. Historical engagement data for parameter inference via clustering. ARMMAN call-center capacity (fixed weekly call budget).

What must hold again. Engagement defined as listening at least 30 seconds to weekly messages. Call budget small relative to cohort. Beneficiary behavior patterns stable enough for transition estimates.
