More mothers keep listening to health messages

mMitra’s call scheduler reduced cumulative engagement drops by 32% compared with regular messaging alone over seven weeks.

Benefits

Benefit

The scheduling policy reduced loss of engagement with health messages. [1]

Category

Continued access to maternal and child health information. [1]

Status

Observed in a randomized field study. [1]

Beneficiary groups

Mothers enrolled in mMitra and ARMMAN’s outreach program. [1]

Beneficiaries

Pregnant women and new mothers enrolled in the program. [1]

Reach

The study included 23,003 women. The model group contained 7,668, of whom about 20% received a staff call. [1]

Distribution and access

Limited staff time meant that only some participants received calls. The measured benefit was an aggregate change in listening. [1]

Duration

Listening was measured over seven weeks. [1]

Expected?

Sustained listening was the intended benefit. [1]

Costs and harms

The program could call only a small share of participants each week. The study did not establish which clinical outcomes followed. [1]

Outcome identifiers

Not assessed

AI system

System

The mMitra service-call scheduler. [1]

Model

A restless multi-armed bandit policy used Whittle indices. K-means grouped past listening patterns, and a random forest assigned new participants to groups. [1]

Release

The 2021 paper identifies the algorithms and training approach, but not a numbered software release. [1]

Provider

Researchers from Google Research, Harvard, and Singapore Management University worked with ARMMAN. [1]

Role

The model ranked participants for staff calls within the weekly calling limit. [1]

Configuration

The system modeled two weekly listening states and used 40 groups derived from historical data. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Registration details and listening logs produced a weekly list of participants for staff to call. [1]

Autonomy

The model selected call recipients. ARMMAN staff placed the calls and supported participants. [1]

Workflow

Participants received recorded health messages. Each week, the scheduler selected a subset for live calls that encouraged continued listening. [1]

Human oversight

ARMMAN staff made the calls without knowing the participants’ assigned study group. [1]

Relation to AI

Machine learning estimated listening behavior, and a bandit policy allocated staff calls. [1]

AI contribution

The scheduler selected who received a call. Comparing it with round-robin calling tested that selection policy. [1]

Measurement

Studies

AIBD-0099-E01, AIBD-0099-E02, AIBD-0099-E03 [1] [4] [5]

Study design

Researchers randomly assigned participants to model-selected calls, round-robin calls, or regular messaging alone. [1]

Measurement

Call logs identified weeks in which a participant listened to at least one message for more than 30 seconds. [1]

Setting and sample

The groups contained 7,668, 7,668, and 7,667 participants, respectively. [1]

Measures

Cumulative weekly drops in listening engagement. [1]

Comparison

Regular messages alone, and regular messages with staff calls allocated in rotation. [1]

Results

Engagement drops fell by 32.0% versus regular messages alone and 28.3% versus round-robin calling. [1]

Additional value from AI

The comparison with round-robin calling favored the model, but its p-value was .098. The comparison with messaging alone had p = .044. [1]

Limitations

The study measured listening, not clinical health. Cumulative drops can count the same participant in more than one week. [1]

Denominators

Each calling group received about 225 staff calls a week for seven weeks. [1]

Materials for replication

Public SAHELI code and a data dictionary cover the later deployed system. They do not identify the software release used in the 2021 trial. [1] [5]

Kind of value

Amount

Currency and prices

No monetary estimate is reported. [1]

Period and unit

Resources were allocated weekly over a seven-week study. [1]

Realization

The engagement improvement was observed. No monetary saving was measured. [1]

Recipients and payers

ARMMAN supplied the staff calls. The paper does not report their cost. [1]

Gross or net

No gross or net financial benefit was calculated. [1]

Included costs

The resource constraint was staff capacity: about 225 calls per week in each calling group. [1]

Comparison

The study compared calling policies at similar call capacity, rather than comparing cash expenditure. [1]

Method

The authors did not assign a monetary value to the engagement improvement. [1]

Return and payback

No return or payback estimate is reported. [1]

People and places

Participants

ARMMAN ran mMitra and supplied health workers. The research partners developed and evaluated the scheduler with the NGO. [1]

Jurisdictions

The field study took place in ARMMAN’s mMitra program in India. [1]

Ownership and control

ARMMAN operated the health program and held its participant records. Research partners used anonymized data. [1]

Dependencies

The program needed telephone access, listening logs, registration records, and staff who could place live calls. [1]

Cross-border connections

Researchers were based in India, the United States, and Singapore. The paper does not identify where participant data were hosted or transferred. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The scheduler needs usable listening histories, current registration details, and a known limit on staff calls. [1]

Replication attempts

A 2022 deployment study also found fewer engagement drops. A 2024 study reported some better health behaviors among selected survey respondents. [4] [5]

Later findings

SAHELI entered routine use in April 2022. Later studies evaluated revised models and health-behavior outcomes. [4] [5]

Availability

The developers reported continuous SAHELI use from April 2022. [5]
