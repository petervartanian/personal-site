Forest sensors alert West Sumatra patrols to chainsaws

Rainforest Connection's Guardian sensors use AI to detect chainsaws and alert patrols; in West Sumatra an alert led a patrol to a logger in August 2019, and a patroller said that logging stopped.

What happened

Rainforest Connection (RFCx), a US non-profit founded by Topher White, deploys solar-powered “Guardian” acoustic sensors. In its own words, they use “sound & artificial intelligence” to track chainsaws, vehicle engines, hunting dogs and gunshots. They send real-time alerts to a mobile app so that “an agent nearby” can respond. RFCx states that it has protected over 726,000 hectares (impact page) or 750,000 hectares (guardian page) across 8,662+ projects in 137 countries. At a KKI Warsi press conference reported by Antara on 16 July 2020, Warsi said that the first 12 Guardian units went up in July 2019 in Sirukam and other nagari forests. In August 2019, a Guardian alert led the Sirukam patrol to a logger, who was caught and then escaped. Warsi counted 12 chainsaw alerts from sensors at 17 sites, and it said that not all of them were illegal. A Thomson Reuters Foundation report published by The Straits Times on 11 February 2021 is the one independent account. It is from Solok regency, West Sumatra, Indonesia, where 27 Guardian sensors had been installed more than a year earlier. Audio streams are analyzed by AI trained to pick out chainsaw sounds, and an alert goes to community patrols. Patroller Jasrialdi said that logging “has totally stopped” in the area. The head of West Sumatra's forestry department, Yozarwardi Usama Putra, said that he would like to expand the “early-warning system”. The same report attributes the decline in logging to a wider enforcement crackdown as well. It reports that about 200 of Sirukam's 6,000 residents had worked in illegal logging. It reports that one former logger quit in 2018, before the sensors, because of the crackdown. It says that RFCx planned to collect data for peer review to prove that the system curbs logging. No park authority, ministry or court document records an alert that led to an interception.

Details

AI system

Role

The system classified recorded sounds and alerted responders to possible logging.

Configuration

Guardian G1 used repurposed smartphones in 2015–2019. Later deployments used G2 from 2020 and G3 from 2026.

Inputs and outputs

Guardian sensors streamed forest audio to a classifier that identified possible chainsaws and sent mobile alerts to patrols.

Workflow

Acoustic sensors with mobile-network or satellite uplink placed in forest canopy. Cloud AI classifier trained on chainsaw and other threat sounds. Community patrols or rangers able to reach alert locations. Parallel government enforcement crackdown in the reported area.

Human oversight

Guardian alerts went to community patrols, who can check on the ground for logging. In August 2019 an alert led the Sirukam patrol to a logger, who was caught and then escaped. Warsi said that not all of the 12 chainsaw alerts that it counted were illegal. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Duration

The cited sources cover the Sirukam deployment from July 2019 to February 2021. The 2021 report says that RFCx planned to collect data for peer review that year. No later source on Solok is cited. [1] [2] [3] [4]

People and places

Dependencies

The deployment required powered acoustic sensors, a network connection, cloud analysis and people who could reach the alerted area.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Network coverage or satellite link at the sensor site. Patrol capacity to respond to alerts on the ground. Low false-alert rate so responders keep acting. Independent evaluation to separate the sensor effect from enforcement.
