Generate Biomedicines' AI-designed GB-0669 completes phase 1

Phase 1 of the AI-designed antibody GB-0669 in 51 healthy adults showed tolerability, a 54-day half-life and dose-dependent virus neutralization.

ⓘ

What happened

Generate Biomedicines used machine-learning models and laboratory testing to optimize the antibody that became GB-0669. The original design study describes a structure-conditioned generative model followed by sequence-to-function models trained on experimental results. The subsequent phase 1 study enrolled 51 healthy adults in Orlando, with 36 receiving GB-0669 and 15 placebo across five dose groups. The authors reported no dose-limiting toxicity and no serious adverse events, although one grade 2 infusion reaction led to interruption of the infusion. Serum testing showed dose-dependent viral neutralization at 600 and 1,200 mg. This was laboratory testing of participant serum, not evidence that patients avoided infection or recovered from illness. The results appeared as an author preprint on October 8, 2025 and in the Journal of Infectious Diseases in July 2026.

The trial registry was first posted after the study ended. Its structured allocation field says nonrandomized, while the manuscript describes randomization; that inconsistency remains unresolved.

Purpose. Use generative machine learning to design a therapeutic antibody against a conserved, poorly immunogenic region of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein.

AI’s contribution

The original design study describes machine-learning optimization of a known antibody using a structure-conditioned generative model and experimentally trained sequence-to-function models. The clinical study evaluates the resulting antibody’s safety and laboratory activity. It does not quantify AI’s advantage over an alternative discovery process or establish a clinical benefit to patients.

Further evidence

Setting. A generative protein-design platform aimed at the conserved S2 stem helix. A randomized, placebo-controlled single-ascending-dose trial in healthy adults. Live-virus neutralization assays on participants' serum.

Conditions. Healthy adult volunteers, single intravenous ascending doses from 100 to 2,400 mg. Live-virus neutralization assay against circulating sarbecoviruses. Antibody manufactured as in the trial.
