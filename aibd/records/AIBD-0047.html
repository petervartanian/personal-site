AI-designed COVID-19 antibody is tolerated in its first trial in people

In a phase 1 trial in 51 healthy adults, GB-0669, an antibody optimized with Generate Biomedicines' machine-learning models, was tolerated, had a 54-day half-life and neutralized virus in serum.

  • Not rated a person

    The trial enrolled healthy volunteers and measured safety, pharmacokinetics and serum neutralization; no person obtained a therapeutic benefit.

  • Not rated an organization

    The authors report a completed phase 1 and recommend a phase 2 trial; no source reports a realized organizational outcome.

  • Not rated society

    No source shows that the antibody prevents or treats infection in patients, so no population-level benefit is demonstrated.

What happened

Generate Biomedicines used machine-learning models and laboratory testing to optimize the antibody that became GB-0669. The original design study describes a structure-conditioned generative model followed by sequence-to-function models trained on experimental results. The phase 1 trial began on 18 July 2023, according to the sponsor's registry entry. It enrolled 51 healthy adults in Orlando, with 36 receiving GB-0669 and 15 receiving placebo across five dose groups. Company staff presented interim results at IDWeek on 17 October 2024. Their abstract states that each dose cohort was randomized to GB-0669 or placebo. The trial was completed on 22 November 2024, according to the registry entry. The registry entry was first posted after the study ended. Its structured allocation field says that the trial was nonrandomized, and the manuscript describes randomization. That inconsistency remains unresolved. The results appeared as an author preprint on 8 October 2025 and in the Journal of Infectious Diseases in July 2026. The authors reported no dose-limiting toxicity and no serious adverse events. One grade 2 infusion reaction led to interruption of the infusion. Serum testing showed dose-dependent live-virus neutralization at 600 and 1,200 mg. These were laboratory tests of participant serum. They do not show that patients avoided infection or recovered from illness. The authors propose a phase 2 trial at 1,200 mg in immunocompromised people. As of 29 October 2026, no later GB-0669 trial is registered.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

A generative model proposed changes to an existing antibody sequence. Laboratory results then trained sequence-to-function models that guided another design round and selection of GB-0669.

Workflow

A generative protein-design platform aimed at the conserved S2 stem helix. A randomized, placebo-controlled single-ascending-dose trial in healthy adults. Live-virus neutralization assays on participants' serum.

Human oversight

Researchers selected and tested the antibody variants, manufactured the candidate, and conducted the human safety study with clinical monitoring.

Benefits

Reach

The trial enrolled 51 healthy adults, of whom 36 received GB-0669 and 15 received placebo. No source reports that any of them gained a health benefit. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The trial gave single doses to healthy adults, and the authors report a 54-day half-life. No source follows a health benefit over time. The authors propose a phase 2 trial at 1,200 mg in immunocompromised people. As of 29 October 2026, no later GB-0669 trial is registered. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Measurement

Measurement

Safety monitoring, serum drug concentrations and live-virus neutralization assays of participants' serum over 43 weeks.

Setting and sample

51 healthy adults aged 18 to 55 at one phase 1 unit in Orlando, Florida; 36 received GB-0669 and 15 placebo across five single intravenous dose cohorts from 100 to 2,400 mg.

Measures

Dose-limiting toxicities, adverse reactions, half-life and serum live-virus neutralization.

Results

No dose-limiting toxicities, adverse reactions of grade 1 or 2 only, a half-life of 54 days, and dose-dependent neutralization at 600 and 1,200 mg separated from placebo.

Additional value from AI

Not measured; the trial does not compare the AI-optimized antibody with one found by another method.

Limitations

Healthy volunteers only, small cohorts, serum neutralization in the laboratory with no infection or clinical endpoint; the authors are company staff, and the registry lists the allocation as non-randomized.

Denominators

One intravenous dose.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Healthy adult volunteers, single intravenous ascending doses from 100 to 2,400 mg. Live-virus neutralization assay against circulating sarbecoviruses. Antibody manufactured as in the trial.

Availability

GB-0669 remained an investigational antibody in the reported study. Its use required trial enrollment and controlled manufacture rather than access to a general AI design service.

Evidence

Original evidence

Generate Biomedicines staff wrote or co-wrote every cited source. Company staff presented interim results at IDWeek on 17 October 2024. The full clinical results appeared as the authors' preprint of 8 October 2025 and then in the Journal of Infectious Diseases in July 2026. The machine-learning design traces to a separate company preprint of 31 March 2025. The structured allocation field of the registry entry says that the trial was nonrandomized, and the IDWeek abstract and the paper describe randomization. [1] [2] [3] [4]
