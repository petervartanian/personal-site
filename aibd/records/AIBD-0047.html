Generate Biomedicines' AI-designed GB-0669 completes phase 1

off the scale

dose-dependent serum live-virus neutralization occurred at 600 and 1,200 mg

Phase 1 of the AI-designed antibody GB-0669 in 51 healthy adults showed tolerability, a 54-day half-life and dose-dependent virus neutralization.

What happened

Generate Biomedicines designed GB-0669, a monoclonal antibody against the conserved SARS-CoV-2 spike S2 stem helix, using machine learning. A phase 1 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled single-ascending-dose trial registered as NCT07050511 enrolled 51 healthy adults, 36 receiving GB-0669 and 15 placebo across five dose cohorts from 100 to 2,400 mg, running from 18 July 2023 to 22 November 2024. The results were published in the Journal of Infectious Diseases on 14 July 2026: no dose-limiting toxicities occurred, all adverse reactions were grade 1 or 2, pharmacokinetics were dose-proportional with a 54-day half-life, and dose-dependent serum live-virus neutralization occurred at 600 and 1,200 mg. No patient benefit was demonstrated; the authors propose phase 2 evaluation in immunocompromised patients.

What it was built to do. Use generative machine learning to design a therapeutic antibody against a conserved, poorly immunogenic region of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein.

The system's part

The antibody itself was AI-designed and optimized against the spike S2 stem helix, so the AI output is the investigational product. The in-human findings are safety, pharmacokinetic and neutralization measurements from a randomized placebo-controlled trial, not evidence of clinical benefit. The peer-reviewed report is written by the developer's staff; the independent corroboration available is the trial registry record.
