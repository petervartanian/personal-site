Insulin pump cuts low blood sugar, and a mother gives birth at term

A Medtronic MiniMed 640G pump with predictive low glucose suspend cut hypoglycemic episodes for a woman with type 1 diabetes, who gave birth at 39 weeks after two miscarriages.

  • 3 a person

    After two miscarriages, her hypoglycemia frequency and glucose variability fell on the device, and the pregnancy reached term with no significant complications. The source does not show that the device caused the live birth.

What happened

A 22-year-old Brazilian woman in Japan had had type 1 diabetes for 11 years. She miscarried at 7 and 9 weeks in two earlier pregnancies, when she used a MiniMed 620G sensor-augmented pump without predictive low glucose suspend. At about 6 weeks of her third pregnancy she was switched to a Medtronic MiniMed 640G sensor-augmented pump with predictive low glucose suspend. The pump suspended insulin for about 249 minutes per day. Her hypoglycemic episodes fell from 2.1–2.5 to 1.4 per day, and time below 70 mg/dL fell from 1.1–1.4% to 0.5%. She delivered a 4,137 g boy by cesarean section at 39 weeks and 3 days with no significant complications. After the delivery she went back to multiple daily injections at her own request, and her glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) stayed at 5 to 6% while she breastfed. Clinicians at the University of Toyama described the case in Diabetology International on 6 March 2021, without event dates. In a study published on 30 March 2024, clinicians in Tokyo with no connection to the case reported that 11 pregnant women on the same pump reached glucose targets without more hypoglycemia. They state that the pump was launched in Japan in 2018.

Details

AI system

Role

The pump predicted low glucose and suspended insulin delivery.

Configuration

The initial event involved a MiniMed 640G. A later report involved a MiniMed 780G.

Autonomy

The MiniMed 640G predicted falling glucose and suspended basal insulin, averaging about 249 minutes of suspension per day in this pregnancy.

Workflow

A person with type 1 diabetes and frequent or unrecognized hypoglycemia uses a Medtronic pump with CGM and predictive low glucose suspend enabled. Sensor glucose is predicted to fall and the algorithm suspends basal insulin. Hypoglycemia frequency and variability fall and the clinical goal (pregnancy, restored awareness) is reached.

Human oversight

Clinicians adjusted insulin doses and continued diabetes and obstetric care. The patient used the pump during pregnancy and returned to injections after delivery at her request.

Benefits

Reach

One woman benefited in this case. In the 2024 Tokyo study, 11 pregnant women on the same pump reached glucose targets without more hypoglycemia. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

After the delivery she went back to multiple daily injections at her own request, and her glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) stayed at 5 to 6% while she breastfed. No source reports on her after the 2021 case report. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Measurement

Measurement

Retrospective review of glucose records, insulin suspension time and neonatal outcomes. [4]

Setting and sample

22 women with type 1 diabetes who delivered between 2011 and 2021 at two Tokyo hospitals; 11 used the MiniMed 640G with predictive low glucose suspend. [4]

Measures

Time in range, time below range and heavy-for-date infants. [4]

Results

One heavy-for-date infant among 11 on the pump against five among 11 without it; time in range above 70% in the second and third trimesters on the pump. [4]

Additional value from AI

Longer insulin suspension went with higher time in range (r = 0.62) and lower time below range (r = -0.40). [4]

Limitations

Small retrospective sample with historical controls who lacked real-time continuous glucose monitoring. [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Sensor is worn and calibrated with suspend enabled. The user does not override suspensions. Clinical follow-up adjusts settings.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2.

Availability

The MiniMed 640G had just become commercially available in Japan before the third pregnancy. Cost had led her to stop using the earlier pump after her second miscarriage.

Evidence

Original evidence

The case is known only from the University of Toyama clinicians' report in Diabetology International, cited at PubMed Central and at the publisher's address. The 2024 study by Tokyo clinicians and the 2025 case report from Czechia concern other patients. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Purpose

Predictive low glucose suspend is built to stop insulin delivery when sensor glucose is predicted to fall low, to avoid hypoglycemia. The benefit was that purpose, with a successful pregnancy as the downstream outcome.
