MiniMed 640G cuts hypoglycemia; mother delivers a 4,137 g boy

MiniMed 640G predictive low-glucose suspend cut hypoglycemia in a T1D woman after two miscarriages; term birth of a 4,137 g boy.

ⓘ

What happened

A case report appeared in Diabetology International on 6 March 2021 (event dates not reported). It describes a 22-year-old Brazilian woman in Japan with type 1 diabetes for 11 years. She miscarried at 7 and 9 weeks in two earlier pregnancies. At about 6 weeks of her third pregnancy she was switched to a Medtronic MiniMed 640G sensor-augmented pump with predictive low glucose suspend. The pump suspended insulin for about 249 minutes per day. Hypoglycemic episodes fell from 2.1–2.5 to 1.4 per day and time below 70 mg/dL from 1.1–1.4% to 0.5%. She delivered a 4,137 g boy by cesarean section at 39 weeks and 3 days with no significant complications.

Purpose. Predictive low glucose suspend is built to stop insulin delivery when sensor glucose is predicted to fall low, to avoid hypoglycemia. The benefit was that purpose, with a successful pregnancy as the downstream outcome.

AI’s contribution

The pump's predictive algorithm autonomously suspended insulin for about four hours a day. The authors link this to fewer hypoglycemic episodes and lower variability. Obstetric and diabetes care were delivered by clinicians. The causal link to carrying the pregnancy is inferred, not demonstrated.

Beneficiaries

    After two miscarriages, hypoglycemia frequency and glucose variability fell on the device and the pregnancy reached term with a healthy infant. A life-changing benefit, though the source does not prove the device caused the live birth.

Further evidence

Setting. A person with type 1 diabetes and frequent or unrecognized hypoglycemia uses a Medtronic pump with CGM and predictive low glucose suspend enabled. Sensor glucose is predicted to fall and the algorithm suspends basal insulin. Hypoglycemia frequency and variability fall and the clinical goal (pregnancy, restored awareness) is reached.

Conditions. Sensor is worn and calibrated with suspend enabled. The user does not override suspensions. Clinical follow-up adjusts settings.
