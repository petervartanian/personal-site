MiniMed 640G predictive low-glucose suspend cut hypoglycaemia in a T1D woman after two miscarriages; term birth of a 4,137 g boy.

What happened

In a case report published in Diabetology International on 6 March 2021 (dates of events not stated; publication date used), a 22-year-old Brazilian woman living in Japan with type 1 diabetes for 11 years, who had miscarried at 7 and 9 weeks in two earlier pregnancies, was switched at about 6 weeks of her third pregnancy to a Medtronic MiniMed 640G sensor-augmented pump with predictive low glucose suspend. The pump suspended insulin for about 249 minutes per day; hypoglycaemic episodes fell from 2.1-2.5 to 1.4 per day and time below 70 mg/dL from 1.1-1.4% to 0.5%. She delivered a 4,137 g boy by caesarean section at 39 weeks and 3 days with no significant complications.

What it was built to do. Predictive low glucose suspend is built to stop insulin delivery when sensor glucose is predicted to fall low, to avoid hypoglycaemia; the benefit was that purpose, with a successful pregnancy as the downstream outcome.

The system's part

The pump's predictive algorithm autonomously suspended insulin for about four hours a day, which the authors link to fewer hypoglycaemic episodes and lower variability; obstetric and diabetes care were delivered by clinicians, and the causal link to carrying the pregnancy is inferred, not demonstrated.

Replication

What produced it

  • A person with type 1 diabetes and frequent or unrecognised hypoglycaemia uses a Medtronic pump with CGM and predictive low glucose suspend enabled
  • Sensor glucose is predicted to fall and the algorithm suspends basal insulin
  • Hypoglycaemia frequency and variability fall and the clinical goal (pregnancy, restored awareness) is reached

What must hold again

  • Sensor is worn and calibrated with suspend enabled
  • The user does not override suspensions
  • Clinical follow-up adjusts settings
