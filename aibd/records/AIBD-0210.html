TIAA’s AI may have helped stop a scam involving retirement savings

TIAA’s CEO said that an AI flag, staff intervention, and contact with a participant’s daughter protected retirement savings of more than $3 million.

ⓘ

What happened

TIAA CEO Thasunda Brown Duckett told Fortune that an AI alert helped staff stop a scam involving a 76-year-old participant’s retirement savings. A portfolio manager referred the concern to the fraud team, which spent hours with the participant and contacted his daughter. Duckett said that the money did not move. The interview describes retirement holdings above $3 million, but does not itemize an attempted transfer. The original episode was released on June 17, 2026, with its transcript published five days later. No participant statement, transaction record, or separate investigation is supplied.

Purpose. The unnamed AI tool flagged suspected fraud for human review. Staff investigation and contact with the participant’s family were necessary to stop the money from moving.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. Duckett attributes the initial flag to AI and the successful intervention to a portfolio manager, the fraud team, and the participant’s daughter. The transcript does not establish the model’s design, the requested transfer amount, or what would have happened without its flag.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    The CEO describes a 76-year-old retaining retirement savings of more than $3 million after a suspected scam was interrupted. The participant is unnamed, and no transaction record or independent account establishes the amount at risk or the counterfactual loss.

  • an organization

    The CEO describes a successful fraud intervention by TIAA staff. The source does not quantify TIAA’s own avoided cost or the cost of the intervention.

Further evidence

Setting. An AI alert that a portfolio manager reviewed and referred to the fraud team. Staff who spent hours discussing the suspected scam with the participant. Contact with a family member who could help stop the money from moving.

Conditions. The flagged request must actually be fraud. Duckett gives no false-positive rate, and the scam itself is not described. The firm must be able to hold a transfer while it persuades the customer. Duckett says AI by itself would not necessarily have protected him. The customer or a relative must be reachable and open to persuasion before the funds move.
