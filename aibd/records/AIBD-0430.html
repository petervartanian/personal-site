An Indian college builds a deep-learning model to predict microclimates

Sri Sivasubramaniya Nadar College of Engineering is developing a Vision Transformer model to predict local microclimates for urban design, heat-risk management and irrigation.

  • Not rated an organization

    The team reports a research model. Validation with urban authorities and agricultural technology firms is still planned.

  • Not rated a person

    Thermal comfort and reduced heat-related health risks are intended uses of the forecasts, with no outcome measurement.

  • Not rated society

    Urban heat mitigation and resource savings remain proposed downstream uses.

What happened

The project comes from Sri Sivasubramaniya Nadar College of Engineering in India, which describes a Vision Transformer prototype in academic use on a college campus. The team describes deep-learning prediction of local climate conditions. The proposed benefits include better planning and thermal comfort. No source documents a change in an implemented service or intervention. Additional locations, seasons, model development, and external validation are planned. The paper that the International Telecommunication Union entry names as its pilot is a 2024 study by a National University of Singapore team on a different model. By October 2026, the code repository that the entry names was not public, and no later report of results had appeared.

Details

AI system

Model

The campus prototype uses a Vision Transformer trained on visual and numerical data.

Inputs and outputs

The model combines satellite imagery, street-level images, and campus weather-station readings to predict temperature, humidity, wind speed, and solar irradiance.
