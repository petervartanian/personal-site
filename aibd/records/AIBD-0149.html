Frankfurt camera AI flags a missing 16-year-old, and police find her

Frankfurt police found a missing 16-year-old in crisis within a day, after the face recognition software VAS Videmo360 flagged her on a Bahnhofsviertel camera under a judge-confirmed order.

  • 3 a person

    A 16-year-old whose farewell letter led police to assume an acute psychological crisis was located in the Bahnhofsviertel within a day. She was returned to psychiatric care. The police frame this as averting danger to life and limb. But no source reports what would otherwise have happened, so life-saving is not established.

  • 1 an organization

    The Frankfurt police recorded the first confirmed success of a system in use since July 2025. The police president and the interior minister cited it as validation. netzpolitik.org reported in April 2026 that it was still the only success among a low double-digit number of searches. The operational gain is modest.

The system’s part
The police press release documents the system's contribution step by step. After a judge-confirmed emergency order, the girl's photo was entered into the database. Late on 9 October the AI produced an automated match alert for Taunusstraße. A video operator checked it and a patrol confirmed it in person. The police had only indications that she might be in the district, so the AI supplied the place and the moment. Human verification and the patrol did the rest. Whether and how soon conventional patrols would have found her is not reported.
Limits
The located person and protective intervention are reported outcomes. A suicide prevented is not an observed endpoint, and this single positive match cannot establish overall accuracy or proportionality of public-space surveillance. The account supplies no long-term clinical outcome.

What happened

On 8 October 2025 a 16-year-old was reported missing after failing to return to the psychiatric facility in Hesse where she lived. A farewell letter pointed to an acute psychological crisis. Indications placed her in Frankfurt's Bahnhofsviertel. Citing imminent danger, Frankfurt's police president issued an emergency order, which a judge confirmed. The order let the police enter her photograph into the AI-assisted camera system that had run in the district since July 2025. The system operates under the Hessian police law amended in February 2025. Late on 9 October the system flagged a match on Taunusstraße. A video operator checked it and a patrol confirmed it. Officers took the girl into protective custody and returned her to the child and adolescent psychiatric facility. On 16 October 2025 the police announced the case and called it the system's first genuine positive hit. In December 2025 the Gesellschaft für Freiheitsrechte (GFF) and Doña Carmen sued at the Frankfurt Administrative Court. They asked the court to refer the sections of the police law that allow remote identification to the Federal Constitutional Court. In April 2026 netzpolitik.org reported that a low double-digit number of persons had been searched for with the system since July 2025, and that this case was the only success. By then the Hessian Greens had also asked the Staatsgerichtshof to review the amendment to the police law behind real-time face recognition. In their filing of 22 April 2026 the plaintiffs argue that the law sets no danger threshold for searches for missing persons. The filing cites the police data protection impact assessment, which names the software as VAS Videmo360. By April 2026 the GFF reported that the police had extended the system to two more places in the city center. The system kept running, with an evaluation phase due from July 2026.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

VAS Videmo360 video analysis system (product named in the police data protection impact assessment, as cited in the GFF filing); the police say that only the vendor knows the exact specifications of the face-template model

Role

The software matched a photograph against surveillance video and sent an alert for human verification.

Configuration

The facial-recognition component compared images with the BKA’s database of facial images.

Inputs and outputs

Police entered the missing girl's photograph into the system. Matching against live camera views produced an alert locating a possible match on Taunusstraße.

Workflow

A missing-person report with concrete signs of danger to life (a farewell letter) and indications of the person's whereabouts inside the camera-covered district. A legal basis, the Hessian police law amended in February 2025, plus a police-president emergency order confirmed by a judge. These let the girl's photo be entered into the system. An AI-assisted camera network already running in the Bahnhofsviertel since July 2025, with a staffed Video-Operation-Center. Patrols close enough to verify and act on a match the same evening.

Human oversight

A judge confirmed the emergency search order. A video operator checked the match, and a patrol verified the girl's identity before taking her into protective custody.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The missing girl and the Frankfurt officers who searched for her.

Reach

One girl.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Suitable cameras must cover the person’s route. Operators and police patrols must verify the match, and the search needs a lawful basis.

The Bahnhofsviertel Videoschutzanlage's AI performs real-time biometric remote identification. The police press release called it the system's first genuine positive hit.
