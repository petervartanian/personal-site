Smartwatch readings lead a father and daughter to a long QT diagnosis

A Withings ScanWatch displayed a prolonged QT interval that led clinicians in Germany to diagnose inherited long QT syndrome in a father and his daughter and to prescribe nadolol.

Brief from Peter

A man came to the cardiology clinic of University Medical Centre Mannheim, Germany, because of readings from his smartwatch, and the visit led to a diagnosis. The man, an asymptomatic 39-year-old, had recorded single-lead electrocardiograms (ECGs) on his Withings ScanWatch out of curiosity, and the watch displayed values such as a QT interval of 506 ms and a corrected QT interval (QTc) of 457 ms. Genetic screening showed a previously unpublished KCNH2 variant, which established congenital long QT syndrome. Nadolol shortened his QTc to 430 ms, and his 7-year-old daughter, who carried the same variant, was also prescribed nadolol.1 The case report appeared on 4 July 2024 and gives no dates for the events. The diagnosis rested on clinical ECGs and genetic testing, and the report does not say who first noticed the watch's values. Researchers at University Hospital Basel with no tie to Withings tested the ScanWatch's automated QT measurement in 317 patients. They found that the watch measured QTc automatically in 56% of them and concluded that its algorithm needed improvement for clinical use.2

  1. 1 The report states that the father is followed at regular intervals without further interventions. Rudic B et al., HeartRhythm Case Reports, July 4, 2024. europepmc.org
  2. 2 In the same study, 29% of the watch's measurements differed from manual readings by more than 30 ms. Mannhart D et al., Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine, June 23, 2022. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Details

AI system

Role

The watch analyzed ECG recordings and displayed QT and QTc values.

Configuration

The participant used a Withings ScanWatch.

Inputs and outputs

ScanWatch produced single-lead ECG recordings and QT measurements that prompted clinical assessment.

Workflow

The wearer records ScanWatch ECGs and the QT function reports a prolonged interval. The wearer or a clinician takes the displayed values to a cardiology service. Clinical ECG and genetic testing confirm an inherited channelopathy.

Human oversight

Clinical ECGs and genetic testing established long QT syndrome, and clinicians prescribed nadolol after assessing the findings.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The man and his seven-year-old daughter both received a diagnosis and treatment after family testing identified the same variant.

Reach

Two people benefited. The father and his 7-year-old daughter both received the diagnosis and a prescription for nadolol. [1] [2]

Duration

The case report states that the father is followed at regular intervals without further interventions. No later development is reported, so the duration of the benefit is not established. [1] [2]
