Withings ScanWatch ECGs lead to a long-QT diagnosis and nadolol

Withings ScanWatch ECGs showing prolonged QT led to a KCNH2 long-QT diagnosis and nadolol for a father and daughter (Germany).

ⓘ

What happened

An asymptomatic 39-year-old man came to the cardiology outpatient clinic of University Medical Centre Mannheim, Germany, because of QT prolongation recorded with his Withings ScanWatch. He had made its single-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) recordings out of curiosity. The watch displayed values such as QT 506 ms and QTc 457 ms. Genetic screening found a previously unpublished nonsense variant in KCNH2, establishing congenital long QT syndrome. Nadolol 40 mg daily shortened his QTc by 50–70 ms to 430 ms. His 7-year-old daughter carried the same variant with a borderline QTc of 460 ms and was also prescribed nadolol. The case report appeared on 4 July 2024 (event dates not reported).

Purpose. The ScanWatch records a single-lead ECG and, in the version described, includes a QT monitoring function. Flagging a prolonged QT interval was within what it was built to do.

AI’s contribution

The watch's automated ECG analysis displayed the QT and QTc values that prompted the clinic visit. No automatic alert is described. The report does not say who first noticed the values. Diagnosis rested on clinical ECGs and genetic testing.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Two identifiable people (father and daughter) received a genetic long QT diagnosis and prophylactic beta-blockade against sudden arrhythmic death. A life-changing benefit, though no event was imminent.

Further evidence

Setting. The wearer records ScanWatch ECGs and the QT function reports a prolonged interval. The wearer or a clinician takes the displayed values to a cardiology service. Clinical ECG and genetic testing confirm an inherited channelopathy.

Conditions. The QT function is available in the wearer's region and firmware. Recordings are of adequate quality. Cardiogenetic evaluation and family screening are accessible.
