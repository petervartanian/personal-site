Google flood alerts in Bihar cut reported illness by 16.6%

Bihar RCT, 319 panchayats: households relayed Google flood alerts reported 16.6% fewer illness symptoms and about 30% lower medical costs.

ⓘ

What happened

Google's AI flood-forecasting system, run with India's Central Water Commission (CWC), issues riverine flood alerts as Android push notifications. In Bihar, India, economists Maulik Jagnani (Tufts) and Rohini Pande (Yale) ran a randomized evaluation across 319 panchayats in 12 flood-prone districts. It covered the 2022 and 2023 monsoon seasons (June to October). The non-governmental organization Yuganter trained community volunteers to relay the alerts. Treatment households got 2.41 more alerts in 2022 and were 46.5% more likely to get an alert before water reached their area. They reported 16.56% fewer illness symptoms and about 30% lower medical spending than controls. No specific evacuation tied to an alert is documented.

Purpose. The forecasting system was built to warn people of riverine floods before water arrives so they can prepare or evacuate. The measured preparedness and health effects are that intended benefit, but only at cohort level.

AI’s contribution

The AI forecast generated the alerts. The randomized contrast, however, is between community dissemination of those alerts and alerts alone. So the measured effect is attributable to the dissemination of AI forecasts rather than to forecast accuracy itself. A 2019 pilot in which the only added treatment was raising alert awareness with a single local leader found no treatment impact.

Further evidence

Setting. Google/CWC flood alerts available to all 319 study panchayats (both arms); the randomized element was volunteer dissemination. Trained community volunteers relaying smartphone alerts in villages with low literacy and limited location-services use. Repeated flood seasons (2022 was more intense than 2023) allowing effects to be measured.

Conditions. Alerts reach households before water arrives (46.5% more likely in treatment communities in 2022). Households trust alerts despite more false positives (8.09% vs 3.64%). Dissemination capacity is maintained. From 2023 two volunteers per panchayat replaced three, supplemented by direct WhatsApp/SMS messages to treatment households enrolled in the 2022 endline survey.
