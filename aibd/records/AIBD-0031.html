Flood alerts relayed by volunteers cut home flooding in Bihar

In a Bihar trial, volunteers relayed Google and Central Water Commission flood alerts, and treated households under severe flooding were 12.7 percentage points less likely to have their home flooded.

  • Not rated a person

    The effects are averages across treated households, and no source documents a single household whose outcome changed because of an alert.

  • 2 society

    Across treated village councils under severe flooding, households were 12.7 percentage points less likely to have their home flooded and borrowed less after floods. In their four-season analysis, the authors find no effect on physical health.

What happened

Google's AI flood-forecasting system, run with India's Central Water Commission, issues riverine flood alerts as Android push notifications. In Bihar, India, the economists Maulik Jagnani (Tufts) and Rohini Pande (Yale) ran a randomized evaluation across 319 panchayats, or village councils, in 12 flood-prone districts. The non-governmental organization Yuganter trained community volunteers to relay the alerts in the treatment villages. Control villages received the same forecasts and alerts but had no volunteers. A working paper of 3 April 2024 covers the 2022 and 2023 monsoon seasons (June to October). The authors report that treatment households got 2.41 more alerts in 2022 and were 46.5% more likely to get an alert before water reached their area. In 2023 treatment households received 4.35 more alerts and were 88% more likely to receive an alert before water reached their panchayat. Treatment households reported 16.56% fewer illness symptoms and about 30% lower medical spending than controls. The authors report that households in treatment areas spent roughly INR 6,500 less on illness treatment, a 32.87% decrease. No specific evacuation tied to an alert is documented. A September 2026 version of the working paper covers four monsoon seasons from 2022 to 2025. The authors state that Google alerts were unavailable in 2022, when volunteers relayed Central Water Commission forecasts. From 2023 the volunteers relayed Google Flood Hub alerts and Google's flood maps. The authors state that the experiment isolates the value of community delivery. They state that it does not measure the value of the forecasting systems. Among control households under severe flooding, the chance of home flooding was 23.7 percentage points higher than in a season with no flood. The authors report that treated households under severe flooding were 12.7 percentage points less likely to have their own home flooded and borrowed about INR 12,600 less. They report that welfare gains concentrate in flooded villages, where the program raised a post-flood welfare index by 0.3 standard deviations. They also report gains in food security. They report that the program had no effect on physical health. This version does not repeat the illness and medical spending results of April 2024. The authors acknowledge financial support from Google.org.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Google/CWC flood alerts available to all 319 study panchayats (both arms); the randomized element was volunteer dissemination. Trained community volunteers relaying smartphone alerts in villages with low literacy and limited location-services use. Repeated flood seasons (2022 was more intense than 2023) allowing effects to be measured.

Human oversight

Volunteers trained by Yuganter relayed the forecasts to households in the treatment villages, and both study arms received the same forecasts and alerts. In 2022, when Google alerts were unavailable, the volunteers relayed Central Water Commission forecasts. From 2023 they relayed Google Flood Hub alerts and Google's flood maps. [1] [2] [3]

Benefits

Reach

No source counts the households that benefited. The evaluation covered households in 319 panchayats in 12 flood-prone districts of Bihar. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

The April 2024 version covers the June to October monsoons of 2022 and 2023, and 2022 had the more intense floods. The September 2026 version extends the evaluation to four monsoon seasons from 2022 to 2025. [1] [2] [3]

Measurement

Measurement

Household panel surveys at midline and endline each season, with a satellite measure of flood severity for each village and season. [3]

Setting and sample

319 village councils (gram panchayats) in 12 districts of North Bihar, 160 treated and 159 control; panel of 5,582 households over four monsoon seasons. [3]

Measures

Receipt of alerts, advance preparation, a post-flood welfare index, home flooding, food security, distress borrowing and physical health. [3]

Results

Post-flood welfare index up 0.3 standard deviations in flooded villages; home flooding down 12.7 percentage points under severe floods; distress borrowing down by about INR 12,600; no effect on physical health. [3]

Additional value from AI

Not measured: both arms received the same AI and CWC forecasts. [3]

Limitations

Working paper without peer review; outcomes are self-reported; the authors acknowledge Google.org funding; the April 2024 health findings are not repeated. [3]

Denominators

Four monsoon seasons, 2022 to 2025. [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Alerts reach households before water arrives (46.5% more likely in treatment communities in 2022). Households trust alerts despite more false positives (8.09% vs 3.64%). Dissemination capacity is maintained. From 2023 two volunteers per panchayat replaced three, supplemented by direct WhatsApp/SMS messages to treatment households enrolled in the 2022 endline survey.

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.
