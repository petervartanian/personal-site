What happened

BattlegroundAI describes advertising for an unnamed advocacy organization that supported school-board candidates in Virginia, Michigan, and North Carolina. In a post of 19 December 2024, the company places this three-state campaign in 2024. The company reports that the campaign cut cost per impression by 9% and cost per click by 5%. It also reports that combined human and AI copy had a 3% higher click-through rate than human or AI copy alone. The post does not name the baseline for the cost figures. On its website, the vendor reports that AI-generated copy accounted for five of the ten highest click-through rates and five of the lowest costs per click. Its submission to the AI Opportunity Inventory also claims a 9% reduction in advertising costs and a 16–32% increase in reach. The submission cites an Analyst Institute write-up that is open only to members, so those percentages remain claims made in the submission. The public summaries do not provide the comparison design. They do not separate the effect of AI from human editing, targeting, spending, and platform delivery. Election wins do not show that AI caused electoral success.
