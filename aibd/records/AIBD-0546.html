Falls drop after six Dorset care homes install acoustic sensors

Dorset Council reports that total falls fell by 49.2% and hospital transfers by 79.3% after six care homes installed Ally Labs acoustic and movement sensors.

  • 3 a person

    Dorset Council reports that unwitnessed bedroom falls fell by 58.2% and hospital transfers by 79.3%, and half of the homes reported better sleep. This is a reduced risk of harm for residents, reported by the deployer and supported by a resident's account to the BBC.

  • 2 an organization

    The council reports that ambulance callouts fell by 63.7% and that two thirds of homes cut scheduled night checks. The acting manager of one participating home told the BBC that the home will pay to keep the system after the trial ended.

The system’s part
Not isolated. The sensors learn each resident's usual night pattern and alert staff to sounds, movement and changes in that pattern. The council compared counts in the same homes before and after installation, with no comparison group. Two thirds of the homes also cut scheduled night checks. The council and the maker both say that local leadership and practice shaped the results, so the share due to the AI alerts is unknown.
Limits
The council published percentage changes and no baseline counts of falls, ambulance callouts or hospital transfers. The comparison has no control group. The full report that was due from the National Health Service had not appeared. One of the six homes recorded more falls.
Costs and harms
Two thirds of the homes cut scheduled night checks alongside the sensors, and one home recorded more falls. In a comparable trial in two South Australian aged care facilities, audio and video monitoring reportedly produced more than 12,000 false alerts. No source reports false alert rates in Dorset.
Later
Persisted, 28 April 2026. The BBC reported that Culliford House in Dorchester will pay to keep the sensors after the trial and its funding ended. DHSC told the BBC that it would set out a second phase of the program later.

What happened

Dorset Council planned to install Ally Labs acoustic and movement monitoring in six care homes to detect changes and support nighttime care. The Local Government Association case study describes 184 planned sensors, with installation starting in January 2025 and outcome reviews at three and six months. The case study reports no outcome. On 21 January 2026 Dorset Council reported that, comparing records before and after installation in six homes, total falls fell by 49.2% and hospital transfers by 79.3%. The maker adds that one home recorded more falls. On 28 April 2026 BBC News interviewed a resident whose fall the sensors detected. The BBC reported that one home will pay to keep the system after the funded trial.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The proposed Ally installation would use acoustic and movement signals to detect changes relevant to nighttime care.

Workflow

The source describes planned installation of 184 sensors across six care homes, with follow-up reviews at three and six months. The system combines microphones and infrared sensing with an application for care staff.

Benefits

Reach

The pilot covered six care homes with 184 planned sensors. No source gives the number of residents monitored or the number of falls avoided. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

Installation was scheduled to begin in January 2025, and the council reported its before-and-after results on 21 January 2026. In April 2026 the BBC reported that one home will pay to keep the sensors after the funded trial ended. No source reports outcomes after that date. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Measurement

Measurement

Care home records of falls, ambulance callouts, hospital transfers, hospital days and safeguarding notices, compared before and after installation and normalized for different reporting periods. [4]

Setting and sample

Six care homes in the Dorset Council and BCP Council areas, 184 planned sensors. [4]

Measures

Total falls, unwitnessed bedroom falls, ambulance callouts, hospital transfers, hospital days, safeguarding notices. [4]

Results

Falls down 49.2%, unwitnessed bedroom falls down 58.2%, ambulance callouts down 63.7%, hospital transfers down 79.3%, hospital days down 72.6%, safeguarding notices down 51.9%. [4]

Limitations

The deployer ran the comparison and published no baseline counts, periods or resident numbers. There is no control group. One of six homes recorded more falls. The NHS report on the pilot had not been published. [4]

Denominators

Night-time acoustic and infrared monitoring with alerts to staff devices. [4]

People and places

Dependencies

Participating homes need reliable Wi-Fi, bedroom power outlets, and digital social-care records that can receive notes from the monitoring app.
