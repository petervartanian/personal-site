Falls drop after six Dorset care homes install acoustic sensors

Dorset Council reports that total falls fell by 49.2% and hospital transfers by 79.3% after six care homes installed Ally Labs acoustic and movement sensors.

Relation to AI

The sensors learn each resident's usual night pattern and alert staff to sounds, movement and changes in that pattern. The council compared counts in the same homes before and after installation, with no comparison group. Two thirds of the homes also cut scheduled night checks. The council and the maker both say that local leadership and practice shaped the results, so the share due to the AI alerts is unknown.

Citable summary

Dorset plans 184 acoustic and movement sensors to support care. The source reports intended sleep and falls benefits, before outcome evaluation. The February 2025 source describes scheduled installation and planned three- and six-month reviews. It supplies no observed reduction in falls, nighttime disturbance, ambulance use, or hospital stays. The 184 sensors are a rollout target rather than a count of improved resident outcomes. The planned system uses machine learning to establish resident behavior profiles and alert staff to sounds, movement, or changed patterns. The source precedes outcome evaluation, so AI’s contribution to better sleep, fewer falls, or improved care is not established.
