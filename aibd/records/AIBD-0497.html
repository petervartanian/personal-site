Students improve with the CherryPot math assistant, its developer says

ZEZEDU reports that students improved after one month of using its CherryPot math assistant and that internal benchmark scores rose about 17 percentage points, with no independent evaluation.

What happened

ZEZEDU reports academic improvement after one month of CherryPot use across Korean schools and describes plans to expand deployment. The ITU account, current as of June 2025, gives no effect size and no independent outcome evaluation. In 2026 ZEZEDU reported that CherryPot ran in more than 200 partner schools and that internal benchmark scores rose by about 17 percentage points on average. The company gives no method or comparison group for that figure. On its home page the company states that automated grading saves each teacher more than 10 hours a week, without giving a method. The company says that U.S. district pilots are underway and that it plans a quasi-experimental study with U.S. districts in the 2026-27 school year.

Details

AI system

Role

CherryPot provides process-level feedback and assessment during mathematics learning.

Inputs and outputs

Students submit photos, handwritten work on a tablet, or typed solutions. CherryPot analyzes the solution process and returns feedback, follow-up questions, and partial-score assessments.

Workflow

Conditions that could produce the benefit are unspecified.

Human oversight

The benefit depends on teachers who review and correct the AI grading. No source reports how often teachers change an automated grade. [1] [2] [3]

Benefits

Reach

ZEZEDU reports more than 200 partner schools and more than 4,000 student users in grades 6 to 12. These figures count users, and no source reports how many of them improved. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

The ITU account reports improvement after one month of use. In 2026 ZEZEDU reports that CherryPot runs in more than 200 partner schools, up from more than 190 in June 2025. The company reports that U.S. District pilots are underway for the 2026-27 school year. No source follows the scores of the same students over time. [1] [2] [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Images or records of students' mathematical working. Teacher review and correction of AI grading.

Evidence

Original evidence

All three sources come from ZEZEDU: its project account in the ITU report and its district page and home page on cherrypot.net. The company gives no method or comparison group for its figures. No independent evaluation is cited. [1] [2] [3]
