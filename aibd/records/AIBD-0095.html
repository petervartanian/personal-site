GiveDirectly pays over 4,600 people in Kogi on Flood Hub forecasts

GiveDirectly used Google Flood Hub forecasts to trigger $105 payments to over 4,600 people in Ibaji, Kogi State, most of them before floods peaked on 21 October 2024.

  • 2 a person

    GiveDirectly sent $105 to over 4,600 individuals, and a $210 transfer followed. The sources do not state how many people received the second transfer. In its own survey, GiveDirectly reports that weekly income more than doubled and that food shortages fell. No independent source verifies these survey figures.

  • 1 an organization

    GiveDirectly gained a tested trigger pipeline and documented lessons. It acknowledged to The New Humanitarian that it had used “household” and “individual” interchangeably. Its page note says that the page was updated in July 2026, before the investigation was published on 31 August 2026.

What happened

In 2024 GiveDirectly ran an anticipatory cash program in six wards of Ibaji, Kogi State, Nigeria. JBA Global Resilience pulled Google Flood Hub forecasts through an application programming interface, made inundation maps and set thresholds. GiveDirectly monitored the forecasts daily and cross-checked them with community observations. The trigger was met when forecasts showed that 20% of a community's area would flood, and verified residents were then paid. The New Humanitarian later described an automated payout command to verified residents. GiveDirectly reports that it sent $105 to over 4,600 individuals in 53 communities. It paid 3,250 people between 29 August and 6 October 2024 and 1,400 on 24 October, after floods peaked on 21 October. A $210 transfer followed. In its own survey, GiveDirectly reports weekly incomes rising from ₦4,500 to ₦10,000 and food shortages falling from 97% to 7%. On 14 October 2025 Rest of World reported that GiveDirectly planned a 2026 randomized trial of the approach in northern Bangladesh, covering over 100,000 families. The outlet gave 4,463 as the number of people paid early in Kogi, a lower figure than GiveDirectly's count of over 4,600. A note on GiveDirectly's page says that the page was updated in July 2026. On 31 August 2026 an investigation by The New Humanitarian found that data mismatches and barriers to phone access excluded some of the most vulnerable residents. GiveDirectly told the outlet that it had used “household” and “individual” interchangeably.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

JBA converted Google flood forecasts into inundation maps and triggers, which GiveDirectly checked against community observations before paying verified residents.

Workflow

Google Flood Hub forecasts via API. 20%-of-area trigger rule defined by GiveDirectly and JBA. USSD pre-enrollment, Smile ID verification and call-center checks. Cash transfers using recipients' self-reported bank details. GiveDirectly and the IRC received a $4.6 million Google.org grant in 2024 for AI-assisted anticipatory cash (The New Humanitarian). It is not stated to have funded the Kogi transfers.

Human oversight

GiveDirectly monitored the forecasts daily and cross-checked them with community observations. Individual eligibility rested on checks without AI: ward residence, Smile ID verification and calls from a call center. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Residents of 53 communities in Ibaji, Kogi State, Nigeria.

Reach

GiveDirectly reports more than 4,600 recipients in 53 communities, and Rest of World gave 4,463 as the number of people paid early. GiveDirectly acknowledged that it had used “household” and “individual” interchangeably. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Distribution and access

Identity mismatches and phone-access barriers excluded some residents, and multiple verified individuals in one household could receive separate payments.

Duration

3,250 people were paid from 29 August to 6 October 2024. Another 1,400 received payment on 24 October, after the 21 October flood peak.
