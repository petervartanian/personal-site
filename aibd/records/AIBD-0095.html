Google Flood Hub triggers pay $105 to 4,600 people in Kogi

weekly incomes rising from ₦4,500 to ₦10,000 and food shortages falling from 97% to 7%

Google Flood Hub forecast triggers paid $105 to 4,600 people in Ibaji, Kogi State, mostly before floods peaked on 21 Oct 2024

What happened

In 2024 GiveDirectly ran an anticipatory cash program in six wards of Ibaji, Kogi State, Nigeria. JBA Global Resilience pulled Google Flood Hub forecasts by API, generated inundation maps and set thresholds; GiveDirectly monitored forecasts daily and cross-checked them with community observations, and when 20% of a community's area was forecast to flood the trigger was met and verified residents were paid (The New Humanitarian describes an automated payout command to verified residents). GiveDirectly reports $105 to over 4,600 individuals in 53 communities: 3,250 paid between 29 August and 6 October 2024 and 1,400 on 24 October, after floods peaked on 21 October; a $210 transfer followed. Its own survey reports weekly incomes rising from ₦4,500 to ₦10,000 and food shortages falling from 97% to 7%. A New Humanitarian investigation (31 August 2026) found data mismatches and phone-access barriers excluded some of the area's most vulnerable residents; GiveDirectly acknowledged to the outlet that it had used 'household' and 'individual' interchangeably, and its page note says the page was updated in July 2026, before the investigation was published.

What it was built to do. Trigger and target anticipatory cash transfers to flood-prone communities before river flooding.

The system's part

Decisive for timing and geography: GiveDirectly told The New Humanitarian that satellite/AI flood modeling determined which communities were paid and when. Individual eligibility was decided by non-AI verification (ward residence, Smile ID checks, call-center calls), which the investigation identifies as the main source of exclusions; GiveDirectly's own page attributes the late payments to identity checks in two wards.

Replication

What produced it
  • Google Flood Hub forecasts via API
  • 20%-of-area trigger rule defined by GiveDirectly and JBA
  • USSD pre-enrollment, Smile ID verification and call-center checks
  • Cash transfers using recipients' self-reported bank details; GiveDirectly and the IRC received a $4.6 million Google.org grant in 2024 for AI-assisted anticipatory cash (The New Humanitarian), which is not stated to have funded the Kogi transfers
What must hold again
  • Forecast skill on the Niger and Benue floodplain
  • Phone access, charging and matching ID records among the poorest
  • Funds released and verification completed before the hazard
