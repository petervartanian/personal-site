Google Flood Hub triggers pay $105 to 4,600 people in Kogi

Google Flood Hub forecast triggers paid $105 to 4,600 people in Ibaji, Kogi State, mostly before floods peaked on 21 October 2024

ⓘ

AI system

System

Google Flood Hub AI river-flood forecasts accessed via API, with JBA Global Resilience hydrology analysis and a 20%-of-community-area trigger rule

Model

Not assessed

Not assessed

Workflow

GiveDirectly and JBA used Flood Hub forecasts to identify communities where at least 20% of the area was expected to flood. GiveDirectly verified residents’ identities and bank details before sending payments.

Human oversight

Not assessed

Relation to AI

Decisive for timing and geography: GiveDirectly told The New Humanitarian that satellite/AI flood modeling determined which communities were paid and when. Individual eligibility was decided by non-AI verification (ward residence, Smile ID checks, call-center calls). The investigation identifies that as the main source of exclusions. GiveDirectly's own page attributes the late payments to identity checks in two wards.

Beneficiaries

Residents of 53 communities in Ibaji, Kogi State, Nigeria.

Reach

GiveDirectly reports more than 4,600 recipients. It acknowledged that it had used “household” and “individual” interchangeably.

Distribution and access

Some residents were excluded because they lacked phone access or their details did not match government or bank records.

Limitations

The income and food figures are self-reported and not independently verified. About 1,400 recipients were paid on 24 October 2024, after floods peaked on 21 October. A later US$210 transfer has no stated recipient count.

Conditions for replication

Forecast skill on the Niger and Benue floodplain. Phone access, charging and matching ID records among the poorest. Funds released and verification completed before the hazard.

Reasons

It acknowledged that its recipient units were inconsistent. The investigation documents that some of the poorest residents were excluded.
