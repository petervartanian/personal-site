Google Flood Hub triggers pay $105 to 4,600 people in Kogi

Google Flood Hub forecast triggers paid $105 to 4,600 people in Ibaji, Kogi State, mostly before floods peaked on 21 October 2024

ⓘ

What happened

In 2024 GiveDirectly ran an anticipatory cash program in six wards of Ibaji, Kogi State, Nigeria. JBA Global Resilience pulled Google Flood Hub forecasts by application programming interface (API), made inundation maps and set thresholds. GiveDirectly monitored forecasts daily and cross-checked them with community observations. When 20% of a community's area was forecast to flood the trigger was met and verified residents were paid. The New Humanitarian describes an automated payout command to verified residents. GiveDirectly reports $105 to over 4,600 individuals in 53 communities. 3,250 were paid between 29 August and 6 October 2024 and 1,400 on 24 October, after floods peaked on 21 October. A $210 transfer followed. Its own survey reports weekly incomes rising from ₦4,500 to ₦10,000 and food shortages falling from 97% to 7%.

A New Humanitarian investigation (31 August 2026) found data mismatches and phone-access barriers excluded some of the most vulnerable residents. GiveDirectly told the outlet it had used “household” and “individual” interchangeably. Its page note says the page was updated in July 2026, before the investigation appeared.

Purpose. Trigger and target anticipatory cash transfers to flood-prone communities before river flooding.

AI’s contribution

Decisive for timing and geography: GiveDirectly told The New Humanitarian that satellite/AI flood modeling determined which communities were paid and when. Individual eligibility was decided by non-AI verification (ward residence, Smile ID checks, call-center calls). The investigation identifies that as the main source of exclusions. GiveDirectly's own page attributes the late payments to identity checks in two wards.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    $105 to over 4,600 individuals, followed by a $210 transfer (recipient count not stated). Implementer survey reports weekly income doubling and fewer food shortages (self-reported, not independently verified).

  • an organization

    GiveDirectly gained a tested trigger pipeline and documented lessons. It acknowledged to The New Humanitarian that it had used “household” and “individual” interchangeably. Its page note says the page was updated in July 2026, before the investigation was published on 31 August 2026.

  • society

    Material to a group of about 4,600 people in 53 communities. The investigation documents that some of the poorest were excluded, limiting wider benefit.

Further evidence

Setting. Google Flood Hub forecasts via API. 20%-of-area trigger rule defined by GiveDirectly and JBA. USSD pre-enrollment, Smile ID verification and call-center checks. Cash transfers using recipients' self-reported bank details. GiveDirectly and the IRC received a $4.6 million Google.org grant in 2024 for AI-assisted anticipatory cash (The New Humanitarian). It is not stated to have funded the Kogi transfers.

Conditions. Forecast skill on the Niger and Benue floodplain. Phone access, charging and matching ID records among the poorest. Funds released and verification completed before the hazard.
