A surgical robot helps a surgeon remove a brain clot from an awake man

The Modus V Synaptive robot’s software fused brain scans for planning and warned when the instrument strayed while a neurosurgeon removed a 4 cm clot from an awake stroke patient.

  • 3 a person

    The surgeons said that without urgent surgery he faced hemiplegia, speech loss, blurred vision or death. After the guided awake operation, computed tomography showed no remaining clot. His movement, vision and cognition were normal, and he went home on day six. Any life saved belongs to the operation as a whole. The robot's own contribution lay in preserving function.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The sources attribute three things to the robot's AI. It fused MRI, DTI, CT and DSA into one 3D image showing fiber tracts and clot. It allowed a rehearsal so the skull opening and path avoided the tracts. It gave green-amber-red warnings during surgery when the instrument strayed from the planned path. The surgeon placed a BrainPath port, a separate access device, along that path and drew out about 40 ml of clot. Clot removal, hemostasis and the awake function tests were done by the surgical and anesthesia teams. No source compares this with conventional surgery for this patient. None says what in the system is machine learning and what is image processing, so the counterfactual is unproven.
Limits
The operation combined several teams, so its outcome cannot be assigned to the software alone. No source measures this patient's outcome against conventional surgery, and no long-term neurological follow-up is reported. The hospital's account names the patient both Hải and Vinh, and it calls the gap between a 14:00 arrival and a 17:00 start two hours.

What happened

On 10 August 2023 a 58-year-old man, Mr. Hải, reached Tam Anh General Hospital at 14:00. More than 24 hours after a hemorrhagic stroke, he was drowsy and weak. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed a clot up to 4 cm pressing on motor and language areas. The surgeons said that without urgent surgery he faced hemiplegia, speech loss, blurred vision or death. Surgery began at 17:00 under local anesthesia. First the Modus V Synaptive robot's software fused MRI, diffusion tensor imaging, computed tomography (CT) and digital subtraction angiography scans into one 3D view of fiber tracts and clot. In that view neurosurgeon Chu Tấn Sĩ rehearsed the approach. During the operation the robot gave green, amber and red warnings when the instrument strayed from the planned path. The awake patient talked and moved on request, and he sang with the surgeon. The hospital's account states that the surgeon drew out the clot through a BrainPath port placed along the path that he had planned on the robot's software. About 40 ml of clot came out in just over 30 minutes. CT later showed none left. His movement, vision and cognition were normal, and he went home on day six. In a 2025 Cochrane review of 24 trials, Wilting and colleagues report low-certainty evidence that surgery to remove the clot may improve function and survival compared with medical care alone. They do not address robotic guidance.

Details

AI system

Configuration

The deployment used the Modus V system.

Inputs and outputs

The navigation system combined MRI, DTI, CT, and angiographic images into a 3D view of the clot and fiber tracts, then displayed warnings when instruments departed from the planned path.

Workflow

A large (4 cm) hemorrhagic-stroke clot compressing motor and language areas in a patient still able to cooperate with awake surgery. A hospital that owns the Modus V Synaptive robot with navigation software and a team trained to run it. Pre-operative fusion of MRI, DTI, CT and DSA and a simulated approach that avoids fiber tracts. Local anesthesia and intensive-care anesthesiology able to keep the patient awake and stable for function testing.

Human oversight

The surgeon planned the approach and removed the clot. The awake patient performed speech and movement checks while the surgical and anesthesia teams monitored function.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Surgery within hours of arrival. The surgeon notes about two million brain cells are lost per minute after a stroke. The surgeon follows the rehearsed trajectory and heeds the robot's warnings. Awake testing during clot removal so any tract injury is noticed immediately.
