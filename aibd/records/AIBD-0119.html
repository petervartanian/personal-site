Modus V Synaptive guides awake removal of a 4 cm brain clot

depth 3 for a person

About 40 ml of clot came out in just over 30 minutes

Awake surgery guided by the Modus V Synaptive robot removed a 58-year-old stroke patient's 4 cm clot at Tam Anh Hospital; full recovery.

What happened

On 10 August 2023 a 58-year-old man, Mr. Hải, reached Tam Anh General Hospital at 14:00, drowsy and weak over 24 hours after a hemorrhagic stroke; MRI showed a clot up to 4 cm pressing on motor and language areas. Surgery began at 17:00 under local anesthesia. First the Modus V Synaptive robot's AI fused his MRI, DTI, CT and DSA scans into one 3D view of fiber tracts and clot, where neurosurgeon Chu Tấn Sĩ rehearsed the approach; during surgery its traffic-light warnings policed the path while the awake patient talked and moved on request. About 40 ml of clot came out in just over 30 minutes; CT later showed none left and he went home on day six.

What it was built to do. Tam Anh launched the Modus V Synaptive robot in May 2023 for brain tumor, hemorrhagic stroke and other neurosurgery, to show fiber tracts on fused imaging, allow a 3D rehearsal and warn when the instrument leaves the planned path; this use was that purpose.

Who benefited

  • 3
    personal

    The surgeons said that without urgent surgery he faced hemiplegia, speech loss, blurred vision or death; after the guided awake operation CT showed no residual clot, his movement, vision and cognition were normal and he went home on day six. The life-saving element belongs to the operation as a whole; the robot's own contribution was preserving function.

The system's part

The sources attribute three things to the robot's AI: fusing MRI, DTI, CT and DSA into one 3D image showing fiber tracts and clot; a rehearsal of the approach so the skull opening and path avoided the tracts; and green-amber-red warnings during surgery when the instrument strayed from the planned path. Clot removal, haemostasis and the awake function tests were done by the surgical and anesthesia teams. No source compares this with conventional surgery for this patient, and none says what in the system is machine learning rather than image processing, so the counterfactual is unproven. One sentence in two reports calls the patient Mr. Vinh; all otherwise call him Mr. Hải.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A large (4 cm) hemorrhagic-stroke clot compressing motor and language areas in a patient still able to cooperate with awake surgery. A hospital that owns the Modus V Synaptive robot with navigation software and a team trained to run it. Pre-operative fusion of MRI, DTI, CT and DSA and a simulated approach that avoids fiber tracts. Local anesthesia and intensive-care anaesthesiology able to keep the patient awake and stable for function testing.

What must hold again. Surgery within hours of arrival; the surgeon notes about two million brain cells are lost per minute after a stroke. The surgeon follows the rehearsed trajectory and heeds the robot's warnings. Awake testing during clot removal so any tract injury is noticed immediately.
