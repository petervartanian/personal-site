EMBL and Max Planck groups used AI structure prediction plus cryo-ET to build a 70-MDa model covering over 90% of the human NPC scaffold.

What happened

A study published in Science on 10 June 2022 by groups at EMBL, the Max Planck Institute of Biophysics and collaborators used AI-based structure prediction to generate models of human nucleoporins and their subcomplexes, then fitted those models into cryo-electron tomographic maps of both the constricted and dilated states of the human nuclear pore complex. The authors benchmarked the predictions against previous and unpublished X-ray and cryo-EM structures and reported unprecedented accuracy. The resulting 70-MDa atomically resolved model covers more than 90% of the NPC scaffold, roughly doubling previous structural coverage, and resolves the anchoring sites of intrinsically disordered linker nucleoporins. The authors are at EMBL (Heidelberg and Hamburg), the Max Planck Institute of Biophysics, Goethe University Frankfurt, the Centre for Structural Systems Biology and the University of Michigan; the readable part of the source does not name the prediction tool or its developer.

What it was built to do. Predict protein and subcomplex structures to enable integrative modeling of a 120-MDa macromolecular machine that resisted conventional structural biology.

The system's part

AI structure prediction generated the nucleoporin and subcomplex models that formed the input to integrative modeling; the authors state that the predictions were benchmarked against existing and new experimental structures and that the resulting model was validated against earlier and new experimental data, so the AI output was independently checked rather than accepted as given.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Cryo-electron tomographic maps of the human nuclear pore complex in two states. AI structure predictions for nucleoporins and subcomplexes, benchmarked against experimental structures. Integrative modeling by groups holding prior structural data on the complex.

What must hold again. Cryo-electron tomographic maps of sufficient resolution for fitting. Nucleoporin sequences and homologues available for prediction. Independent experimental structures available for benchmarking.
