AI predictions help model over 90% of the human nuclear pore scaffold

Researchers combined AlphaFold and RoseTTAfold structure predictions with electron microscopy maps to model more than 90% of the human nuclear pore scaffold, about twice the previous coverage.

  • 2 an organization

    The collaborating laboratories obtained a model that roughly doubles the structural coverage of the human nuclear pore complex scaffold. That is a material advance for the research programs of those groups.

  • 2 society

    A 70-MDa atomically resolved model covering over 90% of the scaffold is a durable public resource for nuclear transport research. The source reports no direct patient or population benefit.

What happened

Researchers at the European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL), the Max Planck Institute of Biophysics and collaborating institutions used AlphaFold and RoseTTAfold to predict structures of nuclear-pore proteins. They used ColabFold to model complexes and fitted the predictions to electron-microscopy maps. Their model covered more than 90% of the scaffold, roughly twice the coverage of the previous model. The authors checked the predictions against existing and new experimental structures. The work appeared as a preprint on 27 October 2021 and in Science in June 2022. An independent Harvard group used the same mix of cryo-electron microscopy and AlphaFold to build an almost full model of the frog nuclear pore's cytoplasmic ring, published in Science on 10 June 2022. The human models were released in the Protein Data Bank in 2022: the constricted-state model as entry 7R5K on 22 June and the dilated-state model as entry 7R5J on 21 September. In a 2023 review, Gaoxingyu Huang, Chao Zeng and Yigong Shi of Westlake University credit machine-learning structure prediction for nuclear pore complex models of unprecedented accuracy, and they cite this study.

Details

AI system

Model

AlphaFold and RoseTTAfold. [1]

Role

The models predicted protein structures and complexes for comparison with microscopy data. [1]

Configuration

Researchers used AlphaFold and RoseTTAfold for protein structures, ColabFold for complexes, and Assembline to fit the models to microscopy data. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Protein sequences supplied the basis for predictions of individual structures and complexes. Researchers fitted those predictions to electron-microscopy maps. [1]

Workflow

Cryo-electron tomographic maps of the human nuclear pore complex in two states. AI structure predictions for nucleoporins and subcomplexes, benchmarked against experimental structures. Integrative modeling by groups holding prior structural data on the complex.

Human oversight

Researchers fitted predicted protein structures to electron-microscopy maps and checked them against existing and newly measured experimental structures.

Benefits

Reach

The benefit went to the collaborating laboratories, and the released models are a public resource for nuclear transport research. The record gives no count of the researchers who use them. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

The authors deposited the constricted-state model in the Protein Data Bank as entry 7R5K, released on 22 June 2022, and the dilated-state model as 7R5J, released on 21 September 2022. The Westlake University group cites the study in its 2023 review. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

People and places

Participants

DeepMind developed AlphaFold. Researchers at the University of Washington and Harvard developed RoseTTAFold. The EMBL and Max Planck groups used the software in this study. [1] [3] [4]

Dependencies

The work required protein sequences, usable cryo-electron tomography maps, and experimental reference structures as well as the prediction software.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Cryo-electron tomographic maps of sufficient resolution for fitting. Nucleoporin sequences and homologues available for prediction. Independent experimental structures available for benchmarking.
