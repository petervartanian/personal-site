Pindrop traces a fake Biden robocall in New Hampshire to ElevenLabs

Pindrop's deepfake audio analysis, run against more than 120 voice engines, traced a fake Biden robocall before New Hampshire's January 2024 primary to ElevenLabs; the FCC later cited it.

What happened

Voters in New Hampshire received an AI-generated robocall impersonating President Biden before the 23 January 2024 primary. On 25 January 2024 Pindrop, a company that builds synthetic-audio detection tools, published its analysis of a 39-second clip on its website. Pindrop checked the clip's patterns against more than 120 voice synthesis engines. Its chief executive, Vijay Balasubramaniyan, told WIRED that the result came back well north of 99 percent for ElevenLabs. Hany Farid's team at the University of California, Berkeley, was initially skeptical and ran its own model on the same sample. The team concluded with high confidence that the audio was AI-generated and likely made with ElevenLabs. ElevenLabs' own detector returned 84 percent, a result that WIRED reproduced. New Hampshire Public Radio reported on 23 May 2024 that the New Hampshire Attorney General had announced 26 charges against Steven Kramer. It also reported that the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) had proposed a $6 million fine. The sources do not connect the state charges to Pindrop's analysis. On 24 May 2024 the FCC stated in its proposed fine against Kramer that the robocall had been made with software from a website run by Eleven Labs. For that statement it cites Pindrop's analysis and an NBC News report. A New Hampshire jury acquitted Kramer of the state charges on 13 June 2025. Kramer told the Associated Press that he would not pay the FCC's fine.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

A 39-second robocall recording was compared with signatures of more than 120 synthesis engines to estimate whether and how it was generated.

Workflow

A clean audio sample of the deepfake. A library of fingerprints for many commercial voice-synthesis engines. A second, independent model run on the same sample.

Human oversight

Researchers compared several detector results, with a separate Berkeley team corroborating synthetic audio and a likely ElevenLabs origin.

Benefits

Duration

The FCC repeated the attribution to Eleven Labs software on 24 May 2024 and cited Pindrop's analysis for it. A New Hampshire jury acquitted Steven Kramer of the state charges on 13 June 2025, and he still faced the FCC's proposed $6 million fine. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The generating engine must be among those fingerprinted. Sample length and quality sufficient for attribution.

Evidence

Original evidence

WIRED reported the result of more than 99 percent from Pindrop's chief executive, and Pindrop published its own analysis. WIRED reproduced the 84 percent result from ElevenLabs' own detector. It also obtained a separate analysis from Hany Farid's Berkeley team. The FCC cites Pindrop's post and an NBC News report, in which the magician says that he used ElevenLabs, for its statement that the robocall was made with Eleven Labs software. [1] [2] [3] [4]
