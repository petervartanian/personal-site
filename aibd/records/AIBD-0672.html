A demand-routing app helps less skilled taxi drivers find fares

In Yokohama in December 2019, the AI Navi app cut taxi drivers' cruising time by 5% on average and 7% for low-skilled drivers, narrowing the skill gap by 13.4%.

What happened

A tech company that the authors do not name gave its AI Navi app free to about 500 taxi drivers, about 6% of drivers working for taxi firms in Yokohama, from 3 to 31 December 2019. The app suggested routes where demand was predicted to be high. Kyogo Kanazawa, Daiji Kawaguchi, Hitoshi Shigeoka and Yasutora Watanabe, economists at the University of Tokyo, compared each driver's cruising time with and without the app turned on, holding place and hour fixed. They report that the app shortened the search for customers by 5% on average and by 7% for low-skilled drivers, with a near-zero effect for high-skilled drivers. The 2022 working paper puts the narrowing of the productivity gap at 14%, and the version that Management Science published in February 2026 puts it at 13.4%. Drivers chose when to turn the app on, which the authors address with fixed effects and an instrument based on how unfamiliar the driver was with the area. More than half of the drivers offered the app never tried it. The study covers one city for one month. The company supplied the data, the authors report no financial support from it, and Japanese public research agencies funded the work.

Details

AI system

Workflow

The app predicted demand from recent driving records in the city. Drivers turned the app on while cruising for fares.

Measurement

Measurement

Cruising time until the next customer, from app and driving records. [2]

Setting and sample

About 500 Yokohama taxi drivers over 29 days. [2]

Measures

Log cruising time. [2]

Results

5% shorter on average, 7% for low-skilled drivers, near zero for high-skilled drivers. [2]

Additional value from AI

Productivity gap narrowed by 13.4% (published) or 14% (working paper). [2]

Limitations

Drivers chose when to use the app; one city; one month; developer not named. [2]

Denominators

App turned on while cruising. [2]

Materials for replication

Data from the company; not public. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The model must have recent local driving data. Gains depend on drivers who lack their own knowledge of demand.
