ChatGPT saves science teachers planning time each week

In an NFER trial in 68 schools, teachers using ChatGPT with a guide spent 56.2 minutes a week on Year 7 and 8 science lesson preparation against 81.5 minutes for teachers without it.

What happened

The National Foundation for Educational Research (NFER) ran a school-randomized trial in 2024, commissioned in December 2023 by the Education Endowment Foundation and the Hg Foundation. It involved 259 Year 7 and Year 8 science teachers from 68 state-funded secondary schools in England. Teachers in the ChatGPT group were asked to use ChatGPT, with an online guide, to prepare lessons and resources for ten weeks of the summer term, from April to July 2024; the other group was asked not to use any generative AI. Teachers used the free version of ChatGPT, which OpenAI updated during the trial. In weeks six to ten, a typical teacher in the ChatGPT group spent about 56.2 minutes a week on this preparation, against 81.5 minutes in the comparison group, a saving of 25.3 minutes or 31%, and the EEF gives this finding a high security rating. Teachers used ChatGPT mostly for one or two activities per lesson, such as quizzes or new activity ideas. An expert panel that did not know how the resources had been made reviewed resources from 30 teachers, 15 in each group, and found no evidence of a difference in quality. Teachers used ChatGPT less often as the trial went on, and the times came from teachers' own weekly diaries. NFER published the report on 12 December 2024.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Teachers received ChatGPT access and an online guide to using it for planning. The trial covered Year 7 and 8 science lessons for ten weeks.

Measurement

Measurement

Teacher-reported weekly preparation time; blind expert panel review of sampled resources. [2]

Setting and sample

259 Year 7 and 8 science teachers in 68 schools. [2]

Measures

Minutes per week on lesson and resource preparation. [2]

Results

56.2 against 81.5 minutes per week (69%). [2]

Additional value from AI

25.3 minutes saved per week. [2]

Limitations

Self-reported diary times; quality reviewed for 30 teachers; one subject and two year groups; free ChatGPT version changed during the trial. [2]

Denominators

Ten weeks of ChatGPT use with a guide. [2]

Materials for replication

Full report published by the EEF. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Teachers must learn to use the tool for specific activities such as quizzes. Schools must permit its use for planning.
