ProRail uses Genetec analytics on 7,000 cameras to flag trespass

ProRail replaces 288 lineside cameras with smart cameras in 2026 and runs Genetec video analytics on its 7,000 cameras to flag trespassers to dispatchers; no results are given.

  • Not rated society

What happened

ProRail, the Dutch rail infrastructure manager, said in a press release of 31 January 2026 that AI software in its smart cameras flags unwanted situations to staff in its Smart Monitoring Room. Those staff alert the rail control room when needed. ProRail also cites research that it says shows that suicides on the track fall 45 percent at locations with both physical measures and cameras. It does not name the study or separate the AI cameras. SpoorPro.nl reported on 2 February 2026 that ProRail is replacing 288 lineside cameras in 2026 with new “smart” cameras. ProRail is also testing drones that fly autonomously and analyze images. SpoorPro quotes ProRail as saying that these forms of camera surveillance help it see and prevent incidents sooner, making the track safer with fewer disruptions and delays. The article's photo caption says that the AI cameras' images are monitored 24/7. The rest of the text is behind a paywall. SpoorPro reported on 3 March 2026 that ProRail is bringing the 7,000 security cameras it manages into one Genetec platform. It is monitored from ProRail's Smart Monitoring Room. Genetec Mission Control filters and analyzes alerts and forwards relevant images to an operator with suggested actions. Video analysis is used among other things to flag spoorlopers (people on the track). In that case the train dispatcher can warn drivers. The article quotes ProRail project lead Kristian Ruiter and Genetec's Bob van Keulen. On 10 September 2026 Genetec, the platform vendor, published a customer story titled “From incident to response in under a minute at ProRail”. No source gives a detection count, a stopped train or a trespass reduction attributable to the analytics. No cited source identifies a trespass-detection trial of the International Union of Railways (UIC).

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Camera images produce alerts and relevant video for operators, including flags for people or vehicles entering monitored areas.

Workflow

Thousands of lineside and station security cameras brought into one Genetec video-management platform. Operators in ProRail's Smart Monitoring Room receiving filtered alerts with suggested actions. Video analytics configured to flag people on the track.

Human oversight

Operators assess the flagged video and escalate incidents to the rail control room, where staff can warn train drivers.

AI contribution

No incident is documented. ProRail says that AI software in the smart cameras flags unwanted situations to staff in its Smart Monitoring Room. SpoorPro reports that video analysis on the Genetec platform flags people on the track. No source reports whether the analytics changed any outcome. [1] [2] [3] [4]

People and places

Dependencies

The service depends on working cameras, video transmission to the Smart Monitoring Room, Genetec management software, and a staffed route to train dispatchers.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

A dispatcher able to warn drivers once a trespasser is flagged. Privacy masking and blurring applied to passenger images, as the article states ProRail does.

Evidence

Original evidence

SpoorPro's report of 2 February 2026 quotes ProRail on the smart cameras that ProRail's press release of 31 January 2026 announced. SpoorPro's report of 3 March 2026 quotes ProRail's project lead and Genetec. Genetec's customer story of 10 September 2026 comes from the vendor. [1] [2] [3] [4]
