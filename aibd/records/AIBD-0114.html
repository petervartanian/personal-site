Intelligent Anti-bacterial Platform flags resistance in an hour

depth 4 for a person

flagged ceftazidime resistance in 1 hour

AI read of a MALDI-TOF spectrum flagged ceftazidime resistance in 1 hour; 78-year-old septic woman switched to levofloxacin and recovered.

What happened

A 78-year-old woman with diabetes, hypertension and chronic kidney disease was admitted to China Medical University Hospital, Taichung, Taiwan, in mid-April 2022 with swollen legs; she developed rhabdomyolysis, acute kidney failure and coma, was intubated, and in late May developed pneumonia, treated with ceftazidime while sputum culture confirmed Stenotrophomonas maltophilia. The hospital's 智抗菌平台 (Intelligent Anti-bacterial Platform), a machine-learning model over routine MALDI-TOF mass spectra, predicted within one hour that the isolate was ceftazidime-resistant and suggested levofloxacin; the team switched, the lung infection improved within a week, she was extubated in early July (day not given; first of month used) and went home in late July 2022. The hospital presented the case on 26 October 2022.

What it was built to do. The platform's core, the 智能抗藥性細菌快速預測系統 (intelligent rapid prediction system for antibiotic-resistant bacteria), was built to predict resistance from routine MALDI-TOF spectra within an hour instead of the two to three days the hospital gives for conventional susceptibility testing, so that an effective antibiotic is given earlier; this case is that intended use.

Who benefited

  • 4
    personal

    The hospital describes a patient in mortal danger from sepsis and severe pneumonia caused by a multidrug-resistant organism it says carries 33% mortality, on an antibiotic the model predicted to be ineffective; after the switch she improved within a week, was extubated and discharged, and the hospital calls the rescue timely and life-saving. This rests on the treating hospital's own account at its press conference.

The system's part

The platform's model read the isolate's routine MALDI-TOF spectrum and predicted ceftazidime resistance within one hour, about two days ahead of the conventional susceptibility result the hospital says takes two to three days, and suggested levofloxacin; the clinicians made the switch and the antibiotic did the curing. The hospital puts the model's accuracy at 92% and argues that each hour of delayed effective therapy in sepsis raises mortality by 7.6%, but the case is reported only by the treating hospital, without independent review, and the counterfactual is earlier rather than different treatment.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • The hospital's own laboratory identifies isolates by MALDI-TOF, so a spectrum exists as soon as a culture grows
  • A machine-learning classifier trained on the hospital's spectra and susceptibility records for Stenotrophomonas maltophilia and ceftazidime, one of nine species the platform covers
  • The platform returns the resistance prediction and a suggested alternative to the treating team within an hour

What must hold again

  • The organism and antibiotic are ones the model was trained for
  • Clinicians act on the prediction before the conventional susceptibility result arrives
  • An effective alternative antibiotic is available and tolerated by the patient
