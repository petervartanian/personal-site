Brief from Peter

A 78-year-old woman at China Medical University Hospital in Taichung developed severe pneumonia during a prolonged admission. The hospital's Intelligent Anti-Microbial System predicted within an hour, from a bacterial mass spectrum, that her Stenotrophomonas maltophilia isolate resisted ceftazidime, and it suggested levofloxacin. Po-Ren Hsueh, a vice-superintendent of the hospital, said that conventional susceptibility testing takes two to three days.1 Clinicians changed the antibiotic in June 2022, and the hospital reported that she recovered and went home in late July 2022.2 The benefit rests on one patient, and every account of her comes from the hospital and its staff. In a paper published on 30 September 2026, researchers with no tie to the hospital rated the hospital's own before-after study of the platform at serious risk of bias because it had no concurrent control group.3 The hospital's count of more than 100,000 platform uses describes the wider service and gives no number of recoveries.4 The hospital's account supports an earlier change of antibiotic, and no source establishes that the prediction saved her life.

The system supplied a prediction and treatment suggestion. Clinicians chose and administered the antibiotic. [1]

The laboratory cultured the organism and obtained its mass spectrum. The system predicted resistance, and the treating team changed the antibiotic. [1]
