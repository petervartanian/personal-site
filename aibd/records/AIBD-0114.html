Intelligent Anti-bacterial Platform flags resistance in an hour

AI read of a MALDI-TOF spectrum flagged ceftazidime resistance in 1 hour; 78-year-old septic woman switched to levofloxacin and recovered.

ⓘ

What happened

A 78-year-old woman with diabetes, hypertension and chronic kidney disease was admitted to China Medical University Hospital, Taichung, Taiwan, in mid-April 2022 with swollen legs. She developed rhabdomyolysis, acute kidney failure and coma and was intubated. In late May she got pneumonia, treated with ceftazidime while sputum culture confirmed Stenotrophomonas maltophilia. The hospital's 智抗菌平台 (Intelligent Anti-bacterial Platform), a machine-learning model on routine MALDI-TOF mass spectra, predicted within one hour the isolate was ceftazidime-resistant and suggested levofloxacin. The team switched and the lung infection improved within a week. She was extubated in early July and went home in late July 2022. The hospital presented the case on 26 October 2022.

Purpose. The platform's core, the 智能抗藥性細菌快速預測系統 (intelligent rapid prediction system for antibiotic-resistant bacteria), was built to predict resistance from routine MALDI-TOF spectra within an hour. That replaces the two to three days the hospital gives for conventional susceptibility testing, so an effective antibiotic is given earlier. This case is that intended use.

AI’s contribution

The platform's model read the isolate's routine MALDI-TOF spectrum and predicted ceftazidime resistance within one hour. That was about two days ahead of the conventional result, which the hospital says takes two to three days. It suggested levofloxacin; the clinicians made the switch and the antibiotic did the curing. The hospital puts the model's accuracy at 92% and argues each hour of delayed effective therapy in sepsis raises mortality by 7.6%. The case is reported only by the treating hospital, without independent review, and the counterfactual is earlier rather than different treatment.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

This rests on the treating hospital's account at its press conference.

Further evidence

Setting. The hospital's own laboratory identifies isolates by MALDI-TOF, so a spectrum exists as soon as a culture grows. A machine-learning classifier trained on the hospital's spectra and susceptibility records for Stenotrophomonas maltophilia and ceftazidime, one of nine species the platform covers. The platform returns the resistance prediction and a suggested alternative to the treating team within an hour.

Conditions. The organism and antibiotic are ones the model was trained for. Clinicians act on the prediction before the conventional susceptibility result arrives. An effective alternative antibiotic is available and tolerated by the patient.
