An early resistance warning helped doctors change a woman’s antibiotic

An early resistance warning from CMUH’s Intelligent Anti-Microbial System helped doctors change a 78-year-old woman’s antibiotic before she recovered.

ⓘ

AI system

System

智抗菌平台, also called the Intelligent Anti-Microbial System or i.A.M.S. [1]

Model

The sources identify a machine-learning model for resistance prediction but do not establish its exact architecture. [1] [4]

Release

The hospital account does not report a model release. [1] [4]

Provider

China Medical University Hospital, Taichung, Taiwan. [1]

Role

The resistance-prediction component analyzed the bacterial spectrum. The broader platform supported antibiotic selection. [1]

Configuration

The exact instrument model, software settings, and prediction threshold are not reported in the accessible patient account. [1] [4]

Inputs and outputs

A routine MALDI-TOF mass spectrum informed a prediction of ceftazidime resistance and a suggestion to consider levofloxacin. [1]

Autonomy

The system supplied a prediction and treatment suggestion. Clinicians chose and administered the antibiotic. [1]

Workflow

The laboratory cultured the organism and obtained its mass spectrum. The system predicted resistance, and the treating team changed the antibiotic. [1]

Human oversight

Laboratory staff prepared and identified the isolate. Physicians interpreted the warning and made the treatment decision. [1]

Relation to AI

The platform analyzed a bacterial mass spectrum to predict resistance and suggest an alternative antibiotic. Clinicians selected and administered treatment. The hospital attributes earlier selection to the system, but the available account cannot isolate its effect on survival.

AI contribution

The reported contribution was an earlier resistance warning and antibiotic suggestion. The antibiotic and clinical care treated the infection. [1]
