An MIT neural network flags halicin, which treats infections in mice

A directed-message-passing neural network built at MIT identified halicin, which treated infections in mice; independent laboratories confirmed its activity, and no human trial is reported.

What happened

Jonathan Stokes, Regina Barzilay, James Collins and colleagues at MIT, the Broad Institute, Harvard and McMaster University published in Cell on 20 February 2020. They reported halicin, a molecule identified by a directed-message-passing neural network trained on 2,335 molecules to predict inhibition of Escherichia coli growth. Halicin was originally investigated as a kinase inhibitor for diabetes. It is structurally divergent from conventional antibiotics. It reduced Acinetobacter baumannii in a mouse wound model and cleared Clostridioides difficile in a mouse infection model. An independent laboratory at King Abdulaziz City for Science and Technology in Riyadh confirmed its antibacterial activity in vitro in December 2021. An independent laboratory at Central South University in Changsha confirmed its activity in vitro and in a mouse abscess model in September 2022. In May 2024 researchers at Qilu University of Technology and the Shandong Academy of Agricultural Sciences reported that oral halicin treated a Clostridium perfringens intestinal infection in mice. They found low acute toxicity, with weight loss and slight kidney inflammation in rats at a high dose, and they found that halicin is poorly absorbed. In a News-Medical review last updated on 28 August 2025, a science writer states that halicin had not yet been tested in human trials. No clinical use or human trial result appears in any cited source.

Details

AI system

Model

A directed-message-passing neural network.

Role

The model predicted antibacterial activity to select compounds for laboratory testing.

Inputs and outputs

A neural network trained on bacterial growth-inhibition data ranked compounds for potential activity, leading researchers to test halicin as an antibiotic candidate.

Workflow

A growth-inhibition training set of a few thousand molecules. A message-passing network ranking large chemical libraries. Wet-lab confirmation of predicted actives and animal-model testing.

Human oversight

Researchers tested predicted compounds in bacterial cultures and mice. Later laboratories separately tested halicin's antibacterial activity.
