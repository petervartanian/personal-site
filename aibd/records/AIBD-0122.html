CBP machine-learning flag at San Ysidro finds 75 kg of narcotics

depth 3 for society, depth 2 for an organization

review found 75 kg of narcotics and the driver was arrested

CBP ML model flagged a car's crossing history at San Ysidro in 2023; review found 75 kg of narcotics and the driver was arrested.

What happened

In 2023 a car driving north from Mexico approached the Point of Entry at San Ysidro; one of U.S. Customs and Border Protection's machine-learning models identified a suspicious pattern in its border-crossing history and flagged it for further review, which revealed 75 kilograms of narcotics hidden in the vehicle and led to the driver's arrest (DHS AI page, 27 November 2023). At the Axios AI+ Summit on 28 November 2023 Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas described a matching case: the Office of Field Operations model flagged a 'truck' in 1.4 seconds that held about 75 kilograms of narcotics. DHS's 2024 AI Roadmap repeats it: a secondary-review flag yielding over 75 kg of drugs. No source names the drug or the seizure date.

What it was built to do. CBP's model was built to identify potentially suspicious patterns in vehicle border-crossing histories and flag vehicles for secondary review at ports of entry (DHS AI Roadmap 2024); the San Ysidro seizure was that intended use, not a side effect.

The system's part

The model's contribution was the referral: DHS says it identified a suspicious pattern in the car's border-crossing history and flagged it for review, and Mayorkas said the anomaly was identified in 1.4 seconds; the discovery of the narcotics and the arrest were done by officers in secondary review. No source states whether the car would have been referred without the flag, so the model's counterfactual contribution is asserted by DHS rather than demonstrated. The Monitor entry also bundles Mayorkas's claim that AI age-progression imagery has recovered abducted children; that claim names no case, date or child and is not part of this record.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • A CBP machine-learning model scoring vehicle border-crossing histories was running on traffic at the San Ysidro Point of Entry
  • The model's flag reached officers in time to send the car to further (secondary) review before it left the port
  • Officers physically searched the car, found the 75 kilograms of narcotics and arrested the driver

What must hold again

  • The crossing-history pattern the model keys on must actually distinguish smuggling loads; no source gives hit rates or false-positive rates
  • Officers must have the discretion and capacity to act on a flag within normal crossing processing
  • The seizure is documented only by DHS and the Secretary; an independent CBP release or court filing giving the date, drug and quantity has not been found
