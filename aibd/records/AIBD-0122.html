Border model flags a car at San Ysidro; officers find 75 kg of narcotics

A U.S. Customs and Border Protection machine-learning model flagged a car's crossing history at San Ysidro in 2023, and officers found over 75 kilograms of narcotics and arrested two people.

    U.S. Customs and Border Protection obtained a seizure and two arrests from a flag that Mayorkas said took 1.4 seconds. The Department of Homeland Security leads both its artificial intelligence page and its 2024 roadmap with the case as its example of artificial intelligence in use.

What happened

In 2023 a car driving north from Mexico approached the Port of Entry at San Ysidro. A U.S. Customs and Border Protection machine-learning model identified a suspicious pattern in its border-crossing history and flagged it for review. An alert generated in under 1.4 seconds prompted an officer to refer the car to secondary inspection. Officers found over 75 kilograms of narcotics in the rear quarter panels and gas tank. They seized the drugs and vehicle and arrested the driver and passenger. Eric Hysen, chief information officer of the Department of Homeland Security, described the event in congressional testimony on 14 September 2023. His testimony says that the car crossed recently and gives no date. He said that without the model an officer would have had no cause to inspect the car more closely than at a normal stop. He also told Congress that referrals from these models had led to 240 seizures in 2023 by mid-September. The department's artificial intelligence page, last updated on 27 November 2023, describes the same seizure and the arrest of the driver. At the Axios AI+ Summit on 28 November 2023, Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas described a matching case. He said that the Office of Field Operations model flagged a “truck” in 1.4 seconds that held about 75 kilograms of narcotics. The department's March 2024 Artificial Intelligence Roadmap repeats the case as a flag for secondary review that yielded over 75 kilograms of drugs. No source names the drug or the seizure date.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The CBP model analyzed the vehicle's border-crossing history and flagged an unusual pattern for secondary review.

Workflow

A CBP machine-learning model scoring vehicle border-crossing histories was running on traffic at the San Ysidro Point of Entry. The model's flag reached officers in time to send the car to further (secondary) review before it left the port. Officers physically searched the car, found the 75 kilograms of narcotics and arrested the driver.

Human oversight

The model's alert prompted an officer to refer the car to secondary inspection. Officers searched the car, found the narcotics and arrested the driver and passenger. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

Reach

No beneficiary count is established. The sources report one seizure of over 75 kilograms of narcotics and two arrests. Eric Hysen told Congress that referrals from these models had led to 240 seizures in 2023 by mid-September. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The crossing-history pattern the model keys on must actually distinguish smuggling loads. No source gives hit rates or false-positive rates. Officers must have the discretion and capacity to act on a flag within normal crossing processing. The seizure is documented only by DHS and the Secretary. An independent CBP release or court filing giving the date, drug and quantity has not been found.

Entry details

Sector

Border security and law enforcement. Narcotics interdiction at a land port of entry.

Purpose

U.S. Customs and Border Protection built the model to identify potentially suspicious patterns in vehicle border-crossing histories, according to the 2024 Artificial Intelligence Roadmap of the Department of Homeland Security. The model flags vehicles for secondary review at ports of entry, and the San Ysidro seizure came from that intended use.
