Pano AI cameras spot the Bear Creek Fire; held to three acres

depth 2 for society, depth 2 for an organization

helicopters were dispatched within 30 minutes and the fire was held to three acres

Utility-funded Pano AI cameras detected smoke from the 16 June 2024 Bear Creek Fire in Douglas County, Colorado; helicopters were dispatched within 30 minutes and the fire was held to three acres.

What happened

On 16 June 2024 Pano AI wildfire-detection cameras installed on Xcel Energy and CORE Electric Cooperative towers detected smoke from a lightning-caused fire in Douglas County, Colorado. According to Renewable Energy World (18 July 2024), emergency services dispatched two helicopters within 30 minutes of the identification, when the fire was 0.25 acres; the helicopters dropped 17,765 gallons of water over six hours and the fire was limited to three acres. Denver7 (17 July 2024) reported that the fire was contained within a day and was first spotted by the AI-equipped cameras; CORE's wildfire mitigation manager Jordan Ambrogi said alerts typically arrive 10 to 12 minutes after smoke is detected. The utilities, however, said the cameras identified the fire "simultaneously" with the U.S. Forest Service, so the cameras' independent contribution to containment rests on the operators' own statements (CORE interim CEO Pam Feuerstein). The OECD AI Incidents Monitor carries a separate positively framed record of Xcel's November 2023 Pano AI deployment.

What it was built to do. Pano AI cameras rotate 360 degrees on mountaintop towers and use AI to detect smoke plumes, then alert utilities and fire agencies with a triangulated location; detecting this fire early was the intended use.

The system's part

The AI smoke detection produced the alert and location that the utilities and Denver7 describe; the sources are independent news reports quoting operator staff, not an official filing, and the operators themselves say the Forest Service identified the fire simultaneously, so the counterfactual contribution of the AI to the three-acre outcome is unverified.

Replication

What produced it. Camera stations with 360-degree views sited on cell towers within roughly 10 miles of the ignition. Alert routing to both the utility and local fire agencies with a triangulated location. Lightning-caused ignition in daylight in a remote area, with aerial resources available for dispatch.

What must hold again. Camera coverage of the ignition point and functioning AI smoke detection. An agency able to act on the alert within minutes. Independent confirmation of who detected the fire first, since the utilities report simultaneous Forest Service identification.
