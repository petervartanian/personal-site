Pano AI cameras detect the Bear Creek Fire, held to three acres

Utility-funded Pano AI cameras detected smoke from the 16 June 2024 Bear Creek Fire in Douglas County, Colorado; helicopters were dispatched within 30 minutes and the fire was held to three acres.

  • 2 society

    The fire was held to three acres after helicopters were dispatched within 30 minutes. CORE's CEO called the detection and location “crucial”. Simultaneous identification by the Forest Service limits what can be attributed to the cameras.

  • Not rated an organization

What happened

On 16 June 2024, at about 9:30 am, Pano AI wildfire-detection cameras on Xcel Energy and CORE Electric Cooperative towers detected smoke. It came from a lightning-caused fire in Douglas County, Colorado. The original joint utility statement says that the cameras and the U.S. Forest Service spotted the smoke simultaneously and that helicopters launched at 9:54 am. According to Renewable Energy World (18 July 2024), emergency services dispatched two helicopters within 30 minutes of the identification, when the fire was 0.25 acres. The joint statement specifies 5.5 hours of helicopter operations and 17,765 gallons of water. It reports containment at three acres by 7 pm. In the statement, Mike Alexander of the Douglas County Sheriff's Office says that the situation could have escalated significantly by noon without the early data. Denver7 (17 July 2024) reported that the fire was contained within a day and was first spotted by the AI-equipped cameras. In that report, CORE's wildfire mitigation manager Jordan Ambrogi said that alerts typically arrive 10 to 12 minutes after smoke is detected. Because the utilities report simultaneous detection by the Forest Service, the cameras' independent contribution to containment rests on the operators' own statements, among them those of CORE interim CEO Pam Feuerstein. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor carries a separate, positively framed record of Xcel's November 2023 Pano AI deployment in Colorado. CBS Colorado reported in May 2025 that Pano had 86 camera stations in Colorado, with 163 planned.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Pano cameras detected smoke and supplied a location that could be routed to utility staff and fire agencies.

Workflow

Camera stations with 360-degree views sited on cell towers within roughly 10 miles of the ignition. Alert routing to both the utility and local fire agencies with a triangulated location. Lightning-caused ignition in daylight in a remote area, with aerial resources available for dispatch.

Human oversight

Emergency services dispatched helicopters, and crews used aerial water drops to contain the fire.

Purpose

Pano AI cameras rotate 360 degrees on mountaintop towers and use AI to detect smoke plumes. They alert utilities and fire agencies with a triangulated location. Detecting this fire early was the intended use.
