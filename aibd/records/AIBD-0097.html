Facebook's ML settlement maps cut Red Cross mapping for Malawi's June 2017 measles vaccination campaign (Measles & Rubella Initiative) from 94,080 km² to 2,745 km²

What happened

For Malawi's June 2017 measles vaccination campaign (Measles & Rubella Initiative), targeting more than 7.5 million children, the American Red Cross's Missing Maps team needed base maps for campaign monitoring. Facebook's population maps, generated by a machine-learning computer-vision method that detects buildings in satellite imagery, were used to identify where people lived and which areas were already mapped in OpenStreetMap. Concentrating volunteers on settlements filtered out 97.1% of the land area, reducing the area to map from 94,080 km² to 2,745 km² and completing the base map in a fraction of the normal time. The base map was to feed a dashboard for government and Red Cross monitors during the campaign; the article predates the campaign and reports no monitoring outcome. No vaccination-coverage outcome attributable to the maps is reported. The date given is the Red Cross publication date.

What it was built to do. Produce high-resolution population and settlement maps from satellite imagery for planning and humanitarian use.

The system's part

The Facebook settlement data determined where volunteer mapping effort went and which areas were skipped; the campaign itself was a Measles & Rubella Initiative effort supported by the American Red Cross and other partners, with monitors from the national government, and the Red Cross attributes efficiency, not coverage, to the maps. The Red Cross account is the implementing organization's and names only 'Facebook's population datasets', without mentioning machine learning; the identification with the High Resolution Settlement Layer and the machine-learning description are taken from the vendor-authored Tiecke et al. preprint, which does not mention the Malawi campaign.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Facebook settlement detection from satellite imagery. Missing Maps volunteer tracing into OpenStreetMap. Comparison with existing OpenStreetMap coverage. Mobile data-collection dashboard for campaign monitors.

What must hold again. Imagery recent enough to reflect current settlements. Building-detection accuracy in rural Malawi. Volunteer and local mapping capacity.
