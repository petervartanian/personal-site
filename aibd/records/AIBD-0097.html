What happened

Malawi's June 2017 measles vaccination campaign (Measles & Rubella Initiative) targeted more than 7.5 million children. The American Red Cross's Missing Maps team needed base maps for campaign monitoring. Facebook's population maps come from a machine-learning computer-vision method that detects buildings in satellite imagery. They were used to identify where people lived and which areas were already mapped in OpenStreetMap. Concentrating volunteers on settlements filtered out 97.1% of the land area. That reduced the area to map from 94,080 km² to 2,745 km² and completed the base map in a fraction of the normal time. The base map was to feed a dashboard for government and Red Cross monitors during the campaign. The article predates the campaign and reports no monitoring outcome. No vaccination-coverage outcome attributable to the maps is reported.

The date given is the Red Cross publication date.

What it was built to do. Produce high-resolution population and settlement maps from satellite imagery for planning and humanitarian use.

Basis
The source measured it.Much of Malawi is rural, so concentrating on population settlements allowed us to filter out 97.1% of the total land area that we sought to map. This technique reduced our mapping area from 94,080km2 to 2,745 km2. Volunteers could then spend their time mapping communities instead of searching through acres of uninhabited areas; they were able to complete the mapping in a fraction of the normal time. source
Since then
Persisted on 10 August 2017: After the June 2017 campaign the Red Cross's Missing Maps team reported that the traced base map underpinned a real-time monitoring dashboard covering 13,743 children, and again credited Facebook's population data with filtering uninhabited areas out of the tracing tasks.We are also grateful for Facebook’s high-resolution population data for Malawi, which helped us to identify areas with and without concentrations of people. This made the tracing much, much faster – we filtered uninhabited areas out of the tracing tasks so that we could direct remote mappers towards areas that had features for them to map. source

The system's part

The Facebook settlement data determined where volunteer mapping effort went and which areas were skipped. The campaign itself was a Measles & Rubella Initiative effort supported by the American Red Cross and other partners, with monitors from the national government. The Red Cross attributes efficiency, not coverage, to the maps. The Red Cross account is the implementing organization's and names only “Facebook's population datasets”, without mentioning machine learning. The identification with the High Resolution Settlement Layer and the machine-learning description are taken from the vendor-authored Tiecke et al. preprint. It does not mention the Malawi campaign.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Facebook settlement detection from satellite imagery. Missing Maps volunteer tracing into OpenStreetMap. Comparison with existing OpenStreetMap coverage. Mobile data-collection dashboard for campaign monitors.

What must hold again. Imagery recent enough to reflect current settlements. Building-detection accuracy in rural Malawi. Volunteer and local mapping capacity.
