In a simulation, Fujitsu software helps controllers warn ships sooner

In a simulation of past near-miss cases at a Tokyo Bay traffic center, collision-risk predictions from Fujitsu Human Centric AI Zinrai helped controllers send warnings about two minutes earlier.

What happened

From December 2019 to March 2020, Fujitsu Limited and the Japan Coast Guard tested an artificial-intelligence system to predict vessel collisions at the Tokyo Wan Vessel Traffic Service Center. The center provides navigation support under an outsourcing contract with the Coast Guard. The system uses Fujitsu Human Centric AI Zinrai from Fujitsu Laboratories. The technology detects near misses between vessels and predicts areas where collision risk concentrates in Tokyo Bay. In the center's training environment, six experienced maritime traffic controllers and instructors simulated control tasks based on past near-miss accidents. They compared conventional operation with operation supported by risk information that the system computed from past Automatic Identification System (AIS) data and Coast Guard risk-recommendation records. The risk information shortened the time before a controller sent a risk warning to a vessel by an average of about two minutes. In the simulation, the number of warnings issued to risk-prone vessels nearly doubled. Fujitsu reports that the technology was particularly effective for newcomers. The 2020 results come from a simulation of past cases. Fujitsu announced the figures in a press release of 15 April 2020, and The Maritime Executive restated them the same day. The article cites the Japan Transport Safety Board's count of 2,863 marine collisions in Japan from 2009 to 2019. In the release the Japan Coast Guard said that it would verify more complex traffic conditions and improve the accuracy of the technology as it planned to implement the development. Fujitsu then ran a second field trial at the same center from 17 November 2020 to 2 September 2021 with an added course-following algorithm. Fujitsu reported about 90% fewer unnecessary alerts than with the earlier technology, and it planned a commercial navigation-support service from March 2022.

Details

AI system

Role

The system estimated collision risk from vessel-position and radar data.

Configuration

The deployment used Fujitsu Human Centric AI Zinrai.

Inputs and outputs

Archived vessel-position data and risk-recommendation records produced collision-risk information for controllers during replayed near-miss scenarios.

Autonomy

Controllers chose whether and when to warn vessels after reviewing the computed risk information.

Workflow

A VTS center with radar and AIS feeds and archived risk recommendations. Replayed near-miss scenarios in a training environment. Experienced controllers comparing conventional and AI-supported operation.

Human oversight

Controllers decided when to warn vessels, and the system supplied risk information that they used. The trial took place in the center's training environment on past near-miss cases. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Duration

The simulated trial ran from December 2019 to March 2020. Fujitsu ran a second field trial at the same center from 17 November 2020 to 2 September 2021, reported about 90% fewer unnecessary alerts and planned a commercial navigation-support service from March 2022. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Measurement

Measurement

Controllers handled simulated control tasks built from past near-miss cases, once with conventional methods and once with risk information computed from past AIS data and Coast Guard risk-recommendation records; Fujitsu reports a statistical analysis of the two conditions. [4]

Setting and sample

Six experienced controllers and instructors in the training environment of the Tokyo Wan Vessel Traffic Service Center, December 2019 to March 2020, with 36 analysis patterns across monitoring areas and controller experience. [4]

Measures

Time before a controller sent a risk warning to a vessel; number of warnings issued to risk-prone vessels. [4]

Results

Warnings about two minutes earlier on average; the number of warnings to risk-prone vessels nearly doubled. [4]

Limitations

Six participants, replayed cases and no live traffic. Fujitsu, the developer, reports no variance or significance figures. Fujitsu's 2021 release says that conventional collision-risk prediction, which assumes straight-line travel, raised many unnecessary alerts on curved routes, so some of the added warnings may have been unneeded. [4]

Denominators

Simulated sessions; no hours or vessel counts reported. [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Risk predictions are computed in real time on live traffic. Controllers trust and act on the predictions.
