What happened

The Maritime Executive reported on 15 Apr 2020 that Fujitsu Limited and the Japan Coast Guard had field-tested, from December 2019 to March 2020 at the Tokyo Wan Vessel Traffic Service Center, which provides navigation support under an outsourcing contract with the Coast Guard, an artificial-intelligence system for predicting vessel collisions. Using Fujitsu Human Centric AI Zinrai developed by Fujitsu Laboratories, the technology detects near misses between vessels and predicts areas where collision risk concentrates in Tokyo Bay. In the center's training environment, six experienced maritime traffic controllers and instructors simulated control tasks based on past near-miss accidents, comparing conventional operation with operation supported by risk information computed from past AIS data and Coast Guard risk-recommendation records. The technology shortened the time before a controller sent a risk warning to a vessel by an average of about two minutes, the number of warnings issued to risk-prone vessels nearly doubled, and it was found particularly effective for newcomers. The article cites the Japan Transport Safety Board's count of 2,863 marine collisions in Japan from 2009 to 2019. The results come from a simulation, not live traffic; the date given is the publication date.

What it was built to do. The technology was built to detect near misses and predict collision-risk areas to help vessel traffic controllers warn ships earlier.

The system's part

The AI produced risk information that controllers used to decide warnings; the two-minute gain and doubled warning count were measured against conventional operation in replayed near-miss scenarios with six controllers.

Replication

What produced it

  • A VTS center with radar and AIS feeds and archived risk recommendations
  • Replayed near-miss scenarios in a training environment
  • Experienced controllers comparing conventional and AI-supported operation

What must hold again

  • Risk predictions are computed in real time on live traffic
  • Controllers trust and act on the predictions
