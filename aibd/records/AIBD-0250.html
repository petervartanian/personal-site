Fujitsu Zinrai trial speeds vessel warnings by about two minutes

The Maritime Executive (15 Apr 2020): in a Dec 2019 to Mar 2020 trial, six experienced controllers re-ran past near-miss cases with and without AI risk predictions from AIS and radar data; warnings...

ⓘ

What happened

The Maritime Executive reported on 15 Apr 2020 that Fujitsu Limited and the Japan Coast Guard had field-tested an artificial-intelligence system to predict vessel collisions. The test ran from December 2019 to March 2020 at the Tokyo Wan Vessel Traffic Service Center. It provides navigation support under an outsourcing contract with the Coast Guard. It uses Fujitsu Human Centric AI Zinrai from Fujitsu Laboratories. The technology detects near misses between vessels and predicts areas where collision risk concentrates in Tokyo Bay. In the center's training environment, six experienced maritime traffic controllers and instructors simulated control tasks based on past near-miss accidents. They compared conventional operation with operation supported by risk information computed from past AIS data and Coast Guard risk-recommendation records.

It shortened the time before a controller sent a risk warning to a vessel by an average of about two minutes. The number of warnings issued to risk-prone vessels nearly doubled, and it was found particularly effective for newcomers. The article cites the Japan Transport Safety Board's count of 2,863 marine collisions in Japan from 2009 to 2019. The results come from a simulation, not live traffic.

Purpose. The technology was built to detect near misses and predict collision-risk areas to help vessel traffic controllers warn ships earlier.

AI’s contribution

The AI produced risk information that controllers used to decide warnings. The two-minute gain and doubled warning count were measured against conventional operation in replayed near-miss scenarios with six controllers.

Basis
Measured.It was found that the new technology could shorten the time before a risk warning is sent to a vessel by the operation controller by an average of approximately two minutes, enabling the early detection of vessels at risk of collision. It was also confirmed that the number of warnings issued for risk-prone vessels nearly doubled with the technology, indicating proactive preventive measures and the potential to enhance safety in Tokyo Bay. source
Since then
Fujitsu ran a second field trial at the same center from 17 November 2020 to 2 September 2021 with an added course-following algorithm, reported about 90% fewer unnecessary alerts than the earlier technology, and planned a commercial navigation-support service from March 2022.Results of the trial demonstrated that this technology can reduce unnecessary alerts by about 90% on all sea routes, especially on curved sections where alerts frequently occurred. source

Further evidence

Setting. A VTS center with radar and AIS feeds and archived risk recommendations. Replayed near-miss scenarios in a training environment. Experienced controllers comparing conventional and AI-supported operation.

Conditions. Risk predictions are computed in real time on live traffic. Controllers trust and act on the predictions.
