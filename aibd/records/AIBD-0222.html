Karolinska eye-tracking SVM flags reading risk at 95.6% accuracy

reached 95.6% (± 4.5%) accuracy with balanced sensitivity and specificity

Nilsson Benfatto et al. trained an SVM on under-one-minute eye-tracking records from 97 high-risk and 88 low-risk Swedish children; 95.6% accuracy; two authors own equity in Optolexia.

What happened

Mattias Nilsson Benfatto, Gustaf Öqvist Seimyr, Jan Ygge, Tony Pansell and Agneta Rydberg of Karolinska Institutet with Christer Jacobson of Linnaeus University published in PLoS ONE on 9 December 2016 a study showing that eye-movement recordings of less than one minute of reading can identify 9-10-year-old children at risk of persistent reading difficulties. Using cross-validation on 97 high-risk and 88 low-risk control subjects, a support-vector machine with recursive feature elimination selecting 48 of 168 features reached 95.6% (± 4.5%) accuracy with balanced sensitivity and specificity. The competing-interests statement says that Benfatto and Seimyr own equity in Optolexia, a company aiming to offer technologies for assessing reading deficits in school-age children, in a venture funded by Sweden's innovation agency VINNOVA; the work was also supported by the Ulla and Ingemar Dahlberg Foundation and the Promobilia Foundation, and data are on Figshare. The date observed is the publication date. This candidate was examined as the research basis of the Lexplore school screener, but that name does not appear in the fetched text and no deployment source was found.

What it was built to do. The method was developed to screen for dyslexia risk from eye movements; the classification accuracy is that purpose.

The system's part

The classifier is the whole result; the authors describe it as a first step toward making eye tracking a viable screening method, and nothing fetched shows it used in schools or affecting a child's support.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • Eye-tracking records of under one minute of reading
  • A labeled sample of 97 high-risk and 88 low-risk 9-10-year-olds
  • Feature selection (SVM-RFE) within repeated cross-validation

What must hold again

  • Eye-tracking hardware in the screening setting
  • Reading material and norms for the child's language
  • Validation on an independent sample, which the fetched source does not report
