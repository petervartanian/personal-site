AI cameras alert train crews to elephants on a Madukkarai rail stretch

The Tamil Nadu Forest Department's AI thermal cameras at Madukkarai sent over 7,116 alerts by 7 July 2026, with no elephant deaths there; a train killed two elephants nearby on 13 August 2026.

  • Not rated a person

    The reports count train stops and crossings, and no source counts injuries avoided by crews or passengers.

  • 2 an organization

    The Forest Department reports no elephant deaths as of July 2026 on a stretch that recorded eleven deaths from 2008 to 2022, including three in November 2021.

  • 3 society

    The department reports about 9,481 safe crossings and no track deaths for wild elephants as of July 2026 on a stretch that recorded eleven deaths from 2008 to 2022. The number of deaths that would otherwise have occurred remains unknown. A train killed two elephants near Puthuppathi on 13 August 2026.

The system’s part
AI classification of thermal-camera feeds generated the alerts that triggered staff intervention and train slow-downs. The figures (alerts, stops, crossings, zero deaths) are the department's own statements as reproduced verbatim in news reports. No official document is among the sources. No per-event counterfactual is established.
Limits
Safe crossings and driver responses support a reported operational benefit, but they do not equal a count of elephants saved. Human patrols and railway action also contributed. The retrieved report gives 3,281 slowdowns or stops, while another account gives 3,821; that discrepancy and the absence of a counterfactual remain unresolved.

What happened

Eleven elephants were killed on the railway tracks of the Madukkarai stretch between 2008 and 2022, the last three in November 2021. Down To Earth reported that the Madras High Court had directed action on elephant deaths on the tracks in 2021. The Tamil Nadu Forest Department installed 24 hybrid thermal cameras with AI detection on 12 towers. They cover 7.05 km of the Ettimadai–Walayar railway section in Madukkarai forest range, Coimbatore district. A trial run had already generated alerts by 7 January 2024. The system has operated since 9 February 2024. Alerts go to a control room at Puthupathi. Its staff warn station masters and loco pilots and drive elephants away from the tracks. Down To Earth reported on 9 March 2025 that the Tamil Nadu government allocated 7.24 crore rupees for the system. On 21 December 2025 The New Indian Express reported no elephant deaths on the railway tracks of the stretch in the previous 22 months. By 7 July 2026 the department's Additional Chief Secretary reported over 7,116 alerts and 3,281 train slow-downs or stops (DT Next reports 3,821). The same statement gave about 9,481 safe elephant crossings and no elephant deaths on the stretch. The department had also added AI-powered drones to extend surveillance beyond the fixed cameras. On 13 August 2026 a train killed a mother elephant and her calf near Chavadippara Puthuppathi, and The New Indian Express placed the collision on the Tamil Nadu side of the border. The Times of India reported that no warning reached the loco pilot. A legal notice reported by The Hindu on 31 August 2026 places this collision and a February 2026 death on the Walayar-Kanjikode corridor, where the Kerala detection system was still in testing.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Thermal-camera classifiers detected elephants near the monitored railway and sent alerts to the Puthupathi control room.

Workflow

Thermal cameras covering a known elephant crossing corridor. 24-hour staffed control room that relays alerts to station masters and loco pilots. Field staff able to move elephants away from the track. Railway agreement to slow or stop trains on alert.

Human oversight

Control-room staff warned station masters and locomotive drivers, while field staff moved elephants away and trains slowed or stopped.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Camera coverage of the full vulnerable section. Low false-alert rate so crews keep responding. Communication chain (intercom, walkie-talkie, WhatsApp) functioning at night. Elephant movement continuing to cross at monitored points.
