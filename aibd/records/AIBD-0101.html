AI thermal cameras stop trains for elephants at Madukkarai

What happened

The Tamil Nadu Forest Department installed 24 hybrid thermal cameras with AI detection on 12 towers. They cover 7.05 km of the Ettimadai–Walayar railway section in Madukkarai forest range, Coimbatore district. They have operated since 9 February 2024. A trial run already generated alerts by 7 January 2024. Alerts go to a control room at Puthupathi. Its staff warn station masters and loco pilots and drive elephants away from the tracks. By 7 July 2026 the department's Additional Chief Secretary reported over 7,116 alerts and 3,281 train slow-downs or stops (DT Next reports 3,821). It also gave about 9,481 safe elephant crossings and no elephant deaths on the stretch. Eleven elephants were killed there between 2008 and 2022.

What it was built to do. Detect elephants near railway tracks with thermal cameras and AI, alert forest staff and railway crews, and prevent train-elephant collisions.

The system's part

AI classification of thermal-camera feeds generated the alerts that triggered staff intervention and train slow-downs. The figures (alerts, stops, crossings, zero deaths) are the department's own statements as reproduced verbatim in news reports. No official document is among the sources. No per-event counterfactual is established.

Replication

What produced it. Thermal cameras covering a known elephant crossing corridor. 24-hour staffed control room that relays alerts to station masters and loco pilots. Field staff able to move elephants away from the track. Railway agreement to slow or stop trains on alert.

What must hold again. Camera coverage of the full vulnerable section. Low false-alert rate so crews keep responding. Communication chain (intercom, walkie-talkie, WhatsApp) functioning at night. Elephant movement continuing to cross at monitored points.
