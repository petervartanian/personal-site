AI thermal cameras stop trains for elephants at Madukkarai

TN Forest Dept AI cameras on a 7.05 km rail stretch: 7,116+ alerts, thousands of train stops, zero elephant deaths since Feb 2024.

ⓘ

What happened

The Tamil Nadu Forest Department installed 24 hybrid thermal cameras with AI detection on 12 towers. They cover 7.05 km of the Ettimadai–Walayar railway section in Madukkarai forest range, Coimbatore district. They have operated since 9 February 2024. A trial run already generated alerts by 7 January 2024. Alerts go to a control room at Puthupathi. Its staff warn station masters and loco pilots and drive elephants away from the tracks. By 7 July 2026 the department's Additional Chief Secretary reported over 7,116 alerts and 3,281 train slow-downs or stops (DT Next reports 3,821). It also gave about 9,481 safe elephant crossings and no elephant deaths on the stretch. Eleven elephants were killed there between 2008 and 2022.

Purpose. Detect elephants near railway tracks with thermal cameras and AI, alert forest staff and railway crews, and prevent train-elephant collisions.

AI’s contribution

AI classification of thermal-camera feeds generated the alerts that triggered staff intervention and train slow-downs. The figures (alerts, stops, crossings, zero deaths) are the department's own statements as reproduced verbatim in news reports. No official document is among the sources. No per-event counterfactual is established.

Since then
At two and a half years the department reported about 9,481 safe crossings and still no elephant deaths on the stretch, and had added AI-powered drones to extend surveillance beyond the fixed cameras.These are the unsung heroes whose constant vigilance, dedication, and hard work have facilitated around 9,481 safe elephant crossings, ensuring zero elephant deaths on railway tracks and demonstrating how technology, human vigilance, and teamwork can come together to protect wildlife," said Supriya Sahu. source

Further evidence

Setting. Thermal cameras covering a known elephant crossing corridor. 24-hour staffed control room that relays alerts to station masters and loco pilots. Field staff able to move elephants away from the track. Railway agreement to slow or stop trains on alert.

Conditions. Camera coverage of the full vulnerable section. Low false-alert rate so crews keep responding. Communication chain (intercom, walkie-talkie, WhatsApp) functioning at night. Elephant movement continuing to cross at monitored points.
