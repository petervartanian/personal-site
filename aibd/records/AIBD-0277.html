Tel Aviv and Ariel University researchers trained CNN-based models translating Akkadian from cuneiform glyphs or transliteration to English, best on short sentences; a capability demonstration only.

What happened

Gutherz, Gordin, Saenz, Levy and Berant (PNAS Nexus, 2 May 2023) trained neural machine translation models to render Akkadian into English directly from cuneiform Unicode glyphs (C2E) and from transliteration (T2E), reporting BLEU4 scores of 36.52 and 37.47, improvements of 9.43 and 13.96 over a translation-memory baseline, and best results on sentences of about 118 characters or fewer; they propose a human-in-the-loop system in which scholars correct outputs. The earlier verifier noted that no specific passage read and accepted by scholars is named and that the authors report hallucinations in longer or fragmented texts, so it treated the work as a capability demonstration. The group's 2020 PLoS ONE paper (Akkademia) reported up to 97% accuracy for automatic transliteration and segmentation. The date is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Automatically translate Akkadian cuneiform texts into English to assist scholars

The system's part

The models' translation quality is reported by their developers in a peer-reviewed paper; no scholar-accepted translation of a specific text is attributed to them, and the authors note limits on longer and fragmented texts.

Replication

What produced it. Convolutional neural network translation models trained on digitized Akkadian-English pairs.

What must hold again. Human scholars correct outputs, especially for long or fragmentary texts.
