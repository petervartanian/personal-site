Tel Aviv and Ariel models translate Akkadian at BLEU4 36.5–37.5

Convolutional translation models by Tel Aviv University and Ariel University researchers rendered Akkadian into English with BLEU4 scores of 36.52 and 37.47, best on short sentences.

What happened

In a 2020 paper in PLoS ONE (Akkademia), Gordin, Gutherz and colleagues reported up to 97% accuracy for automatic transliteration and segmentation. Gutherz, Gordin, Saenz, Levy and Berant (PNAS Nexus, 2 May 2023) trained neural machine translation models to render Akkadian into English. One works directly from cuneiform Unicode glyphs (C2E), one from transliteration (T2E). They report BLEU4 scores of 36.52 and 37.47, improvements of 9.43 and 13.96 over a translation-memory baseline. Results were best on sentences of about 118 characters or fewer. The authors propose that scholars correct the outputs. They report hallucinations in longer or fragmented texts. No passage read and accepted by scholars is named. The work stands as a capability demonstration. In June 2023 the Hebrew University Assyriologist Nathan Wasserman told The Times of Israel that the tool was still far from useful for deeper and less formulaic texts. The authors planned to add the model to the Babylonian Engine portal, and no source shows that this happened. In a preprint of 14 May 2026, Zhaohui Wang of the University of Southern California cited the 2023 system. Wang reported 49.1 BLEU for Akkadian-to-English with a bidirectional model on the same Akkademia validation split.
