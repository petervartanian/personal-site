Tel Aviv and Ariel models translate Akkadian at BLEU4 36.5–37.5

Tel Aviv and Ariel University researchers trained CNN-based models translating Akkadian from cuneiform glyphs or transliteration to English, best on short sentences; a capability demonstration only.

ⓘ

What happened

Gutherz, Gordin, Saenz, Levy and Berant (PNAS Nexus, 2 May 2023) trained neural machine translation models to render Akkadian into English. One works directly from cuneiform Unicode glyphs (C2E), one from transliteration (T2E). They report BLEU4 scores of 36.52 and 37.47, improvements of 9.43 and 13.96 over a translation-memory baseline. Results were best on sentences of about 118 characters or fewer. They propose a human-in-the-loop system in which scholars correct outputs. No passage read and accepted by scholars is named. The authors report hallucinations in longer or fragmented texts. The work stands as a capability demonstration. The group's 2020 PLoS ONE paper (Akkademia) reported up to 97% accuracy for automatic transliteration and segmentation.

Purpose. Automatically translate Akkadian cuneiform texts into English to assist scholars

AI’s contribution

The models' translation quality is reported by their developers in a peer-reviewed paper. No scholar-accepted translation of a specific text is attributed to them. The authors note limits on longer and fragmented texts.

Since then
The approach was built upon rather than contradicted: a May 2026 arXiv preprint by Zhaohui Wang (USC) cites the 2023 system and reports a bidirectional ByT5-based model reaching 49.1 BLEU for Akkadian-to-English on the same Akkademia validation split; no later report from the Tel Aviv and Ariel group itself was found.On the held-out Akkademia validation split (2,812 samples), we report 49.1 BLEU for Akkadian-to-English and 48.5 BLEU for English-to-Akkadian source

Further evidence

Setting. Convolutional neural network translation models trained on digitized Akkadian-English pairs.
