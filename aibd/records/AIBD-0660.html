A predicted model unlocks a stubborn crystal structure

What happened

Irène Barbarin-Bocahu and Marc Graille of the Laboratoire de Biologie Structurale de la Cellule (CNRS, École polytechnique) in Palaiseau, France, had a 2.45 Å X-ray data set for a protein involved in nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. They report that they struggled for two years to solve the structure, and that isomorphous replacement, anomalous diffraction and molecular replacement all failed. Models from AlphaFold and RoseTTAFold then let them solve it straightforwardly by molecular replacement, and they conclude that in their case the AlphaFold model largely outcompeted the other models. Acta Crystallographica Section D published the case report online on 16 March 2022. The authors describe one protein in one laboratory.
