AI-guided algae absorb more carbon in a Hong Kong laboratory

The team behind the Polar Eco-Carbon Intelligence Platform reports that AI-guided microalgae cultivation raised carbon dioxide absorption by 30%, against an unstated baseline.

  • 2 an organization

    The project team attributes to AI a 30% improvement in the carbon absorption of microalgae grown in the laboratory. The team does not state the baseline.

  • Not rated society

    The project intends to avoid net emissions and to improve the environment more widely, and the laboratory claim does not establish either effect.

What happened

Professor Ho Kin-chung initiated the Polar Eco-Carbon Intelligence Platform (PECIP). A profile of 15 August 2023 on InnoPort of the Chinese University of Hong Kong describes him as the founder of the Hong Kong polar research center. The profile also describes him as a scientist with more than 40 years of research who built a microalgae carbon-capture venture. The Cooperative Open Intelligent Computing Industry Alliance submitted the project jointly with the Polar Research Institute of Hong Kong and Intel. Intel supplied the AI platform and tools. PECIP combines fine-tuned large language models with retrieval and environmental models. The team applies them to polar analysis and to microalgae cultivation. The submission describes laboratory work and attributes a 30% improvement in carbon absorption to AI. It does not specify the experiment or its baseline, and it reports no deployment. The net climate effects remain unmeasured. Courses and partnerships on environmental, social and governance topics accompany the research. No published study of the 30% absorption gain had appeared by October 2026.
