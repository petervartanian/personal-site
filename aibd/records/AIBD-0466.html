AI transcribes ten radio stations in eastern Congo to flag hate speech

Fondation Hirondelle reports that AI software, which no source names, transcribes ten radio stations in eastern Congo in Swahili and French so that keyword searches can flag hate speech.

What happened

Fondation Hirondelle reports in its 2024 annual report that a pilot that uses AI to detect hate speech began in 2024 with the community radio stations in Goma and Bukavu. In its account for the International Telecommunication Union, current as of June 2025, the foundation reports that AI transcribes broadcasts from ten radio stations in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, in Swahili and French. Machine searches then use keywords and knowledge supplied by local journalists to flag disinformation and other problematic content. The foundation writes that Swahili speech recognition needs improvement and that monitoring topics need regular updates. On a 2025 project page for the Paris Peace Forum, the foundation states that it uses natural language processing to monitor community radio stations in the east of the country and to detect violent speech. Its 2026 project sheet says that the pilot was implemented in 2025 and sets a goal of monitoring 100 stations in 2026. None of these reports gives a measured result for transcription quality, staff time, audience benefit or content detected.
