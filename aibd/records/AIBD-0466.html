AI transcribes radio broadcasts for monitoring in eastern DRC

depth unassessed

Fondation Hirondelle reports that AI transcribes ten radio stations in Swahili and French for content monitoring in eastern DRC. Transcription quality and downstream impact remain unmeasured.

What happened

Fondation Hirondelle reports that AI transcribes broadcasts from ten radio stations in eastern DRC in Swahili and French. Machine searches then use keywords and knowledge supplied by local journalists to flag disinformation and other problematic content. The chapter identifies current use of AI transcription, despite its broader discussion of future collaboration and language access. It says that Swahili speech recognition needs improvement and that monitoring topics need regular updates. No transcription-quality comparison, staff-time result, or downstream audience benefit is quantified.

What it was built to do. Transcribe local radio broadcasts to support journalists’ monitoring of problematic content.

The system's part

AI converts Swahili and French broadcasts into text. Keyword searches informed by local journalists support monitoring; the chapter does not establish that those searches themselves use learned AI.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. AI transcription of Swahili and French broadcasts combined with journalist-informed content monitoring.

What must hold again. Access to radio audio in the monitored languages. Improved Swahili speech recognition and regularly updated journalist-supplied monitoring topics.
