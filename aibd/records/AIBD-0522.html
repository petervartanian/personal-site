What happened

vivo reports that it released vivo Sight, a visual assistance product, in 2023. It reports that it listed an upgraded version for vivo phone users in May 2024. In the CCL24-Eval shared task of July 2024, organizers from Beijing Union University and Xiamen University had deaf sign language users and certified translators rate the four sign language avatar systems that passed screening. They ranked vivo's system first with 3.513 of 5, against 2.119 for the baseline. The shared task measured the quality of avatar output on four test sentences. In its project account for the International Telecommunication Union, current as of June 2025, vivo describes on-device speech, vision and sign language features that work offline. vivo reports direct accessibility benefits for users. No source measures everyday use or the wider social effects.

Details

AI system

Configuration

Vivo runs speech, vision, and sign-language accessibility functions on consumer devices, including offline operation.

Workflow

Conditions that could produce the benefit are unspecified.

Benefits

Reach

The project account of vivo states its aim to "Serve over 500 million global users (300 million in China), empowering 80 million individuals with disabilities and 300 million elderly users". These figures are vivo's own. No source counts the people who benefited. [1] [2] [3]

Measurement

Measurement

A shared-task evaluation in which panels of sign language users rated each system's sign language avatar output on test sentences for grammar accuracy, naturalness, readability and cultural adaptability. [2]

Setting and sample

Ten teams submitted systems and four passed preliminary screening; 18 professional evaluators from the Chinese Association of the Deaf's Committee for Sign Language Research and Promotion and 9 general evaluators rated the output; the training set held 1,074 text and video sentence pairs. [2]

Measures

Weighted total score out of 5. [2]

Results

Vivo ranked first with 3.513, which is 1.394 points above the baseline; the expert group scored cultural adaptability lowest at 2.36. [2]

Limitations

The organizers report no confidence intervals. The test set held four sentences in an evaluation setting, and it does not measure whether deaf users communicate better in daily life. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Compatible smartphones and on-device models. Accessible input/output interfaces and supported languages.

Evidence

Original evidence

Two sources come from vivo: its project account for the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) and a company press release of 16 May 2024. The CCL24-Eval shared task report is independent. Its organizers from Beijing Union University, Xiamen University and Tsinghua University have no stake in vivo. [1] [2] [3]
