What happened

Darren Zhou, 25, a Goldman Sachs analyst from South Palm Beach, wrote to ChatGPT between March and May 2026. He wrote that he would rape and kill his 21-year-old ex-girlfriend and her family, claiming to own an AR-15. OpenAI's reviewers reported the conversations to the FBI. The FBI passed two months of his messages to the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office (PBSO) on 23 May 2026. Deputies matched the chats to anonymous threatening texts she had kept but not reported, and booked him on 29 May. On 13 August 2026 he pleaded guilty to written threats to kill, aggravated stalking and misuse of a two-way device. Adjudication was withheld, with eight years' probation, two on a Global Positioning System (GPS) monitor, a firearm ban and a no-contact order.

What it was built to do. OpenAI says that when it detects users planning to harm others, it routes the conversations to a small team trained on its usage policies. It says that if human reviewers find an imminent threat of serious physical harm to others, it may refer the case to law enforcement. This referral is that designed use. ChatGPT itself is a general-purpose assistant, which Zhou used to rehearse his plans.

The system's part

OpenAI's detection flagged the account, and its human reviewers judged the threat serious enough for an emergency disclosure to the FBI. The records contained only Zhou's messages, not the chatbot's replies, and no source reports what ChatGPT answered him. Everything after was human. The FBI alerted PBSO on 23 May 2026. Deputies did a welfare check, matched the chats to anonymous texts the victim had preserved, found a credible threat and booked him on 29 May. An assistant state attorney charged him in June, and he pleaded guilty in August. A deputy who reviewed the material said it showed rehearsal and planning rather than venting.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. About two months of ChatGPT messages (March to May 2026) stating dated plans to kill a named ex-girlfriend, with her routine and claimed weapons. OpenAI's detection flagged them, and its reviewers judged an imminent, credible threat. An OpenAI emergency disclosure to the FBI. It was passed to the Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office on 23 May 2026, with about two months of his messages. A PBSO investigation that corroborated the chats against anonymous threatening texts the victim had preserved, establishing probable cause for stalking and threat charges.

What must hold again. The user states the plan in the chat in enough detail for reviewers to judge it imminent and credible. OpenAI says it does not refer self-harm cases. The referral reaches a local agency able to act. Here six days passed between the FBI's alert to PBSO and the booking. Independent corroboration exists. The victim had preserved the anonymous texts but had not reported them, so without the referral police had no case. The counterfactual is not established. No attack had begun, he pleaded to stalking and threat offenses with adjudication withheld, and his lawyer says he owned no firearms.
