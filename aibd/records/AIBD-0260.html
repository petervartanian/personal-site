OpenAI refers Darren Zhou's threats to the FBI; he pleads guilty

An OpenAI disclosure brought Darren Zhou’s threats to police attention; he pleaded guilty to stalking and threat charges and received probation, GPS monitoring, and contact and weapons restrictions.

ⓘ

What happened

Court reporting describes an OpenAI emergency disclosure that reached the Palm Beach County Sheriff’s Office through the FBI after Darren Zhou wrote threats against his ex-girlfriend in ChatGPT. Deputies compared those messages with anonymous threats that she had preserved. The sheriff’s booking entry lists May 29, 2026 as his arrest date. BocaNewsNow reports that he pleaded guilty on August 13 to written threats, aggravated stalking, and unlawful use of a two-way communication device. Adjudication was withheld, with eight years of probation, two years of GPS monitoring, and restrictions on contact and weapons. The accessible reporting cites court documents but does not reproduce the complete affidavit or plea order. It supports a police intervention and court restrictions, rather than proof that an attack would otherwise have occurred.

Purpose. OpenAI says that when it detects users planning to harm others, it routes the conversations to a small team trained on its usage policies. It says that if human reviewers find an imminent threat of serious physical harm to others, it may refer the case to law enforcement. This referral is that designed use. ChatGPT itself is a general-purpose assistant, which Zhou used to rehearse his plans.

AI’s contribution

The reporting identifies an OpenAI disclosure as the route that brought Zhou’s messages to police attention. OpenAI’s general policy describes detection followed by human review before possible referral to law enforcement. That policy does not document the exact classifier, trigger, reviewer decisions, or ChatGPT replies in this case.

Further evidence

Setting. A reported OpenAI emergency disclosure of threatening messages to the FBI. Referral to local police and comparison with messages that the threatened person had preserved. Investigation and court restrictions following a guilty plea.

Conditions. A disclosure must reach an agency able to investigate. Investigators must establish identity and assess the threat using supporting evidence. A general detection-and-review policy does not establish the details of a particular referral. Arrest and court restrictions do not prove that an attack would otherwise have occurred.
