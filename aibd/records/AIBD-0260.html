What happened

Darren Zhou wrote threats against his ex-girlfriend in ChatGPT. Court reporting describes an OpenAI emergency disclosure that reached the Palm Beach County Sheriff’s Office through the FBI. According to the probable cause affidavit cited by the Sun Sentinel, the FBI told the sheriff's office about the messages six days before the arrest. Deputies compared those messages with anonymous threats that the ex-girlfriend had preserved. The woman told detectives that she had not reported Zhou to police because she did not want to cause his behavior to escalate. The sheriff’s booking entry lists 29 May 2026 as his arrest date. BocaNewsNow reports that he pleaded guilty on 13 August 2026 to written threats, aggravated stalking, and unlawful use of a two-way communication device. Adjudication was withheld, and he received eight years of probation, two years of GPS monitoring, and restrictions on contact and weapons. The reporting cites court documents, and it does not reproduce the complete affidavit or plea order. It supports a police intervention and court restrictions. No source shows that an attack would otherwise have occurred.

Details

AI system

Workflow

A reported OpenAI emergency disclosure of threatening messages to the FBI. Referral to local police and comparison with messages that the threatened person had preserved. Investigation and court restrictions following a guilty plea.

Human oversight

OpenAI’s general policy calls for human review of serious threats before a possible law-enforcement referral. In this case, police independently compared the disclosed messages with threats that the victim had preserved.

AI contribution

Reporting identifies OpenAI’s disclosure as the route through which police learned of the threats. OpenAI’s general policy describes automated detection followed by human review, but the sources do not identify the classifier or review process used here. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

A disclosure must reach an agency able to investigate. Investigators must establish identity and assess the threat using supporting evidence. A general detection-and-review policy does not establish the details of a particular referral. Arrest and court restrictions do not prove that an attack would otherwise have occurred. Purpose

OpenAI says that when it detects users planning to harm others, it routes the conversations to a small team trained on its usage policies. It says that if human reviewers find an imminent threat of serious physical harm to others, it may refer the case to law enforcement. This referral is that designed use. ChatGPT itself is a general-purpose assistant, which Zhou used to rehearse his plans.
