What happened

UC Santa Barbara's news office (Sonia Fernandez, 21 May 2026) announced the first node of a whale-detection network. The Benioff Ocean Science Laboratory (BOSL) installed it with the U.S. Coast Guard's Vessel Traffic Service and The Marine Mammal Center. The node sits on a USCG communication station on Angel Island, pointing across San Francisco Bay toward Treasure Island and the Bay Bridge. A second system is planned on San Francisco Bay Ferry's MV Lyra. FLIR thermal cameras coupled with AI detection developed by WhaleSpotter identify the heat signature of a whale blow at up to four nautical miles. A WhaleSpotter marine mammal specialist reviews each detection before an alert. The detection is then mapped on the Whale Safe website and shared with mariners and the Vessel Traffic Service, which can radio vessels.

Project scientist Rachel Rhodes said 21 gray whales died in the bay the previous year and seven more had already died in 2026. BOSL director Douglas McCauley said the system will save whales' lives. Mongabay's David Brown (4 Jun 2026) reported two cameras in operation, at Angel Island and on a ferry. He quoted Rhodes on human confirmation of detections and on mariners slowing or altering course near recent detections. On 20 Oct 2026 the Whale Safe site showed zero thermal-camera sightings for the day. No detection-to-action event or strike outcome is documented.

What it was built to do. The network was built to detect whales in the bay around the clock and alert vessels so they can slow or alter course.

The system's part

Not assessed. The AI flags thermal signatures, and a human specialist confirms each before alerts are posted. No counts of detections, confirmations or vessel responses have been published in the sources.

Replication

What produced it. A fixed vantage point over a whale-vessel overlap hotspot. Thermal cameras able to see blows at several nautical miles. Human review of each AI detection before alerting. A Vessel Traffic Service willing to relay alerts by radio.

What must hold again. Whales continue to enter the bay in the detection footprint. Mariners and ferry operators act on posted detections. Funding sustains camera nodes and specialist review.
