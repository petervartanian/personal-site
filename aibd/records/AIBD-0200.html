AI teams out-earn growers in Wageningen's greenhouse challenges

2018 cucumber challenge: winning algorithm raised sellable yield 6% and net profit 17% over reference growers; 2020 cherry-tomato challenge: all five AI compartments beat the growers' net profit.

ⓘ

What happened

Wageningen University & Research runs the Autonomous Greenhouse Challenge in six identical 96 m² high-tech compartments at Bleiswijk, with the organizing team publishing the results. In the first challenge in 2018, five international teams grew cucumber remotely for four months. They set climate, irrigation and crop setpoints with their own AI algorithms. One team outperformed the manually operated reference. The 2020 paper reports that the winning algorithm increased commercially sellable production by 6% and net profit by 17% over the reference growers. In the second challenge, cherry tomato cv. Axiany was transplanted on 16 December 2019. Five teams (Automatoes, AiCU, DIGILOG, IUA.CAAS, The Automators) took remote control on 20 December 2019. Harvest ran from 13 February to 29 May 2020.

Net profit ranged from 3.10 to 6.86 €/m² and all AI-operated compartments exceeded the Dutch growers' reference compartment. Compartment 306 (Automatoes) had the highest income (37.2 €/m²) and highest net profit, while the reference had high energy and labor costs. The challenge was funded by Tencent. A third edition grew lettuce in 2023. Net profit is computed from the organizers' price and cost model in an experimental facility, not a commercial farm.

Purpose. Control greenhouse climate, irrigation and crop management remotely with AI algorithms to maximize net profit with fewer resources.

AI’s contribution

Teams' algorithms set climate and crop setpoints remotely and their compartments out-earned a single manually run reference compartment in both editions. The organizers (WUR) authored the peer-reviewed reports and are independent of the competing teams. Results come from a sponsored competition, not a production deployment.

Further evidence

Setting. Six identical 96 m² compartments with heating, ventilation, screening, lighting, fogging, CO2, water and nutrient actuators. Teams combining horticultural and AI expertise setting setpoints via a digital interface. A reference compartment run manually by Dutch growers.

Conditions. The organizers' simulated price and cost model for net profit. Sensor data exchanged with teams throughout the season.
