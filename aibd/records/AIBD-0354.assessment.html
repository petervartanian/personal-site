Summary
OCHA frameworks (2021, 2023, 2025) use 510's XGBoost house-damage model as activation trigger; near-activation Dec 2021 (Rai); the Nov 2025 activation for Uwan ran on the 185 km/h wind trigger.
Account
OCHA's Anticipatory Action (AA) Framework for Philippine typhoons was posted on ReliefWeb on 15 September 2021. It set up a US$7.5 million CERF pilot for about 270,500 people in Regions 5 and 8 with FAO, IOM, UNFPA, UNICEF and WFP. It has a two-stage trigger. The readiness trigger is PAGASA and 510 forecasts of a category 4 or stronger cyclone. The activation trigger, within 72 hours of landfall, is the IFRC/Netherlands Red Cross 510 probabilistic typhoon impact prediction model's predicted number of totally damaged houses. The framework says that model partially uses a machine learning model relating wind speed to housing damage. 510's 2024 NHESS paper (vendor-authored) states the XGBoost model served in 2021 as the trigger model for the UN OCHA AA pilot. OCHA's After Action Review of 31 March 2022 records that Typhoon Rai (Odette) in December 2021 rapidly intensified and did not meet the activation criteria. There was a near activation with staff pre-deployed. The 2023 revision retained the 510 model. The framework endorsed on 19 October 2025 covers US$6 million CERF and about 955,507 people in Regions 5, 8 and 13. OCHA and its Centre for Humanitarian Data compute predicted totally damaged buildings with the 510 model every 6–12 hours. It adds an observational trigger for super typhoons of 185 km/h or more reported by PAGASA. OCHA's June 2025 report says the readiness trigger was met for Super Typhoon Pepito (Man-yi) in its November 2024 to April 2025 reporting period. Activation thresholds were not reached and CERF AA resources were reallocated to the response. On 6 November 2025 CERF AA was activated for Super Typhoon Fung-Wong (Uwan) after the observational wind-speed threshold was triggered. OCHA reports nearly US$6 million released and WFP alone reaching 42,100 families. No cited document records an activation produced by the 510 model's output.

Account
OCHA's Anticipatory Action (AA) Framework for Philippine typhoons was posted on ReliefWeb on 15 September 2021. It set up a US$7.5 million CERF pilot for about 270,500 people in Regions 5 and 8 with FAO, IOM, UNFPA, UNICEF and WFP. It has a two-stage trigger. The readiness trigger is PAGASA and 510 forecasts of a category 4 or stronger cyclone. The activation trigger, within 72 hours of landfall, is the IFRC/Netherlands Red Cross 510 probabilistic typhoon impact prediction model's predicted number of totally damaged houses. The framework says that model partially uses a machine learning model relating wind speed to housing damage. 510's 2024 NHESS paper (vendor-authored) states the XGBoost model served in 2021 as the trigger model for the UN OCHA AA pilot. OCHA's After Action Review of 31 March 2022 records that Typhoon Rai (Odette) in December 2021 rapidly intensified and did not meet the activation criteria. There was a near activation with staff pre-deployed. The 2023 revision retained the 510 model. The framework endorsed on 19 October 2025 covers US$6 million CERF and about 955,507 people in Regions 5, 8 and 13. OCHA and its Centre for Humanitarian Data compute predicted totally damaged buildings with the 510 model every 6–12 hours. It adds an observational trigger for super typhoons of 185 km/h or more reported by PAGASA. OCHA's June 2025 report says the readiness trigger was met for Super Typhoon Pepito (Man-yi) in its November 2024 to April 2025 reporting period. Activation thresholds were not reached and CERF AA resources were reallocated to the response. On 6 November 2025 CERF AA was activated for Super Typhoon Fung-Wong (Uwan) after the observational wind-speed threshold was triggered. OCHA reports nearly US$6 million released and WFP alone reaching 42,100 families. No cited document records an activation produced by the 510 model's output.
Replication
Produced by
  • A pre-arranged CERF allocation with agency action plans ready before the typhoon season
  • ECMWF ensemble track, wind and rainfall forecasts fed to the 510 impact model every 6–12 hours
  • Historical house-damage data linking hazard, vulnerability and exposure to train the model
  • A threshold matrix of predicted totally damaged houses per region with a 1-in-3.5-year return period (2025)
Must hold
  • Forecast lead time of at least 72 hours; rapid intensification (Rai 2021) shortened lead time below the trigger window
  • Model impact estimates reaching the regional threshold matrix, which no read activation record documents
  • Agency delivery capacity (financial service providers, data-sharing agreements) in the 72 hours before landfall
  • PAGASA and ECMWF data availability

Statement
OCHA's 2021, 2023 and 2025 frameworks name the IFRC/510 impact model as the activation-trigger model. The 2021 framework describes it as partially a machine learning model. This is documented by the deployer, not only the maker. The model's forecasts were monitored during the December 2021 near activation (Typhoon Rai) without meeting criteria. The 2024 readiness phase for Pepito ended with CERF AA resources reallocated to response. OCHA's flash update attributes the 6 November 2025 activation for Uwan to the observational threshold (forecast peak 195 km/h against the 185 km/h trigger). It does not state that the 510 model's estimates reached the activation matrix. The AI's contribution to any payout is therefore not established. The maker's NHESS paper is the only source describing the algorithm (XGBoost).
