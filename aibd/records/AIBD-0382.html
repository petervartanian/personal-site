What happened

Şırnak Provincial Police stopped a vehicle at the Köy Hizmetleri (Village Services) police checkpoint after ASENA selected it for inspection. DHA calls ASENA police software “in artificial intelligence format”. The narcotics dog Hector then found 86 kilograms 600 grams of skunk in 119 packages hidden in the trunk. Three occupants, M.E., H.K. and Ş.A., were detained (Sözcü/DHA and Habertürk/DHA, 17 March 2025). Diken, citing İHA, gives ASENA's full name as Analiz Sistemleri Narkotik Ağı (Narcotics Analysis Systems Network). It says the three were lawyers registered with the Mardin Bar Association. They were taken to court after police processing and remanded in custody (Diken, 18 March 2025). None of the reports gives the day of the stop, what data ASENA analyzed or how it chose the car, or any official's name. The first report date is used.

What it was built to do. Police analysis software built to pick out vehicles and people likely to be carrying narcotics for checkpoint stops. This seizure was that use.

The system's part

ASENA supplied the selection and nothing more. DHA's wording is that the vehicle was identified and stopped “thanks to the ASENA software”. The dog Hector then found the packages and officers made the arrests. No report describes ASENA's inputs, its ranking method, or whether an analyst reviewed the flag before the stop.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A police analysis program that ranks vehicles for interdiction. A fixed checkpoint at which a flagged car can be stopped and searched. A narcotics dog and officers prepared to search the trunk.

What must hold again. The flag must be followed by a lawful stop and a physical search. The software only selects. Seized material and arrests must be carried through prosecution.
