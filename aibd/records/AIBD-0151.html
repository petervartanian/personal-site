Relation to AI

The detector flagged seven sperm, two motile, in roughly 2.5 million frames of a 3.5 mL sample that manual examination had called sperm-free. The microfluidic recovery step delivered them intact. Without a usable sperm the couple's remaining options were donor sperm or adoption. Everything after that was conventional IVF done by people. Embryologists injected the two motile sperm into one fresh and one thawed egg, cultured two embryos and transferred them on day 3. Obstetric care followed. Limits on the attribution: the much-quoted comparison (44 sperm in an hour after two days of manual search) concerns a different sample. Whether a longer manual search of this sample would have found the two sperm was not tested. The letter's model version is not stated in any source on record (INCIID reports a YOLOv8 architecture, Columbia's patent filings describe YOLOv4).

Purpose

STAR (Sperm Tracking and Recovery) was built over about five years at the Columbia University Fertility Center. It finds, and recovers undamaged, the few sperm in semen samples of men with azoospermia or cryptozoospermia. They can then be used for IVF by intracytoplasmic sperm injection. This pregnancy was exactly that intended use and its first clinical result.
