Vyntra says its AI risk models stopped a USD 500,000 ATM fraud

fraudsters were arrested while attempting to withdraw USD 500,000 (KSh 58 million)

Vendor 'Fraud Cases' page lists blocked payments from CHF 19,990 to USD 850,000 and a USD 500,000 (KSh 58 million) ATM fraud ending in arrests; customers, banks and dates are not given.

What happened

Vyntra's 'Fraud Cases' page (published 4 April 2022 according to the page metadata returned by the reader proxy; the cases themselves are undated) states that 'Our solutions stop the fraud before the money leaves the bank' and lists about two dozen short case studies at unnamed banks: an account takeover after pharming stopped before a CHF 19,990 payment; an impersonation that led a bank employee to validate a CHF 1,000,000 transaction, blocked because the beneficiary was unusual at bank level; vishing, tech-support, CEO-fraud, business-email-compromise, romance and invoice-redirection cases with payments of EUR 22,114, EUR 33,170, USD 7,500, GBP 21,000, EUR 125,000, EUR 170,000, USD 100,000, USD 170,000 and USD 850,000 stopped because transaction or e-banking session variables (beneficiary, country, amount, currency, browser, screen resolution, language) did not match the customer's or the bank's usual profile; two internal-fraud cases; and one case in which 'fraudsters were arrested while attempting to withdraw USD 500,000 (KSh 58 million) from the second bank's ATMs' after the models flagged unusual timing and frequency of withdrawals. One entry still names 'NetGuardians solution', the Swiss company that merged with Belgium's Intix to form Vyntra in June 2025 (FinTech Global, 19 June 2025). No customer, bank, country, date, regulator or court record is given for any case, and the earlier verifier found none; the page is the vendor's own account.

What it was built to do. The risk models are built to score payments and e-banking sessions against customer and bank profiles in real time and block fraudulent transfers; each case is that intended use.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • Behavioural profiles of each customer's and the bank's usual transaction and e-banking session variables (beneficiary, country, amount, currency, browser, screen resolution, language)
  • Real-time scoring before a payment is released
What must hold again
  • The fraudulent transaction departs from the profile on several variables; the vendor notes cases with little customer history relied on bank-level anomalies
  • The bank acts on the alert before the money leaves
