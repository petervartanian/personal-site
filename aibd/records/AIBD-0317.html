What happened

Vyntra's “Fraud Cases” page was published 4 April 2022 per its page metadata; the cases themselves are undated. It states that “Our solutions stop the fraud before the money leaves the bank” and lists about two dozen short case studies at unnamed banks. One is an account takeover after pharming stopped before a CHF 19,990 payment. Another is an impersonation that led a bank employee to validate a CHF 1,000,000 transaction, blocked because the beneficiary was unusual at bank level. There are vishing, tech-support, CEO-fraud, business-email-compromise, romance and invoice-redirection cases. Their payments of EUR 22,114, EUR 33,170, USD 7,500, GBP 21,000, EUR 125,000, EUR 170,000, USD 100,000, USD 170,000 and USD 850,000 were stopped.

Transaction or e-banking session variables (beneficiary, country, amount, currency, browser, screen resolution, language) did not match the customer's or bank's usual profile. There are two internal-fraud cases. In one case “fraudsters were arrested while attempting to withdraw USD 500,000 (KSh 58 million) from the second bank's ATMs”. The models had flagged unusual timing and frequency of withdrawals. One entry still names “NetGuardians solution”, the Swiss company that merged with Belgium's Intix to form Vyntra in June 2025 (FinTech Global, 19 June 2025). No customer, bank, country, date, regulator or court record is given for any case, and none was found. The page is the vendor's own account.

What it was built to do. The risk models are built to score payments and e-banking sessions against customer and bank profiles in real time and block fraudulent transfers. Each case is that intended use.

The system's part

Not assessed. Only the maker's own account could be read. Vyntra's page attributes each block to its AI risk models detecting variables outside the customer's or bank's usual profile. No customer, bank, date or official record is available to assess whether the models, rather than staff or other controls, produced the outcome. The AI's contribution is unassessed.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Behavioral profiles of each customer's and the bank's usual transaction and e-banking session variables (beneficiary, country, amount, currency, browser, screen resolution, language). Real-time scoring before a payment is released.

What must hold again. The fraudulent transaction departs from the profile on several variables; the vendor notes cases with little customer history relied on bank-level anomalies. The bank acts on the alert before the money leaves.
