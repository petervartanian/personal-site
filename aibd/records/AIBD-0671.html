A GPT-4 tool helps lone P&G staff match human teams

In a field experiment with 776 P&G professionals, individuals using a GPT-4-based tool raised solution quality by 0.37 SD and spent 16.4% less time, matching teams without AI.

What happened

Between May and July 2024, researchers from Harvard Business School, Wharton and ESSEC, with Procter & Gamble staff, ran a preregistered field experiment with experienced P&G professionals working on real product innovation challenges in the company's new product development process. The working paper analyzes 776 of 811 participants, and the version that Organization Science published in 2026 reports 791. Professionals were randomly assigned to work alone or in two-person teams of one R&D and one commercial professional, with or without a generative AI tool built on GPT-4 and accessed through Microsoft Azure; July participants had GPT-4o, and those with AI received one hour of training. Expert evaluators who did not know the conditions scored the solutions. In the working paper, individuals with AI scored 0.37 standard deviations above individuals without it, teams with AI scored 0.39 above, and teams without AI scored 0.24 above. Individuals with AI spent 16.4% less time and teams with AI 12.7% less. Participants with AI also reported more positive emotions. The tasks were one-day virtual workshops, and the solutions were proposals that experts scored. P&G gave gifts to the Harvard institute that ran the study, and four authors worked for P&G.
