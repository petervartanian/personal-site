ActiveRemediation guides Flint digs to a 70% lead-pipe hit rate

A University of Michigan XGBoost and active-learning model guided Flint's FAST Start digs; 6,228 of 8,833 homes inspected through 2017 had lead pipes replaced (70%), versus 15% in 2018 without it.

ⓘ

ActiveRemediation: XGBoost classifier with hierarchical Bayesian spatial model and importance-weighted active learning for service-line selection

Workflow

Digitized historical parcel and service-line records plus water-sample data. Contractor hydrovac inspections and replacements feeding labels back to the model. City officials who followed the model's recommendations when selecting homes.

Relation to AI

AI contribution

The model's predictions guided which homes were excavated through 2017. The hit-rate collapse after AECOM discarded them supports its contribution. The comparison is observational and confounded by the City's simultaneous shift to digging across wards. Sources are the makers' peer-reviewed paper and an independent news report with named sources.

Depth

Reasons

People: Households had lead or galvanized lines replaced faster under model-guided targeting (6,228 replacements through 2017). Outcomes are documented in aggregate, no individual named. Organizations: FAST Start's hit rate was 70 percent through 2017, above 80 percent in late 2017 per McDaniel, against 15 percent in 2018 without the model. The KDD paper reports 91.6% holdout accuracy. Society: Flint's court-ordered lead-removal mandate was served faster while the model guided digs. The Atlantic reports thousands of homes kept lead pipes longer after it was dropped.
