ActiveRemediation guides Flint digs to a 70% lead-pipe hit rate

depth 2 for a person, depth 3 for an organization, depth 3 for society

6,228 of 8,833 homes inspected through 2017 had lead pipes replaced (70%)

A University of Michigan XGBoost and active-learning model guided Flint's FAST Start digs; 6,228 of 8,833 homes inspected through 2017 had lead pipes replaced (70%), versus 15% in 2018 without it.

What happened

From summer 2016 Jacob Abernethy, Eric Schwartz and colleagues at the University of Michigan worked with Flint's FAST Start service-line replacement program, building a statistical model (gradient-boosted trees in XGBoost with a hierarchical Bayesian spatial layer) and an active-learning procedure, ActiveRemediation, to choose which homes to inspect and replace (KDD 2018 paper, arXiv 1806.10692). The paper reports 91.6% accuracy on a 1,606-home holdout set and, by September 2017, 4,419 hazardous service lines replaced with the composition of 6,506 homes identified. The Atlantic (Alexis Madrigal, 3 January 2019) reported that through 2017 workers inspected 8,833 homes and replaced pipes at 6,228, a 70 percent rate, and quoted program head Michael McDaniel saying accuracy was above 80 percent in late 2017; after the City hired AECOM in 2018, which discarded the model's predictions, 10,531 properties were explored by mid-December 2018 and only 1,567 digs found lead, a 15 percent hit rate. In a court filing Schwartz estimated 4,964 to 6,119 homes with hazardous lines remained. The date used is the KDD paper's publication date; the model-guided phase ran through 2017.

What it was built to do. The model and active-learning rules were built to predict which Flint homes had lead or galvanized service lines and to direct inspections and replacements; the improved hit rate was the intended use.

The system's part

The model's predictions guided which homes were excavated through 2017, and the hit-rate collapse after AECOM discarded them supports its contribution; the comparison is observational and confounded by the City's simultaneous shift to digging across wards. Sources are the makers' peer-reviewed paper and an independent news report with named sources.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Digitised historical parcel and service-line records plus water-sample data. Contractor hydrovac inspections and replacements feeding labels back to the model. City officials who followed the model's recommendations when selecting homes.

What must hold again. Decision-makers continue to use the predictions (the 2018 hit rate fell when they were dropped). Inspection results are returned to the model to update it. Equity constraints that do not override targeting entirely.
