ActiveRemediation guides Flint digs to a 70% lead-pipe hit rate

A University of Michigan XGBoost and active-learning model guided Flint's FAST Start digs; 6,228 of 8,833 homes inspected through 2017 had lead pipes replaced (70%), versus 15% in 2018 without it.

ⓘ

What happened

From summer 2016 Jacob Abernethy, Eric Schwartz and colleagues at the University of Michigan worked with Flint's FAST Start service-line replacement program. They built a statistical model (gradient-boosted trees in XGBoost with a hierarchical Bayesian spatial layer) and an active-learning procedure, ActiveRemediation. They chose which homes to inspect and replace (KDD 2018 paper, arXiv 1806.10692). The paper reports 91.6% accuracy on a 1,606-home holdout set. By September 2017, 4,419 hazardous service lines were replaced, with the composition of 6,506 homes identified. The Atlantic (Alexis Madrigal, 3 January 2019) reported that through 2017 workers inspected 8,833 homes and replaced pipes at 6,228, a 70 percent rate. It quoted program head Michael McDaniel saying accuracy was above 80 percent in late 2017. The City hired AECOM in 2018, which discarded the model's predictions.

By mid-December 2018, 10,531 properties were explored and only 1,567 digs found lead, a 15 percent hit rate. In a court filing Schwartz estimated 4,964 to 6,119 homes with hazardous lines remained. The model-guided phase ran through 2017.

Purpose. The model and active-learning rules were built to predict which Flint homes had lead or galvanized service lines and to direct inspections and replacements. The improved hit rate was the intended use.

AI’s contribution

The model's predictions guided which homes were excavated through 2017. The hit-rate collapse after AECOM discarded them supports its contribution. The comparison is observational and confounded by the City's simultaneous shift to digging across wards. Sources are the makers' peer-reviewed paper and an independent news report with named sources.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Households had lead or galvanized lines replaced faster under model-guided targeting (6,228 replacements through 2017). Outcomes are documented in aggregate, no individual named.

  • an organization

    FAST Start's hit rate was 70 percent through 2017, above 80 percent in late 2017 per McDaniel, against 15 percent in 2018 without the model. The KDD paper reports 91.6% holdout accuracy.

  • society

    Flint's court-ordered lead-removal mandate was served faster while the model guided digs. The Atlantic reports thousands of homes kept lead pipes longer after it was dropped.

Further evidence

Setting. Digitized historical parcel and service-line records plus water-sample data. Contractor hydrovac inspections and replacements feeding labels back to the model. City officials who followed the model's recommendations when selecting homes.

Conditions. Decision-makers continue to use the predictions (the 2018 hit rate fell when they were dropped). Inspection results are returned to the model to update it. Equity constraints that do not override targeting entirely.
