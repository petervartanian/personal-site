Harvard's GPT-4 tutor more than doubles median learning gains

median learning gains over double those of the class session

Harvard PS2 RCT (fall 2023, 194 students): LLM-based tutor (GPT-4) produced over double the learning gains of active-learning class in less time.

What happened

In autumn 2023, in Harvard University's Physical Sciences 2 course, the course team ran a randomized controlled crossover experiment with 194 students across two topics, surface tension and fluid flow. Each student learned one topic in an in-class active-learning session and the other at home with a purpose-built AI tutor. The paper does not state outright which model the tutor ran on: its Methods say 'we avoided relying solely on GPT-4 to generate solutions for these activities', and its Discussion lists 'a large language model capable of closely following complex prompts (e.g., GPT-4)' among the factors behind the gains. The paper, published in Scientific Reports on 3 June 2025, reports that 'students learn significantly more in less time when using the AI tutor', with median learning gains over double those of the class session, a median of 49 minutes on task, and higher engagement and motivation. The paper places the study in 'the Fall 2023 semester' and 'the ninth and tenth weeks of the course' without a calendar date, so 1 September 2023 is used as a placeholder; the lessons themselves came later in the term.

What it was built to do. The tutor was built to teach these physics lessons with research-based pedagogy; the learning gain was that purpose.

The system's part

The LLM delivered the instruction; the comparison condition was active learning taught in class. The authors credit the research-based design (prompts, scaffolding) alongside the model.

Replication

What produced it

  • Lesson content pre-structured by instructors into the tutor
  • Prompts enforcing research-based pedagogy rather than answer-giving
  • Students with access to the tutor at home and a pre/post test design

What must hold again

  • The model follows complex pedagogical prompts
  • Topics are self-contained and short (two lessons tested)
