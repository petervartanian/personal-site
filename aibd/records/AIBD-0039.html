In a Harvard trial, an AI tutor more than doubles median learning gains

Harvard students who used PS2 Pal, powered by GPT-4-0613, learned more in less time than students in an active-learning class.

  • 2 a person

    Students achieved a measurable improvement in immediate post-test scores. The trial did not establish a lasting change in their capabilities.

  • 2 an organization

    The course demonstrated a teaching format with better post-test results. This rating rests on the same trial as the student rating, and no separate organizational study is cited.

What happened

In autumn 2023 the course team of Harvard University's Physical Sciences 2 ran a randomized controlled crossover experiment with 194 students. The paper places the study in “the Fall 2023 semester” and its lessons in “the ninth and tenth weeks of the course”, later in the term, without a calendar date. It covered two topics, surface tension and fluid flow. Each student learned one topic in an in-class active-learning session and the other at home with a purpose-built AI tutor. The supplement identifies the tutor as PS2 Pal, built in TeachGPT and powered by GPT-4-0613. Instructors supplied the solutions and teaching prompts. Harvard reported on 5 September 2024 that colleagues were trying the tutor framework and that Math 21a and the Derek Bok Center would pilot similar tutors that fall. The Hechinger Report noted on 16 September 2024 that the at-home sessions were proctored over Zoom and questioned whether students would use the tutor on their own. The paper was published in Scientific Reports on 3 June 2025. The authors report that “students learn significantly more in less time when using the AI tutor”. They report median learning gains over double those of the class session, with a median of 49 minutes on task, and higher engagement and motivation.

Details

AI system

Model

GPT-4. [1]

Release

GPT-4-0613. [1]

Provider

Harvard University developed the tutor. OpenAI supplied GPT-4. [1]

Role

GPT-4 supplied explanations and feedback during physics exercises. [1]

Configuration

TeachGPT presented each problem in stages. Instructors supplied teaching instructions and worked solutions in its prompts. [1]

Inputs and outputs

The tutor used students’ messages and course material to generate explanations and feedback. [1]

Autonomy

The tutor conversed directly with students within lessons that instructors had prepared and tested. [1]

Workflow

Students completed a quiz, studied with PS2 Pal at home or attended an active-learning class, and took another quiz. They switched formats for the second lesson. [1]

Human oversight

Instructors supplied the solutions and teaching prompts for the tutor. The Hechinger Report noted that the at-home sessions with the tutor were proctored over Zoom. [1] [2] [3]

AI contribution

PS2 Pal answered questions and gave feedback within lessons that instructors had prepared. The trial tested this entire teaching format, including videos and self-paced work. [1]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Students in Harvard’s Physical Sciences 2 course, and the course that provided the lessons. [1]

Reach

The course enrolled 233 students, of whom 194 were eligible for the study. The paper does not count individual beneficiaries. [1]

Distribution and access

Higher post-test scores occurred among students with lower and higher prior physics scores. The at-home lesson required internet access. [1]

Duration

Learning was tested immediately after each lesson. The study does not report later retention. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

Researchers used quizzes before and after each lesson, logged time with the tutor, and asked students about their experience on a five-point scale. [1]

Setting and sample

Harvard’s introductory physics course for life-science students. The paper reports 194 eligible students and compares 142 AI post-tests with 174 classroom post-tests. [1]

Editor’s note. Methods reports 194 eligible students. Results gives 142 AI post-tests and 174 classroom post-tests. Whether these counts describe the same analysis population is unclear.

Measures

Post-test scores, time spent learning, and students’ ratings of engagement, motivation, enjoyment, and growth mindset. [1]

Results

Median post-test scores were 4.5 with AI and 3.5 in class. The adjusted advantage was 0.63 standard deviations. [1]

Additional value from AI

The AI lesson produced higher scores than the classroom lesson. The comparison does not separate the model’s contribution from the videos, prepared solutions, or self-paced format. [1]

Limitations

The study covered two lessons at one university. High scores limited the test’s ability to distinguish further gains. [1]

Denominators

Each participant was assigned one AI lesson and one classroom lesson in consecutive weeks. The AI lessons took a median of 49 minutes. [1]

Materials for replication

The supplement contains teaching prompts and methods. The authors also published a Stata data file on GitHub. [1]

People and places

Dependencies

Students needed internet access to use TeachGPT with GPT-4-0613. Instructors supplied videos, questions, and worked solutions. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Instructors supplied carefully tested prompts, worked solutions, and videos. Students needed internet access and time to work at their own pace. The study covered two introductory topics. [1]

Replication attempts

The cited Nigerian program used Microsoft Copilot. It was a related study, not a replication of PS2 Pal.

Availability

Students used PS2 Pal through TeachGPT for the study. The supplement includes its teaching prompts. Public access to this particular tutor is not established. [1]

Evidence

Language

English. No translation was used. [1]

Original evidence

All three sources trace to one trial run by the tutor's developers. The Harvard Gazette is Harvard's own news office, and The Hechinger Report interviewed the developer and adds context without new outcome data. [1] [2] [3]

Evidence links

The results establish the test scores and time spent. The supplement identifies the model and teaching prompts. [1]
