Harvard's GPT-4 tutor more than doubles median learning gains

Harvard PS2 RCT (fall 2023, 194 students): LLM-based tutor (GPT-4) produced over double the learning gains of active-learning class in less time.

ⓘ

What happened

In autumn 2023 the course team of Harvard University's Physical Sciences 2 ran a randomized controlled crossover experiment with 194 students. It covered two topics, surface tension and fluid flow. Each student learned one topic in an in-class active-learning session and the other at home with a purpose-built AI tutor. The paper does not state outright which model the tutor ran on. Its Methods say “we avoided relying solely on GPT-4 to generate solutions for these activities”. Its Discussion lists “a large language model capable of closely following complex prompts (e.g., GPT-4)” among the factors behind the gains. The paper was published in Scientific Reports on 3 June 2025. It reports that “students learn significantly more in less time when using the AI tutor”.

Median learning gains were over double those of the class session, with a median of 49 minutes on task, and higher engagement and motivation. The paper places the study in “the Fall 2023 semester” and “the ninth and tenth weeks of the course” without a calendar date. The lessons themselves came later in the term.

Purpose. The tutor was built to teach these physics lessons with research-based pedagogy. The learning gain was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The language model delivered the instruction. The comparison was active learning taught in class. The authors credit the research-based design (prompts, scaffolding) beside the model.

Further evidence

Setting. Lesson content pre-structured by instructors into the tutor. Prompts enforcing research-based pedagogy rather than answer-giving. Students with access to the tutor at home and a pre/post test design.

Conditions. The model follows complex pedagogical prompts. Topics are self-contained and short (two lessons tested).
