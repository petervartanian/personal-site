DeepSeek-R1 flags familial Mediterranean fever, which gene tests confirm

A patient's dialogue with DeepSeek-R1-0528 pointed to familial Mediterranean fever, genetic testing confirmed it, and colchicine stopped attacks after ~30 years of symptoms.

  • 3 a person

    Inflammatory attacks that had recurred since early childhood ceased on colchicine after a genetically confirmed diagnosis. The benefit is life-changing, tempered by a documented transaminase rise from self-dosing before medical supervision.

The system’s part
The large language model (LLM) generated the FMF hypothesis and the suggested work-up over a multi-turn dialogue. Confirmation came from MEFV genotyping and clinical response to colchicine. The authors stress that the model repeatedly advised specialist supervision. The self-directed genetic testing and self-medication were the patient's own actions.
Limits
This single case cannot establish diagnostic accuracy or safety for unsupervised users. Genetic confirmation and clinician review were necessary, and self-medication was followed by raised liver enzymes. The authors of the 2025 study found that three other chatbots gave their weakest answers on the diagnosis of familial Mediterranean fever. That finding limits how far one correct chatbot diagnosis can be generalized.
Costs and harms
The useful diagnostic lead was followed by unsupervised treatment and elevated liver enzymes. Repeated advice to seek supervision did not prevent self-medication.
Later
Persisted, 29 September 2026. After a colchicine pause for raised liver enzymes and resumption at a lower dose, the patient remained attack-free under clinic follow-up as of publication.

Brief from Peter

A 34-year-old man of Egyptian origin had recurrent inflammatory attacks from early childhood, and despite almost three decades of symptoms, extensive investigations had not established a diagnosis. On 28 June 2025 he began a dialogue with the DeepSeek-R1-0528 model, which refined its differential diagnosis toward familial Mediterranean fever and repeatedly advised specialist supervision. He arranged genetic testing himself, and it showed two pathogenic MEFV variants. His attacks stopped on colchicine that he obtained from an Egyptian pharmacy and took on his own, but his liver enzymes rose and the drug was paused.1 It was resumed at a lower dose under close supervision. In February 2026 he presented to the University Medical Center Hamburg-Eppendorf, which confirmed the diagnosis, and he has remained free of attacks.2 The model's part was the hypothesis and the suggested work-up; the confirmation came from genetic testing and his response to colchicine. The evidence for the benefit comes from one patient. In a study published on 31 October 2025, two rheumatologists in Istanbul found that ChatGPT, Gemini and Grok answered patient questions on familial Mediterranean fever best on recovery and follow-up and less well on diagnosis.3

Details

AI system

Model

DeepSeek-R1-0528.

Role

The model suggested a possible diagnosis and further investigations during a conversation.

Inputs and outputs

DeepSeek used the patient's evolving symptom history, prior test results, and family information to refine a differential diagnosis toward FMF.

Workflow

A patient with a long-standing, characteristic but undiagnosed phenotype describing it in detail to a reasoning LLM over multiple turns. Access to genetic testing for the suspected condition.

Human oversight

The patient arranged testing and started medication himself. His primary-care physician later monitored liver enzymes, and specialists subsequently confirmed the diagnosis.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The patient gained an explanation for recurrent attacks and sustained symptom control after treatment was adjusted under supervision.

Reach

One patient, a 34-year-old man, benefited. The 2025 study of chatbot answers to patient questions has no beneficiaries to count. [1] [2]

Duration

The attacks ceased on colchicine after the dialogue that began on 28 June 2025. The authors of the case report of 29 September 2026 say that, after a pause for raised liver enzymes and resumption at a lower dose, the patient has remained free of attacks with regular clinical and laboratory follow-up. [1] [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Genetic or clinical confirmation by a specialist before the diagnosis is accepted. Treatment initiated under medical supervision (the case documents hepatotoxicity from self-dosing).
