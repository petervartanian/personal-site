GPT-4 speeds up legal drafting for law students

In a randomized trial with 60 Minnesota law students, GPT-4 cut time on four legal tasks by 11.8% to 32.1%, with slight and uneven gains in quality.

What happened

Jonathan Choi of the University of Southern California and Amy Monahan and Daniel Schwarcz of the University of Minnesota recruited students from the University of Minnesota Law School in April 2023. They split 60 students at random into two groups of thirty, and each group used GPT-4 on two of four tasks typical of junior lawyers and no AI on the other two: drafting a complaint, a contract, a section of an employee handbook and a client memo. Fifty-nine students finished. The students first took about two hours of online training in GPT-4 and then worked remotely from June to early August 2023, and graders scored the work blind. Access to GPT-4 cut time on every task: by 24.1% on the complaint, 32.1% on the contract, 21.1% on the handbook and 11.8% on the memo. Quality rose only slightly and inconsistently, with a significant gain on the contract task, and where the tool helped, the lowest-skilled participants gained most. On the tasks where GPT-4 helped most, participants reported more satisfaction from using it. The Minnesota Law Review published the study in November 2024. The participants were students, the sample was small, and the tasks were written for the study. A University of Minnesota Law School faculty fund paid for the work.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Students took about two hours of online training in using GPT-4 for legal work. Tasks mirrored the work of junior associates. Graders scored the work blind to the assignment.

Measurement

Measurement

Self-tracked time on task and blind grading on a 4.0 scale. [2]

Setting and sample

60 University of Minnesota law students, 240 task completions. [2]

Measures

Minutes per task and grade. [2]

Results

Time down 24.1% (complaint), 32.1% (contract), 21.1% (handbook), 11.8% (memo); grade gain significant only for the contract. [2]

Additional value from AI

Faster on all four tasks. [2]

Limitations

Students in place of practicing lawyers; small sample; designed tasks. [2]

Denominators

GPT-4 access after several hours of training. [2]

Materials for replication

Task materials reproduced in the article appendices. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The user must check the model's legal output for errors. The task must be one where the model helps; it helped least on the client memo.
