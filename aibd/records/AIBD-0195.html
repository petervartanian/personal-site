What happened

In a release that Space in Africa published on 6 January 2021, Selina Wamucii, a produce platform based in Nairobi, announced that Kuzi had been launched. TRT World reported on 13 January 2021 that Selina Wamucii had developed and funded Kuzi, which is named after the Swahili word for the locust-eating wattled starling. Kuzi generates a real-time heat map of locusts across Africa, shows potential migration routes and offers a breeding index. It uses satellite data, soil sensor data, ground meteorological observation and machine learning. The chief executive, John Oroko, said that farmers and pastoralists could sign up for free text-message (SMS) alerts in Kiswahili, Amharic and Somali, with up to three months' notice. He said that the app was available in Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia and Somalia and that funders were being sought for enhancements. Synced reported the same claims on 18 January 2021. SciDev.Net and the Africa Defense Forum repeated the chief executive's description in January and June 2021. None of these sources gives a forecast evaluation, official adoption or an alert that changed an outcome. The maker's Kuzi page still offered free sign-up for SMS alerts in October 2026, and no study or official body has reported on its forecasts.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Kuzi combined satellite, soil-sensor, and weather observations to produce locust heat maps, possible migration routes, breeding indices, and SMS warnings.

Workflow

Satellite, soil-sensor and ground meteorological data feeds. SMS delivery in Kiswahili, Amharic and Somali.
