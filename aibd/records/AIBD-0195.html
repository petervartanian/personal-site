Kuzi's SMS alerts may warn of locusts up to three months ahead

Selina Wamucii's Kuzi combines satellite, soil-sensor and weather data with machine learning to map locust risk for Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia and Somalia; maker statements only, no outcome evidence.

ⓘ

What happened

TRT World reported on 13 January 2021 on Kuzi, named after the Swahili word for the locust-eating wattled starling. Nairobi-based produce platform Selina Wamucii developed and funded it. Kuzi generates a real-time heat map of locusts across Africa, shows potential migration routes and offers a breeding index. It uses satellite data, soil sensor data, ground meteorological observation and machine learning. CEO John Oroko said farmers and pastoralists could sign up for free SMS alerts, in Kiswahili, Amharic and Somali, with up to three months' notice. He said the app was available in Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia and Somalia, and funders were being sought for enhancements. Synced reported the same claims on 18 January 2021. Neither source gives any forecast evaluation, official adoption or an alert that changed an outcome.

Purpose. Predict where desert locusts will breed and migrate and warn farmers and pastoralists by SMS months in advance.
