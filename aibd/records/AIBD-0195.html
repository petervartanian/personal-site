Kuzi offers SMS locust alerts up to three months ahead, CEO says

Selina Wamucii's Kuzi combines satellite, soil-sensor and weather data with machine learning to map locust risk for Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia and Somalia; maker statements only, no outcome evidence.

What happened

TRT World reported on 13 January 2021 that Kuzi, named after the Swahili word for the locust-eating wattled starling and developed and funded by the Nairobi-based produce platform Selina Wamucii, generates a real-time heat map of locusts across Africa, shows potential migration routes and offers a breeding index, using satellite data, soil sensor data, ground meteorological observation and machine learning. Its CEO John Oroko said farmers and pastoralists could sign up for free SMS alerts giving up to three months' notice, in Kiswahili, Amharic and Somali, with the app available in Kenya, Uganda, Ethiopia and Somalia, and that funders were being sought for enhancements. Synced reported the same claims on 18 January 2021. Neither source gives any forecast evaluation, official adoption or an alert that changed an outcome; the date is TRT's publication date.

What it was built to do. Predict where desert locusts will breed and migrate and warn farmers and pastoralists by SMS months in advance.
