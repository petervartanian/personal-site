Source information as of June 2025

Leigong links TCM knowledge with cultivation and production

The Leigong submission reports faster herbal sorting, lower Poria return costs, and improved knowledge access, alongside proposed health benefits.

ⓘ

What happened

Huawei and Yunnan Baiyao describe a traditional Chinese medicine model spanning cultivation, production, research, and guidance. The submission associates AI applications with 30–50-fold sorting-efficiency gains, CNY 50 million lower annual Poria return costs, and 30–50% higher patient satisfaction. It does not specify the measurement methods or isolate each result to one deployment. Health effects remain unmeasured.

Purpose. Improve knowledge use and operational decisions across the TCM supply chain.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. The account does not isolate AI’s contribution from the surrounding workflow.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    The submission reports improved sorting accuracy and 30–50-fold efficiency gains, plus lower Poria return costs; the system-specific attribution and baseline definitions remain unclear.

  • a person

    The submission claims a 30–50% improvement in patient satisfaction with the knowledge service; no survey design or health outcome is supplied.

  • unrated
    society

    Global acceptance of traditional medicine is an asserted aim, not a measured population outcome.

Further evidence

Setting. The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

Conditions. The account does not establish requirements for reproducing the benefit in other settings.
