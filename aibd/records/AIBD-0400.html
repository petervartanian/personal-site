Yunnan Baiyao reports faster herb sorting with its Leigong model

Huawei and Yunnan Baiyao present the Leigong model for traditional Chinese medicine and report faster herbal sorting and lower Poria return costs; health benefits remain proposed.

  • 2 an organization

    The companies report improved sorting accuracy, 30–50-fold efficiency gains and lower Poria return costs. The attribution to the system and the baseline definitions remain unclear.

  • Not rated a person

    The companies associate the applications with 30–50% higher patient satisfaction, but they tie the figure to no deployment, survey or method, so its extent cannot be established, and no health outcome is measured.

  • Not rated society

    The companies state global acceptance of traditional medicine as an aim. No source measures a population outcome.

What happened

Yunnan Baiyao reports that the Leigong model took second prize in the national final of the 2024 Data Elements x contest on 24 October 2024. In their submission to the International Telecommunication Union, current as of June 2025, Huawei and Yunnan Baiyao describe a traditional Chinese medicine model that spans cultivation, production, research, and guidance. The companies associate AI applications with 30–50-fold gains in sorting efficiency and with annual Poria return costs lower by CNY 50 million. They also associate the applications with 30–50% higher patient satisfaction. The submission does not specify the measurement methods. It does not tie each result to one deployment. Health effects remain unmeasured. No independent source reports the sorting, cost or satisfaction figures.

Details

AI system

Model

Leigong was adapted from Huawei’s Pangu large model using Yunnan Baiyao’s traditional Chinese medicine data and a retrieval knowledge base.

Application developer

Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd. And Yunnan Baiyao Group Co., Ltd.

Inputs and outputs

Text, prescriptions, herb images, and reference documents supported retrieved answers and applications for herb identification, sorting, cultivation, and knowledge services.

Autonomy

The knowledge service supplied guidance and cited sources, with an explicit limit that it did not replace professional diagnosis or treatment. Sorting and recognition were separate applications.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

Human oversight

Experts selected and annotated source material. The described output checks combined automated filtering with daily review of a 5% sample by practitioners.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The account does not establish requirements for reproducing the benefit in other settings.

Availability

The submission reports deployed herb-recognition, sorting, and knowledge applications. Access to the underlying knowledge database was restricted to partner customers.

Evidence

Original evidence

Both sources come from the companies: the submission that Huawei and Yunnan Baiyao made to the International Telecommunication Union, and a Yunnan Baiyao notice of 26 October 2024 about a contest prize. No independent source reports the sorting, cost or satisfaction figures. [1] [2]
