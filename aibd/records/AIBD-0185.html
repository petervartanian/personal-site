What happened

The New Indian Express (6 January 2026) reported on a 77-year-old resident of Binnypet, Bengaluru. In November 2025 a man calling himself Yatin Shah contacted him. He claimed to be an adviser with IIFL Wealth Management and promised assured allotment of shares before an initial public offering (pre-IPO shares). The victim first transferred Rs 5 lakh for a supposed ICICI Prudential AMC pre-IPO and was shown profits in a digital wallet. Over the following weeks he made multiple real-time gross settlement (RTGS) transfers totaling Rs 67.10 lakh. His requests to withdraw even part of the funds were denied, and the fraudsters demanded more payments to repay a loan before any withdrawal. He then searched for details about the company and its representatives using ChatGPT. It reportedly flagged complaints, inconsistencies in company credentials and indicators associated with investment fraud. He approached the police, who registered a case under the Information Technology Act. The police said that efforts were under way to trace the money. On 6 January 2026 the Deccan Herald also published a report by Prajwal D'Souza under a headline that says that the ChatGPT response saved him from losing more money to cybercriminals. The reports name no police official and do not name the victim. They report no recovery of the money, and no news report through 29 October 2026 describes an arrest or a recovery. ABC7 had reported a similar case on 6 December 2025: A widow in San Jose, California, recognized a pig-butchering scheme after ChatGPT told her that it was a scam and that she should go to the police; she had already lost nearly $1 million.
