ChatGPT flags fake adviser; victim reports Rs 67.10 lakh fraud

After withdrawals were refused, a 77-year-old Binnypet resident checked the firm's credentials with ChatGPT, which flagged complaints and inconsistencies; he reported the Rs 67.10 lakh fraud to Ben...

ⓘ

What happened

The New Indian Express (6 January 2026) reported on a 77-year-old resident of Binnypet, Bengaluru. In November 2025 a man calling himself Yatin Shah contacted him. He claimed to be an adviser with IIFL Wealth Management and promised assured allotment of pre-IPO shares. The victim first transferred Rs 5 lakh for a supposed ICICI Prudential AMC pre-IPO and was shown profits in a digital wallet. Over the following weeks he made multiple RTGS transfers totaling Rs 67.10 lakh. When requests to withdraw even part of the funds were denied, he searched for details about the company and its representatives using ChatGPT. It reportedly flagged complaints, inconsistencies in company credentials and indicators associated with investment fraud. He then approached the police, who registered a case under the Information Technology Act. The police said that efforts were under way to trace the money.

Deccan Herald (Prajwal D'Souza, 6 January 2026) framed the ChatGPT response as saving him from losing more money. No police official is named, no recovery is reported and the victim is unnamed.

Purpose. General-purpose conversational assistant that answers users' questions.

AI’s contribution

Both reports attribute the discovery to ChatGPT's response flagging complaints and credential inconsistencies when the victim queried the firm. That came after his own suspicion at refused withdrawals. The exact output is not reproduced. OpenAI had no role beyond providing the consumer product.
