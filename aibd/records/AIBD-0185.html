What happened

The New Indian Express (6 January 2026) reported that a 77-year-old resident of Binnypet, Bengaluru, was contacted in November 2025 by a man calling himself Yatin Shah, who claimed to be an adviser with IIFL Wealth Management and promised assured allotment of pre-IPO shares; the victim first transferred Rs 5 lakh for a supposed ICICI Prudential AMC pre-IPO, was shown profits in a digital wallet, and over the following weeks made multiple RTGS transfers totaling Rs 67.10 lakh. When requests to withdraw even part of the funds were denied, he searched for details about the company and its representatives using ChatGPT, which reportedly flagged complaints, inconsistencies in company credentials and indicators associated with investment fraud; he then approached the police, who registered a case under the Information Technology Act and said that efforts were under way to trace the money.

Deccan Herald (Prajwal D'Souza, 6 January 2026) framed the ChatGPT response as saving him from losing more money. No police official is named, no recovery is reported and the victim is unnamed; the date used is the publication date.

What it was built to do. General-purpose conversational assistant that answers users' questions.

The system's part

Both reports attribute the discovery to ChatGPT's response flagging complaints and credential inconsistencies when the victim queried the firm, after his own suspicion at refused withdrawals; the exact output is not reproduced, and OpenAI had no role beyond providing the consumer product.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A victim querying a large language model about a company and its representatives after withdrawals were refused. Public complaints and credential inconsistencies about the scheme that the model could surface.

What must hold again. Suspicion arising before further payments. Model output that points to specific red flags rather than generic caution. Police registration for any tracing or recovery.
