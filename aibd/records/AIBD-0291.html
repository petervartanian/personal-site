What happened

Drynan, Leroux and Zywiel reported a case in JBJS Case Connector (volume 11, issue 2, April-June 2021). They are from the Division of Orthopedic Surgery, Toronto Western Hospital, University of Toronto. It was published online 8 June 2021, used as the date because the event date is not available. In the abstract's words, a smart wristwatch prompted a patient's readmission to the emergency department for postoperative pulmonary embolism investigation and management. The abstract adds that constant patient monitoring and alerts are advantages of community-based smart technology. It says surrounding issues must be considered in implementation. Only the abstract could be read. The full text was not available and is not among the sources. The patient's age, sex, operation, what the watch measured or alerted, the device's brand, and the clinical outcome are therefore not known.

What it was built to do. Consumer activity monitors are built to track heart rate and activity and to alert the wearer. The abstract credits the watch with prompting the readmission but does not say which function did so.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A postoperative patient at home wearing a personal activity monitor. The device producing a reading or alert that the patient or family took as a reason to return to hospital. The abstract does not say which. An emergency department that investigated for pulmonary embolism.

What must hold again. The physiological change from the embolism is one the watch's sensors register. The wearer acts on the device rather than waiting for scheduled follow-up.
