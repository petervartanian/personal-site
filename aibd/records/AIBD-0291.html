Brief from Peter

In a case report published online on 8 June 2021, Drynan, Leroux and Zywiel of the orthopedic surgery division at Toronto Western Hospital credit a smart wristwatch with prompting a patient's readmission to the emergency department for investigation and management of a postoperative pulmonary embolism. They count constant patient monitoring and alerts among the advantages of community-based smart technology and add that surrounding issues must be considered in implementation. The abstract is the only available account.1 It does not give the patient's age or sex, the operation, the watch's brand, what the watch measured or the outcome. It does not establish that AI contributed to the patient's care.2

  1. 1 The full text is paywalled. Drynan, Leroux and Zywiel, “Acute Postoperative Pulmonary Embolism Detected at Home by a Patient's Personal Activity Monitor: A Case Report,” JBJS Case Connector, volume 11, issue 2, June 8, 2021. doi.org
  2. 2 The abstract does not say whether an automatic alert fired or whether the patient or family acted on a reading. Drynan, Leroux and Zywiel, JBJS Case Connector, June 8, 2021, abstract. doi.org
