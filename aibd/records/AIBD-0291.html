Smart wristwatch prompts ED readmission for pulmonary embolism

Toronto Western Hospital orthopedic surgeons report a smart wristwatch prompting ED readmission for postoperative pulmonary embolism investigation and management; details are limited to the abstract.

ⓘ

What happened

Drynan, Leroux and Zywiel reported a case in JBJS Case Connector (volume 11, issue 2, April-June 2021). They are from the Division of Orthopedic Surgery, Toronto Western Hospital, University of Toronto. It was published online 8 June 2021. The event date is not available. In the abstract's words, a smart wristwatch prompted a patient's readmission to the emergency department for postoperative pulmonary embolism investigation and management. The abstract adds that constant patient monitoring and alerts are advantages of community-based smart technology. It says that surrounding issues must be considered in implementation. The abstract is the only available account. The patient's age, sex, operation, what the watch measured or alerted, the device's brand, and the clinical outcome are therefore not known.

Purpose. Consumer activity monitors are built to track heart rate and activity and to alert the wearer. The abstract credits the watch with prompting the readmission but does not say which function did so.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. The abstract is the only available account. It credits a smart wristwatch with prompting the readmission and refers to monitoring and alerts. It does not say what the device measured, whether an automatic alert fired, or what brand or algorithm was involved. The device's contribution cannot be assessed. The accessible abstract does not establish that AI contributed to the patient’s care.

Further evidence

Setting. A postoperative patient at home wearing a personal activity monitor. The device producing a reading or alert that the patient or family took as a reason to return to hospital. The abstract does not say which. An emergency department that investigated for pulmonary embolism.

Conditions. The physiological change from the embolism is one the watch's sensors register. The wearer acts on the device rather than waiting for scheduled follow-up.
