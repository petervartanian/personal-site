Ant's AI health assistant reaches millions of users in China

Ant Group reports that its medical AI assistant, later the AQ app, reached over 13 million users and that its annotation system improved efficiency by 50%; no health effect is reported.

  • 1 an organization

    Ant reports that an annotation system that it developed with specialist doctors improved annotation efficiency by 50%. No clinical outcome is reported.

  • Not rated a person

    The submission reports wide uptake and a 98% satisfaction rate. It does not quantify a change in any user's health or experience of care.

  • Not rated society

    The submission asserts healthcare equity and rural access as aims. No source measures a change in how care is distributed.

What happened

In a submission to the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), Alipay describes medical assistants for residents, clinicians and underserved areas. The submission reports that, since its launch in June 2024, the service has reached over 13 million users and delivered more than 78 million service instances. It reports a 98% user satisfaction rate. It also reports a 50% improvement in annotation efficiency from an annotation system developed with specialist doctors, and it gives no baseline figure. The submission names improved health and a more equitable distribution of care as broader intended benefits. Ant released the assistant as a standalone app, AQ, on 26 June 2025. Ant renamed it Ant Afu on 15 December 2025, when Xinhua reported Ant's figure of more than 15 million monthly users. In January 2026 the Economic Observer reported more than 30 million monthly users without naming a source for the figure. No source reports a measured effect on users' health.

Details

AI system

Configuration

The medical model was pretrained on medical text and multimodal data, then fine-tuned on expert-annotated questions and conversations. Retrieval and a medical knowledge graph supported its responses.

Inputs and outputs

The assistants accepted medical questions, symptom descriptions, and documents through text, images, or voice. They generated guidance, interpreted reports, and helped users navigate care and insurance services.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

Human oversight

Medical specialists contributed training annotations and expertise. The described referral pathway left local doctors responsible for diagnosing patients who needed in-person care.

Benefits

Reach

Ant reports that the service reached over 13 million users and delivered more than 78 million service instances between its launch in June 2024 and June 2025; these figures count reach. Xinhua reported Ant's figure of more than 15 million monthly users in December 2025. No source counts people whose health improved. [1]

Duration

Ant released the service as a standalone app named AQ on 26 June 2025 and renamed it Ant Afu on 15 December 2025. On that date, Xinhua reported Ant's figure of more than 15 million monthly users. In January 2026, the Economic Observer reported more than 30 million monthly users without naming a source. No source reports a lasting effect on users' health. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The account does not establish requirements for reproducing the benefit in other settings.

Availability

Alipay reports that the service launched in June 2024 and integrated guidance from more than 1,000 healthcare facilities. The described support was strongest in Chinese and English.

Evidence

Original evidence

Ant's submission to the International Telecommunication Union is the only source for its user counts and its annotation figure. Later user figures come from news reports, and the Economic Observer named no source for its figure of more than 30 million monthly users. No source audits these figures independently. [1]
