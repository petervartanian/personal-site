What happened

The Austin City Council approved Austin Energy's Pano AI wildfire detection program on 5 October 2023. Austin Energy budgeted $500,000 a year for a five-year contract. Austin Energy, the City of Austin's electric utility, announced the full deployment of the Pano AI system on 29 August 2024. The system combines 13 ultra-high-definition 360-degree cameras, artificial intelligence and real-time monitoring across the utility's 437-square-mile service territory. The territory covers most of Travis County and parts of Hays, Bastrop, Williamson and Burnet counties. Once a fire is identified, the system alerts Austin Energy and local fire departments with location data and imagery. The utility says that the system supports decisions such as power shut-offs. The release quotes wildfire program manager Chris Vetromile and Austin Fire Department wildfire mitigation officer Justice Jones. It also quotes Arvind Satyam of Pano AI. In its Electric System Resiliency Plan of November 2025, the utility lists the Pano AI cameras among the wildfire tools that it uses. None of the three documents reports a fire that the cameras detected or contained. All three come from Austin Energy, a public body, and the 2024 release carries the vendor's statement. No independent report is among the sources.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Camera siting agreed with the Austin Fire Department and county emergency service districts. Alerts delivered to both the utility and fire departments. A public-utility coalition (Texas Public Power Corridor Committee) backing the deployment.

Human oversight

Utility and fire-service personnel were expected to use alerts for response and decisions such as power shutoffs.

Benefits

Duration

Austin Energy lists the Pano AI cameras among the wildfire tools of its Electric System Resiliency Plan of November 2025, so the system remained in use. The plan reports no detection or alert count, so no benefit is shown to persist. [1] [2] [3]

People and places

Dependencies

The deployment used thirteen 360-degree cameras covering Austin Energy's 437-square-mile territory, with location and imagery routed to utility and fire staff.
