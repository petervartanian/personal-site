ChatGPT names an insulinoma doctors missed for three years

ChatGPT suggested an insulinoma after three undiagnosed years; a glucose sensor and ER blood tests confirmed it and surgery cured him.

Application developer

Role

ChatGPT suggested insulinoma as a possible explanation for the reported symptoms.

Configuration

Dani described using the most intelligent model available in ChatGPT. The account does not name that model.

Workflow

Dani entered his symptoms, test results, and medical history into ChatGPT. He used a glucose sensor that recorded readings of 30 to 40 mg/dL, then sought emergency care. An internist confirmed the insulinoma, and a surgical team that treated him removed about two-thirds of his pancreas.

Relation to AI

ChatGPT produced the insulinoma hypothesis from a history that two doctors had reviewed without reaching it. That prompted the glucose sensor and the emergency visit. The diagnosis was made by an internist who, Dani wrote, “lo identificó sin nosotros decirle lo que creíamos que era”. The surgery and recovery were the hospital's work. Dani says he is not a doctor but has “formación biosanitaria de muchos años”, which let him understand what he was doing. Every account traces to his own video.

Entry details

Field

Healthcare (rare-disease diagnosis). Consumer use of a general chatbot as a diagnostic aid.

Purpose

ChatGPT is a general-purpose conversational assistant. Dani used it as a diagnostic aid, entering symptoms, results and history. OpenAI told the Mirror that its services “are not intended for use in the diagnosis or treatment of any health condition”. The Economic Times quoted that. So this benefit lay outside the product's stated purpose.
