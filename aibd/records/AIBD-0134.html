ChatGPT flags an insulinoma that doctors missed; surgery cures the man

ChatGPT named an insulinoma as the likely cause of a man's three years of undiagnosed symptoms; hospital blood tests confirmed it, and surgery cured him.

  • 3 a person

    A tumor that had gone undiagnosed for three years, producing glucose readings of 30 to 40 mg/dL, was found and removed. Dani says that he is cured, and COPE reported on 7 August 2025 that his symptoms had gone. He and the outlets call it life-saving (El Español: episodes that could have ended in fainting, brain damage or death). That counterfactual and the whole chain rest on his own account. No clinician who treated him has spoken.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
ChatGPT produced the insulinoma hypothesis from a history that two doctors had reviewed without reaching it. That prompted the glucose sensor and the emergency visit. The diagnosis was made by an internist who, Dani wrote, “lo identificó sin nosotros decirle lo que creíamos que era”. The surgery and recovery were the hospital's work. Dani says he is not a doctor but has “formación biosanitaria de muchos años”, which let him understand what he was doing. Every account traces to his own video.
Limits
All reports trace to the patient's or his partner's account, and they provide no pathology or testimony from a clinician who treated him. The only physician quoted, David Céspedes, had no part in his care. Insulinoma is not by itself evidence of malignant pancreatic cancer, despite some headlines.

What happened

Dani, 35, had three years of tingling, numbness, dizziness, double vision and a 15 kg weight gain. A neurologist and a primary-care doctor called them nothing important. He entered his symptoms, results and history into ChatGPT, which named an insulinoma as the likely cause. He bought a glucose sensor and recorded readings of 30 to 40 mg/dL. He went to an emergency department. An internist at Hospital Puerta de Hierro Majadahonda, Madrid, confirmed an insulinoma by blood tests. A month and a half later surgeons removed about two thirds of his pancreas and his symptoms went. His video was posted on Instagram on 3 August 2025. The dates of the query, visit and surgery were not given. In a COPE radio interview on 7 August 2025, the patient, named as Daniel Torralba, said that the emergency internist named the insulinoma before he told her his suspicion. COPE reported that his recovery after surgery had been hard and that his symptoms had gone. On Antena 3, ABC reported on 8 August 2025, the public-health physician David Céspedes said that the result depended on how well Torralba used the chatbot.

Details

AI system

Role

ChatGPT suggested insulinoma as a possible explanation for the reported symptoms.

Configuration

Dani described using the most intelligent model available in ChatGPT. The account does not name that model.

Inputs and outputs

Dani entered his symptoms and previous medical information. ChatGPT suggested insulinoma, which prompted him to measure glucose and seek urgent medical assessment.

Workflow

A rare condition (Dani: “una enfermedad muy poco común (1 entre 1millon)”) with diffuse symptoms. A neurologist, after neck and brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans, and a primary-care doctor had dismissed them over three years. A user with “formación biosanitaria de muchos años” who entered the full symptom list, test results and clinical history into the chatbot. He could interpret its answer. A cheap self-test, a continuous glucose sensor, that could corroborate the hypothesis before the emergency visit. It read 30 to 40 mg/dL at night and between meals. An emergency internist at Puerta de Hierro who confirmed the insulinoma by blood tests, and a surgical team that removed two thirds of the pancreas.

Human oversight

A hospital internist confirmed the insulinoma by blood tests, and surgeons removed it. The patient told COPE that the internist named the insulinoma before he told her his suspicion. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The chatbot's hypothesis is treated as a reason to be tested, not as a diagnosis. Dani says he went to doctors before, during and after. The suspected condition has an objective marker that a patient or clinician can measure quickly. The user can state symptoms and prior results in enough detail for the model to rank a rare cause. Two doctors had the same information.

Purpose
