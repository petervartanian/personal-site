ChatGPT names an insulinoma doctors missed for three years

depth 3 for a person

Independently reported, 4 sources.

ChatGPT suggested an insulinoma after three undiagnosed years; a glucose sensor and ER blood tests confirmed it and surgery cured him.

What happened

Dani, 35, had three years of tingling, numbness, dizziness, double vision and a 15 kg weight gain. A neurologist and a primary-care doctor called them nothing important. He entered his symptoms, results and history into ChatGPT, which named an insulinoma as the likely cause. He bought a glucose sensor and recorded readings of 30 to 40 mg/dL. He went to an emergency department. An internist at Hospital Puerta de Hierro Majadahonda, Madrid, confirmed an insulinoma by blood tests. A month and a half later surgeons removed about two thirds of his pancreas and his symptoms went. His video was posted on Instagram on 3 August 2025. The dates of the query, visit and surgery were not given.

Not what it was built to do. ChatGPT is a general-purpose conversational assistant. Dani used it as a diagnostic aid, entering symptoms, results and history. OpenAI told the Mirror that its services “are not intended for use in the diagnosis or treatment of any health condition”. The Economic Times quoted that. So this benefit lay outside the product's stated purpose.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    A tumor that had gone undiagnosed for three years, producing glucose readings of 30 to 40 mg/dL, was found and removed. Dani says he is cured. He and the outlets call it life-saving (El Español: episodes that could have ended in fainting, brain damage or death). But that counterfactual and the whole chain rest on his own account. No clinician has spoken.

The system's part

ChatGPT produced the insulinoma hypothesis from a history that two doctors had reviewed without reaching it. That prompted the glucose sensor and the emergency visit. The diagnosis was made by an internist who, Dani wrote, “lo identificó sin nosotros decirle lo que creíamos que era”. The surgery and recovery were the hospital's work. Dani says he is not a doctor but has “formación biosanitaria de muchos años”, which let him understand what he was doing. Every account traces to his own video.

Replication

What produced it. A rare condition (Dani: “una enfermedad muy poco común (1 entre 1millon)”) with diffuse symptoms. A neurologist, after neck and brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans, and a primary-care doctor had dismissed them over three years. A user with “formación biosanitaria de muchos años” who entered the full symptom list, test results and clinical history into the chatbot. He could interpret its answer. A cheap self-test, a continuous glucose sensor, that could corroborate the hypothesis before the emergency visit. It read 30 to 40 mg/dL at night and between meals. An emergency internist at Puerta de Hierro who confirmed the insulinoma by blood tests, and a surgical team that removed two thirds of the pancreas.

What must hold again. The chatbot's hypothesis is treated as a reason to be tested, not as a diagnosis. Dani says he went to doctors before, during and after. The suspected condition has an objective marker that a patient or clinician can measure quickly. The user can state symptoms and prior results in enough detail for the model to rank a rare cause. Two doctors had the same information.
