CBP facial comparison at Dulles flags a French-passport impostor

CBP's new facial comparison system at Dulles reported a mismatch for a man presenting a French passport on 22 August 2018; a Congolese ID was found in his shoe and he was returned to Brazil.

ⓘ

What happened

On 22 August 2018, the third day of facial comparison biometrics at Washington Dulles International Airport, a 26-year-old man arrived from Sao Paulo. He presented a French passport at primary inspection. CBP's release states the officer used the facial comparison biometric technology, which confirmed the man was not a match to the passport. In secondary inspection officers found his authentic Republic of Congo identification card concealed in his shoe. CBP Area Port Director Daniel Mattina told NBC News the man was apprehended, processed and returned to Brazil. CBP called him the first impostor detected with the technology. CBP releases record a second (8 September 2018) and third (1 October 2018) impostor at Dulles and, by September 2021, more than 900 impostors nationally. The sources do not name the matching-algorithm vendor.

Purpose. Verify arriving international travelers' identities by comparing a live photo with passport or visa photos in government holdings, to detect impostors and expedite entry.

AI’s contribution

The facial comparison match (less than two seconds per CBP) produced the mismatch that triggered secondary inspection. The search and removal were human actions. The documentation is CBP's own release (deployer, not developer) plus NBC with a named CBP official. The algorithm vendor and the match threshold are not in the sources. CBP's “99% match rate” claim is unaudited here.

Beneficiaries

  • society

    An attempt to enter the United States on another person's genuine passport was stopped and the traveler removed. Sources document no further threat beyond the document fraud.

  • an organization

    CBP gained a verified first detection for a system then three days into use at Dulles. Subsequent releases report repeated catches.

Since then
By September 2021 CBP reported that facial biometrics had stopped more than 900 impostors nationwide since September 2018 and that close to 100 million travelers had been processed.Since September 2018, CBP has leveraged facial biometrics to prevent more than 900 impostors from illegally entering the United States by using genuine travel documents that were issued to other people. source

Further evidence

Setting. Live photo at primary inspection compared against passport/visa photos retrieved from government holdings via airline manifests. Officer referral to secondary inspection on a reported mismatch. Traveler using a genuine document issued to someone else.

Conditions. A reference photo of the document's true bearer must exist in DHS holdings. Officers must act on mismatches rather than override them. Photo quality and demographic performance of the matcher must be adequate.
