North Yorkshire reports faster social-care data retrieval

North Yorkshire Council reports that its Microsoft Azure AI proof of concept for children's social-care records cut the time and cost of some data-retrieval tasks by 90% in testing.

What happened

North Yorkshire Council developed a Microsoft Azure AI proof of concept for semantic search and relationship maps in children's social-care records held in the Liquidlogic case-management system. In testing, some data-retrieval tasks reportedly required 90% less time and cost than in the current system. The Local Government Association account states that the Department for Education fund for the pilot ended on 31 March 2024. At a webinar on 3 July 2025, the council's head of data and insight still described the tool as the Children's Social Care Knowledge Mining Prototype. Global Government Forum reported on 16 July 2025 that the prototype scans documents related to a case and produces a diagram of the child's connections. Operational adoption and improved safeguarding outcomes remain unverified.

    To address this, a team at the council have created the Children's Social Care Knowledge Mining Prototype, which scans documents related to a case and produces a complex diagram of all of the child's connections in their community and beyond.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Case records and images support semantic search and generated maps of relationships around a child. [1]

Workflow

The project uses existing Liquidlogic case-management material, including notes, forms, and images. It was supported by the Department for Education’s Data and Digital Solutions Fund and evaluated as a proof of concept.

Human oversight

The council's account says that the tool provides information and insights to support social workers' decisions. No source describes a check on the accuracy of the relationship maps. [1] [2]

Benefits

Reach

No source counts the staff or the children whom the prototype served. [1] [2]

Duration

The Department for Education fund for the pilot ended on 31 March 2024. In July 2025 the council's head of data and insight still described the tool as a prototype, and no source reports a move to routine use. [1] [2]
