Shenzhen students' goose AI lifts survival 30%, Xinhua reports

depth 2 for an organization

helped the farm increase survival of lion-head geese by 30 percent

Sixteen Shenzhen University students with Tencent engineers built a camera system from 6,000 labeled images; Xinhua (13 Oct 2023) reports real-time alerts and 30% higher survival at the cooperative.

What happened

Xinhua reported on 13 October 2023 (syndicated by CGTN the next day) that a team of 16 students from the Shenzhen University-Tencent Cloud AI BEng program, invited in 2022 by goose farmer Jin Shutao to his lion-head goose cooperative in Houxi Village, Chenghai District, Shantou, built an AI program that identifies sick geese by measuring how long each bird stays still, supplemented by detecting feverish goslings. Working in a 500 m² space holding over 4,000 geese, they used existing farm cameras, manually labeled 6,000 images of 300,000 geese, and with Tencent engineers improved recognition in crowded scenes and tracking of each goose's stillness. The program now gives real-time alerts for 'listless geese' and 'feverish geese' and displays temperature, humidity and PM2.5 trends; Xinhua states this has helped the farm increase survival of lion-head geese by 30 percent, quoting student Wang Yifeng and Shen Linlin of Shenzhen University's visual research institute. The article recalls a 2018 avian influenza outbreak that left five of thousands of geese in the village alive. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor lists the event (2023-10-13-d63c). The 30% figure is undated and unsupported by method, so the date is the report's.

What it was built to do. Identify sick geese from camera footage by how long they stay still (and fever in goslings) and alert breeders in real time.

The system's part

The system's real-time alerts are the only mechanism described for the survival gain; the reports do not separate the AI's effect from other changes on the farm. Sources are independent news reports (Xinhua, CGTN) with named sources; no veterinary or peer-reviewed confirmation was found.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Existing farm cameras and 6,000 manually labeled images of 300,000 geese. Stillness duration as the sickness proxy, with fever detection in goslings. Over 4,000 geese in a 500 m² space.

What must hold again. Camera coverage in dense flocks. Continued tuning of the algorithm to the specific farm (the students found no one-size-fits-all algorithm).
