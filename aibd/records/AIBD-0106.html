Machine learning helps name a second species of Micrognathozoa

Machine-learning methods run on single-specimen transcriptomes, including a variational autoencoder and CLADES, separated the Crozet population, which the authors named Limnognathia desmeti.

The system’s part
The paper's title credits machine learning with the identification. Each machine-learning method run (unsupervised PCA, random forest, t-SNE and VAE; supervised delimitR and CLADES) separated the Crozet population. The adopted two-species result rests on the authors' most conservative estimate across PTP, VAE and custom CLADES. It does not rest on delimitR's three-species model, which would also have split Greenland from the Pyrenees. The conclusion also rests on deep phylogenetic divergence, extreme geographical separation and differences in COI and 18S rRNA sequences. So the machine-learning output was one of several lines of evidence, not the sole basis.
Limits
Species delimitation rests on several genetic and analytical approaches with differing proposed splits. The evidence does not isolate the benefit of machine learning over the non-ML analyses or establish independent replication of the new species boundary.

What happened

Micrognathozoa, the most recently discovered animal phylum, contained a single species, Limnognathia maerski, known from Greenland, the Pyrenees and a Subantarctic island. A French Polar Institute project awarded to co-author David Renault funded a collection in the Crozet Archipelago. Specimens were collected at Pointe du Bougainville on Île de la Possession on 10 and 11 December 2019. Sato and colleagues at the University of Copenhagen, Harvard University, the Natural History Museum London and partner institutions generated transcriptomes from single specimens. They tested species hypotheses with standard approaches and with machine-learning methods. The unsupervised methods were principal component analysis (PCA), random forest, t-SNE and a variational autoencoder (VAE). The supervised methods were delimitR and CLADES. The analyses separated the Subantarctic population, which the authors described as a new species, Limnognathia desmeti sp. nov. The analyses did not definitively separate the Greenland and Pyrenees populations. The authors preferred the most conservative estimate across the Poisson tree processes (PTP) method, VAE and custom CLADES, which delimits only the Crozet population. The paper appeared in Proceedings of the Royal Society B on 19 February 2025.

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The models analyzed genetic data from the three populations and proposed how to group the specimens into species.

Workflow

Field collection of all three known populations. Single-specimen transcriptomics for microscopic animals. Species-delimitation models run alongside conventional phylogenetics.

Human oversight

The authors compared the model results with conventional genetic analyses and chose the conservative two-species interpretation.
