What happened

Micrognathozoa, the most recently discovered animal phylum, contained a single species, Limnognathia maerski, known from Greenland, the Pyrenees and a Subantarctic island. Specimens were collected at Pointe du Bougainville on Île de la Possession, Crozet Archipelago, on 10–11 December 2019. A French Polar Institute project awarded to co-author David Renault funded the collection. Sato and colleagues at the University of Copenhagen, Harvard University, the Natural History Museum London and partner institutions generated transcriptomes from single specimens. They tested species hypotheses with standard approaches and with unsupervised (PCA, random forest, t-SNE, VAE) and supervised (delimitR, CLADES) machine-learning methods. The authors preferred the most conservative estimate across PTP, VAE and custom CLADES, which delimits only the Crozet population.

The analyses delimited the Subantarctic population as a new species, Limnognathia desmeti sp. nov., while not definitively separating the Greenland and Pyrenees populations. The paper appeared in Proceedings of the Royal Society B on 19 February 2025.

What it was built to do. Test species hypotheses within Micrognathozoa using transcriptomic data and machine-learning species delimitation.

The system's part

The paper's title credits machine learning with the identification. Each machine-learning method run (unsupervised PCA, random forest, t-SNE and VAE; supervised delimitR and CLADES) separated the Crozet population. The adopted two-species result rests on the authors' most conservative estimate across PTP, VAE and custom CLADES. It does not rest on delimitR's three-species model, which would also have split Greenland from the Pyrenees. The conclusion also rests on deep phylogenetic divergence, extreme geographical separation and differences in COI and 18S rRNA sequences. So the machine-learning output was one of several lines of evidence, not the sole basis.

Replication

What produced it. Field collection of all three known populations. Single-specimen transcriptomics for microscopic animals. Species-delimitation models run alongside conventional phylogenetics.

What must hold again. Sufficient genomic signal between populations. Agreement between machine learning (ML) and standard delimitation. Morphological comparison to document cryptic status.
