Nuru phone app identifies cassava disease better than extension agents

In a field evaluation in Kenya and Tanzania, the offline PlantVillage Nuru app identified cassava disease symptoms with 65% accuracy, above extension agents (40–58%) and farmers (18–31%).

The system’s part
The study measures the model's diagnostic accuracy against humans. It does not document any alert that changed a management decision. Authors include the model's developers at IITA and Penn State, so the source is peer-reviewed rather than independent of the makers.
Limits
Nuru's measured symptom recognition varies with the number of leaves assessed and with symptom severity. Better test accuracy than farmers or extension agents does not establish changed treatment decisions, crop yields or household income. Extension officers showed no significant gain in accuracy after two weeks of using the app (49.9% to 50.5%).
Costs and harms
The practical result depended on examining several leaves and on visible symptoms. Accuracy rose to 74–88% with six leaves per plant but fell to 13–37% for mild symptoms.
Later
Scaled up, 13 September 2024. WAVE reported on 13 September 2024 that it had disseminated Nuru to cassava farmers in Côte d'Ivoire since 2020. Of 146 users surveyed in three departments, 45% said that they kept using the app after the project ended and 56% said that they had stopped. WAVE staff wrote the study.

What happened

Nuru is a deep-learning object-detection model inside the free PlantVillage Android app, trained on 2,756 cassava leaf images and able to run offline. PlantVillage at Pennsylvania State University developed it with the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations, the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture, the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center and others. Researchers at the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture compared Nuru with cassava experts, agricultural extension officers and farmers. The targets were cassava mosaic disease, cassava brown streak disease and cassava green mite damage. They inspected plants in the field and scored a 170-image cassava symptom recognition assessment tool built from a farm in Mkuranga, Tanzania. The assessment tool was also used with 30 extension agents and 50 farmers from Busia County, western Kenya, before and after they were introduced to the app. The 2020 test used Nuru V2.6.0-39, released in June 2020. Nuru diagnosed cassava disease symptoms at 65% accuracy in 2020 versus 40–58% for extension agents and 18–31% for farmers. It rose to 74–88% with six leaves per plant assessed. Mild symptoms were recognized at only 13–37%. Extension officers showed no significant gain in accuracy after two weeks of using the app (49.9% to 50.5%). No farm-level outcome is reported. In September 2024, staff of WAVE, the center that spread the app in Côte d'Ivoire, reported on 146 surveyed users in three departments. They reported that 45% kept using the app after the project ended and that 56% had stopped.

Details

AI system

Release

PlantVillage Nuru 2.6.0-39.

Role

Nuru detected signs of cassava disease in photographs.

Configuration

The deployment used PlantVillage Nuru V2.6.0-39.

Inputs and outputs

Nuru analyzed camera images of cassava leaves for symptoms of mosaic disease, brown streak disease, and green-mite damage, with inference available offline on Android.

Workflow

A model trained on 2,756 cassava leaf images. Field plants and a 170-image CaSRAT set scored by consensus of cassava experts. Comparison groups of 30 extension agents and 50 farmers from Busia County.

Benefits

Reach

No count of farmers who benefited is reported. The evaluation involved 30 extension agents and 50 farmers from Busia County, and the 2024 survey covered 146 users in Côte d'Ivoire. [1] [2]

Duration

The accuracy figure of 65% comes from a single test in 2020 with Nuru V2.6.0-39. In Côte d'Ivoire, WAVE staff reported in September 2024 that 45% of 146 surveyed users kept using the app after the project ended and that 56% had stopped. [1] [2]

Measurement

Measurement

The percentage of leaf images that matched expert diagnoses. [1]

Setting and sample

The CaSRAT comparison used 170 images from Tanzania. [1]

Measures

Diagnostic accuracy on the CaSRAT image set. [1]

Results

The 2020 app averaged 65% correct diagnoses, compared with 49% for trained extension officers. [1]

Additional value from AI

Nuru exceeded the trained extension officers by 16 percentage points on the image benchmark. This does not establish better field decisions or higher crop yields. [1]

Limitations

Performance varied by symptom severity. The 25-point increase between app versions used different phone sets, so it does not isolate a software improvement. Table 4 reports spreads of ±10% and ±3% without identifying their type. [1]

Denominators

170 images in the CaSRAT comparison. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Symptoms expressed on the assessed leaves (mild symptoms were recognized at only 13–37%). Six leaves per plant assessed to reach 74–88%.
