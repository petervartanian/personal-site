PlantVillage Nuru out-diagnoses extension agents, 65% vs 40–58%

IITA-led evaluation in Kenya and Tanzania: the offline deep-learning app out-diagnosed extension officers (40–58%) and farmers (18–31%); six-leaf assessment raised accuracy to 74–88%.

ⓘ

What happened

Nuru is a deep-learning object-detection model inside the free PlantVillage Android app, trained on 2,756 cassava leaf images and able to run offline. PlantVillage (Penn State) developed it with FAO, IITA, CIMMYT and others. IITA researchers compared Nuru with cassava experts, agricultural extension officers and farmers. The targets were cassava mosaic disease (CMD), cassava brown streak disease (CBSD) and cassava green mite damage. They inspected plants in the field and scored a 170-image cassava symptom recognition assessment tool (CaSRAT) built from a farm in Mkuranga, Tanzania. CaSRAT was also used with 30 extension agents and 50 farmers from Busia County, western Kenya, before and after they were introduced to the app. Nuru diagnosed cassava disease symptoms at 65% accuracy in 2020 versus 40–58% for extension agents and 18–31% for farmers.

It rose to 74–88% with six leaves per plant assessed. Mild symptoms were recognized at only 13–37%. Two weeks of practical use gave extension workers only a slight gain. No farm-level outcome is reported.

Purpose. Diagnose cassava mosaic disease, cassava brown streak disease and cassava green mite damage from leaf images on an offline Android phone.

AI’s contribution

The study measures the model's diagnostic accuracy against humans. It does not document any alert that changed a management decision. Authors include the model's developers at IITA and Penn State, so the source is peer-reviewed rather than independent of the makers.

Further evidence

Setting. A model trained on 2,756 cassava leaf images. Field plants and a 170-image CaSRAT set scored by consensus of cassava experts. Comparison groups of 30 extension agents and 50 farmers from Busia County.

Conditions. Symptoms expressed on the assessed leaves (mild symptoms were recognized at only 13–37%). Six leaves per plant assessed to reach 74–88%.
