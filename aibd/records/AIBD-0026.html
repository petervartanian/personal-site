Fitbit flags a racing heart, and doctors treat ventricular tachycardia

A Fitbit Sense watch detected an irregular heart rate of 164 beats per minute in a man in Jamshedpur, India, who sought care and was treated for ventricular tachycardia.

Brief from Peter

In October 2021 a 55-year-old man in Jamshedpur, India, had symptoms at work, and his Fitbit Sense showed an irregular rhythm at 164 beats per minute. He felt the need to visit a doctor immediately and went to Tata Main Hospital, where cardiologists diagnosed idiopathic fascicular ventricular tachycardia from a 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) and further tests. They restored sinus rhythm by electrical cardioversion, and on verapamil he was later reported to be doing well.1 The U.S. Food and Drug Administration cleared the watch's ECG app to tell atrial fibrillation from sinus rhythm, and ventricular tachycardia lies outside the rhythms that it was cleared to classify.2 The benefit also rests on an assumption that he would have waited longer without the reading, and a single case report gives no way to check it.

  1. 1 His palpitations, sweating, anxiety and mild dizziness had begun at work half an hour before he arrived at the hospital. Kumar S and Banerjee A, case report, Cureus, July 18, 2023. europepmc.org
  2. 2 Fitbit's summary says that the app's atrial fibrillation detection is not recommended for people with other known arrhythmias. Fitbit, Inc., 510(k) summary and FDA clearance letter for the Fitbit ECG App, K200948, September 11, 2020. accessdata.fda.gov

Details

AI system

Role

The watch analyzed cardiac rhythm and flagged an irregular reading.

Configuration

The participant used a Fitbit Sense.

Inputs and outputs

The Fitbit recorded a rapid, irregular rhythm during symptoms, prompting the patient to seek hospital assessment.

Workflow

The wearer has a Fitbit with the ECG app or heart-rate alerts. A symptomatic tachyarrhythmia occurs while the device is worn and the result prompts care-seeking. Clinicians confirm the rhythm and start treatment.

Human oversight

Cardiologists diagnosed ventricular tachycardia from a clinical ECG and further tests, performed cardioversion, and corrected severe hypokalemia.

Purpose
