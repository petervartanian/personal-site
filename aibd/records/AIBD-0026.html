Fitbit Sense flags a 164 bpm rhythm, leading to a VT diagnosis

depth 2 for a person

detected an irregular heart rate of 164 beats per minute with irregular rhythm

Fitbit Sense flagged an irregular 164 bpm rhythm; fascicular VT was diagnosed and controlled with verapamil (India, 2023).

What happened

In a case report published in Cureus on 18 July 2023 (event date not stated; publication date used), a 55-year-old man in Jamshedpur, India, had symptoms at work while his Fitbit Sense watch detected an irregular heart rate of 164 beats per minute with irregular rhythm and bizarre QRS morphology, and he sought care at Tata Main Hospital. A 12-lead ECG showed tachycardia at 166 beats per minute with bizarre QRS complexes under 130 ms and right axis deviation; idiopathic fascicular ventricular tachycardia was diagnosed, and Holter monitoring, echocardiography and coronary angiography excluded structural heart disease. He was treated with oral verapamil and telmisartan, and after four cardiology visits was reported to be doing well with no further complaints.

What it was built to do. The Fitbit Sense ECG app records a single-lead ECG to assess heart rhythm (its clearance is for atrial fibrillation); capturing an abnormal rhythm for medical review was its purpose, although VT is outside its validated classification.

The system's part

The watch's rhythm analysis flagged an irregular 164 bpm rhythm with abnormal QRS morphology, which sent the patient to hospital; the diagnosis and treatment were made by cardiologists from a 12-lead ECG and further tests.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. The wearer has a Fitbit with the ECG app or heart-rate alerts. A symptomatic tachyarrhythmia occurs while the device is worn and the result prompts care-seeking. Clinicians confirm the rhythm and start treatment.

What must hold again. The device result is taken to a clinician promptly. Confirmatory ECG and structural work-up are available. Appropriate therapy is accessible.
