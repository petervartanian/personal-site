SKMC cites AI-enhanced planning in separating craniopagus twins

separated at 15 months in four procedures over six months

PureHealth/SKMC statement (5 Jun 2026) cites 'AI-enhanced surgical planning'; XRlabs' own release claims AI-designed implants. The twins were separated in four operations and returned to Nigeria.

What happened

The Abu Dhabi Media Office published PureHealth's statement on 5 June 2026 that Sheikh Khalifa Medical City (SKMC) had performed the GCC's first separation of craniopagus twins: Mercy and Goodness, born in Nigeria with 'heads joined at the top, fused skulls, and intertwined brain tissue and blood vessels', separated at 15 months in four procedures over six months by 'more than 60 doctors and clinicians from the UAE, UK, Brazil, and Nigeria', led by Professor Noor Ul Owase Jeelani of Gemini Untwined. The statement lists 'mixed reality-guided implant design and AI-enhanced surgical planning' among the methods, names no software, and says the skulls were reconstructed with custom 3D-printed titanium grafts and the twins were recovering well. The National and Khaleej Times (both 5 June 2026) report the same facts without the words AI or artificial intelligence. A CNN report carried by WKRC (22 June 2026) says the teams 'used artificial intelligence, mixed reality and virtual simulations to map out the procedures', that AI 'helped surgeons design custom skull implants and model how the girls' skin could be expanded', and that the four surgeries totalled over 40 hours across four months in 2025, with the twins since home in Nigeria. The only system-specific claim is XRlabs' own Business Wire release of 19 June 2026 (read via Medical Product Outsourcing), which says its platform let surgeons 'design implants within seconds' from CT data in mixed reality using an AI model 'trained on similarly aged infant head shapes'. The earlier verifier found no case report of these twins in Europe PMC and noted that the surgical group's 2026 Radiology paper on craniopagus imaging does not mention AI; this pass found none either. The date observed is the hospital announcement; the surgery dates are not published.

What it was built to do. XRlabs says its platform is built to design patient-specific implants and plan operations in mixed reality; the hospital's 'AI-enhanced surgical planning' is described only generically. Implant design for this separation was the vendor's stated purpose.

The system's part

The hospital's own statement credits 'AI-enhanced surgical planning' without naming a system; CNN says AI helped design the skull implants and model skin expansion; the earlier verifier read People/AOL as attributing AI vaguely to skin-expander timing. The only identification of a system is XRlabs' own release, which cites no clinical publication. The AI supported planning and implant design for a 60-person team; no counterfactual is given, and the separation itself was surgical work.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • A multidisciplinary team of more than 60 clinicians operating in four staged procedures over six months
  • CT data rendered as 3D models in a mixed-reality environment for implant design (vendor's description) and 3D-printed titanium grafts
  • Gemini Untwined's prior experience, nine craniopagus separations according to CNN

What must hold again

  • A surgical team able to act on AI-assisted plans and patient-specific implants
  • Imaging of sufficient quality to model the twins' shared vasculature and skull
  • The vendor's claims about the implant-design AI are not independently documented, so the AI's share cannot be assumed to transfer
