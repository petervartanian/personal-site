AI planning may have helped SKMC separate craniopagus twins

What happened

The Abu Dhabi Media Office published PureHealth's statement on 5 June 2026. Sheikh Khalifa Medical City (SKMC) had performed the GCC's first separation of craniopagus twins. Mercy and Goodness were born in Nigeria with “heads joined at the top, fused skulls, and intertwined brain tissue and blood vessels”. They were separated at 15 months in four procedures over six months. The team was “more than 60 doctors and clinicians from the UAE, UK, Brazil, and Nigeria”. Professor Noor Ul Owase Jeelani of Gemini Untwined led the work. The statement lists “mixed reality-guided implant design and AI-enhanced surgical planning” among the methods and names no software. It says the skulls were reconstructed with custom 3D-printed titanium grafts and the twins were recovering well. The National and Khaleej Times (both 5 June 2026) report the same facts without the words AI or artificial intelligence.

A CNN report carried by WKRC (22 June 2026) says the teams “used artificial intelligence, mixed reality and virtual simulations to map out the procedures”. It says AI “helped surgeons design custom skull implants and model how the girls” skin could be expanded'. It says the four surgeries totaled over 40 hours across four months in 2025, with the twins since home in Nigeria. The only system-specific claim is XRlabs' own Business Wire release of 19 June 2026, read via Medical Product Outsourcing. It says its platform let surgeons “design implants within seconds” from computed tomography (CT) data in mixed reality. It used an AI model “trained on similarly aged infant head shapes”. No case report of these twins has been found in Europe PMC. It noted that the surgical group's 2026 Radiology paper on craniopagus imaging does not mention AI.

The date observed is the hospital announcement, and the surgery dates are not published.

What it was built to do. XRlabs says its platform is built to design patient-specific implants and plan operations in mixed reality. The hospital's “AI-enhanced surgical planning” is described only generically. Implant design for this separation was the vendor's stated purpose.

The system's part

The hospital's own statement credits “AI-enhanced surgical planning” without naming a system. CNN says AI helped design the skull implants and model skin expansion. People/AOL attributes AI vaguely to skin-expander timing. The only identification of a system is XRlabs' own release, which cites no clinical publication. The AI supported planning and implant design for a 60-person team. No counterfactual is given, and the separation itself was surgical work.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A multidisciplinary team of more than 60 clinicians operating in four staged procedures over six months. CT data rendered as 3D models in a mixed-reality environment for implant design (vendor's description) and 3D-printed titanium grafts. Gemini Untwined's prior experience, nine craniopagus separations according to CNN.

What must hold again. A surgical team able to act on AI-assisted plans and patient-specific implants. Imaging of sufficient quality to model the twins' shared vasculature and skull. The vendor's claims about the implant-design AI are not independently documented, so the AI's share cannot be assumed to transfer.
