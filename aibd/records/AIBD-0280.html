AI planning may have helped SKMC separate craniopagus twins

PureHealth/SKMC statement (5 June 2026) cites “AI-enhanced surgical planning”; XRlabs' own release claims AI-designed implants. The twins were separated in four operations and returned to Nigeria.

ⓘ

What happened

PureHealth reported on June 5, 2026 that surgeons at Sheikh Khalifa Medical City in Abu Dhabi had separated Nigerian twins Mercy and Goodness. More than 60 clinicians took part in four staged procedures, led by Noor ul Owase Jeelani of Gemini Untwined. The hospital credited AI-assisted planning, mixed reality, and custom titanium skull grafts. On June 19, XRlabs identified its implant-design platform and said that its model used infant head shapes to generate patient-specific implants from CT data. The company also described surgical rehearsal in a shared virtual environment and reported that the twins had returned home. These are accounts from the treating institution and technology supplier. They establish a claimed AI contribution to a successful surgical procedure, without measuring how much AI improved the result.

The reports differ on age and timing, and do not provide an exact operation date.

Purpose. XRlabs says its platform is built to design patient-specific implants and plan operations in mixed reality. The hospital's “AI-enhanced surgical planning” is described only generically. Implant design for this separation was the vendor's stated purpose.

AI’s contribution

The treating institution identifies AI-assisted planning, and XRlabs describes an AI model for patient-specific skull-implant design. Both participated in the procedure. Neither provides a comparison without AI or a clinical evaluation that separates the model’s contribution from the surgical team’s work.

Since then
XRlabs reported that the twins had recovered and returned home. The release gives no clinical follow-up interval or independent assessment of the AI contribution.

Further evidence

Setting. A multidisciplinary surgical team performing four staged procedures. CT-derived 3D models, mixed-reality rehearsal, and patient-specific skull implants. Specialist coordination by Gemini Untwined and the treating hospital.

Conditions. A surgical team able to act on AI-assisted plans and patient-specific implants. Imaging of sufficient quality to model the twins' shared vasculature and skull. The vendor's claims about the implant-design AI are not independently documented, so the AI's share cannot be assumed to transfer.
