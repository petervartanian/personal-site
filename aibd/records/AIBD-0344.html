Operators in Iraq divert gas from a flare after UN satellite alerts

After alerts from UNEP's Methane Alert and Response System, which uses AI, operators diverted gas from an Iraqi source by 25 February 2026; UNEP estimates that it emitted 60,500 tonnes of methane.

What happened

UNEP states that its International Methane Emissions Observatory (IMEO) uses data from more than 30 satellite instruments, coupled with scientific expertise and advanced AI models, to notify governments and companies of emissions. UNEP’s original case document for source IRQ_S_050 records first detection at an Iraqi oil and gas facility on 21 August 2025. The document records two detected plumes and an estimated 60,500 tonnes of methane emissions. Operators diverted production and associated gas to newer infrastructure, and the document records mitigation on 25 February 2026. UNEP updated the document on 15 April 2026, and it does not name the operator. On 5 May 2026 The Chemical Engineer reported that the largest source mitigated that year was a flare in Iraq, which had released an estimated 1.7 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent since 2023. The report links the source to the Zubair area and reports a leak-detection campaign by Eni. Eni did not confirm closure of this specific source. UNEP’s September 2026 snapshot lists no Iraqi source among its top 50 emitters. Satellite non-detection is limited by the instrument’s sensitivity and observation conditions.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Satellite detection of a persistent flare plume emitting since 2023. MARS alerts to operators with a newer facility able to take the diverted gas. UNEP confirmation through later satellite observation (as described for MARS cases generally).

Human oversight

Operators diverted production and associated gas to newer infrastructure. UNEP's case document records later satellite verification of the mitigation. [1] [2] [3]

UNEP's case document gives 21 August 2025 as the first detection and 25 February 2026 as the reported mitigation. UNEP's September 2026 snapshot lists no Iraqi source among its top 50 emitters. [1] [2] [3]
