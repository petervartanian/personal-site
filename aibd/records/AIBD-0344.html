UNEP MARS alerts lead operators to divert gas from Iraqi flare

the flare had released an estimated 1.7m tCO2e since 2023

The Chemical Engineer (5 May 2026), relaying UNEP: the largest source mitigated in 2026 was an Iraqi flare, 1.7m tCO2e since 2023, apparently in the Zubair field; Eni did not confirm closure.

What happened

The Chemical Engineer reported on 5 May 2026, relaying UNEP's Methane Alert and Response System (MARS) figures, that MARS had curbed 41 methane super-emitters since launching in 2023 and that six significant sources had stopped emitting in 2026 following MARS alerts, including three in Turkmenistan and one each in Libya, Iraq and Kazakhstan. It states that the largest source mitigated this year was a flare in Iraq, where operators diverted gas to a newer facility, that the flare had released an estimated 1.7m tCO2e since 2023, and that the source appears to have been at a facility in the Zubair oil and gas field near Basra. The article reports that the operator Eni acknowledged running a leak detection and repair campaign at Zubair but did not confirm closure of the specific source. The article says MARS uses data from 35 satellite instruments to identify large plumes and alert operators; UNEP's MARS page adds that IMEO couples satellite data with scientific expertise and advanced AI models. No date for the mitigation is given beyond 2026, so the publication date is used. The earlier verifier could not reach unep.org press releases or the Wayback copies, and no UNEP case document for this source was fetched here.

What it was built to do. Satellite-based detection and notification of large methane plumes so that operators mitigate them; this case was that purpose.

The system's part

The trade press attributes the mitigation to MARS alerts; MARS is described as satellite-based with advanced AI models only on UNEP's general page, so the AI contribution to this detection is not isolated. The case is documented by a single news item relaying the system operator, with the named field operator declining to confirm closure of the specific source.

Replication

What produced it

  • Satellite detection of a persistent flare plume emitting since 2023
  • MARS alerts to operators with a newer facility able to take the diverted gas
  • UNEP confirmation through later satellite observation (as described for MARS cases generally)

What must hold again

  • Operator capacity and willingness to re-route gas rather than flare it
  • Satellite coverage to confirm cessation
  • Operator or government confirmation, absent here (Eni did not confirm the specific closure)
