Ukraine's AI-aimed Sky Sentinel turret shoots down Shahed drones

A prototype of Sky Sentinel, a Ukrainian AI-aimed machine-gun turret, downed six Shaheds in six uses, a unit commander said; more than 12 turrets were on duty in Kyiv by October 2026.

What happened

Sky Sentinel is an automated turret, a heavy machine gun on a 360-degree mount. Its software, written by Ukrainian engineers, takes radar input and separates drones from birds. It tracks the target and computes the ballistic solution. UNITED24 Media, the outlet of the UNITED24 fundraising platform, reported on 26 May 2025 that one prototype had downed four Shahed drones at the front. Dates and places were withheld for security. UNITED24 said that its $1.5 million campaign for 10 turrets at about $150,000 each was complete. Forbes (David Hambling, 27 May 2025), as relayed by Euromaidan Press on 28 May 2025, repeats the four kills. It gives a pointing precision of 17 micro-radians. Business Insider reported on 2 June 2025 that the commander of a Ukrainian air defense group said that one prototype had downed six Shaheds in six uses. He said that the turret needs human authorization before it fires. The OECD Monitor entry also states that a human authorizes each shot. The Defense Post (3 June 2025) describes the turret as firing on its own. It adds that 10 to 30 units would be needed to protect a city and that production is scaling to dozens a month. UNITED24 reported that it had raised $3.3 million for 22 systems by February 2026. Five systems went to the 636th Separate Anti-Aircraft Machine Gun Battalion in Sumy region in August 2026. On 5 October 2026 the Air Force spokesperson Yurii Ihnat said that AI-aimed turrets had downed Russian jet-powered drones. The Kyiv Independent, which relayed his statement, names no turret model. On 23 October 2026 UNITED24 reported that more than 12 turrets were on duty in Kyiv.

Details

AI system

Role

The developers reported that the system classified radar detections, tracked drones, and calculated firing corrections.

Configuration

The project used a prototype.

Inputs and outputs

Radar observations fed target classification and tracking, followed by firing commands to the mounted gun.

Autonomy

The reports describe the prototype as automatically classifying, tracking and firing at drone targets, without a documented requirement for authorization of each shot.

Workflow

Radar cueing that hands the turret a track on a slow, low-flying drone. A fixed heavy-machine-gun mount with near-zero mechanical play and fast optics. Rules that let the system engage without a human in the loop.

Human oversight

The commander of a Ukrainian air defense group told Business Insider that the turret needs human authorization before it fires. UNITED24 describes an operator who monitors the system while the turret handles aiming. The Defense Post describes the turret as firing on its own. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Targets fly within the gun's effective range and are not masked by birds or clutter. Enough units are fielded to cover a city, which the makers put at 10 to 30.
