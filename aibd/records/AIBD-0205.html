Sensors flag a Gosport fatberg, and a crew clears it before a spill

Southern Water's machine-learning sewer monitors flagged a forming fatberg in Rowner, Gosport, and a crew cleared it before any overflow into the River Alver.

  • 2 society

    The company says that a sewage overflow into the River Alver was averted. The Andover Advertiser says that the river is home to herons, water voles and kingfishers. No source shows what would have happened without the alert, and no pollution occurred. This is a specific avoided harm of unmeasured size.

  • 1 an organization

    Southern Water cleared one blockage before it became a pollution incident or a customer flooding. The company says that blocked sewers are the leading cause of its pollution incidents. This is one of the more than 4,000 interventions that it reports in a year.

The system’s part
The level monitors detected an abnormal flow at Rowner. The machine-learning model raised the alert to the control room; by the company's account it distinguishes a forming blockage from daily rushes and rainfall. The jetting crew found and removed the fatberg. The articles are built on a Southern Water statement and a single quoted manager. They do not name the developer of the model and give no sensor data. What happened

Southern Water runs around 34,000 sewer level monitors with machine learning. Its proactive operations control manager Daniel McElhinney says that the system learns the normal behavior of sewers. He says that it can tell a forming blockage from daily rushes or rain. Shortly before 22 April 2026 the sensors detected an abnormal flow in Rowner, Gosport, and alerted the control room. A crew jetted the sewer and found a fatberg of wet wipes and other unflushable items. The company said that, left unchecked, the sewer could have overflowed from a manhole into a surface water drain leading to the River Alver. The case was reported on 22 April 2026. A second flagged blockage at Warlands Lane, Shalfleet, Isle of Wight, was reported on 23 April. BBC Future reported on 12 May 2026 that the sensors use radar to measure sewer levels and that a model compares them with rainfall and weather data. The BBC reported that Southern Water had cleared 700 blockages with AI so far that year. It noted that the company still had more than 15,500 sewage spills in 2025, a drop of 47 percent from 2024. The BBC story does not mention the Rowner case. In October 2026 the same sensor network flagged a blockage of wet wipes before it overflowed into the Lymington River in the New Forest, by the company's account.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Monitors measured sewage levels under manholes. The anomaly model distinguished unusual behavior from typical daily peaks and rain, then alerted the control room to a possible blockage.

Autonomy

The AI supplied a blockage alert. Investigation and clearing remained human work.

Workflow

Level monitors in sewer blockage hotspots feeding a model that has learned the sewer's normal flow pattern and flags deviations from it. A staffed control room that receives the alert and can dispatch a jetting crew quickly.

Human oversight

Control-room staff sent a crew to investigate, and the crew physically removed the fatberg by jetting the sewer.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The flagged anomaly is a forming blockage rather than rain or a daily peak, which the company says the model distinguishes but for which no accuracy figures are given. A crew reaches the site before the sewer surcharges; the articles do not say how long that took here. The overflow route the company describes (manhole to surface water drain to river) exists as stated; it is the company's own account.
