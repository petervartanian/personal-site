Southern Water's sewer monitors flag a fatberg before overflow

Southern Water's AI sewer monitors flagged a forming fatberg in Rowner, Gosport; a crew cleared it before any overflow into the River Alver.

ⓘ

What happened

Southern Water runs around 34,000 sewer level monitors with machine learning. Its proactive operations control manager Daniel McElhinney says the system learns the normal behavior of sewers. He says it can tell a forming blockage from daily rushes or rain. Shortly before 22 April 2026 the sensors detected an abnormal flow in Rowner, Gosport, and alerted the control room. A crew jetted the sewer and found a fatberg of wet wipes and other unflushable items. The company said that, left unchecked, the sewer could have overflowed from a manhole into a surface water drain leading to the River Alver. The case was reported on 22 April 2026. A second flagged blockage at Warlands Lane, Shalfleet, Isle of Wight, was reported on 23 April.

Purpose. The monitors and the anomaly model were installed to spot blockages and leaks forming in sewers. Crews can then clear them before they cause pollution or flooding. This case was that intended use.

AI’s contribution

The level monitors detected an abnormal flow at Rowner. The machine-learning model raised the alert to the control room; by the company's account it distinguishes a forming blockage from daily rushes and rainfall. The jetting crew found and removed the fatberg. The articles are built on a Southern Water statement and a single quoted manager. They do not name the developer of the model and give no sensor data. No independent party confirmed that an overflow was imminent.

Beneficiaries

  • society

    The company says a sewage overflow into the River Alver was averted. The Andover Advertiser says the river is home to herons, water voles and kingfishers. The counterfactual is asserted rather than shown, and no pollution occurred. This is a specific avoided harm of unmeasured size.

  • an organization

    Southern Water cleared one blockage before it became a pollution incident or a customer flooding. The company says blocked sewers are the leading cause of its pollution incidents. This is one of the more than 4,000 interventions it reports in a year.

Further evidence

Setting. Level monitors in sewer blockage hotspots feeding a model that has learned the sewer's normal flow pattern and flags deviations from it. A staffed control room that receives the alert and can dispatch a jetting crew quickly.

Conditions. The flagged anomaly is a forming blockage rather than rain or a daily peak, which the company says the model distinguishes but for which no accuracy figures are given. A crew reaches the site before the sewer surcharges; the articles do not say how long that took here. The overflow route the company describes (manhole to surface water drain to river) exists as stated; it is the company's own account.
