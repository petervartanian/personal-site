Southern Water's sewer monitors flag a fatberg before overflow

Southern Water's AI sewer monitors flagged a forming fatberg in Rowner, Gosport; a crew cleared it before any overflow into the River Alver.

What happened

Southern Water runs around 34,000 sewer level monitors with machine learning that, its proactive operations control manager Daniel McElhinney says, learns the normal behavior of sewers and can tell a forming blockage from daily rushes or rain. Shortly before 22 April 2026 the sensors detected an abnormal flow in Rowner, Gosport, and alerted the control room; a crew jetted the sewer and found a fatberg of wet wipes and other unflushable items. The company said that, left unchecked, the sewer could have overflowed from a manhole into a surface water drain leading to the River Alver. The case was reported on 22 April 2026; a second flagged blockage at Warlands Lane, Shalfleet, Isle of Wight, was reported on 23 April.

What it was built to do. The monitors and the anomaly model were installed to spot blockages and leaks forming in sewers so that crews can clear them before they cause pollution or flooding; this case was that intended use.

  • 1
    organizational

    Southern Water cleared one blockage before it became a pollution incident or a customer flooding; the company says blocked sewers are the leading cause of its pollution incidents, but this is one of the more than 4,000 interventions it reports in a year.

The system's part

The level monitors detected an abnormal flow at Rowner and the machine-learning model, which by the company's account distinguishes a forming blockage from daily rushes and rainfall, raised the alert to the control room; the jetting crew found and removed the fatberg. The articles are built on a Southern Water statement and a single quoted manager, do not name the developer of the model, give no sensor data, and no independent party confirmed that an overflow was imminent.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • Level monitors in sewer blockage hotspots feeding a model that has learned the sewer's normal flow pattern and flags deviations from it
  • A staffed control room that receives the alert and can dispatch a jetting crew quickly
What must hold again
  • The flagged anomaly is a forming blockage rather than rain or a daily peak, which the company says the model distinguishes but for which no accuracy figures are given
  • A crew reaches the site before the sewer surcharges; the articles do not say how long that took here
  • The overflow route the company describes (manhole to surface water drain to river) exists as stated; it is the company's own account
