Transpara helps Swedish breast screening find more cancers

In the MASAI trial of 105,934 women, Transpara-supported screening found 6.4 cancers per 1,000 against 5.0 with standard double reading and cut screen reading by 44.2%.

Brief from Peter

Lund University researchers ran the MASAI trial within the Swedish national screening program. Between 12 April 2021 and 7 December 2022, they randomly assigned 105,934 women in southwest Sweden to Transpara-supported screening or to standard double reading by two radiologists without AI. The AI group had 6.4 screen-detected cancers per 1,000 women against 5.0 in the control group, and it needed 44.2% fewer screen readings.1 The primary outcome, published in The Lancet in 2026, was the interval cancer rate: 1.55 per 1,000 with AI and 1.76 per 1,000 without, which met the trial's non-inferiority margin. That difference was not statistically significant.2 The trial tested one product in one region, and its design does not separate the effect of the triage by risk score from that of the marks on suspicious areas.3

  1. 1 The extra invasive cancers were mainly small and had not spread to the lymph nodes. Recall and false-positive rates were not significantly higher in the AI group. V. Hernström and colleagues, The Lancet Digital Health, February 3, 2025. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39904652/
  2. 2 The ratio of interval cancer rates was 0.88 (95% confidence interval 0.65 to 1.18). J. Gommers and colleagues, The Lancet, January 2026. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41620232/
  3. 3 Transpara version 1.7.0 from ScreenPoint Medical gave each examination a risk score from 1 to 10. In the AI group, examinations scored 10 went to two readers and all others to one. The risk score of every examination, and the marks for those scored 8 to 10, were available to the readers. K. Lång and colleagues, The Lancet Oncology, August 2023. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37541274/

Workflow

Transpara 1.7.0 scored every screening examination on a scale from 1 to 10. Examinations scored 10 went to double reading and all others to single reading. Readers saw the AI risk scores and detection marks while reading. The trial ran inside an organized population screening program with experienced breast radiologists.

Sources

  1. Peer-reviewed study Independent author

    Gommers J, Hernström V, Josefsson V, et al. Interval cancer, sensitivity, and specificity comparing AI-supported mammography screening with standard double reading without AI in the MASAI study. Lancet. 2026. doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(25)02464-X

    Interval cancer rates were 1·55 (95% CI 1·23-1·92) and 1·76 (1·42-2·15) per 1000 participants in the intervention and control group respectively, a non-inferior proportion ratio of 0·88 (95% CI 0·65-1·18; p=0·41).

  2. Peer-reviewed study Independent author

    Hernström V, Josefsson V, Sartor H, et al. Screening performance and characteristics of breast cancer detected in the Mammography Screening with Artificial Intelligence trial (MASAI). Lancet Digit Health. 2025. doi:10.1016/S2589-7500(24)00267-X

    Cancer-detection rates were 6·4 per 1000 (95% CI 5·7-7·1) screened participants in the intervention group and 5·0 per 1000 (4·4-5·6) in the control group, a ratio of 1·29 (95% CI 1·09-1·51; p=0·0021).

  3. Peer-reviewed study Independent author

    Lång K, Josefsson V, Larsson AM, et al. Artificial intelligence-supported screen reading versus standard double reading in the Mammography Screening with Artificial Intelligence trial (MASAI). Lancet Oncol. 2023. doi:10.1016/S1470-2045(23)00298-X

    The AI system (Transpara version 1.7.0) provided an examination-based malignancy risk score on a 10-level scale that was used to triage screening examinations to single reading (score 1-9) or double reading (score 10)

  4. Peer-reviewed study Independent author

    Ferre R, Benefield T, Kuzmiak CM. Artificial intelligence-supported double reading in European population breast cancer screening: a systematic review and meta-analysis of prospective programs. Clin Imaging. 2026;139:110923. doi:10.1016/j.clinimag.2026.110923

    Efficiency signals included 44.3% fewer total readings in MASAI

  5. Peer-reviewed study Independent author

    Ciurescu S, Buciu V, Ilaș DG, Pârvănescu R, Șerban D. Ten years of artificial intelligence in screening mammography: a systematic review and meta-analysis of diagnostic accuracy and clinical implementation (literature published 2015-2025). Diagnostics (Basel). 2026;16(18):3045. doi:10.3390/diagnostics16183045

    Across randomised and paired prospective trials (k = 3), the pooled detection rate ratio was 1.13 (0.83-1.55), with the MASAI randomised trial alone reporting 1.29 (1.09-1.51) and a 44% reduction in screen reading.
