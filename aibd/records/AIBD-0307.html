Odakyu starts Panasonic fall-detection AI on station cameras

From 1 April 2020 Odakyu's system analyzes existing platform camera video to flag people who fall onto the tracks or linger at the platform edge and alerts station staff; no outcomes are reported.

What happened

Odakyu Electric Railway announced on 30 March 2020 (press release republished by Nikkei) that from the first train on 1 April 2020 it would operate a 転落検知システム (fall detection system) at Kyōdō (経堂) and Soshigaya-Ōkura (祖師ヶ谷大蔵) stations on the Odakyu Line, using the video of existing station cameras. Built with Panasonic System Solutions Japan, the system applies Panasonic's image-analysis and deep-learning technology to analyze the camera feeds in real time; when it detects a person who has fallen from the platform onto the tracks, or a person who has stayed at the platform edge for a set time and risks contact with a train, it raises an alert on a dedicated terminal in the station office and shows the camera image, so that staff can take safety measures such as an emergency stop. Pre-learning to set each camera's detection area and to distinguish people from trains had run at both stations since December 2019. Impress Watch reported the launch on 31 March 2020. Neither source reports any detection, prevented accident or performance figure, and no later outcome report was found. Impress Watch (7 October 2024) reported a separate trial of Asilla's behavior-recognition 'AI Security asilla' on all 20 existing cameras at Kakio and Tamagawa-Gakuen-mae stations from 10 October 2024 to 9 January 2025, testing detection of falls from the platform, wheelchair and white-cane users, people lingering on the platform, disputes between passengers and suspicious objects; results were not reported. Romanisations of station names are ours; the sources give them in Japanese.

What it was built to do. Built to detect people falling from the platform onto the tracks or lingering at the platform edge and alert station staff; no detection event is documented.

The system's part

not assessed No event involving the system is documented; the record rests on Odakyu's own launch release and Impress Watch's report of it, which describe what the deep-learning analysis is meant to detect and how staff are alerted. The 2024 Asilla trial at two other stations is a separate system whose results were not reported.

Replication

What produced it

  • Existing station cameras covering the platform edge and track at two stations
  • Panasonic deep-learning image analysis trained from December 2019 to set detection areas and tell people from trains
  • A dedicated terminal in the station office that shows the alert and video for staff to act on

What must hold again

  • Station staff present to confirm the alert and order an emergency stop
  • Detection areas calibrated per camera; the sources give no accuracy or false-alarm figures
