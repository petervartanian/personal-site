Camera software flags a passenger staggering at an Odakyu platform edge

Odakyu's Panasonic-built fall detection system alerts staff to people on the tracks or at the platform edge; the vendor reports that it flagged a staggering passenger on 3 April 2020.

  • 3 a person

The vendor's heading calls it a prevented fall, and no source says that a train was near, so the rating records a risk removed. The account comes only from the vendor.

  • Not rated an organization

What happened

Pre-learning ran at Kyōdō (経堂) and Soshigaya-Ōkura (祖師ヶ谷大蔵) stations on the Odakyu Line from December 2019. It set each camera's detection area and taught the system to distinguish people from trains. On 30 March 2020 Odakyu Electric Railway announced a 転落検知システム (fall detection system) in a press release that Nikkei republished. Impress Watch reported the launch on 31 March 2020. The system would operate from the first train on 1 April 2020 at both stations, using video from existing station cameras. Built with Panasonic System Solutions Japan, it applies Panasonic's image-analysis and deep-learning technology to analyze the camera feeds in real time. It alerts a dedicated terminal in the station office, showing the camera image, when it detects a person fallen from the platform onto the tracks. It does the same for a person who has stayed at the platform edge for a set time and risks contact with a train. Staff can then take safety measures such as an emergency stop. Neither launch source reports a detection, a prevented accident or a performance figure. Panasonic Connect's case study of September 2020 reports that on 3 April 2020 the system detected a passenger staggering at the platform edge at Kyōdō and that staff took safety measures. The case study says that this prevented an accident. It also reports misdetections caused by sunlight. Impress Watch reported on 7 October 2024 a separate trial of Asilla's behavior-recognition “AI Security asilla” on all 20 existing cameras at Kakio and Tamagawa-Gakuen-mae stations. It ran from 10 October 2024 to 9 January 2025. It tested detection of falls from the platform, wheelchair and white-cane users, people lingering on the platform, disputes between passengers and suspicious objects. Results were not reported. Odakyu's safety reports list the system as operating at both stations through July 2025. The July 2026 report no longer lists it and shows platform doors installed at Soshigaya-Ōkura and scheduled for Kyōdō in fiscal 2026. Romanizations of station names are ours; the sources give them in Japanese.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Existing station cameras supplied video; detected falls or prolonged presence at the platform edge produced an alert with the camera image on a station-office terminal.

Autonomy

The software detected and alerted. Station staff could then confirm the situation and order an emergency stop.

Workflow

Existing station cameras covering the platform edge and track at two stations. Panasonic deep-learning image analysis trained from December 2019 to set detection areas and tell people from trains. A dedicated terminal in the station office that shows the alert and video for staff to act on.

Human oversight

The system shows its alert and the camera image on a terminal in the station office. Station staff then decide on safety measures such as an emergency stop. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]
