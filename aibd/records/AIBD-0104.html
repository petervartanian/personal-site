LAARMA alerts cut fatal crashes 31% in Kuranda cassowary trial

TMR three-month trial at Kuranda: 97% cassowary detection, drivers slowed 6.6 km/h, fatal crashes down 31% (reported 30 July 2025).

ⓘ

What happened

The Large Animal Activated Roadside Monitoring and Alert System (LAARMA) was developed by the University of Sydney's Australian Centre for Robotics and QUT's CARRS-Q. They worked with the Queensland Department of Transport and Main Roads (TMR) under an iMOVE project. It uses a machine-learning pipeline to detect large animals up to 200 m away and triggers roadside variable message signs. The ABC reported on 30 July 2025 that TMR had used the AI for three months at a busy cassowary crossing. That is on the Kennedy Highway at Kuranda, Far North Queensland. Trial dates are not given. The consortium's iMOVE project page instead describes a five-month field trial: three months of data collection, then two months of on-road trialling. TMR told the ABC that the system identified cassowaries with 97 percent accuracy.

It said drivers slowed by an average of 6.6 km/h at the flashing sign and fatal crashes fell 31 percent during the trial. The iMOVE page reports speed reductions averaging 6.30 km/h and 5.06 km/h at two test sites and gives no collision figure. About two dozen cassowaries were killed on Queensland roads the previous year.

Purpose. Detect large animals near the road in real time and warn drivers via variable message signs to reduce animal-vehicle collisions.

AI’s contribution

Machine-learning detection of cassowaries activated the warning signs. TMR attributes the speed reduction and the 31 percent fall in fatal crashes to the system. But the figures are trial aggregates without a peer-reviewed analysis. The 31 percent figure is reported only by the ABC citing TMR. The consortium's project page reports detection and speed results without a collision figure.

Further evidence

Setting. A known animal crossing hotspot with high strike rates. Self-training detection pipeline that improved over the trial. Variable message signs drivers can see in time to slow.

Conditions. Detection accuracy on the target species. Driver compliance with flashing warnings. Permanent funding, which TMR said it was still pursuing.
