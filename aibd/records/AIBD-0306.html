Azure AI Vision app helps blind, low-vision adults in 354 tasks

depth 1 for a person

2.76 out of 5 for satisfaction and 2.43 out of 4 for trust on average

Gonzalez Penuela et al. (CHI 2024) log 354 diary entries from 16 BLV participants using an Azure AI Vision app; uses include telling sunglasses from reading glasses without calling a spouse.

What happened

Ricardo Gonzalez Penuela, Jazmin Collins, Cynthia Bennett and Shiri Azenkot (Cornell Tech and Google) ran a two-week diary study in which 16 blind and low-vision adults in the United States, recruited through National Federation of the Blind mailing lists (five men, 11 women, aged 24 to 67), used an iPhone scene-description app the researchers built on Microsoft's Azure AI Vision image description API, chosen because it 'exposes the latest deep learning algorithms' and is also used by Seeing AI. The participants submitted 354 entries (13 to 39 each). Documented uses include P1 photographing a pair of glasses because she 'was not sure if this was my husband's sunglasses or my old pair of reading glasses from 4 years ago' and getting an answer without calling her husband; P5 photographing a hotel bed to check his roommate was not in it; and P15 photographing wine bottles in a shop rather than touching them. Participants scored descriptions 2.76 out of 5 for satisfaction and 2.43 out of 4 for trust on average, and the authors say satisfaction did not track accuracy because users inferred information from partially correct descriptions. The paper appeared at CHI 2024 (doi 10.1145/3613904.3642211); the arXiv version was submitted on 22 March 2024, the date used because the study dates are not given in the fetched text. The earlier verifier notes that the CHI EA 2025 follow-up used a custom app, VisionPal, and documented failures such as hallucinated braille labels.

What it was built to do. The study app was built to describe photographed scenes for blind and low-vision users, emulating Seeing AI's scene channel; the documented identifications were that purpose.

The system's part

Azure AI Vision generated every description; the researchers' app only submitted photos and collected ratings. The paper documents individual successful uses and an overall judgment that descriptions 'still need significant improvements'. Participants were allowed to use other tools during the study, so the diary covers only what they chose to photograph with this app.

Replication

What produced it. An iPhone app sending photos to the Azure AI Vision image description API. Blind and low-vision users photographing objects they did not want to touch or ask others about. A minimum of one photo per day for two weeks with a short questionnaire per entry.

What must hold again. The description is accurate enough for the user to infer what they need, since users sometimes extracted answers from partially correct output. The task does not require reading text, which the app could not do.
