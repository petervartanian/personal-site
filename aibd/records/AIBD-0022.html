Apple Watch SpO2 of 88% prompts CT that finds pulmonary embolism

Apple Watch Series 7 SpO2 of 88% prompted CT angiography that found pulmonary embolism; anticoagulated and discharged (Japan).

ⓘ

What happened

A case appeared online on 5 September 2022 in QJM (event date not reported). A 50-year-old woman who recently had COVID-19 was referred to the authors' hospital (Oita University, Yufu, Japan) for assessment of dyspnea. Hospital pulse oximetry read 97%, but her Apple Watch Series 7 had shown 88% oxygen saturation in the early morning. Prompted by the watch reading, clinicians did emergent computed tomography (CT) angiography. It showed filling defects in multiple pulmonary artery branches, confirming pulmonary embolism with bilateral deep venous thrombosis (DVT). She was treated with a direct oral anticoagulant, oxygen was stopped on day 6 and she was discharged on day 15. The authors state the watch's low reading led to the diagnosis.

Unexpected use. The Blood Oxygen app measures oxygen saturation for general wellness and is not intended for medical diagnosis. Detecting a pulmonary embolism was not what it was built to do.

AI’s contribution

The watch's pulse-oximetry algorithm produced the 88% reading on the patient's watch. The letter does not say who reported it to the clinicians and describes no automatic alert. Clinicians chose to perform CT angiography because of that reading and state that it led to the diagnosis.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    A pulmonary embolism with bilateral DVT was diagnosed and anticoagulated after hospital oximetry had been normal. A life-changing benefit, though the source does not establish that death was imminent.

Further evidence

Setting. The wearer has an Apple Watch with the Blood Oxygen app and reviews its readings. A hypoxemic condition produces a persistently low reading at home. The wearer reports the reading and a clinician acts on it despite normal spot oximetry.

Conditions. The reading is not dismissed as artifact. Confirmatory testing (CT angiography, co-oximetry) is available. Effective treatment exists for the condition found.
