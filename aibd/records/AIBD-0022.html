An Apple Watch records low oxygen, and doctors find a pulmonary embolism

The Blood Oxygen app on an Apple Watch Series 7 recorded an oxygen saturation of 88%, which led clinicians in Japan to computed tomography angiography that found a pulmonary embolism.

The authors write that it might have been hard to think of pulmonary embolism without the watch data, and clinicians made the diagnosis with CT angiography. The letter describes no automatic alert and does not say who reported the reading.
Limits
This is a single clinical case, and clinicians made the diagnosis with computed tomography angiography. The case report does not describe how the watch's oxygen algorithm works. It describes no automatic alert and does not say who reported the reading. A 2023 systematic review of five studies of the Series 6 with 973 patients found no strong systematic bias against medical-grade pulse oximeters, with outliers of up to 15 points.
Costs and harms
The watch kept a record of a low-oxygen episode outside the hospital, which normal spot readings had missed. The 2023 systematic review reports that Apple Watch readings can differ from medical-grade pulse oximeters by up to 15 points.

Brief from Peter

A 50-year-old woman who had recently had COVID-19 was referred to the hospital of Oita University in Yufu, Japan, for breathlessness. Four days before admission a primary care physician had measured her oxygen saturation at 94 to 98%, and hospital pulse oximetry read 97%, but her Apple Watch Series 7 had shown 88% in the early morning. Prompted by the watch reading, clinicians ordered CT angiography and diagnosed pulmonary embolism with bilateral deep venous thrombosis. She was treated with an anticoagulant and discharged on day 15. The authors state that the low reading led to the diagnosis.1 The Blood Oxygen app is built for general wellness, and detecting an embolism lies outside its purpose. In 2023, other authors who examined five studies of the Series 6 with 973 patients found that the watch shows no strong systematic bias against medical-grade pulse oximeters and that outliers of up to 15 points occur.2 The case depended on clinicians who acted on a watch reading when the hospital reading was normal.3

Details

AI system

Role

The watch estimated blood-oxygen saturation from optical measurements.

Configuration

The initial event involved an Apple Watch Series 7. A later report involved a Series 6.

Inputs and outputs

The watch recorded an early-morning oxygen saturation of 88%, providing information that differed from the later hospital spot reading of 97%.

Workflow

The wearer has an Apple Watch with the Blood Oxygen app and reviews its readings. A hypoxemic condition produces a persistently low reading at home. The wearer reports the reading and a clinician acts on it despite normal spot oximetry.

Human oversight

Clinicians treated the home reading as a reason to investigate, ordered CT angiography, and diagnosed and treated pulmonary embolism.
