REACH VET flags veterans who then attempt suicide less; deaths unchanged

A study by the Veterans Affairs office that runs REACH VET found that veterans flagged by its suicide-risk model had fewer suicide attempts and admissions and no change in deaths.

  • Not rated a person

    The 2021 study reports changes for cohorts of veterans. No individual veteran's outcome is documented, and mortality did not differ in 2021 or 2025.

  • 2 an organization

    VA-authored papers report measurable gains in care. Completed outpatient visits and new safety plans rose for identified veterans, and care evaluations rose from 46.1% to 87.5% of them between March 2017 and December 2018.

  • 4 society

    The VA-authored evaluation reports fewer documented suicide attempts, mental health admissions and emergency visits across 40,816 top-risk veterans. It found no change in suicide or all-cause deaths, so no lives saved are documented.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

Study
Quasi-experimental.
Compared with

Veterans in the top 0.1% risk tier during the pre-REACH VET period (March 2014 to December 2015) and subthreshold-risk cohorts, in a triple-difference design. Measured.

The system’s part
The predictive model selects the monthly top 0.1% risk tier. Coordinators and providers then re-evaluate care and reach out, so the clinical response, not the model alone, produces any change. The evaluation is observational, uses a triple-difference design to handle non-parallel trends, and is authored by the VA office that built and runs REACH VET. It found no mortality association, replicated in 2025 by the same group. Independent reporting documents that the original model weighted being a white man heavily and omitted military sexual trauma.
Limits
The model triggered a package of clinician outreach and enhanced care, so the observed association is not an isolated effect of prediction. Historical and subthreshold comparisons require assumptions about trends and confounding. The retained null mortality findings must not be converted into a claim that deaths were prevented.
Costs and harms
The Fuller Project reported on 30 May 2024 that the model favors men and that officials left military sexual trauma out of its 61 variables. Nextgov reported in 2024 that research found that the model “considered being a white man more of an indicator of potential self-harm than other factors”. Nextgov also reported that a REACH VET 2.0 adding military sexual trauma and intimate partner violence as risk factors was planned for early 2025.
Later
Persisted, 17 July 2025. Nextgov reported in July 2025 that REACH VET still scans records monthly with the 61-variable model and that VA is updating it to a 2.0 model; a VA cohort study published 8 July 2025 found no association between REACH VET inclusion and suicide or all-cause mortality, in line with the 2021 evaluation's mortality subanalyses.

What happened

The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) announced REACH VET (Recovery Engagement and Coordination for Health - Veterans Enhanced Treatment) on 3 April 2017. It had begun as a pilot the previous October and was fully implemented across VA. It uses a predictive model on veterans' health records to identify those at statistically elevated risk of suicide and other adverse outcomes. Once a veteran is identified, a VA mental health or primary care provider reaches out. The provider reviews the condition and treatment plan and decides whether enhanced care is needed. Nextgov/FCW (17 July 2025) says that the model scans records monthly for the top 0.1% risk tier. It says that the variable set was cut from 381 to 61 and that about 30,000 veterans were identified in the first year. Nextgov (18 October 2024) gives about 6,700 veterans a month. McCarthy and colleagues published the evaluation in JAMA Network Open on 18 October 2021. They are at the VA Office of Mental Health and Suicide Prevention, which built and runs the program. It covered 173,313 individuals, 40,816 of them in the top 0.1% tier during REACH VET (March 2017 to December 2018). The pre-period (March 2014 to December 2015) had 36,604. Triple-difference analyses found that REACH VET was associated with more completed outpatient appointments (adjusted triple difference 0.31; 95% confidence interval 0.06 to 0.55). The proportion with new safety plans rose (0.08; 0.06 to 0.10). Mental health admissions (-0.08; -0.10 to -0.05), emergency department visits (-0.03; -0.06 to -0.01) and documented suicide attempts (-0.05; -0.06 to -0.03) fell. Subcohort analyses found no difference in suicide or all-cause mortality. Care evaluations of identified veterans rose from 46.1% to 87.5% over the period. A 2022 Psychiatric Services paper reports that by October 2020, 98% of identified veterans (N=6,579) were contacted and had care evaluated. On 30 May 2024 The Fuller Project, in a story copublished by The Markup, reported from VA documents and interviews that the model favors men and that officials left military sexual trauma out of its 61 variables. Matthew Miller, VA's executive director for suicide prevention, told the reporter that officials excluded it because it was not among the strongest predictors of suicide risk. Nextgov (2024) reports that research found that the model “considered being a white man more of an indicator of potential self-harm than other factors”. Nextgov reports that a REACH VET 2.0 adding military sexual trauma and intimate partner violence as risk factors was planned for early 2025. Dent, Cooper and McCarthy (JAMA Network Open, 8 July 2025; 266,246 observations) again found no association with suicide, external-cause or all-cause mortality. The 6-month suicide hazard ratio was 1.18 (95% confidence interval 0.83–1.69). The program's benefit is a cohort-level association over 2017–2018, with no single dated event.

Release

Role

The model ranked patients by predicted suicide risk for clinical follow-up.

Configuration

The original 2017 REACH VET model selected 61 variables from 381. The later REACH VET 2.0 announcement concerns a different version.

Autonomy

The model selected patients for attention; clinicians decided whether and how to enhance their care.

Workflow

The monthly risk list prompted VA providers to contact selected veterans, reassess treatment and add care or safety planning when needed.

Human oversight

Once the model identifies a veteran, a VA mental health or primary care provider reaches out, reviews the condition and treatment plan and decides whether enhanced care is needed. Benefits

Reach

The 2021 evaluation covered 40,816 veterans in the top 0.1% risk tier from March 2017 to December 2018. These are veterans exposed to the program. The study reports changes in rates for the cohort and no count of individuals who benefited. Nextgov/FCW says that about 30,000 veterans were identified in the first year. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Duration

The principal evaluation covered March 2017 through December 2018. A later cohort analysis found no statistically detectable mortality reduction through 24 months. [1] [2] [6]

Measurement

Measurement

Triple-difference analysis of 6-month changes in outcomes after compared with before cohort entry, using VHA health records and national death data. [1]

Setting and sample

173,313 individuals across all VHA facilities; 40,816 in the top 0.1% tier from March 2017 to December 2018 and 36,604 in the top tier from March 2014 to December 2015. [1]

Measures

Completed outpatient appointments, new safety plans, mental health admissions, emergency department visits, documented suicide attempts, and suicide and all-cause mortality. [1]

Results

Suicide attempts ATD -0.05 (95% CI -0.06 to -0.03); mental health admissions -0.08; emergency visits -0.03; outpatient visits +0.31; new safety plans +0.08; no difference in suicide or all-cause mortality. [1]

Additional value from AI

The design compares program cohorts with similar risk-tier cohorts before the program. It does not separate the model's ranking from the outreach and care review that follow it. [1]

Limitations

Observational design by the VA office that runs the program. Cause-of-death data covered only a subcohort, and the authors write that the mortality analyses may be underpowered. The authors also note that closer clinical attention may raise recorded attempts, so the drop in attempts may be underestimated. [1]

Denominators

Monthly identification in the top 0.1% risk tier at the veteran's facility, followed by provider outreach and care review. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Providers act on the list: care evaluations rose from 46.1% to 87.5% of identified veterans between March 2017 and December 2018. The model's variables fit the population; the original model under-weighted factors relevant to women veterans, per Nextgov. Outcome ascertainment is comparable across periods; the authors note that closer attention may increase recorded attempts.
