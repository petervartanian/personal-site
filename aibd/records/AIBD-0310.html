VA study: REACH VET tied to fewer suicide attempts, not deaths

off the scale

documented suicide attempts (-0.05; -0.06 to -0.03)

VA-authored triple-difference cohort study (173,313 veterans): REACH VET inclusion linked to more completed visits and safety plans and fewer admissions, ED visits and attempts; no mortality effect.

What happened

The Department of Veterans Affairs announced on 3 April 2017 that REACH VET (Recovery Engagement and Coordination for Health - Veterans Enhanced Treatment), which uses a predictive model on veterans' health records to identify those at statistically elevated risk of suicide and other adverse outcomes, had begun as a pilot the previous October and was fully implemented across VA; once a veteran is identified, a VA mental health or primary care provider reaches out, reviews the veteran's condition and treatment plan and decides whether enhanced care is needed. Nextgov/FCW (17 July 2025) reports that the model scans records monthly for the top 0.1% risk tier, that the variable set was cut from 381 to 61, and that about 30,000 veterans were identified in the first year; Nextgov (18 October 2024) gives about 6,700 veterans a month. McCarthy and colleagues of the VA Office of Mental Health and Suicide Prevention, which built and runs the program, published the evaluation in JAMA Network Open on 18 October 2021: among 173,313 individuals, including 40,816 in the top 0.1% tier during REACH VET (March 2017 to December 2018) and 36,604 in the pre-period (March 2014 to December 2015), triple-difference analyzes found REACH VET associated with more completed outpatient appointments (adjusted triple difference 0.31; 95% CI 0.06 to 0.55), a higher proportion with new safety plans (0.08; 0.06 to 0.10), and fewer mental health admissions (-0.08; -0.10 to -0.05), emergency department visits (-0.03; -0.06 to -0.01) and documented suicide attempts (-0.05; -0.06 to -0.03); subcohort analyzes found no difference in suicide or all-cause mortality. Care evaluations of identified veterans rose from 46.1% to 87.5% over the period, and a 2022 Psychiatric Services paper reports that by October 2020, 98% of veterans identified (N=6,579) were contacted and had their care evaluated. Dent, Cooper and McCarthy (JAMA Network Open, 8 July 2025; 266,246 observations) again found no association with suicide, external-cause or all-cause mortality (6-month suicide HR 1.18; 95% CI 0.83-1.69). Nextgov (2024) reports research finding the model 'considered being a white man more of an indicator of potential self-harm than other factors' and omitted military sexual trauma, and that a REACH VET 2.0 adding such factors was planned for early 2025. The date observed is the publication date of the 2021 evaluation; the program's benefit is a cohort-level association over 2017-2018, not a dated event.

What it was built to do. Built to identify veterans in the top 0.1% of predicted suicide risk each month so that clinicians re-evaluate and enhance their care; the measured associations are that purpose.

The system's part

The predictive model selects the monthly top 0.1% risk tier; coordinators and providers then re-evaluate care and reach out, so the clinical response, not the model alone, produces any change. The evaluation is observational, uses a triple-difference design to handle non-parallel trends, and is authored by the VA office that built and runs REACH VET; it found no mortality association, replicated in 2025 by the same group, and independent reporting documents that the original model weighted being a white man heavily and omitted military sexual trauma.

Replication

What produced it. A predictive model run monthly on VHA electronic health records to rank patients and select the top 0.1% risk tier. Facility REACH VET coordinators and providers who acknowledge the list, evaluate care, document safety plans and attempt outreach. A national health system with the data, dashboards and metrics to implement the program at every facility.

What must hold again. Providers act on the list: care evaluations rose from 46.1% to 87.5% of identified veterans between March 2017 and December 2018. The model's variables fit the population; the original model under-weighted factors relevant to women veterans, per Nextgov. Outcome ascertainment is comparable across periods; the authors note that closer attention may increase recorded attempts.
