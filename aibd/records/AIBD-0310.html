REACH VET flags veterans who then attempt suicide less; deaths unchanged

A study by the Veterans Affairs office that runs REACH VET found that veterans flagged by its suicide-risk model had fewer suicide attempts and admissions and no change in deaths.

Human oversight

Once the model identifies a veteran, a VA mental health or primary care provider reaches out, reviews the condition and treatment plan and decides whether enhanced care is needed. Care evaluations of identified veterans rose from 46.1% to 87.5% between March 2017 and December 2018. By October 2020, 98% of identified veterans were contacted and had their care evaluated. Relation to AI

The predictive model selects the monthly top 0.1% risk tier. Coordinators and providers then re-evaluate care and reach out, so the clinical response, not the model alone, produces any change. The evaluation is observational, uses a triple-difference design to handle non-parallel trends, and is authored by the VA office that built and runs REACH VET. It found no mortality association, replicated in 2025 by the same group. Independent reporting documents that the original model weighted being a white man heavily and omitted military sexual trauma.
