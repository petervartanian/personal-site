Odakyu trains stop when software detects a person left in a crossing

Odakyu's AI crossing image-analysis system reads existing crossing cameras and signals approaching trains to stop when someone is left inside; trains made 17 emergency stops in two months.

What happened

Meitetsu EI Engineer, Toyota Systems and Toho Electric Industries developed an AI crossing image-analysis system. It was trialed from January 2023, with work on detection accuracy at night and in rain. ITmedia's 岡田有花 reported on 22 June 2026 that Odakyu Electric Railway would introduce an “AI踏切画像解析システム” from 24 June 2026. It covers four crossings on its Odawara Line. They are Minami-Shinjuku Nos. 2, 4 and 5 between Minami-Shinjuku and Sangubashi stations, and Mukogaoka-Yuen No. 9 between Mukogaoka-Yuen and Ikuta stations. The AI analyzes footage from the existing safety-confirmation cameras in real time. It identifies and tracks pedestrians, wheelchair users, bicycles and motorcycles. When it detects someone left inside after the barriers come down, it sends a signal through the signaling equipment. That signal applies the brakes of approaching trains and lights the crossing's special signal lamps to alert crews. Both are released automatically once the crossing is clear. Images are used only for hazard detection, and the system does not identify individuals. Analysis data are destroyed within a year. railf.jp carried the same announcement on 25 June 2026. Yomiuri reported on 19 September 2026 that trains made 17 emergency stops on detected anomalies in the system's first two months. The report does not say whether any stop averted an accident. It adds that the transport ministry now pays up to half the installation cost of such systems. Professor Ryo Takagi of Kogakuin University told the paper that such systems are very effective for accident prevention. He also warned that AI does not make crossings fully safe.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Existing crossing cameras supplied live images. The system tracked people, wheelchairs, bicycles, and motorcycles and sent stop signals when a tracked road user remained inside after the barriers lowered.

Autonomy

A detection automatically triggered train-braking signals and warning lamps. Both were automatically released when the crossing cleared.

Workflow

Existing crossing cameras with sufficient coverage of the enclosed area. Integration with signaling equipment able to command train braking. Three years of trials covering night and rain.

Human oversight

The stop signal applies the brakes of approaching trains and lights special signal lamps that alert the crews. Both measures are released automatically once the crossing is clear. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

AI contribution

The AI analyzes footage from the existing crossing cameras and sends the stop signal when it detects someone left inside. Yomiuri reports 17 emergency stops in the first two months but does not say how many came from AI detections or how many were real hazards. Yomiuri also reports that such hazards are usually noticed by the driver's sight or through the crossing's emergency button. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

Reach

No source counts people who were protected. The system covers four crossings on the Odawara Line, and trains made 17 emergency stops in its first two months. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The system started on 24 June 2026. Yomiuri reported on 19 September 2026 that trains had made 17 emergency stops in its first two months. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

People and places

Dependencies

The system reused crossing cameras and connected to railway signaling and special warning lamps. It supplemented existing emergency buttons and, at some crossings, laser obstacle detectors.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Detection accuracy holds in poor weather and lighting. False detections do not stop trains so often that the function is disabled. Signaling interlocks remain in place.
