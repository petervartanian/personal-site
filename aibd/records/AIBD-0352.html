What happened

A multi-center observational study was published in Malaria Journal on 12 April 2022 (Das et al.). That date is used because the field work is dated only to 2018 and 2019. It evaluated the EasyScan GO, a digital microscopy device whose convolutional-neural-network software was developed by Intellectual Ventures' Global Good Fund. Motic built its production prototype. The 11 sites were in 11 countries: Burkina Faso, Kenya, Republic of the Congo, Senegal, South Africa, Uganda, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Nepal, Thailand and Brazil. In total 2,250 Giemsa-stained blood films were read independently by expert microscopists and by the device. Diagnostic sensitivity was 91.1% (95% CI 88.9–92.7) and specificity 75.6% (73.1–78.0); with good-quality slides specificity rose to 85.1%. Species were identified accurately in 93% of P. falciparum and 92% of P. vivax samples.

Parasite density estimates were within 25% of the reference in 23% of slides. The authors conclude the device meets WHO-TDR level 2 criteria for detection and species identification and level 4 for quantification and false-positive rate. They say all parameters were significantly affected by slide quality, and the study did not evaluate performance under routine field operation. The study was funded by the Global Good Fund, which donated the devices; six co-authors were its employees. No patient-management outcome was measured.

What it was built to do. Automated detection, species identification and quantification of malaria parasites in blood films to replace or support expert microscopy. The evaluated benefit (diagnostic accuracy) is that purpose.

The system's part

The machine-learning device produced the readings compared against expert microscopy, so the measured accuracy is the AI's own performance. The evidence is a single peer-reviewed study funded by the maker (Global Good Fund) with maker-employed co-authors who provided inputs to study design and training. The authors state the study did not evaluate performance under routine field operation and that all parameters were significantly affected by slide quality. No independent or outcome-level evidence was found.

Replication

What produced it. EasyScan GO devices donated and set up with manufacturer training at each site. Giemsa-stained thick blood films read in parallel by two expert microscopists as reference. STARD-reported multi-center observational design across 11 countries (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03512678).

What must hold again. Good slide quality, which the authors found significantly affected all performance parameters. Parasitemia above about 200 parasites/uL, below which sensitivity fell to 57%. Thick-film-only software (the tested version did not separate non-falciparum species).
