EasyScan GO reaches 91.1% sensitivity for malaria at 11 sites

Against expert microscopy, the EasyScan GO machine-learning microscope read 2,250 malaria blood films at 11 sites with 91.1% sensitivity and 75.6% specificity; no patient outcome was measured.

The system’s part
The machine-learning device produced the readings compared against expert microscopy, so the measured accuracy is the AI's own performance. The evidence is a single peer-reviewed study funded by the maker (Global Good Fund) with maker-employed co-authors who provided inputs to study design and training. The authors state the study did not evaluate performance under routine field operation and that all parameters were significantly affected by slide quality. No independent or outcome-level evidence was found.
Limits
Specificity of 75.6% and poor parasite quantification limit the benefit implied by high sensitivity. Slide quality changes performance, and routine field use, treatment decisions, patient outcomes, and comparison with actual local practice were not assessed.
Costs and harms
Poor slide quality and low parasite density could undermine results, and imprecise parasite counts limited use for measuring infection intensity. The authors of the London study found that the device labeled every non-falciparum infection as P. Vivax.
Later
Persisted, 10 August 2023. A second evaluation in London, with the same funder and software team, found similar sensitivity and higher specificity. It also found that parasite counts fell short of WHO expert level and that the device reported every non-falciparum infection as P. vivax. No independent field evaluation, WHO prequalification or program deployment was found as of 29 October 2026.

What happened

A multi-center observational study, carried out in 2018 and 2019, evaluated the EasyScan GO, a digital microscopy device whose convolutional-neural-network software was developed by Intellectual Ventures' Global Good Fund. Motic built its production prototype. The 11 sites were in 11 countries: Burkina Faso, Kenya, Republic of the Congo, Senegal, South Africa, Uganda, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Nepal, Thailand and Brazil. In total 2,250 Giemsa-stained blood films were read independently by expert microscopists and by the device. The study was published in Malaria Journal on 12 April 2022 (Das et al.). Diagnostic sensitivity was 91.1% (95% CI 88.9–92.7) and specificity 75.6% (73.1–78.0); with good-quality slides specificity rose to 85.1%. Species were identified accurately in 93% of P. falciparum and 92% of P. vivax samples. Parasite density estimates were within 25% of the reference in 23% of slides. The authors conclude that the device meets WHO-TDR level 2 criteria for detection and species identification and level 4 for quantification and false-positive rate. They say that all parameters were significantly affected by slide quality. They also say that the study did not evaluate performance under routine field operation. The study was funded by the Global Good Fund, which donated the devices, and six co-authors were its employees. No patient-management outcome was measured. In a second evaluation in London, published on 10 August 2023 with the same funder and software team, the authors found that EasyScan GO had 88% sensitivity and 89% specificity against expert microscopy in 1,202 samples from returned travelers. Its authors also report that the device labeled every non-falciparum infection as P. vivax and that its parasite counts fell short of WHO expert level.

Details

AI system

Model

A convolutional neural network that analyzes thick blood films.

Release

Version 1.15.17b.

Role

The network analyzed microscope images for malaria parasites.

Configuration

The deployment used software version 1.15.17b.

Inputs and outputs

The device analyzed Giemsa-stained thick blood films and returned parasite detection, species classifications and density estimates.

Workflow

EasyScan GO devices donated and set up with manufacturer training at each site. Giemsa-stained thick blood films read in parallel by two expert microscopists as reference. STARD-reported multi-center observational design across 11 countries (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03512678).

Human oversight

Staff prepared and loaded the slides. Two qualified microscopists read each slide independently, with additional reads for discrepancies and blinded external quality control. Patients received treatment based on manual microscopy or rapid tests, not on the automated result.

Benefits

Reach

The studies counted samples and no beneficiaries: 2,250 blood films at 11 sites and 1,202 samples from returned travelers in London. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

The authors of a second evaluation in London, published on 10 August 2023, found similar sensitivity and higher specificity. As of 29 October 2026, no source reports an independent field evaluation, WHO prequalification or program deployment, so no lasting benefit in use is documented. [1] [2] [3]

Measurement

Measurement

Giemsa-stained blood films read independently by expert microscopists and by the EasyScan GO; STARD reporting; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03512678. [1]

Setting and sample

2,250 blood films at 11 research sites in 11 countries in Africa, Asia and South America, June 2018 to October 2019. [1]

Measures

Diagnostic sensitivity and specificity for parasite detection; species identification; parasite density within 25% of reference. [1]

Results

Sensitivity 91.1% (95% CI 88.9 to 92.7) and specificity 75.6% (95% CI 73.1 to 78.0); species correct in 93% of P. Falciparum and 92% of P. Vivax samples; density within 25% in 23% of slides. [1]

Additional value from AI

The device's readings are the AI output, so the figures measure the AI's own accuracy against experts; no human-plus-AI arm was tested. [1]

Limitations

All measures depended on slide quality; specificity rose to 85.1% on good slides; sensitivity fell to 57% below about 200 parasites per microliter; no routine field use and no patient outcomes; maker-funded with six maker employees among the authors. [1]

Denominators

Each slide read by the device and by two expert microscopists, with repeat reads on the same or a second device to test reliability; staff trained on device operation at each site at the start of the study. [1]

Materials for replication

Protocol registered as NCT03512678; software version 1.15.17b. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Good slide quality, which the authors found significantly affected all performance parameters. Parasitemia above about 200 parasites/uL, below which sensitivity fell to 57%. Thick-film-only software (the tested version did not separate non-falciparum species).

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2.

Six authors of the 2023 London study work for Global Health Labs, which developed the software, and the Global Good Fund paid for that study. [1] [2] [3]
