Software flags a severe artery blockage that a manual reading underrated

China Medical University Hospital’s Exercise ECG AI-Assisted Interpretation System gave a man a 72 percent chance of severe narrowing, and angiography found a 99 percent stenosis.

  • 3 a person

    A man with a 99 percent stenosis of the left anterior descending artery had it stented after the software flagged a 72 percent probability of severe narrowing. The manual reading of his exercise electrocardiogram had rated his risk moderate and would have left him to drug treatment and follow-up. The cardiologist put his chance of an acute infarction at 8 in 10. He said that the stent removed the risk of sudden death. The man left hospital, and his symptoms resolved. The hospital's headlines call the case life-saving. The harm was prospective, and the only account comes from the hospital that built the model, so a life saved is not established.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The model read the exercise-ECG signals and report features. It returned a 72% probability of severe coronary narrowing, above the moderate-risk verdict of the manual reading and risk score. On that basis the cardiologist ordered angiography, which showed the 99% stenosis, and he stented it. The counterfactual rests on the treating cardiologist's statement, carried by three outlets and the hospital's release. He said the manual reading would have led to drug treatment and follow-up only. The hospital that says so also built the model. The dates of the reading and procedure are not reported. The published model's specificity of 0.60 means such flags are often false positives.
Limits
The 72% figure is a probability of severe narrowing, not a measured percentage of blockage. Angiography established 99% stenosis. The single case and the hospital’s counterfactual statement do not establish deaths prevented, while the later model study reports substantial false-positive risk.

What happened

A 75-year-old man surnamed Chen, with controlled hypertension, woke one day with chest tightness and palpitations that were first taken for reflux. Liberty Times puts the onset in 2021. At China Medical University Hospital in Taichung, his exercise electrocardiogram was read manually and scored with the Duke Treadmill Score. That reading put him at moderate risk with about 50 percent narrowing and called for follow-up only. The hospital's own Exercise ECG AI-Assisted Interpretation System, in use since May 2022, put the probability of severe narrowing (over 70 percent) at 72 percent. Cardiologist 吳宏彬 then ordered angiography. It found a 99 percent stenosis of the left anterior descending artery, which he stented at once. He said that the manual reading would have led to drug treatment and follow-up only. He put the man's chance of an acute infarction at 8 in 10. The man went home, and his chest tightness resolved. The hospital presented the case on 28 December 2022. Test and procedure dates are not reported. The three news reports of 28 December 2022 carry the hospital doctors' account and quote no one outside the hospital.

Details

AI system

Model

A deep-learning model that processes exercise ECG recordings and clinical features.

Role

The model estimated the probability of coronary narrowing from exercise ECGs and clinical features.

Configuration

The model combined exercise ECG recordings from three time points with 12 measured or derived clinical features.

Inputs and outputs

The system analyzed exercise-ECG signals and report features and estimated a 72% probability of severe coronary narrowing.

Workflow

A patient with atypical symptoms has an exercise ECG at a hospital that runs the AI model alongside the manual reading. The model's probability of severe (over 70%) stenosis exceeds what the manual reading and risk score suggest. The cardiologist acts on it by ordering angiography. Angiography confirms a severe stenosis that can be treated at once by stenting.

Human oversight

The cardiologist ordered angiography after considering the higher-risk AI result. Angiography found a 99% LAD narrowing, which he treated with a stent.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The model is run in addition to, not instead of, the clinician's reading. The hospital calls it assisted interpretation and a clinician made every decision. The clinician is willing to send a patient to invasive angiography on an AI probability score. With the published model's specificity of 0.60, some such patients will have no severe lesion. The patient is seen at a hospital that has the system. The sources describe it only at CMUH, in use from May 2022.

Availability

The sources describe use at China Medical University Hospital from May 2022.

Entry details

Sector

Healthcare: cardiology diagnostics (exercise stress ECG). Hospital cardiology; in-house clinical AI.

Purpose

The hospital's AI Center built the system to judge from an exercise electrocardiogram whether a patient's coronary arteries are severely narrowed (over 70%). The hospital says that manual reading of a dozen tracings is slow and only 40–50% accurate. Flagging this patient's severe stenosis falls within that purpose.
