Two legal AI tools improve students' legal work in a trial

In a randomized trial with 137 law students, Vincent AI and o1-preview raised the quality of legal work and raised productivity on five of six tasks by 50% to 130%.

What happened

Daniel Schwarcz and David Cleveland of the University of Minnesota, Sam Manning of GovAI, J.J. Prescott and Patrick Barry of the University of Michigan, and Beverly Rich of the University of Southern California ran a randomized trial with upper-level law students at the Michigan and Minnesota law schools from 1 to 31 October 2024. Of 153 students who enrolled, 137 completed at least one task and 125 completed all six. Each student did six legal tasks, some with Vincent AI, a retrieval-augmented legal research tool from vLex, some with OpenAI's o1-preview reasoning model and some with no AI; random assignment to one of three groups decided which tasks went with which condition. Three of the authors graded the work blind. The authors report that both tools raised the quality of the legal work and that they raised productivity by a statistically significant margin on five of the six tasks, with gains of 50% to 130%. Both tools cut time use by about 20% to 28% on five tasks. The tools did best on complex tasks such as drafting persuasive letters and analyzing complaints. Hallucinated citations were rare: the authors counted 3 in work done with Vincent AI, 4 in work done without AI and 11 in work done with o1-preview. Law firms and the two law schools funded the study, and the authors write that neither OpenAI nor vLex gave financial support, although both gave participants free access. The Journal of Law and Empirical Analysis published the study on 9 April 2026.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

O1-preview; Vincent AI as of 2024

Workflow

Students used either a retrieval-augmented legal tool or a reasoning model on realistic legal tasks. Students received training in using both tools before the tasks. Graders scored the work blind to the condition.

Measurement

Measurement

Graded quality and productivity on six legal tasks. [1]

Setting and sample

153 enrolled upper-level law students at Michigan and Minnesota; 137 completed at least one task, 125 all six. [1]

Measures

Quality grades and productivity. [1]

Results

Significant quality gains for both tools; productivity gains of 50% to 130% on five of six tasks. [1]

Additional value from AI

Productivity up 50% to 130% on five tasks. [1]

Limitations

Students in place of practicing lawyers; designed tasks; graders were co-authors; o1-preview work had more hallucinated citations. [1]

Denominators

Vincent AI or o1-preview during the tasks. [1]

Materials for replication

Task texts in Appendix B of the article. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The tool must ground its analysis in legal sources or reason through the problem before writing. Users must check citations for hallucinations.
