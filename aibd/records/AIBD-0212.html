Delhi students improve their math and Hindi scores

Students offered free access to Mindspark centers gained 0.37 standard deviations in math and 0.23 in Hindi over 4.5 months.

Benefits

Benefit

Students offered a voucher scored higher in math and Hindi after 4.5 months. [1]

Category

Improved learning. [1]

Status

Observed in a randomized evaluation. [1]

Beneficiary groups

Students. [1]

Beneficiaries

Public-school students in Delhi, most of whom were in grades 6–9 and behind their grade’s curriculum. [1]

Reach

The trial enrolled 619 students and offered 314 a voucher. Researchers tested 539 students at the end. [1]

Distribution and access

Gains were similar across initial ability levels. Students who began further behind gained more relative to their previous progress. [1]

Duration

The measured learning gains covered 4.5 months. [1]

Expected?

Improved learning was the intended benefit. [1]

Costs and harms

The centers required staff and equipment. Low enrollment and the loss of subsidies led all three to close. [1]

Outcome identifiers

Not assessed

AI system

System

Mindspark. [1]

Model

Rules written by education specialists selected lessons from students’ answers and error patterns. [1]

Release

The study does not identify a numbered software release. [1]

Provider

Educational Initiatives. [1]

Role

The software assessed each student and selected exercises at the appropriate level. [1]

Configuration

Students used individual computers with headphones for 45 minutes, then spent 45 minutes with an instructor in a small group. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Students’ answers, scores, and errors determined the next exercises and explanations. [1]

Autonomy

The software selected exercises automatically. Instructors could assign particular topics. [1]

Workflow

Each session combined practice on Mindspark with instruction in groups of about 12–15 students. Centers offered six sessions a week. [1]

Human oversight

Education specialists wrote the teaching rules. Trained instructors supervised students and provided group instruction. [1]

Relation to AI

Mindspark used rules for adaptive teaching. It did not use machine learning. [1]

Editor’s note. Treating the system as symbolic AI is an editorial classification of its expert-written rules. The authors expressly state that it did not use machine learning.

AI contribution

The trial evaluated software and instruction together. It did not measure the software’s separate effect. [1]

Measurement

Studies

AIBD-0212-E01 [1]

Study design

A lottery assigned students to a free voucher or a comparison group. Researchers estimated the effect of the offer. [1]

Measurement

Independent paper tests assessed math and Hindi before and after the program. Scores were placed on comparable scales. [1]

Setting and sample

There were 314 voucher recipients and 305 controls. Endline participation was 85% and 90%, respectively. [1]

Measures

Math and Hindi test scores. [1]

Comparison

Students who were not offered a voucher continued their usual schooling and activities. [1]

Results

The voucher offer raised math scores by 0.37 standard deviations and Hindi scores by 0.23. [1]

Additional value from AI

The design did not separate adaptive software from additional teaching and practice. [1]

Limitations

Participants volunteered for the study. Endline attrition differed between groups, and longer-term attainment was not measured. [1]

Denominators

Average attendance was 58%, or about 50 of the 86 available days. [1]

Materials for replication

Replication data are deposited with the American Economic Association at openICPSR. [1]

Money

Kind of value

Program cost and subsidized fee. Separate estimates describe possible costs at scale. [1]

Amount

Delivery cost about INR 1,000 per student per month. The subsidized fee was INR 200, which vouchers waived. [1]

Currency and prices

Indian rupees. The authors gave an approximate equivalent of US$15 for the monthly delivery cost. [1]

Period and unit

Per student per month during the Delhi pilot. [1]

Realization

The INR 1,000 figure describes pilot delivery cost. Lower costs at scale were projections. [1]

Recipients and payers

Families normally paid the subsidized fee. Donors funded the remaining cost, and vouchers covered the fee for selected students. [1]

Gross or net

These are delivery costs and fees, not income or net savings. [1]

Included costs

The delivery estimate included premises, hardware, staff, and a share of software development costs. [1]

Comparison

The authors compared learning and cost with regular public schooling and with separate research on after-school tutoring. [1]

Method

The pilot cost was reported per student. Forecasts for schools used a deployment budget that Educational Initiatives prepared in 2017. [1]

Return and payback

The study does not estimate financial returns or a payback period. [1]

People and places

Participants

Educational Initiatives operated the centers. Academic researchers evaluated the program with J-PAL support. [1]

Jurisdictions

Three centers served low-income neighborhoods in Delhi, India. Students were recruited near five public schools. [1]

Ownership and control

Educational Initiatives developed the commercial product and operated the three centers. [1]

Dependencies

The centers needed computers, headphones, suitable rooms, and instructors. [1]

Cross-border connections

The research involved institutions in several countries. The study does not document a cross-border transfer of students’ data. [1]

Geopolitical implications

Not assessed

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Students need regular access to the software and instructional support. The Delhi program also depended on continued subsidy. [1]

Replication attempts

A separate Rajasthan school trial reported gains of 0.22 standard deviations in math and 0.20 in Hindi after 18 months. [2]

Later findings

The Delhi centers closed in March 2016. Later research evaluated a different school-based delivery model in Rajasthan. [1] [2]

Availability

The three Delhi centers closed in March 2016. Mindspark continued in other settings. [1]

Institutional decision

Donors decided whether to continue subsidizing the centers. [1]

Grounds

Donors required enrollment at or above 80% of capacity. [1]

Action and result

Donors ended the subsidies, and all three centers closed. [1]

Success criterion

At least 80% enrollment at the subsidized fee. [1]
