Mindspark raises Delhi students' math scores by 0.37 SD

off the scale

+0.37 SD math, +0.23 SD Hindi in 4.5 months

Lottery evaluation of Educational Initiatives' Mindspark software plus small-group instruction among 619 Delhi public-school students: +0.37 SD math, +0.23 SD Hindi in 4.5 months.

What happened

Educational Initiatives (EI) ran three after-school Mindspark centers in Delhi serving low-income neighborhoods, where each 90-minute session combined 45 minutes of self-driven work on the Mindspark software, which the J-PAL summary says uses data to identify each student's learning level, deliver content at that level and adjust to progress, with 45 minutes of small-group instruction by a teaching assistant, six days a week. Muralidharan, Singh and Ganimian recruited 619 students from five public middle schools near the centers in September 2015; about half won a lottery for a voucher that waived fees until February 2016, when an endline test was taken. The American Economic Review paper (April 2019) reports that lottery winners scored 0.37 sigma higher in math and 0.23 sigma higher in Hindi over the 4.5-month period, with instrumental-variable estimates of 0.6 and 0.39 sigma for 90 days of attendance; the NBER version states that the intent-to-treat estimates reflect 58% average attendance. J-PAL gives a per-student monthly cost of about INR 1,000 against INR 1,500 in public schools. The date observed is the first day of the recruitment month (September 2015), used as a placeholder; the fetched sources give months, not days.

What it was built to do. Mindspark was built to personalize instruction to each student's measured level; the test-score gains are that purpose.

The system's part

The software delivered half of each session and the other half was small-group instruction by teaching assistants, so the evaluated effect is of the combined program; the papers attribute the gains to precise targeting of instruction to student level.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Software that continuously assessed each student and served content at the measured level rather than the grade level. Ninety-minute sessions six days a week split between software and small-group instruction. A voucher lottery among 619 students from five public middle schools near three centers, with endline testing in February 2016.

What must hold again. Students far below grade level, so targeted content has room to help (the papers report similar absolute gains but larger relative gains for weaker students). Attendance at the centers (ITT estimates reflect 58% average attendance per the NBER version). Instructional support alongside the software.
