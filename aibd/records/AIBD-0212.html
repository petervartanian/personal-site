What happened

Educational Initiatives (EI) ran three after-school Mindspark centers in Delhi serving low-income neighborhoods. Each 90-minute session combined 45 minutes of self-driven Mindspark software work with 45 minutes of small-group instruction by a teaching assistant, six days a week. The J-PAL summary says the software uses data to identify each student's learning level, deliver content at that level and adjust to progress. Muralidharan, Singh and Ganimian recruited 619 students from five public middle schools near the centers in September 2015. About half won a lottery for a voucher that waived fees until February 2016, when an endline test was taken. Over the 4.5-month period, lottery winners scored 0.37 sigma higher in math and 0.23 sigma higher in Hindi (American Economic Review, April 2019). Instrumental-variable estimates are 0.6 and 0.39 sigma for 90 days of attendance.

The NBER version states that the intent-to-treat estimates reflect 58% average attendance. J-PAL gives a per-student monthly cost of about INR 1,000 against INR 1,500 in public schools. The sources give months, not days.

What it was built to do. Mindspark was built to personalize instruction to each student's measured level. The test-score gains are that purpose.

Since then
Scaled on 1 September 2025: A 2025 NBER working paper reports an 18-month experiment of Mindspark adapted into Rajasthan government school schedules, in a sample over 20 times larger, with gains of 0.22 SD in math and 0.20 SD in Hindi, and scale-ups now reaching over 250,000 students.After 18 months, treated students scored 0.22σ higher in Mathematics and 0.20σ higher in Hindi, a 50–66% productivity increase over the control group. Learning gains were proportional to student time on the platform, providing a simple, low-cost metric for monitoring implementation quality in future scale-ups. The adaptation and its experimental validation have informed scale-ups now reaching over 250,000 students. source

The system's part

The software delivered half of each session and the other half was small-group instruction by teaching assistants. So the evaluated effect is of the combined program. The papers attribute the gains to precise targeting of instruction to student level.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. Software that continuously assessed each student and served content at the measured level rather than the grade level. Ninety-minute sessions six days a week split between software and small-group instruction. A voucher lottery among 619 students from five public middle schools near three centers, with endline testing in February 2016.

What must hold again. Students far below grade level, so targeted content has room to help (the papers report similar absolute gains but larger relative gains for weaker students). Attendance at the centers (ITT estimates reflect 58% average attendance per the NBER version). Instructional support alongside the software.
