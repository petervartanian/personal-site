What happened

FDA's update of 22 August 2022 states that its operational imported-seafood model returned sampling recommendations to reviewers at all 328 U.S. ports of entry from February through July 2021. The update says that the pilot proved successful and that the real-time model returned a sample recommendation within seconds. It gives no figure for hazards found. Civil Eats reported on 27 May 2025 that FDA Commissioner Marty Makary, appearing before a Senate committee the previous week, said that the agency was using AI to identify where to concentrate food inspections and that AI can sometimes find patterns faster than people. He added that a human reviews the information. The article notes that FDA had been working for several years on a pilot focused on imported seafood. It also notes that FDA had announced an agency-wide generative AI rollout, and an FDA spokesperson said that all centers would run on a unified platform by 30 June. A 2 June update to the article records the launch of Elsa, FDA's generative AI tool, said to be used in part to identify high-priority inspection targets. Jaydee Hanson of the Center for Food Safety is quoted on the risks of using AI amid staff cuts. FDA's announcement of 2 June 2025 describes Elsa as a large language model tool in a secure GovCloud environment, separate from the seafood model, and says that the agency is already using it to identify high-priority inspection targets. CNN reported on 23 July 2025 that FDA staff said that Elsa makes up studies. Makary told CNN that most FDA scientists used it to find studies and summarize meetings. No source gives a numerical improvement in detection or health outcomes, and no inspection outcome, detection or recall is attributed to the AI.

Details

AI system

Role

The tools prioritized possible food-inspection targets for FDA staff to consider.

Configuration

The imported-seafood pilot and the later Elsa generative tool were distinct initiatives, so the older pilot’s work cannot automatically be attributed to Elsa.

Inputs and outputs

The seafood pilot analyzed import-entry data and returned sampling recommendations to FDA reviewers. Elsa separately helped staff read internal material and identify inspection priorities.

Workflow

An agency-wide generative AI rollout announced in May 2025. A multi-year imported-seafood pilot.

Human oversight

A human reviewed the information before it informed inspection priorities, according to the commissioner.

Relation to AI

FDA’s original reports describe a seafood-import model that returned sampling recommendations to reviewers and Elsa’s separate use in identifying inspection priorities. The seafood pilot operated at all 328 U.S. Ports from February through July 2021; Elsa launched on June 2, 2025 in a secure GovCloud environment. These sources establish the tools’ reported use, but do not quantify improved detection or health outcomes.

AI contribution

FDA reports that its seafood-import model returned sampling recommendations to reviewers at all 328 U.S. Ports from February through July 2021, and that Elsa, launched on 2 June 2025, helped identify inspection targets. No source quantifies improved detection or health outcomes. In July 2025 Makary told CNN that most FDA scientists used Elsa to find studies and summarize meetings. [1] [2] [3] [4]
