What happened

Civil Eats reported on 27 May 2025 that FDA Commissioner Marty Makary, appearing before a Senate committee the previous week, said the agency was using AI to identify where to concentrate food inspections and that AI can sometimes find patterns faster than people, adding that a human reviews the information. The article notes that FDA had been working for several years on a pilot focused on imported seafood and had announced an agency-wide generative AI rollout, with an FDA spokesperson saying all centers would run on a unified platform by 30 June; a 2 June update records the launch of FDA's generative tool Elsa, said to be used in part to identify high-priority inspection targets. Jaydee Hanson of the Center for Food Safety is quoted on the risks of using AI amid staff cuts. No inspection outcome, detection or recall attributable to the AI is given.

FDA's own pilot page was not available, so no official report is among the sources.

What it was built to do. Prioritize food inspection targets, including imported seafood, using pattern recognition over agency data.
