Viz HCM flags hypertrophic cardiomyopathy in a 61-year-old

Viz HCM gave a 61-year-old man's electrocardiogram a probability above 0.95 for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy; imaging confirmed the disease, and mavacamten eased his symptoms by week 8.

What happened

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration granted Viz HCM De Novo classification on 3 August 2023. Its reviewers found that the probable benefits outweigh the probable risks despite a low positive predictive value. A 61-year-old man, unaware of any cardiomyopathy, had lightheadedness, dizziness, fatigue and functional limitation on moderate exertion. He had a baseline electrocardiogram (ECG) at an executive physical at Cleveland Clinic, Cleveland, Ohio (no date given). Clinicians read it as normal sinus rhythm with first-degree atrioventricular block. Under an organization-wide initiative the ECG was uploaded to a cloud platform running the Viz HCM convolutional neural network. The algorithm gave a probability above 0.95 for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, and he was referred to the Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Center. Echocardiography showed 2 cm basal septal hypertrophy, systolic anterior motion and a post-exercise outflow gradient of 72 mm Hg. He started mavacamten 5 mg daily after shared decision-making, and by week 8 the resting gradient was 9 mm Hg. He had less dizziness and dyspnea and no hospitalizations. Cleveland Clinic's hypertrophic cardiomyopathy team reported on 17 March 2025 that the hospital analyzed every ECG for the disease from 19 February 2024 to 1 November 2024. Of the 1,265 patients that the algorithm flagged, 63 received a new diagnosis of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

  2. Regulatory document, by authors with no tie to the system. Context or contrary evidence.

    U.S. Food and Drug Administration, De Novo Classification Request for Viz HCM (DEN230003), decision summary, granted 3 August 2023. FDA reviewers wrote this summary; it establishes that the device flags signs of HCM on 12-lead ECGs with a low positive predictive value, and it says nothing about this patient.

    Due to the low prevalence of the disease, the positive predictive value is low but HCM is hard to diagnose, so the risk of false positives is acceptable given the benefit of identifying HCM in otherwise asymptomatic patients.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Viz HCM analyzed the patient's routine 12-lead ECG and returned a high probability of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.

Workflow

Uploading routine 12-lead ECGs to the Viz HCM cloud platform as an institutional initiative. A referral pathway from AI flag to a specialist HCM center.

Human oversight

Specialists confirmed the diagnosis with cardiac imaging and chose mavacamten through shared decision-making with the patient.

Benefits

Reach

The case report documents one patient. In the hospital program from 19 February 2024 to 1 November 2024, 63 of the 1,265 patients that the algorithm flagged received a new diagnosis of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

The report documents improvement through eight weeks of treatment.

Measurement

Measurement

Prospective analysis of all hospital ECGs with chart review of each flagged patient for confirmed, new or alternative diagnoses. [3]

Setting and sample

Cleveland Clinic, Cleveland, Ohio; 103,492 ECGs in 45,873 patients, 19 February 2024 to 1 November 2024. [3]

Measures

New HCM diagnoses among flagged patients; sensitivity, specificity and positive predictive value at several probability thresholds. [3]

Results

1,265 patients flagged; 511 had known HCM, 63 had a new HCM diagnosis and 691 had another diagnosis; positive predictive value 66% at the 0.95 threshold, with 50% sensitivity. [3]

Limitations

Single center; authors include two consultants for the developer; the abstract reports no patient outcomes after diagnosis. [3]

Denominators

Viz HCM run on every 12-lead ECG. [3]
