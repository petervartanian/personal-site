UCF finds ADS crash odds lower in most scenarios, higher at dusk

off the scale

Compared with human-driven vehicles, the odds of an ADS crash in rain were 0.335 times

Nature Communications (18 Jun 2024): using California DMV, NHTSA Standing General Order and SWITRS data, Abdel-Aty and Ding find ADS crash odds lower in most matched scenarios but 5.25x higher at d...

What happened

Mohamed Abdel-Aty and Shengxuan Ding of the University of Central Florida's Smart and Safe Transportation Lab assembled 2,100 automated-vehicle crash records (1,099 SAE Level 4 ADS and 1,001 Level 2 ADAS) from the California DMV and NHTSA's Standing General Order databases and 35,113 human-driven-vehicle crashes from California's SWITRS, then fitted a matched case-control logistic regression. Compared with human-driven vehicles, the odds of an ADS crash in rain were 0.335 times, and most pre-crash movements reduced crash odds, but the odds of an ADS crash at dawn or dusk were 5.250 times and in turning maneuvers 1.988 times those of a human-driven vehicle. The authors attribute the advantages to object detection, precision control and decision-making and the disadvantages to sensor adaptation to changing light and limited situational awareness. The paper was received 30 Sep 2023, accepted 2 May 2024 and published 18 Jun 2024; the date given is the publication date. The vehicles are not attributed to specific developers in the analysis, so no individual maker is credited. The full text was read through Europe PMC.

What it was built to do. Automated driving systems are built to drive without human intervention; the study measures their crash occurrence against human-driven vehicles.

    The analysis suggests that accidents of vehicles equipped with Advanced Driving Systems generally have a lower chance of occurring than Human-Driven Vehicles in most of the similar accident scenarios.

The system's part

The study compares the conditions under which ADS and human-driven crashes occurred; it does not observe interventions and cannot separate exposure differences from system performance. Independent university authors; regulator databases as sources.

Replication

What produced it. Mandatory crash reporting for automated vehicles to the California DMV and under NHTSA's Standing General Order. A state police-reported crash database (SWITRS) with matching variables for road, environment and pre-crash movement. Matched case-control logistic regression across road, environment and crash type.

What must hold again. Continued regulator collection of ADS and ADAS crash reports. Comparable variable definitions between ADS reports and human crash records. Enough ADS crashes in each condition to estimate odds ratios.
