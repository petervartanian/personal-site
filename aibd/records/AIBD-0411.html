On CT, PANDA detects pancreatic cancers that standard care missed

Alibaba DAMO's PANDA model exceeded mean radiologist sensitivity for pancreatic cancer on CT scans by 34.1% and found five cancers that the initial standard of care had missed.

Brief from Peter

In a study published in Nature Medicine on 20 November 2023, the authors report that PANDA, a deep-learning model, exceeded mean radiologist sensitivity by 34.1% and specificity by 6.3% in identifying pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma on non-contrast CT. In a retrospective test carried out from July to October 2022 on 20,530 patients, PANDA flagged five cancers that the initial standard of care had missed, and one patient then had surgery with curative intent. Eight of the study's authors are employees of Alibaba, whose DAMO Academy developed PANDA.1 In a paper published on 9 October 2026, surgeons in Brussels count PANDA among the more mature evidence for AI in pancreatic imaging. They find that no study shows that the use of PANDA improves stage at diagnosis or survival.2

  1. 1 The surgery with curative intent was for a 1.5 cm pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor. Cao and colleagues, “Large-scale pancreatic cancer detection via non-contrast CT and deep learning,” Nature Medicine, November 20, 2023. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
  2. 2 The authors declare no conflicts of interest. Khan and colleagues, “Artificial Intelligence in Pancreatic Cancer: From Predictive Performance to Clinical Translation,” Cancers, October 9, 2026. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Details

AI system

Role

PANDA uses a three-stage cascade for organ localization, lesion detection, and differentiation of pancreatic lesions.

Inputs and outputs

PANDA analyzes noncontrast CT scans and identifies pancreatic lesions that may include pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

Human oversight

In the real-world test, clinicians confirmed the lesions that PANDA flagged. The curative treatment followed their workup. [1] [2] [3]

Benefits

Reach

In a retrospective test on 20,530 patients, PANDA found five cancers that the initial standard of care had missed. One patient then had surgery with curative intent for a 1.5-centimeter pancreatic neuroendocrine tumor. [1] [2] [3]

Measurement

Measurement

Retrospective diagnostic accuracy study with two reader studies, an external multicenter test and a retrospective real-world test against the clinical standard of truth. [1]

Setting and sample

3,208 training patients at one Shanghai center; a multicenter validation of 6,239 patients from 10 centers, including 5,337 patients from nine external centers in mainland China, Taiwan and the Czech Republic; 20,530 consecutive patients in the real-world test; 33 readers from 12 institutions in the first reader study. [1]

Measures

Sensitivity and specificity for lesion detection and for identifying pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. [1]

Results

Sensitivity 34.1% and specificity 6.3% above the mean radiologist for PDAC; lesion detection sensitivity of 92.9% and specificity of 99.9% in the real-world test. [1]

Additional value from AI

Five cancers and 26 other pancreatic lesions found among 20,530 patients that the initial standard of care had missed. [1]

Limitations

All tests are retrospective, the real-world test took place at one center, and eight authors are Alibaba employees. Follow-up care had already found eight of the 26 missed lesions before the study, and an independent review notes that no study shows a gain in stage, resection or survival. [1]

Denominators

PANDA applied to non-contrast abdominal or chest CT scans. [1]

Materials for replication

Methods and supplementary information; the model and data are not public. [1]
