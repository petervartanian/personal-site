Brief from Peter

Jessica Cicchino of the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety measured what automatic emergency braking that detects pedestrians does for people on foot in the United States. Using police-reported crashes, she compared vehicles of the same models with and without the optional system. She found that vehicles with the system had a 25% to 27% lower pedestrian crash risk and a 29% to 30% lower pedestrian injury crash risk.1 She also reported where she found no evidence of benefit: at night on unlit roads, and at speed limits of 50 mph or more. Nor did she find evidence of benefit when the equipped vehicle was turning. The design is observational, and the abstract names no makers and describes no detection software.2 Later studies from Japan and from Australia and New Zealand also reported benefits. Automatic emergency braking in some Nissan cars has drawn complaints of false braking that endangered occupants and other road users.

  1. 1 Jessica B. Cicchino, “Effects of automatic emergency braking systems on pedestrian crash risk,” Accident Analysis & Prevention, published online May 14, 2022. pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov
  2. 2 Cicchino estimated crash rates per insured vehicle year and checked them with a second method that compares pedestrian crashes with a crash type that the system cannot affect. Cicchino, Accident Analysis & Prevention, May 14, 2022, abstract. pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov

Details

AI system

Workflow

Automakers offered pedestrian-detecting AEB as an option, so equipped and unequipped vehicles of the same models could be compared. The benefit appeared in police-reported crashes in ordinary US driving.

Measurement

Measurement

Pedestrian crash rates per insured vehicle year (Poisson regression) and a quasi-induced exposure comparison with a crash type that the system cannot affect. [1]

Setting and sample

US police-reported pedestrian crashes involving vehicle models on which pedestrian-detecting AEB was optional. [1]

Measures

Pedestrian crash risk and pedestrian injury crash risk. [1]

Results

25% to 27% lower pedestrian crash risk; 29% to 30% lower pedestrian injury crash risk. [1]

Additional value from AI

25% to 27% lower crash risk attributed to the system. [1]

Limitations

Observational; no evidence of benefit at night on unlit roads, at 50 mph or higher speed limits, or in turns; makers and software not named in the abstract. [1]

Denominators

Vehicles fitted with pedestrian-detecting AEB. [1]

Materials for replication

Police crash data and insured vehicle year data; code not public. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The system must detect the pedestrian in time to warn or brake. The study found no benefit on unlit roads at night, at speed limits of 50 mph or more, or in turns, so the benefit depends on the crash setting.
