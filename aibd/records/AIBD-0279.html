FamilySearch and Transkribus make handwritten records searchable

FamilySearch Full-Text Search converts handwritten records into searchable text, and a genealogist used it to help transcribe a 1761 deed; Transkribus claims 200 million pages deciphered.

  4. Preprint Independent author

    Humphries M, Leddy LC, Downton Q, Legace M, McConnell J, Murray I, Spence E. Unlocking the Archives: Large Language Models Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance on the Transcription of Handwritten Historical Documents. arXiv 2411.03340, 2 November 2024; published as "Unlocking the archives: Using large language models to transcribe handwritten historical documents" in Historical Methods 58(3):175-193, 26 May 2025 (doi:10.1080/01615440.2025.2500309). The authors are historians at Wilfrid Laurier University, funded by the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada, and they declare no competing interests; they built a competing open-source tool that uses commercial LLMs. They found that the two Transkribus models read 90 to 92% of characters and 73 to 80% of words correctly on 18th and 19th century English handwriting, and that LLMs did better.

    This means that on the task of achieving a perfect transcription, the Transkribus models correctly transcribed between 90 and 92% of the characters and 73 and 80% of the words in the ground truth document.
