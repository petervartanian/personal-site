FamilySearch and Transkribus make handwritten records searchable

200 million pages deciphered

FamilySearch introduced AI handwriting-recognition full-text search via FamilySearch Labs in 2024; Transkribus (READ-COOP) reports 200+ million pages deciphered. Makers' accounts only; no finding.

What happened

The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints newsroom (2 January 2025), reviewing FamilySearch's 2024, states that FamilySearch 'can convert an image of text into a machine-readable text format, allowing users to search the full text of the records' using handwriting recognition and OCR, that Full-Text Search was introduced through FamilySearch Labs in 2024, and that 2.5 billion searchable records and images were added overall; it gives no example of a user discovery. Transkribus, developed by the European cooperative READ-COOP SCE, says on its site that it 'turns centuries of handwriting into text you can search, study, and share', claims more than 500,000 users and 200 million pages deciphered, and cites projects such as the University of Georgia transcribing 20,000 pages in two months and Archion's portal of more than 200,000 German church books. The earlier verifier found no independent source documenting a specific discovery, reunion or entitlement from either system; FamilySearch's help article on full-text search returned HTTP 403 here. The date is the newsroom article date; the feature launched in 2024.

What it was built to do. Make handwritten historical records searchable by converting images to text with AI handwriting recognition

The system's part

Handwriting-recognition models produce the searchable text in both systems; the only sources fetched are the makers' own (the Church newsroom for FamilySearch, the Transkribus site), which give capability and volume claims but no specific verified finding.
