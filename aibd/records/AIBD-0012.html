FRNSW drone AI finds two lost hikers in under five hours

depth 3 for a person, depth 2 for an organization

located less than five hours after the report

23 Jun 2026: FRNSW drone AI flagged two lost hikers 500 m off track in Kosciuszko NP; found in under five hours, mild exposure

What happened

On Tuesday 23 June 2026 two men in their early 20s failed to reach a rendezvous on the Dead Horse Gap walking track in Kosciuszko National Park, about 35 km south-west of Jindabyne, and were reported overdue at about 7 pm. NSW Police formed an alpine search team with the State Emergency Service and asked Fire and Rescue NSW for drone support; FRNSW launched a remotely piloted aircraft with thermal imaging and AI detection software. The software flagged the pair in bushland about 500 m off the track, the thermal camera confirmed the find, and the drone's spotlight directed ground crews to them. They were located less than five hours after the report, had mild effects of exposure and declined treatment. FRNSW Inspector Phillip Eberle said the technology turned a potential long-term incident into a short-term one.

What it was built to do. Built to detect people, vehicles and vessels in drone imagery for search and rescue; this benefit is that purpose.

The system's part

The AI flagged the hikers in the drone feed and the thermal camera confirmed the find; the inspector overseeing the search credits it with cutting the search short. The hikers also signaled with a phone light, and the software vendor is not named. FRNSW's own media release returned HTTP 403 when fetched and is not cited; no cited source dates it.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Thermal-capable drone with onboard AI person detection launched at night from the search command post. Subjects within drone range whose detection can be confirmed by thermal camera.

What must hold again. Weather permits flight. Detections are verified by the pilot before ground teams are directed.
