FRNSW drone AI finds two lost hikers in under five hours

23 June 2026: FRNSW drone AI flagged two lost hikers 500 m off track in Kosciuszko NP; found in under five hours, mild exposure

ⓘ

What happened

On Tuesday 23 June 2026 two men in their early 20s failed to reach a rendezvous on the Dead Horse Gap walking track. The track is in Kosciuszko National Park, about 35 km south-west of Jindabyne. They were reported overdue at about 7 pm. NSW Police formed an alpine search team with the State Emergency Service and asked Fire and Rescue NSW for drone support. FRNSW launched a remotely piloted aircraft with thermal imaging and AI detection software. The software flagged the pair in bushland about 500 m off the track, and the thermal camera confirmed the find. The drone's spotlight directed ground crews to them. They were located less than five hours after the report, had mild effects of exposure and declined treatment. FRNSW Inspector Phillip Eberle said the technology turned a potential long-term incident into a short-term one.

Purpose. Built to detect people, vehicles and vessels in drone imagery for search and rescue; this benefit is that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The AI flagged the hikers in the drone feed, and the thermal camera confirmed the find. The inspector overseeing the search credits it with cutting the search short. The hikers also signaled with a phone light, and the software vendor is not named. FRNSW's own media release was not available and is not cited. No cited source dates it.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Two young men on a winter night in alpine bushland were found with mild exposure within five hours. The inspector overseeing the search says it could otherwise have become a long-term incident.

  • an organization

    FRNSW, police and SES avoided an overnight multi-agency search and the risk to rescuers that Inspector Eberle described.
