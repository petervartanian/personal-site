Drone software detects two lost hikers, who are found within five hours

On a Fire and Rescue NSW drone, AI software from a vendor that no source names flagged two lost hikers in Kosciuszko National Park, and they were located within five hours.

  • 3 a person

    Two young men lost on a winter night in alpine bushland were found with mild exposure within five hours. The inspector overseeing the search said that it could otherwise have become a long-term incident.

  • 2 an organization

    Fire and Rescue NSW, the police and the State Emergency Service avoided an overnight search by several agencies and the risk to rescuers that Inspector Eberle described.

What happened

On Tuesday 23 June 2026 two men in their early 20s failed to reach a rendezvous on the Dead Horse Gap walking track. The track is in Kosciuszko National Park, about 35 km south-west of Jindabyne. They were reported overdue at about 7 pm. NSW Police formed an alpine search team with the State Emergency Service and asked Fire and Rescue NSW for drone support. Fire and Rescue NSW launched a remotely piloted aircraft with thermal imaging and AI detection software. The software flagged the pair in bushland about 500 m off the track, and the thermal camera confirmed the find. The drone's spotlight directed ground crews to them. They were located less than five hours after the report, had mild effects of exposure and declined treatment. Inspector Phillip Eberle of Fire and Rescue NSW later told About Regional that rescuers took about four hours to reach the pair after the find and guided them to safety around midnight. Fire and Rescue NSW described the rescue in a media release on 25 June 2026. On 27 June 2026 ABC News reported that Inspector Eberle said that the technology turned a potential long-term incident into a short-term one. ABC also reported that Inspector Eberle said that the technology probably reduced the search by several days. Fire and Rescue NSW chief remote pilot Inspector Russell Turner told ABC News that the drones were part of a statewide technology upgrade introduced over the previous 18 months. ABC reports that this was the first time that Fire and Rescue NSW had used the drone's AI detection to find a missing hiker.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The drone software marked possible people in the image stream. Thermal imaging and the hikers' phone light helped confirm the location before ground teams were directed there.

Workflow

Thermal-capable drone with onboard AI person detection launched at night from the search command post. Subjects within drone range whose detection can be confirmed by thermal camera.

Human oversight

A trained operator flew the grid search, checked the detection, and used the spotlight to guide police and SES rescuers to the hikers.

AI contribution

The software flagged the hikers in the drone feed, and the thermal camera confirmed the find. Inspector Eberle credits the technology with cutting the search short. The Fire and Rescue NSW release of 25 June 2026 records that the hikers also signaled with a phone light. No source names the software vendor. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Reach

Two hikers benefited in this case. ABC reports that this was the first time that Fire and Rescue NSW had used the drone's AI detection to find a missing hiker. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The rescue was a single event on 23 June 2026, and the hikers declined treatment for mild exposure. The cited sources report no later development. [1] [2] [3] [4]

People and places

Dependencies

Replication needs flyable weather, a trained drone operator, useful visual or thermal coverage, and ground rescuers who can reach the confirmed location.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Weather permits flight. Detections are verified by the pilot before ground teams are directed.
