No-show predictions steered reminder calls that cut missed visits

At MetroHealth in Cleveland, reminder calls to patients flagged by an Epic no-show model cut no-shows from 36% to 33%, and from 42% to 36% for Black patients, in a 2022 randomized initiative.

What happened

MetroHealth, a safety-net health system in Cleveland, Ohio, ran a randomized quality improvement initiative in an internal medicine primary care clinic from 1 January to 24 August 2022. An Epic random forest model, retrained on local data, predicted the risk that a patient would miss an in-person appointment. For appointments with a predicted risk of 15% or higher, schedulers placed live reminder calls in the augmented arm on top of standard automated reminders. Among 5,840 high-risk appointments, the no-show rate was 33% with calls and 36% without. For Black patients the rate fell from 42% to 36%, while white non-Hispanic patients showed no reduction. The team checked the model's calibration in Black and white patients before use and left race out of its inputs. The initiative covered one clinic, and the calls themselves were the intervention that the model directed.

Purpose

The model was built to predict appointment no-shows so that staff can target outreach. Fewer missed visits is that purpose. The initiative aimed to cut no-shows without widening racial and ethnic gaps in attendance.
