UCSF neuroprosthesis lets Pancho produce sentences at 15.2 wpm

128-electrode ECoG and deep-learning decoder let a man mute since 2003 produce sentences at 15.2 wpm from a 50-word vocabulary

ⓘ

What happened

At the University of California, San Francisco, a 36-year-old man received a 128-electrode subdural electrocorticography array over sensorimotor cortex in the BRAVO trial (NCT03698149). He had anarthria and severe spastic quadriparesis from an extensive pontine stroke at age 20 and is known in press by the pseudonym Pancho. Deep-learning models detected his attempted speech and classified words from a 50-word vocabulary, with a natural-language model and Viterbi decoder assembling sentences. He produced sentences at a median 15.2 words per minute with a median word error rate of 25.6%. He previously typed about five words per minute with a head-controlled pointer (Moses et al., NEJM, 15 July 2021). The paper dates implantation to February 2019 and the start of data collection to April 2019. There were 50 sessions over 81 weeks at the participant's residence or a nearby office.

The session on which the reported sentence decoding was achieved is not dated. So the start of data collection (1 April 2019) is used as the observation date.

Purpose. Built to decode attempted speech into words and sentences for a person with anarthria. Restoring word-level communication was the intended benefit and is what occurred.

AI’s contribution

Deep-learning models detected attempted speech in 98% of attempts and classified words. A language model with Viterbi decoding assembled sentences. The output depended entirely on these models. Funding included a research contract under Facebook's Sponsored Academic Research Agreement. The paper states that no study hardware or data were transferred to any sponsor and that author disclosure forms are available at NEJM.org.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Material benefit to one identifiable person: first decoding of his own full words, three times faster than his pointer typing. But limited to a 50-word vocabulary in research sessions.

Further evidence

Setting. Preserved speech-motor cortical pathways more than 15 years after loss of speech. High-density ECoG array over sensorimotor cortex. Deep-learning models trained on 22 hours of attempted-speech data plus language-model decoding.

Conditions. Participant can attempt speech and retains intact cognition. Implant remains stable with adequate signal quality. Accumulated training data maintained over time.

    System version: ECoG with deep-learning and bilingual natural-language models
