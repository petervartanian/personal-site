Bone-anchored Mia Hand cuts Karin's phantom pain from 5 to 3

Swedish amputee Karin uses a bone-anchored, nerve-linked Mia Hand daily; phantom pain fell 5 to 3, stump pain gone (Sci. Robotics 2023).

ⓘ

What happened

Karin, a Swedish woman born in 1973, lost her right hand below the elbow in a farming accident. She received a neuromusculoskeletal prosthesis at Sahlgrenska University Hospital. It comprises titanium implants in the radius and ulna and severed nerves transferred to muscle grafts. Implanted electrodes feed an embedded controller and a Prensilia Mia Hand. The study ran from September 2018 to April 2021, and the EU DeTOP consortium announced the implantation on 5 February 2019. The case report (Science Robotics, 11 October 2023) states her phantom limb pain fell from 5 to 3 and stump pain vanished. Quality of life rose, and she had used the hand in daily life for more than three years and continues to.

Purpose. The prosthesis was built to give reliable control, feedback and attachment after amputation, with surgical reconstruction to add myoelectric sites and treat neuropathic pain. The reported function and pain outcomes were that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The benefit comes from the whole neuromusculoskeletal interface, not from a learned model alone. The paper reports that in daily life the hand ran on direct control. Signals from two implanted electrodes mapped to open and close, and a sustained open signal switched grasps. Decoding of six movements (100 percent completion) and of five individual fingers (up to 95 percent) was shown in laboratory Motion Tests. The paper never uses the words AI or machine learning. The AI framing comes from the institutions' release (Brånemark: osseointegration, reconstructive surgery, implanted electrodes “and AI”). It also comes from CNN (“AI algorithms inside the prosthesis interpret the signal”). Keep the record only if that framing is acceptable as the AI claim.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    The paper reports phantom limb pain down from 5 to 3, stump pain from 6 to 0 and sleep interference gone. EQ-5D-5L is up from 0.23 to 0.63 and daily use tops three years. Karin says the research “has given me a better life” and that she needs much less medication. Life-changing for one identifiable person, from a single-patient study without a control group.

Further evidence

Setting. Osseointegrated titanium implants giving skeletal attachment and a wired path from implanted electrodes to the hand. Surgical reconstruction that transfers severed nerves to free muscle grafts, adding myoelectric sites and treating neuropathic pain. A self-contained prosthesis with an embedded controller and battery that needs no external equipment, so it can be worn all day.

Conditions. The fixtures must osseointegrate. Here the radial fixture failed, was removed after 5 months and reimplanted with a larger diameter. Implanted electrodes must stay within working range for years. One e-abutment screw broke after 3.5 years and was replaced. Outcomes are from one patient with no control group, so the effect size is not established beyond this case.
