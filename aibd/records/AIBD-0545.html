Copilot saves Buckinghamshire staff time and helps a deaf colleague

Buckinghamshire Council reports that Microsoft Copilot cut call wrap-up by an average of two minutes a call and helped a deaf colleague take part in meetings more independently.

  • 1 an organization

    The council reports less time spent on call wrap-up and executive-assistant tasks.

  • 2 a person

    A deaf colleague can follow speakers, review discussions, and check emails more independently. The duration of that improvement is unknown.

What happened

Buckinghamshire Council gained access to Copilot in October 2023 and measured its first savings in that pilot. In an account published on 9 January 2025, the council reports that Copilot saves two minutes per customer-service call and 60–90 minutes daily for a monitored group of six executive assistants. A deaf colleague reportedly participates in meetings and reviews conversations with less assistance. The source does not establish whether that gain persisted. The council also reports hallucinations and inconsistencies in other uses. In October 2025 the Bucks Herald quoted a council report that said that Copilot minute-taking trials were saving 30 minutes per meeting. No outside body has evaluated the Buckinghamshire results.

Details

AI system

Role

Copilot’s transcripts help the deaf colleague identify speakers, revisit action items and unresolved issues, and check meeting information independently.

Workflow

The early-adopter rollout provided 300 licenses across departments, with staff support and monitoring of selected tasks. The accessibility account concerns a specific colleague using transcription to identify speakers, review discussions, and track actions.

Human oversight

The council assigns final decisions and accountability to people, with an AI governance board overseeing risks and deployment policies. [1]
