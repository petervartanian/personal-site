Taoyuan's AI sound cameras pinpoint a noisy BMW within a day

Taoyuan's AI sound cameras pinpointed a white BMW noise car the day after residents complained; officers caught it on 23 April 2025.

What happened

Residents of Bade District, Taoyuan, complained in the Facebook group 記憶八德 that a white BMW with a loud exhaust kept roaring down Jieshou Road late at night. The Taoyuan City Department of Environmental Protection received the complaint on the night of 22 April 2025. Its Quiet Taoyuan (靜桃) task force immediately set up AI sound camera equipment at the relevant spots and, by analyzing the noise data against the complaints, worked out where and when the car appeared. On 23 April environmental and police officers stopped it near Daxing Road. The car was referred to the motor vehicle office for a modification review, with a Noise Control Act fine of up to NT$3,600 and an improvement order to follow.

What it was built to do. The bureau places mobile AI sound cameras on main roads and residential streets to collect noise data that, combined with complaint data, gives the time and place of illegally modified noisy vehicles; locating this car was that purpose.

Who benefited

  • -2
    personal

    Residents on Jieshou Road, who had written that the car's late-night passes seriously affected their quality of life and sleep, saw it identified and referred for a modification review and fine within a day; the reports do not document whether the noise ceased afterwards.

  • -1
    organizational

    The bureau closed one complaint in 24 hours and used the case to publicize its sound-camera enforcement; its claim that the cameras 'greatly raise the success rate' of tracking noise vehicles comes without figures.

The system's part

The bureau's own description is the only account of the system's contribution: the task force placed AI sound camera equipment at the relevant locations on the night of the complaint, and analysis of the noise data with the complaint reports gave the car's haunts and times, after which environmental and police officers found it near Daxing Road on 23 April. The sources do not say how the cameras tie noise to a vehicle, report no decibel reading, and give no counterfactual for how long a conventional patrol would have taken; the stop, the referral and the pending penalty were the officers' work.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • A complaint that named the road and the time window (late night on Jieshou Road), which told the task force where to place the cameras the same night.
  • Mobile AI sound cameras already in the bureau's inventory under the Quiet Taoyuan project, so deployment did not wait on procurement.
  • A standing environmental-police joint enforcement arrangement that could put officers at the predicted place and time on 23 April.
What must hold again
  • The target must be a repeat offender with a pattern: the method matches noise data against complaint times and places, and cannot catch a one-off pass.
  • Officers must be free to intercept on the night the analysis points to; the cameras locate, they do not stop.
  • A legal basis for the follow-up: here the Noise Control Act (fine up to NT$3,600 and an improvement order) and the Road Traffic Management and Penalty Act (up to NT$24,000 for a removed muffler).
