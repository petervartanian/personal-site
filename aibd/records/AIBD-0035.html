Reykjavik University ASR drafts Althingi speech transcripts

Reykjavik University's ASR has produced Althingi's first-draft speech transcripts in production since January 2019 (Interspeech 2019).

ⓘ

What happened

Alþingi, the Icelandic parliament, has collaborated with Reykjavik University since 2016 to replace the first, manual stage of transcribing members' speeches with automatic speech recognition. Helgadóttir and colleagues' Interspeech 2019 paper states: “The system entered a test phase in October 2018, and has been in production since January 2019.” The production system used a time-delay deep neural network acoustic model with an n-gram language model. Word error rate was 8.52% on the test set and 15.0 ± 6.0% over 625 speeches in the wild. Althingi's transcribers graded 77% of transcripts as Good. Morgunblaðið reported on 16 November 2019 that about 640 hours of speeches had been transcribed.

Purpose. Built specifically to transcribe Althingi speeches for post-editing and publication. This benefit was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The ASR produces the first-draft transcript. Althingi's editors still post-edit, so the benefit is editing time saved rather than full automation.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    Althingi replaced first-stage manual transcription with ASR drafts graded Good for 77% of speeches, with about 640 hours transcribed by November 2019.

  • society

    Vísir reported on 4 March 2018 that the recognizer was released under an open-source license for Icelandic-language developers. A small, diffuse benefit.

Further evidence

Setting. About 6,300 hours of aligned parliamentary speech, of which a re-cleaned 1,000-hour subset trained the production recognizer (Interspeech 2019). The 542 hours of 2005–2016 speeches released as LDC2021S01 are a dataset publication, not the production training set. An existing manual transcription and editing workflow to slot into. Integration with Althingi's publishing systems from October 2018.

Conditions. Editors accept ASR drafts (77% graded Good). Continued model maintenance by the developing lab.
