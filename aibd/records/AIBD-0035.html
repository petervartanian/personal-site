Speech recognizer drafts transcripts of speeches in Iceland's parliament

A speech recognizer built at Reykjavik University has drafted transcripts of speeches in Althingi, the Icelandic parliament, since January 2019, and transcribers graded 77% of transcripts as Good.

  • 2 an organization

    Althingi replaced the first, manual stage of transcription with automatic drafts. Its transcribers graded 77% of the transcripts as Good, and about 640 hours of speeches had been transcribed by November 2019.

  • Not rated society

    Vísir reported on 4 March 2018 that the recognizer was released under an open-source license, and no source reports that anyone outside Althingi used it or that the public gained a measured result from it.

What happened

Alþingi, the Icelandic parliament, has collaborated with Reykjavik University since 2016 to replace the first, manual stage of transcribing members' speeches with automatic speech recognition. Vísir reported on 4 March 2018 that the recognizer was released under an open-source license for Icelandic-language developers. In a three-day test reported in July 2018, the developers found that editors then took longer to edit the automatic drafts than drafts from the contracted manual service. Helgadóttir and colleagues' Interspeech 2019 paper states: “The system entered a test phase in October 2018, and has been in production since January 2019.” The production system used a time-delay deep neural network acoustic model with an n-gram language model. Word error rate was 8.52% on the test set and 15.0 ± 6.0% over 625 speeches in the wild. Althingi's transcribers graded 77% of transcripts as Good. The authors report that the editors at Althingi noted that the transcripts are now available sooner than before. Morgunblaðið reported on 16 November 2019 that about 640 hours of speeches had been transcribed. On the same day, Jón Guðnason of Reykjavik University formally handed the recognizer over to Althingi's secretary general, Ragna Árnadóttir.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The recognizer converted recorded Icelandic parliamentary speech into draft text using a time-delay neural acoustic model and an n-gram language model.

Workflow

About 6,300 hours of aligned parliamentary speech, of which a re-cleaned 1,000-hour subset trained the production recognizer (Interspeech 2019). The 542 hours of 2005–2016 speeches released as LDC2021S01 are a dataset publication, not the production training set. An existing manual transcription and editing workflow to slot into. Integration with Althingi's publishing systems from October 2018.

Human oversight

Alþingi's transcribers edited the generated drafts before publication and assessed whether the drafts were useful.

Benefits

Reach

The beneficiary is Althingi with its transcribers and editors, and no source counts the staff. By November 2019 the recognizer had transcribed about 640 hours of speeches. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The recognizer entered a test phase in October 2018, and the authors reported in 2019 that it had been in production since January 2019. By 16 November 2019 it had transcribed about 640 hours of speeches, and no later report on its use is cited. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Editors timed their editing of each speech; word edit distance between drafts and published text; editors graded the drafts.

Setting and sample

239 speeches (11 hours 33 minutes) delivered at Althingi over three days and transcribed both by the contracted manual service and by the recognizer.

Measures

Edit time per word and editing real-time factor.

Results

Edit time per word 1.52 ± 0.53 seconds for automatic drafts against 1.32 ± 0.51 for manual drafts; real-time factor 3.26 ± 1.24 against 2.66 ± 1.05.

Additional value from AI

Negative at the time of the test: the automatic drafts added about 0.2 seconds of editing per word.

Limitations

Three days of speeches; the developers ran the study on an early version; the manual reference texts were not fully true to the audio.

People and places

Dependencies

A virtual server received speech audio and metadata from Alþingi’s systems, created a draft, and sent it to Documentum for manual editing before publication.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Editors accept ASR drafts (77% graded Good). Continued model maintenance by the developing lab.
