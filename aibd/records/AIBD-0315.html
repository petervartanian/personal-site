HALO's AI may spot mines in Ukraine with about 70% accuracy

HALO announced a US$4m AWS package (25 June 2024) to trial AI on drone imagery of Ukrainian minefields; Reuters (30 April 2026) reports about 70% accuracy; no cleared land or found mine attributed.

ⓘ

What happened

The HALO Trust announced a US$4 million package of support from Amazon Web Services on 25 June 2024. An archived copy of the page, dated 20 May 2026, is among the sources. It “will enable HALO to trial artificial intelligence (AI) to assist in the process of detecting debris of war (including landmines and other explosives) in drone imagery for existing minefields and battlefields in Ukraine”. It also covers machine-learning detection of damaged buildings in satellite imagery with SageMaker. By then HALO had flown 542 drone flights over Ukrainian minefields, producing 11 terabytes of imagery. It said training would require manually labeling thousands of images and ongoing vetting by HALO staff to remove false positives and negatives. A HALO page of 21 February 2025 describes the pilot through its head of research and development, Matthew Abercrombie.

He says an analyst “can take three to five days to look through the imagery for an average minefield”. It reports no results. Reuters (Sergiy Karazy, 30 April 2026, syndicated by KELO) reported from Myrotske, 40 km north-west of Kyiv. HALO “has turned to AI to analyze high-resolution drone imagery of contaminated areas and train systems to identify mines and explosive remnants, achieving around 70% accuracy already”. It quoted HALO media manager Olena Shustova. The article does not mention AWS and attributes no cleared land, found mine or time saving to the model. No later milestone is dated.

Purpose. Built to flag landmines and other debris of war in drone imagery so that HALO analysts spend less time searching thousands of images per minefield. The pilot is that intended use.

AI’s contribution

The model is being trained to detect explosive remnants in drone imagery with HALO analysts labeling and vetting outputs. The only performance figure, around 70% accuracy, is reported by Reuters without its basis. The organization's own June 2024 and February 2025 pages report plans and workflow, not results. No independent evaluation, cleared area or detected-mine count is documented.

Further evidence

Setting. High-resolution drone imagery of known minefields (542 flights, 11 terabytes by June 2024) manually labeled by HALO staff. AWS cloud credits, storage and SageMaker tooling to train and host the models.

Conditions. Trained HALO personnel vet model outputs to remove false positives and negatives before survey decisions. Imagery resolution and ground conditions resemble the training minefields.
