HALO's AI flags mines in Ukrainian drone imagery with about 70% accuracy

The HALO Trust's AI detects mines in drone imagery of Ukrainian minefields with about 70% accuracy, and HALO says that it cuts analysis from days to hours; no cleared land is attributed to it.

  • 1 an organization

    HALO states that its AI models analyze terrain in hours where manual assessment took days. That is a time saving for HALO's analysts, and HALO gives no measured figure.

  • Not rated society

    No cited source links the AI analysis to any cleared hectare, found mine or avoided casualty in Ukraine.

What happened

The HALO Trust announced a US$4 million package of support from Amazon Web Services on 25 June 2024. An archived copy of the page, dated 20 May 2026, is among the sources. The package “will enable HALO to trial artificial intelligence (AI) to assist in the process of detecting debris of war (including landmines and other explosives) in drone imagery for existing minefields and battlefields in Ukraine”. It also covers machine-learning detection of damaged buildings in satellite imagery with SageMaker. By then HALO had flown 542 drone flights over Ukrainian minefields, producing 11 terabytes of imagery. HALO said that training would require manually labeling thousands of images and ongoing vetting by HALO staff to remove false positives and negatives. A HALO page of 21 February 2025 describes the pilot through its head of research and development, Matthew Abercrombie. He says that an analyst “can take three to five days to look through the imagery for an average minefield”. The page reports no results. On 24 April 2026 HALO wrote that Prince Harry had flown an AI-powered drone at one of its sites near Bucha. HALO said that its AI models now analyze terrain in hours where manual assessments took days, and it gave no measured figure. Reuters (Sergiy Karazy, 30 April 2026, syndicated by KELO) reported from Myrotske, 40 km northwest of Kyiv. It reported that HALO “has turned to AI to analyse high-resolution drone imagery of contaminated areas and train systems to identify mines and explosive remnants, achieving around 70% accuracy already”. It quoted HALO media manager Olena Shustova. The article does not mention Amazon Web Services, and it attributes no cleared land, found mine or time saving to the model. No later milestone is dated.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

High-resolution drone images were analyzed for possible mines and other explosive remnants, with staff labeling training examples and checking outputs.

Workflow

High-resolution drone imagery of known minefields (542 flights, 11 terabytes by June 2024) manually labeled by HALO staff. AWS cloud credits, storage and SageMaker tooling to train and host the models.

Human oversight

Trained HALO personnel had to inspect model results and resolve false positives and missed objects before using them in survey decisions.

Benefits

Duration

HALO wrote on 24 April 2026 that its AI models analyze terrain in hours, and Reuters reported on 30 April 2026 that the model had reached about 70% accuracy. No later milestone is dated, so the persistence of either result is not established. [1] [2] [3] [4]

People and places

Dependencies

The pilot required drone imagery, labeled examples, AWS resources and experienced explosive-ordnance survey staff.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Trained HALO personnel vet model outputs to remove false positives and negatives before survey decisions. Imagery resolution and ground conditions resemble the training minefields.
