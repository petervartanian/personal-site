Embrace wristband alerts a mother, and she calls paramedics

The system’s part
Per IEEE Spectrum, the Embrace detected the seizure and its app alerted McGrath's mother, who made the emergency call. The device did not call for help itself. The article calls the algorithm proprietary. The FDA summary describes it as an on-board algorithm validated against video-EEG. Empatica's own 2021 study calls it a predefined machine-learning algorithm. The McGrath event is known only from this single news item. The AES case-study abstract is not among the sources.
Limits
The rescue rests on a single news account, and no clinical record of the event is among the sources. The Empatica case-study abstract is also not among the sources. The same article describes a fatal case despite a successful alert, which shows that a timely human response is a separate requirement.
Costs and harms
False alarms and broken connectivity could reduce the value of monitoring, and seizures without convulsions fell outside the demonstrated detection capability.

Brief from Peter

In November 2016 Morgan McGrath, a 26-year-old teacher from Fulton, Missouri, had an epileptic seizure that left her unresponsive and not breathing.1 Her Embrace wristband detected the seizure and alerted her mother, who called paramedics. McGrath survived, and her rescue rests on a single IEEE Spectrum report. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration cleared the device on 26 January 2018, after the event.2 Later evidence comes from hospital epilepsy monitoring units. In an independent study published on 7 January 2026, researchers in Montreal found that the Embrace2 detected all 16 convulsive seizures that occurred while it was worn and working, with 29 false alarms in 373 days of monitoring. It detected none of 510 focal seizures without convulsions, a type that the device is not cleared to detect.3

  1. 1 The article places the seizure on a day in November 2016, fifteen months before its publication, and does not state which generation of Embrace she wore. Elie Dolgin, IEEE Spectrum, February 6, 2018. spectrum.ieee.org
  2. 2 U.S. Food and Drug Administration, 510(k) Summary K172935, Embrace, Empatica Srl, decision of January 26, 2018. accessdata.fda.gov
  3. 3 Alerts reached caregivers in 15 of the 16 cases, and the one failure came from a Wi-Fi disconnection. Details

AI system

Role

The device classified sensor signals for possible seizures and sent alerts through its phone app.

Configuration

The initial report concerned the Embrace device. Later reports concerned Embrace2.

Inputs and outputs

The wristband measured skin conductance and movement, classified a possible convulsive seizure, and passed an alert to the phone app for caregiver calls and texts.

Workflow

Embrace alerted McGrath’s mother, who called paramedics. The emergency response completed the chain from detection to assistance.

Human oversight

The device alerted McGrath's mother, and she called the paramedics; the device did not call for help itself. In the case of a 20-year-old reported in the same article, the alert reached his family, but nobody arrived for 15 minutes and he died. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Morgan McGrath was the reported beneficiary of this emergency response. The larger monitoring studies involved different patients.

Reach

One person, Morgan McGrath, is reported to have benefited. The 135 patients in the device testing and the 72 patients in the Montreal study were monitored, and neither figure counts rescues. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The article reports that McGrath was alive and well about 15 months after the seizure. The later Montreal study concerns other patients in a monitoring unit and does not extend her follow-up. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Connectivity between watch, phone and caregiver (one of 16 alerts in Montreal failed on a Wi-Fi disconnection). Caregivers tolerate false alarms (29 in 373 monitoring days in Montreal, mostly from routine movements). Focal seizures without convulsions are not detected (none of 510 in Montreal).

Replication attempts

3 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2. Benefit did not recur: 1.

Evidence

The McGrath rescue rests on the IEEE Spectrum article alone. The 2026 Montreal study by Jahani et al. Is independent of the maker. [1] [2] [3] [4]
