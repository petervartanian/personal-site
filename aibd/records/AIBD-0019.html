IBM–Yamagata AI survey confirms 303 new Nazca geoglyphs in Peru

depth 2 for an organization, depth 3 for society

303 previously unknown figurative relief-type geoglyphs, almost doubling the known total

An IBM–Yamagata deep-learning survey guided fieldwork that confirmed 303 new Nazca geoglyphs, published in PNAS (Sep 2024).

What happened

Between September 2022 and February 2023 (the sources give only months, so the first day of September is used) archaeologists led by Masato Sakai of Yamagata University's Institute of Nasca surveyed the Nazca Pampa in Peru using candidate locations proposed by a deep-learning model developed with IBM Research, which screened aerial photographs at 10-centimeter resolution over more than 600 square kilometers. Field verification confirmed 303 previously unknown figurative relief-type geoglyphs, almost doubling the known total; 178 were individually suggested by the model and 125 were found during the AI-guided survey without an individual suggestion. The team screened about 36 AI suggestions per promising candidate and reports a sixteen-fold increase in discovery rate. The results were published in PNAS on 23 September 2024.

What it was built to do. Built to flag candidate geoglyph locations in aerial imagery so that archaeologists could prioritize ground survey; the discoveries were that purpose.

The system's part

The model proposed candidate locations that directed fieldwork; 178 of the 303 confirmed geoglyphs were individually suggested by the AI, and of the remaining 125, 66 were found as part of an AI-discovered group and 59 were found without any help from AI or during screening outside the AI-suggested boxes. Confirmation was by ground survey, not by the model.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. High-resolution aerial imagery of the whole survey area. A detector trained with limited labeled examples of relief-type geoglyphs. Archaeologist screening of candidates followed by ground verification.

What must hold again. Targets must be visible at the imagery resolution (about 10 cm). Each candidate must be confirmed on the ground before being counted.
