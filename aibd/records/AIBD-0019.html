IBM–Yamagata AI survey confirms 303 new Nazca geoglyphs in Peru

An IBM–Yamagata deep-learning survey guided fieldwork that confirmed 303 new Nazca geoglyphs, published in PNAS (Sep 2024).

ⓘ

AI system

System

The geoglyph classifier developed by IBM and Yamagata University, with IBM PAIRS for imagery. [1]

Model

ResNet50 with a two-layer classifier, adapted from ImageNet training. [1]

Release

The study does not give a release or checkpoint identifier. [1]

Provider

IBM Research and Yamagata University, with the German Aerospace Center. [1]

Role

The classifier estimated where relief-type geoglyphs might appear in aerial images. [1]

Configuration

The model analyzed 112 × 112-pixel images at 5-meter intervals. Candidate selection used probability threshold 0.55 and at least two adjoining image patches. [1]

Inputs and outputs

The classifier analyzed aerial photographs and returned a map of probabilities and candidate locations. [1]

Autonomy

Archaeologists screened the candidates and visited selected locations to confirm the geoglyphs. [1]

Workflow

High-resolution aerial imagery of the whole survey area. A detector trained with limited labeled examples of relief-type geoglyphs. Archaeologist screening of candidates followed by ground verification.

Human oversight

Not assessed

Relation to AI

The model proposed candidate locations that directed fieldwork. 178 of the 303 confirmed geoglyphs were individually suggested by the AI. Of the remaining 125, 66 were found as part of an AI-discovered group. The other 59 were found without any help from AI or in screening outside the AI-suggested boxes. Confirmation was by ground survey, not by the model.
