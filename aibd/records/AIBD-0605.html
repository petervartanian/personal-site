Brief from Peter

Between May and August 2018, staff in six California memory care facilities received real-time alerts from SafelyYou Guardian's AI video fall detection. In that period, residents with dementia spent on average 29.6 fewer minutes on the ground after a fall than in a baseline period without alerts, in November and December 2017. The share of fallers who lay on the ground for more than an hour fell from 31% (8 of 26) to none. In 97.6% of the falls, the resident could not stand up alone. The study compares periods in the same facilities without a concurrent control group, and every author had ties to SafelyYou.1 In 2023, authors at Imperial College London, who declare no conflicts of interest, included the study in a synthesis of research on video recording for direct patient care. They listed the same reductions and read them as showing that the system improves care after falls.2 In a year-long pilot in two South Australian aged care facilities, false alerts from AI video monitoring reportedly overwhelmed staff and contributed to a real event being missed.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Cameras covered the bedrooms of consenting residents, where over 80% of falls happened. Staff received a notification with video access as soon as the system detected a fall.

Measurement

Measurement

Time until staff assistance and time on the ground measured automatically from video of each fall. [2]

Setting and sample

66 residents with dementia, 436 falls, six California memory care facilities, November 2017 to August 2018. [2]

Measures

Time on the ground and time until staff assistance. [2]

Results

29.6 minutes less on the ground; 28.3 minutes less until assistance; lies over one hour fell from 31% to zero. [2]

Additional value from AI

About 30 minutes less time on the ground per fall. [2]

Limitations

No concurrent control; period effects possible; authors employed by or holding stock in SafelyYou. [2]

Denominators

Real-time AI fall notifications with video access. [2]

Materials for replication

Not public. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Staff must be available to respond to alerts at night, when two thirds of falls occurred. Residents and families must consent to bedroom video.

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.
