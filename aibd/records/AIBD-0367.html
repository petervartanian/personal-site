What happened

On 28 October 2022 North Rhine-Westphalia's (NRW) Interior Ministry said DAR, the police's Palantir software, was central in several recent cases (WELT; RP Online). In October someone had emailed the Siegen city administration announcing an amok attack. The threat was over the city's “topless” permission for swimming pools. The ministry said the email address revealed the suspect via Palantir in fractions of a second. A shooting in Duisburg-Hamborn between a Turkish-Arab clan and the Hells Angels injured four people. A Duisburg investigator said police quickly identified 51 of the roughly 100 participants, about 20 minutes each (RP Online; WELT). 49 of them were assigned to rival groups. Interior Minister Herbert Reul addressed the Landtag's interior committee on 27 October.

He said DAR links the weapons register, residents' registration, the police case system and the wanted-persons portal. The ministry puts the 2019–2025 cost at about 39 million euros. The opposition attacks it on data-protection and cost grounds (WELT).

What it was built to do. DAR was procured to let North Rhine-Westphalia police search and link entries across police and administrative databases during investigations. Identifying suspects from an email address or from a nickname and phone number was that purpose.

The system's part

The reports describe DAR as software that links entries across the weapons register, residents' registration data, the police case system and the wanted-persons portal. None mentions machine learning. The minister himself describes plain linking, while the OECD AI Incidents Monitor calls it an AI system. On the ministry's account the software's contribution was speed of identification. That was an email address in fractions of a second and about 20 minutes per shooting participant. Whether manual queries would have reached the same people more slowly is not addressed. The claims come from the deploying ministry and an unnamed investigator.

Replication

What produced it. Police and administrative databases (weapons register, residents' registration, case-processing system, wanted-persons portal) linked in one Palantir-based search. An identifier from the case, such as an email address, a nickname or a phone number, that matches an entry somewhere in the linked data.

What must hold again. The person already appears in one of the linked databases. The system links records and does not generate new ones. Investigators have lawful access to the linked registers. The opposition contests the arrangement on data-protection grounds. Licensing continues. The ministry puts the 2019–2025 cost at about 39 million euros.
