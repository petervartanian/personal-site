Palantir-based DAR identifies the author of a Siegen amok threat

The North Rhine-Westphalia Interior Ministry said that Palantir-based DAR traced an emailed amok threat in Siegen in fractions of a second and identified 51 of about 100 people at a Duisburg shooting.

  • 2 an organization

    NRW police resolved an amok-threat email address to a person in fractions of a second. They identified 51 of about 100 shooting participants at about 20 minutes each, per the ministry and a Duisburg investigator. No comparison with conventional methods and no charges or convictions are reported.

  • Not rated society

    Siegen's administration and public had the author of a threatened amok attack identified. The sources do not say whether the threat was credible, whether the person was charged, or what harm was averted.

The system’s part
The reports describe DAR as software that links entries across the weapons register, residents' registration data, the police case system and the wanted-persons portal. None mentions machine learning. The minister himself describes plain linking, while the OECD AI Incidents Monitor calls it an AI system. On the ministry's account the software's contribution was speed of identification. That was an email address in fractions of a second and about 20 minutes per shooting participant. Whether manual queries would have reached the same people more slowly is not addressed. The claims come from the deploying ministry and an unnamed investigator.
Limits
The account describes linking existing registers rather than a documented machine-learning component. No query logs, manual timing comparison, identification-error analysis, convictions, or prevented attack establish AI-specific benefit. The ministry’s reported speed remains an operator claim.
Costs and harms
The ministry puts the 2019–2025 cost at about 39 million euros, and the opposition contests the software on data-protection and cost grounds. The civil-rights group GFF brought a constitutional complaint against the police law that allowed the data analysis. North Rhine-Westphalia rewrote the legal basis at the end of 2025.
Later
Taken to court, 1 February 2026. GFF filed a constitutional complaint against the NRW police law in October 2022. NRW rewrote the legal basis for automated data analysis at the end of 2025, in response to the 2023 Federal Constitutional Court ruling on Hesse and Hamburg. The court declined to decide the complaint in February 2026 and ordered NRW to pay the complainants' necessary costs.

What happened

In October 2022 someone emailed the city administration of Siegen announcing an amok attack. The threat was over the city's “topless” permission for swimming pools. A shooting in Duisburg-Hamborn between a Turkish-Arab clan and the Hells Angels injured four people. Interior Minister Herbert Reul addressed the Landtag's interior committee on 27 October. He said that DAR, the police's Palantir software, links the weapons register, residents' registration, the police case system and the wanted-persons portal. On 28 October 2022 the Interior Ministry of North Rhine-Westphalia said that DAR was central in several recent cases (WELT; RP Online). The ministry said that the email address revealed the suspect via Palantir in fractions of a second. A Duisburg investigator said that police quickly identified 51 of the roughly 100 participants in the shooting, at about 20 minutes each (RP Online; WELT). Of these, 49 were assigned to rival groups. The ministry puts the 2019–2025 cost at about 39 million euros. The opposition attacks the software on data-protection and cost grounds (WELT). In October 2022 the civil-rights group Gesellschaft für Freiheitsrechte (GFF) filed a constitutional complaint against the state police law that allowed this data analysis. North Rhine-Westphalia rewrote the legal basis at the end of 2025. In February 2026 the Federal Constitutional Court declined to decide the complaint and ordered the state to pay the complainants' costs (GFF).

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

DAR linked an email address and other identifiers across police cases, weapons records, residents’ records and wanted-person records.

Autonomy

Investigators used returned links to identify and assess suspects; the described software did not independently establish guilt or make arrests.

Workflow

Police and administrative databases (weapons register, residents' registration, case-processing system, wanted-persons portal) linked in one Palantir-based search. An identifier from the case, such as an email address, a nickname or a phone number, that matches an entry somewhere in the linked data.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The person already appears in one of the linked databases. The system links records and does not generate new ones. Investigators have lawful access to the linked registers. The opposition contests the arrangement on data-protection grounds. Licensing continues. The ministry puts the 2019–2025 cost at about 39 million euros.
