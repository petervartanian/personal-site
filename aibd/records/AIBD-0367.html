Palantir-based DAR identifies the author of a Siegen amok threat

What happened

On 28 October 2022 North Rhine-Westphalia's Interior Ministry said DAR, the police's Palantir software, played a central role in several recent cases (WELT; RP Online, 28 October 2022). In October someone had emailed the Siegen city administration announcing an amok attack over the city's “topless” permission for swimming pools; the ministry said the email address revealed the suspect via Palantir in fractions of a second. After a shooting in Duisburg-Hamborn between a Turkish-Arab clan and the Hells Angels that injured four people, a Duisburg investigator said police quickly identified 51 of the roughly 100 participants, 49 of them assigned to rival groups, about 20 minutes each (RP Online; WELT).

Interior Minister Herbert Reul told the Landtag's interior committee on 27 October that DAR links the weapons register, residents' registration, the police case system and the wanted-persons portal; the ministry puts the 2019–2025 cost at about 39 million euros, and the opposition attacks it on data-protection and cost grounds (WELT).

What it was built to do. DAR was procured to let North Rhine-Westphalia police search and link entries across police and administrative databases during investigations; identifying suspects from an email address or from a nickname and phone number was that purpose.

The system's part

The read reports describe DAR as software that links entries across the weapons register, residents' registration data, the police case system and the wanted-persons portal; none mentions machine learning, and the minister's own description is of plain linking, while the Monitor labels it an AI system. On the ministry's account the software's contribution was speed of identification, from an email address in fractions of a second and about 20 minutes per shooting participant; whether manual queries would have reached the same people, more slowly, is not addressed, and the claims come from the deployer's ministry and an unnamed investigator.

Replication

What produced it. Police and administrative databases (weapons register, residents' registration, case-processing system, wanted-persons portal) linked in one Palantir-based search. An identifier from the case, such as an email address, a nickname or a phone number, that matches an entry somewhere in the linked data.

What must hold again. The person already appears in one of the linked databases; the system links records, it does not generate new ones. Investigators have lawful access to the linked registers; the opposition contests the arrangement on data-protection grounds. Licensing continues; the ministry puts the 2019–2025 cost at about 39 million euros.
