What happened

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority's report 'AML/CFT Regtech: Case Studies and Insights' (January 2021; press release 21 January 2021) presents anonymised bank case studies. Bank F, described as a subsidiary and regional head office of a large global bank in Asia-Pacific, implemented a network (graph) analytics solution in Hong Kong in 2013 that identified high-risk customers and transactions from wholesale client, correspondent banking, trade, payments and external registry data. In an example from 2015, a relationship manager gathered intelligence about trade-based money laundering related to a drug cartel in one account and linked it to a second account through the CRM tool; the network analytics tool then identified additional companies linked to the first two parties through linked phone numbers and email addresses, and account reviews for Companies A, B and C revealed potential money laundering concerns. The report states that by 2016 Bank F covered its top 20 commercial banking sites and about 80% of its trade lending book, that the tool has led to the review of around 100 customer relationships holding around USD 500 million in clean or unsecured exposures, and that even if 10% were unrecoverable the tool contributed around USD 50 million in savings. The foreword adds that such techniques are already neutralising mule account networks linked to face mask scams. The 2015 example carries no day, so the publication date is used.

What it was built to do. Identify high-risk customers, transactions and hidden relationships for AML/CFT and trade-based money laundering investigations.

The system's part

The HKMA report attributes the identification of Company C to the network analytics tool's matching of linked phone numbers and email addresses, after a human relationship manager supplied the initial lead; whether the tool uses machine learning is not stated. The bank built and deployed the tool itself; the HKMA, which wrote the report, is the regulator.

Replication

What produced it

  • Integration of wholesale client, correspondent banking, trade, payments and external registry data in one network model
  • A human intelligence lead (the relationship manager's TBML suspicion) used as the seed for network expansion

What must hold again

  • Data of sufficient quality across sources, which the report says Bank F stressed
  • Governance that lets analytic findings trigger account reviews and STR filings
