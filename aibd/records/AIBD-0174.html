Bank F's network analytics uncover a third linked company

In the HKMA's 2021 AML/CFT Regtech report, an anonymized global bank's network analytics tool linked companies through shared phone numbers and email addresses; account reviews found potential mone...

ⓘ

What happened

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority's report “AML/CFT Regtech: Case Studies and Insights” (January 2021; press release 21 January 2021) presents anonymized bank case studies. Bank F is a subsidiary and regional head office of a large global bank in Asia-Pacific, per the report. It implemented a network (graph) analytics solution in Hong Kong in 2013. It identified high-risk customers and transactions from wholesale client, correspondent banking, trade, payments and external registry data. In a 2015 example, a relationship manager gathered intelligence about trade-based money laundering (TBML) related to a drug cartel in one account. The manager linked it to a second account via the CRM tool. The tool then identified additional companies linked to the first two parties through linked phone numbers and email addresses.

Account reviews for Companies A, B and C revealed potential money laundering concerns. The report states that by 2016 Bank F covered its top 20 commercial banking sites and about 80% of its trade lending book. The tool has led to review of around 100 customer relationships holding around USD 500 million in clean or unsecured exposures, it says. Even if 10% were unrecoverable, the tool contributed around USD 50 million in savings, it says. The foreword adds that such techniques are already neutralizing mule account networks linked to face mask scams. The 2015 example carries no day.

Purpose. Identify high-risk customers, transactions and hidden relationships for AML/CFT and trade-based money laundering investigations.

AI’s contribution

The HKMA report attributes the identification of Company C to the network analytics tool's matching of linked phone numbers and email addresses. A human relationship manager supplied the initial lead. Whether the tool uses machine learning is not stated. The bank built and deployed the tool itself. The HKMA, which wrote the report, is the regulator.

Further evidence

Setting. Integration of wholesale client, correspondent banking, trade, payments and external registry data in one network model. A human intelligence lead (the relationship manager's TBML suspicion) used as the seed for network expansion.

Conditions. Data of sufficient quality across sources, which the report says Bank F stressed. Governance that lets analytic findings trigger account reviews and STR filings.
