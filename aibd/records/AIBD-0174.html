Bank's network tool links a third company to suspected money laundering

Bank F's network analytics tool, which the report does not name, linked a third company to a suspected money laundering network through shared phone numbers and email addresses.

What happened

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority's report “AML/CFT Regtech: Case Studies and Insights” (January 2021; press release 21 January 2021) presents anonymized bank case studies. The report says that Bank F is a subsidiary and regional head office of a large global bank in Asia-Pacific. The report states that before the tool, manual approaches to reviewing documentation for signatories were the only means to make such connections. Bank F implemented a network (graph) analytics solution in Hong Kong in 2013. The solution identifies high-risk customers and transactions from wholesale client, correspondent banking, trade, payments and external registry data. In a 2015 example, a relationship manager gathered intelligence about trade-based money laundering related to a drug cartel in one account. The manager linked it to a second account through the customer relationship management tool. The tool then identified additional companies linked to the first two parties through linked phone numbers and email addresses. Account reviews for Companies A, B and C revealed potential money laundering concerns. The report gives no day for the 2015 example. The report states that by 2016 Bank F covered its top 20 commercial banking sites and about 80% of its trade lending book. It says that the tool has led to review of around 100 customer relationships holding around USD 500 million in clean or unsecured exposures. The report’s USD 50 million figure assumes that 10% of those exposures would otherwise be unrecoverable. It does not document USD 50 million in realized savings. The report adds that a successor tool, built with an outside vendor, was in production across multiple jurisdictions by January 2021 and drew on a data lake of over 40 billion rows. Its foreword adds that such techniques are already neutralizing mule account networks linked to face mask scams.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The network tool linked customer, trade, payment, and registry records, including shared phone numbers and email addresses, to reveal related companies.

Workflow

Integration of wholesale client, correspondent banking, trade, payments and external registry data in one network model. A human intelligence lead (the relationship manager's TBML suspicion) used as the seed for network expansion.

Human oversight

A relationship manager supplied the initial suspicion. Bank staff then reviewed accounts that the graph tool linked to the suspected network.

Benefits

Reach

No source counts beneficiaries. The tool led to the review of around 100 customer relationships holding around USD 500 million in clean or unsecured exposures, and the 2015 example concerns three companies. [1] [2]

Duration

Bank F implemented the tool in Hong Kong in 2013, and by 2016 it covered the bank's top 20 commercial banking sites and about 80% of its trade lending book. By January 2021 a successor tool, built with an outside vendor, was in production in several jurisdictions. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority's report is the only source for these facts. [1] [2]
