Grok flags possible appendicitis in a man sent home; a scan confirms it

xAI's Grok told a man in Norway, sent home with an acid blocker, to demand a computed tomography scan; his appendix, which he later wrote had ruptured, was removed on 3 December 2025.

  • 3 a person

    An appendix that the man says the CT showed close to rupturing was removed on 3 December 2025. The pain was gone and he went home the next day. In August 2026 he wrote that the appendix had ruptured and that he was alive. He and Musk call it life-saving. That rests on his account and a ward photo. No clinician, hospital or record has spoken. A doctor commenting in the thread said that severe abdominal pain in a younger patient almost always gets a CT. So life-saving is not established.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The shared conversation shows what Grok contributed. He described constant pain for six hours, worse lying flat and eased by sitting with knees to chest, with no fever. Grok ranked perforated peptic ulcer first and early or atypical appendicitis second. It called that a pattern doctors take very seriously. It urged an ER visit that night for a CT or ultrasound. Once he said the legevakt had sent him home with Somac, it scripted a request for a senior doctor and a CT scan. He told Grok afterwards that asking for the CT got him prioritized for surgery. Whether his local physician or the hospital would have imaged him without that request is unknown. TipRanks reports from the thread that he told the doctors his sister, a nurse, had suggested the scan. The Reddit post the press quoted was drafted by Grok from his description. It says he walked back to the ER, where his later comments say the physician referred him the next day.
Limits
The story after the operation is self-reported, and Grok drafted the Reddit post. The post and the later comments differ on where the first visit was and on how he got back, and in August 2026 he wrote that his appendix had ruptured, where in December 2025 he had written that it was about to rupture. No clinician, hospital or record has confirmed the CT findings or the surgery.
Costs and harms
The likely benefit was renewed medical attention after a visit that sent him home without imaging. Grok ranked a perforated ulcer first, and by the man's account the cause was the atypical appendicitis that it ranked second. The story does not show that imaging depended on the chatbot's wording. Pediatricians in Istanbul reported in February 2026 that Grok 3 over-triaged 36.3% of cases in a children's emergency department.

He also wrote that the operation lasted six hours and that his appendix had ruptured. In December 2025 he had written that it was about to rupture.

What happened

The night before his surgery on 3 December 2025, a 49-year-old man in Norway, Reddit user u/Tykjen, had constant abdominal pain. He was examined at a legevakt (out-of-hours clinic), given the acid blocker Somac and sent home. He described the pain to xAI's Grok in a browser chat. Grok named perforated ulcer and atypical appendicitis as the likeliest causes. It told him to get computed tomography (CT) or ultrasound that night and scripted a CT request. The next day, by his account, his local physician sent him to hospital. He wrote that a CT showed an appendix close to rupturing. It was removed laparoscopically and he went home on 4 December, when he posted the story. Elon Musk amplified it on 6 December. In later Reddit comments the man wrote that he had contacted a lawyer about the doctor who sent him home. On 1 January 2026 Musk posted again that Grok had saved the life of a man in Norway. In February 2026 pediatricians in Istanbul with no tie to xAI reported that Grok 3 flagged 97.7% of critical children in an emergency department but over-triaged 36.3% of cases. In August 2026 the man wrote that he was alive, and he credited the surgeons first. He wrote then that the operation took six hours and that his appendix had ruptured.

Configuration

Inputs and outputs

The patient described persistent abdominal pain and his earlier examination. Grok proposed serious causes and helped him formulate a request for imaging and further assessment.

Workflow

A patient with hours of constant, severe abdominal pain who had been examined, given an acid blocker and sent home without imaging. A detailed symptom description put to a general chatbot: constant pain, worse lying flat, eased by sitting with knees to chest, no fever. It matched these to surgical causes and said so without hedging. A second clinical contact the next day at which the patient repeated the chatbot's reasoning and asked for a CT.

Human oversight

A physician referred him to hospital, where clinicians ordered CT imaging and operated. Grok's contribution was advice that the patient could raise with them.

AI contribution

Grok recommended urgent abdominal imaging and scripted a request for a CT scan after he had been sent home. He told Grok that asking for the CT got him prioritized for surgery. Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The chatbot's differential includes the true cause. Here it ranked perforated ulcer first and atypical appendicitis second, and the second was right. A clinician agrees to image. The referral here came from his local physician. Whether the request changed her decision is unknown. Such cases are self-published. No clinician, hospital or document has confirmed this one. The first-person post was itself drafted by the chatbot. So the record stands or falls with his account and the shared transcript.
