Grok tells a man sent home to demand a CT; appendix near rupture

depth 3 for a person

Grok told a 49-year-old Norwegian sent home with an acid blocker to demand a CT; it found an appendix near rupture, removed 3 Dec 2025.

What happened

The night before his surgery on 3 December 2025, a 49-year-old man in Norway, Reddit user u/Tykjen, in constant abdominal pain, was examined at a legevakt (out-of-hours clinic), given the acid blocker Somac and sent home. He described the pain to xAI's Grok in a browser chat; Grok named perforated ulcer and atypical appendicitis as the likeliest causes, told him to get a CT or ultrasound that night and scripted a request for a CT. The next day, by his account, his local physician sent him to hospital, a CT showed an appendix close to rupturing, it was removed laparoscopically and he went home on 4 December, when he posted the story; Elon Musk amplified it on 6 December.

What it was built to do. Grok is xAI's general-purpose conversational assistant; answering a user's description of symptoms with possible causes and advice on when to seek care is within that general use, and the man had used it for a year on other topics. xAI did not build it as a medical device, and the benefit was ordinary use rather than an unexpected one; after the event Musk posted 'Try it!' about Grok for medical questions.

The system's part

The shared conversation shows what Grok contributed: after he described constant pain for six hours, worse lying flat and eased by sitting with knees to chest, with no fever, it ranked perforated peptic ulcer first and early or atypical appendicitis second, called that a pattern doctors take very seriously, urged an ER visit that night for a CT or ultrasound and, once he said the legevakt had sent him home with Somac, scripted a request for a senior doctor and a CT scan. He told Grok afterwards that asking for the CT got him prioritized for surgery. Whether his local physician or the hospital would have imaged him without that request is unknown; TipRanks reports from the thread that he told the doctors his sister, a nurse, had suggested the scan. The Reddit post the press quoted was drafted by Grok from his description, and it says he walked back to the ER, where his later comments say the physician referred him the next day.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A patient with hours of constant, severe abdominal pain who had been examined, given an acid blocker and sent home without imaging. A detailed symptom description (constant pain, worse lying flat, eased by sitting with knees to chest, no fever) given to a general chatbot that matched it to surgical causes and said so without hedging. A second clinical contact the next day at which the patient repeated the chatbot's reasoning and asked for a CT.

What must hold again. The chatbot's differential includes the true cause; here it ranked perforated ulcer first and atypical appendicitis second, and the second was right. A clinician agrees to image; the referral here came from his local physician, and whether the request changed her decision is unknown. Such cases are self-published; no clinician, hospital or document has confirmed this one, and the first-person post was itself drafted by the chatbot, so the record stands or falls with his account and the shared transcript.
