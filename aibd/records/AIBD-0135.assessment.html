Account
At about 2 a.m. on 6 August 2025, a woman in her 70s identified as “A” was at home in Deogam-dong, Daedeok-gu, Daejeon, South Korea. She lives alone and has schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. She repeatedly told the city-issued AI care robot Kkumdori “I want to die” and “save me”. The robot's crisis-detection algorithm flagged the words and alerted the operator's control center. A staff member phoned her. When she kept repeating them, staff reported to police on 112 with her location. Officers found her emotionally unstable and contacted her family. Police judged hospital treatment urgent. On the afternoon of 7 August she was admitted with her guardian's consent. Daejeon announced the case on 13 August 2025.
Replication
Produced by
  • A municipal program that put conversational robots with a crisis-word detection algorithm into 1,000 homes of people living alone, 200 per district, from January 2025.
  • A 24-hour human control center run by the supplier that receives alerts, phones the resident, and reports to police (112) with the location.
  • A resident who habitually talked, sang and danced with the robot, so her statements at 2 a.m. were spoken aloud to it.
  • Police able to attend at night, and a family and hospital able to complete a protective admission the next day.
Must hold
  • Alerts go to humans who act at night. The robot itself does nothing beyond detecting words and raising the alarm.
  • The detection catches explicit statements (“죽고 싶다”, “살려줘”). A silent crisis would not trigger it.
  • Residents keep talking to the device. Her guardian said it was a great support to her.
Echoes
  • Setting
    Yonhap, 1 August 2026: at about midnight on 20 July 2026 an 80-something in Daejeon's Seo-gu with severe abdominal pain told the care robot to call 119; the control center relayed it, paramedics arrived at about 00:30 and took the person to hospital (https://www.yna.co.kr/view/AKR20260731144200063).

    unknown
  • Setting
    Yonhap, 1 August 2026: in November 2025 an 80-something in Daedeok-gu, badly hurt after falling from bed, called “Aria, help me” to an AI speaker at home and was rescued by 119 (same article).
    Outcome
