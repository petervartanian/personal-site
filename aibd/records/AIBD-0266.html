Johns Hopkins LSTM BCI lets a man with ALS spell for 90 days

A chronically implanted high-density ECoG BCI with an LSTM click detector trained on under 44 minutes of data supported switch-scan spelling for 626 minutes over 90 days, all in the laboratory.

ⓘ

What happened

Candrea et al. (Communications Medicine, 22 October 2024) report a clinical-trial participant with ALS (NCT03567213) at Johns Hopkins. He had a chronically implanted high-density electrocorticographic BCI over sensorimotor cortex. High-gamma power vectors from 128 channels fed a long short-term memory layer with 25 hidden units and fully connected layers. These classified rest versus grasp every 100 ms. The click detector was trained on almost 44 minutes of data (480 trials) collected up to 21 days before use. It then ran for 90 days without retraining. The participant controlled a switch-scanning speller for a total of 626 minutes with a median spelling rate of 10.2 characters per minute. The paper states all data collection and testing were performed in the laboratory. No home use is reported.

Purpose. Restore text communication for a person with ALS through a single neural click command driving a switch-scanning speller

AI’s contribution

The LSTM click detector is the decoding element. Its stability without retraining is the paper's main result. The source is a peer-reviewed report by the developers, with no outside corroboration and no use outside the laboratory.
