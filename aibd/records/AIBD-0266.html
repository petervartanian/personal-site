What happened

Candrea et al. (Communications Medicine, 22 October 2024) report a clinical-trial participant with ALS (NCT03567213) at Johns Hopkins. He had a chronically implanted high-density electrocorticographic BCI over sensorimotor cortex. High-gamma power vectors from 128 channels fed a long short-term memory layer with 25 hidden units and fully connected layers. These classified rest versus grasp every 100 ms. The click detector was trained on almost 44 minutes of data (480 trials) collected up to 21 days before use. It then ran for 90 days without retraining. The participant controlled a switch-scanning speller for a total of 626 minutes with a median spelling rate of 10.2 characters per minute. The paper states all data collection and testing were performed in the laboratory. No home use is reported. The date is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Restore text communication for a person with ALS through a single neural click command driving a switch-scanning speller

The system's part

The LSTM click detector is the decoding element. Its stability without retraining is the paper's main result. The source is a peer-reviewed report by the developers, with no outside corroboration and no use outside the laboratory.

Replication

What produced it. High-density ECoG over sensorimotor cortex. LSTM click detector trained on under 44 minutes of data. Switch-scanning speller interface.

What must hold again. Signal power modulation remains stable (a transient reduction interrupted use of the fixed model). Laboratory setting with research staff.
