AIMS's ReefScan and ReefCloud automate reef image analysis

AIMS states ReefScan uses machine learning for real-time object classification and ReefCloud automates benthic image analysis; no reef management decision attributed to the AI was found.

What happened

The Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS) describes ReefScan as a modular suite of automated marine monitoring systems that integrates machine learning, imaging sensors and robotics and 'uses machine learning for real-time object identification and classification, freeing human effort from routine tasks', and ReefCloud as a platform on which users upload survey images, apply automated benthic analysis and generate consistent outputs, which 'reduces manual processing time' and lets teams focus on interpretation and management actions. The ReefCloud.ai site claims accelerated image processing with AI giving results 'that would take weeks in hours, with up to 90% accuracy', and AIMS lists a 2022 Asia-Pacific Spatial Excellence Award and a Fast Company honourable mention in the AI and Data category, with in-kind support from Accenture and the Allen Coral Atlas. The earlier verifier sought an AIMS robotics-and-AI reef restoration page (not found: 404) and a study tying AI detection of crown-of-thorns starfish to a Reef Authority control action (none found); AIMS's crown-of-thorns pages fetched here describe eDNA detection and chemical-cue control research, not AI. Only the maker's own pages were fetched; the date used is the date the pages were read.

What it was built to do. Automate classification of reef imagery so that monitoring programs report faster and at larger scale; the claimed benefit is the intended one.

What produced it

What must hold again

  • Validation against expert annotation
