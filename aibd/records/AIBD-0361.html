AIMS's ReefScan and ReefCloud automate reef image analysis

Authors including AIMS staff report that ReefScan surveys made island-scale reef assessments possible; an independent team found that ReefCloud's model annotations agreed poorly with a researcher's.

What happened

The Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS) describes ReefScan as a modular suite of automated marine monitoring systems. It integrates machine learning, imaging sensors and robotics and “uses machine learning for real-time object identification and classification, freeing human effort from routine tasks”. AIMS describes ReefCloud as a platform on which users upload survey images, apply automated benthic analysis and generate consistent outputs. AIMS says that ReefCloud “reduces manual processing time” and lets teams focus on interpretation and management actions. On the ReefCloud.ai site, the makers claim that AI image processing gives results “that would take weeks in hours, with up to 90% accuracy”. AIMS lists a 2022 Asia-Pacific Spatial Excellence Award and a Fast Company honorable mention in the AI and Data category. It lists in-kind support from Accenture and the Allen Coral Atlas. AIMS staff wrote in March 2025 that ReefCloud had a network of over 2,000 practitioners in over 90 countries. In August 2025, authors at a Western Australian government fisheries agency and two universities reported that they had built their own image-analysis models. They wrote that externally hosted platforms such as ReefCloud raise questions of data confidentiality for fisheries agencies. An independent team at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology reported in January 2026 that ReefCloud's model annotations agreed poorly with a researcher's. The team used only the human annotations. A preprint whose authors include AIMS staff, posted on 20 August 2026, reports that ReefScan made island-scale reef assessments possible around Erub, Ugar and Mer, and that Traditional Owners chose it as their preferred method. The authors also report that ReefScan underestimated total hard coral cover against diver photo-transects. No cited study links AI detection of crown-of-thorns starfish to a Reef Authority control action. AIMS's crown-of-thorns pages describe research on detection by environmental DNA and on control by chemical cues. Those pages do not describe any use of AI.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

ReefScan collected marine imagery and classified objects. ReefCloud accepted survey images and produced standardized classifications of the seafloor for monitoring and comparison.

Workflow

Large volumes of standardized reef survey imagery. Machine-learning classifiers for benthic cover and objects. Cloud platform shared by multiple monitoring programs. Institutional partners (Accenture, Allen Coral Atlas).

Human oversight

An independent team in Hong Kong compared ReefCloud's model annotations with a researcher's on the same photoquadrats and kept only the human points after it found low consistency. No cited source describes a reef management action that follows from the AI's output. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Benefits

Reach

No source counts the people or organizations that benefited. AIMS staff wrote in March 2025 that ReefCloud had a network of over 2,000 practitioners in over 90 countries, which counts users. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Duration

No source gives a dated follow-up that shows a lasting benefit. On 30 January 2026, an independent team in Hong Kong reported that ReefCloud's model annotations agreed poorly with a researcher's. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Measurement

Measurement

Images from SCUBA photo-transects, vessel-deployed drop cameras and transom-mounted ReefScan units were analyzed with machine-learning models, and the methods were compared statistically on the cover of six benthic groups. Traditional Owners gave structured feedback on each method. [5]

Setting and sample

Reefs around Erub, Ugar and Mer islands, Kemer Kemer Meriam nation, eastern Torres Strait, Australia. [5]

Measures

Estimated cover of six benthic groups, including total hard coral cover. [5]

Results

Estimates of non-Acropora hard corals and encrusting soft corals were comparable across methods; ReefScan underestimated total hard coral cover relative to diver photo-transects. Traditional Owners chose ReefScan for regional assessments. [5]

Additional value from AI

Not isolated: all three methods used machine-learning image analysis, so the comparison concerns the collection platform. [5]

Limitations

Preprint, not peer reviewed; several authors are AIMS staff; one region. [5]

People and places

Dependencies

ReefScan combined imaging equipment and, in some configurations, robotic vehicles with cloud processing. ReefCloud depended on uploaded survey imagery and a shared online analysis environment.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Classifier accuracy across reef types and image methods. Continued uploads from monitoring programs. Validation against expert annotation. Links from outputs to management decisions, not yet documented.

Two independent teams with no stake in ReefCloud published the other two: one at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and one at a Western Australian government fisheries agency and two universities.
