AIMS's ReefScan and ReefCloud automate reef image analysis

AIMS states ReefScan uses machine learning for real-time object classification and ReefCloud automates benthic image analysis; no reef management decision attributed to the AI was found.

ⓘ

What happened

The Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS) describes ReefScan as a modular suite of automated marine monitoring systems. It integrates machine learning, imaging sensors and robotics and “uses machine learning for real-time object identification and classification, freeing human effort from routine tasks”. AIMS describes ReefCloud as a platform on which users upload survey images, apply automated benthic analysis and generate consistent outputs. It “reduces manual processing time” and lets teams focus on interpretation and management actions. The ReefCloud.ai site claims accelerated image processing with AI giving results “that would take weeks in hours, with up to 90% accuracy”. AIMS lists a 2022 Asia-Pacific Spatial Excellence Award and a Fast Company honorable mention in the AI and Data category. It lists in-kind support from Accenture and the Allen Coral Atlas.

No cited study links AI detection of crown-of-thorns starfish to a Reef Authority control action. AIMS's crown-of-thorns pages describe eDNA detection and chemical-cue control research, not AI. Only the maker's own pages are among the sources.

Purpose. Automate classification of reef imagery so that monitoring programs report faster and at larger scale. The claimed benefit is the intended one.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. AIMS's pages state that machine learning performs the image classification in ReefScan and the automated benthic analysis in ReefCloud. That is the claimed source of efficiency. No independent evaluation, named monitoring result or management decision attributable to these systems is among the sources, and only the maker's own account is cited.

Further evidence

Setting. Large volumes of standardized reef survey imagery. Machine-learning classifiers for benthic cover and objects. Cloud platform shared by multiple monitoring programs. Institutional partners (Accenture, Allen Coral Atlas).

Conditions. Classifier accuracy across reef types and image methods. Continued uploads from monitoring programs. Validation against expert annotation. Links from outputs to management decisions, not yet documented.
