A smartwatch detects a father's atrial fibrillation before heart surgery

Mr. Joseph Holycross said that an emergency room doctor confirmed the atrial fibrillation that the Samsung Health Monitor electrocardiogram app detected on his Galaxy Watch4 Classic.

The system’s part
The watch classified a user-initiated 30-second ECG as atrial fibrillation. Holycross says that he would not have gone to the ER without that result and that an ER doctor confirmed the rhythm. The recording was prompted by his wife's observation, not by a passive irregular-rhythm notification. CyberGuy's original interview records the couple's account. No clinical document or independent clinical verification is cited. Samsung's instructions distinguish the ECG app from its compatible watch hardware. The interview does not disclose the installed app, operating-system, or classifier version.

What happened

In February 2022 cardiologists at Bordeaux University Hospital reported a test of the Samsung electrocardiogram classifier in 200 patients, on a watch that they call the Galaxy Watch Active 3. Of the 100 patients in sinus rhythm, the classifier read 81 recordings correctly, read 6 as atrial fibrillation and left 13 unclassified. Joseph Holycross, 39, is a father of four. He had attributed a racing heart to caffeine for one and a half to two years. His wife Jamie, a respiratory therapist, noticed that he was short of breath. She urged him to run the electrocardiogram feature on his Samsung Galaxy Watch4 Classic. He says that the result read “atrial fibrillation” in bold. He repeated the recording several times and then went to the emergency room, where a doctor reviewed the watch tracing and confirmed atrial fibrillation. He stayed about a week in hospital. About ten days later he returned for open-heart surgery to repair “several things on my heart”. Asked whether he would have gone to the emergency room without the watch, he said, “Nope.” The family says that doctors warned that he “probably would have had a stroke, heart attack, or both” and recommended screening for his siblings and children. CyberGuy published Kurt Knutsson's interview with Joseph and Jamie Holycross on 1 October 2026. The interview does not give the date or the place of the event.

Details

AI system

Configuration

Samsung Health Monitor ran on a Galaxy Watch4 Classic. Holycross took an ECG after his wife noticed a possible problem. [1] [2]

Inputs and outputs

The watch recorded a user-initiated ECG and displayed an atrial-fibrillation classification. The tracing was shared with the emergency physician.

Workflow

A Galaxy Watch with the ECG feature enabled through Samsung Health Monitor on a paired Galaxy phone. A symptomatic user prompted to record an ECG. An ER able to confirm the rhythm on a 12-lead ECG.

Human oversight

His wife recognized the symptoms and prompted the recording. A physician confirmed the rhythm, and the hospital team investigated and treated the underlying heart problems.

AI contribution

The watch classified a 30-second ECG as atrial fibrillation after the patient’s wife prompted him to take a reading. He said that an emergency physician confirmed the rhythm. The original interview provides their testimony without clinical records. [1]
