A father went to the ER after checking his heart with a smartwatch

Mr. Joseph Holycross reported that doctors confirmed the atrial fibrillation that Samsung Health Monitor detected on his Galaxy Watch4 Classic.

ⓘ

What happened

CyberGuy published Kurt Knutsson's interview with Joseph and Jamie Holycross on 1 October 2026. Joseph, 39, is a father of four. The event date and place are not given. He had attributed a racing heart to caffeine for one and a half to two years. His wife Jamie, a respiratory therapist, noticed that he was short of breath. She urged him to run the electrocardiogram (ECG) feature on his Samsung Galaxy Watch4 Classic. He says that the result read “atrial fibrillation” in bold. He repeated the recording several times, then went to the emergency room (ER), where a doctor reviewed the watch tracing and confirmed AFib. He stayed about a week in hospital. About ten days later he returned for open-heart surgery to repair “several things on my heart”. Asked whether he would have gone to the ER without the watch, he said “Nope”.

The family says that doctors warned that he “probably would have had a stroke, heart attack, or both” and recommended screening for his siblings and children.

Purpose. The Samsung Health Monitor ECG feature is built to record a 30-second single-lead ECG and classify it. It flags a possible AFib result so the user contacts a doctor. This result was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The watch classified a user-initiated 30-second ECG as atrial fibrillation. Holycross says that he would not have gone to the ER without that result and that an ER doctor confirmed the rhythm. The recording was prompted by his wife's observation, not by a passive irregular-rhythm notification. CyberGuy's original interview records the couple's account. No clinical document or independent clinical verification is cited. Samsung's instructions distinguish the ECG app from its compatible watch hardware. The interview does not disclose the installed app, operating-system, or classifier version.

Further evidence

Setting. A Galaxy Watch with the ECG feature enabled through Samsung Health Monitor on a paired Galaxy phone. A symptomatic user prompted to record an ECG. An ER able to confirm the rhythm on a 12-lead ECG.

Conditions. The single-lead classifier returns AFib rather than inconclusive or poor recording. The user acts on the result instead of dismissing it.
