DeepMind's AI helps US forecasters predict Hurricane Melissa early

The US National Hurricane Center blended Google DeepMind's AI ensemble into its Melissa forecasts from 21 October 2025; the center's track errors at 120 hours were less than half its five-year mean.

  • Not rated a person

    No cited source attributes an outcome for any individual to the AI guidance. The sources report impacts only for the population as a whole.

  • 2 an organization

    NHC forecasters blended the DeepMind ensemble into official track forecasts from Discussion 1 and set intensity forecasts closest to it from Discussion 10. Melissa's official track error at 120 hours was 87.7 nautical miles, against a five-year mean of 185.4 nautical miles. NHC's Science Operations Officer called the AI guidance very valuable for Melissa.

  • Not rated society

    No source reports that the AI guidance changed preparations or losses in Jamaica. The forecasts covered a Category 5 landfall that affected about 1.6 million people, and the IFRC approved its emergency allocation five days ahead.

What happened

The US National Hurricane Center (NHC) blended Google DeepMind's experimental AI hurricane model into official forecasts for Tropical Storm Melissa from its first advisory on 21 October 2025. NHC's guidance labels the model's ensemble mean GDMI. Discussion 1 placed the official track between the DeepMind ensemble mean and the corrected consensus aid HCCA. Discussion 2 described the track as a blend of the DeepMind mean, HCCA and the simple consensus TVCA. Discussion 8, on 23 October, moved the track toward a blend of the DeepMind mean and HCCA. Later that day, Discussion 10 blended the HCCA and GDMI aids. It set an intensity forecast in best agreement with GDMI after all 50 DeepMind members showed a major hurricane. Also on 23 October, the International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC) approved a CHF 80,000 Imminent DREF (Disaster Response Emergency Fund) allocation with a five-day lead time. On 24 October, Discussion 13 noted that every DeepMind member showed the system becoming a Category 4 hurricane or stronger. Discussion 14 put the intensity forecast closest to GDMI. On 28 October 2025 Melissa made landfall in southwestern Jamaica near New Hope with winds of 185 miles per hour and a pressure of 892 millibars. The IFRC reported 45 deaths and about 1.6 million people affected. It relayed an estimate by the World Bank and the Inter-American Development Bank of US$8.8 billion in physical damage. On 3 November 2025 Michael Lowry, a hurricane specialist, wrote that the model gave NHC forecasters extra confidence to issue uncharacteristically aggressive forecasts. CNN reported on 3 December 2025 that the DeepMind ensemble gave NHC forecasters an unusually high degree of confidence in Melissa's rapid intensification about three days before landfall. NHC's own report on Melissa, first issued on 25 February 2026, says that this was the first time it had predicted Category 5 strength from a Category 1 start. Its Melissa track errors at 120 hours were less than half its five-year mean. The DeepMind ensemble mean alone had lower track errors than the official forecast at 48 hours, 32.6 against 40.3 nautical miles. At 120 hours its errors were higher, 113.2 against 87.7 nautical miles. NHC's 2025 verification report, dated 30 March 2026, says that its track errors were notably lower than average for major hurricanes, including Melissa.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The DeepMind ensemble provided possible storm tracks and intensities, including an ensemble mean that forecasters compared with conventional guidance.

Workflow

Experimental Google DeepMind ensemble guidance available in real time to NHC forecasters. Forecasters choosing to blend GDMI with consensus aids (HCCA) in official track and intensity forecasts. A slow-moving, rapidly intensifying hurricane where models disagreed on track relative to Jamaica.

Human oversight

NHC forecasters selected how much weight to give the AI guidance and issued the official forecast after comparing it with other models and observations.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

National Hurricane Center forecasters. People in Melissa’s path were the intended beneficiaries of the forecasts.

Measurement

Measurement

NHC compared forecasts at matching lead times with its final estimates of the storm’s position and intensity. It compared official forecasts with model guidance on a common sample. [8] [9] [10]

Setting and sample

Hurricane Melissa in the Atlantic, 21–31 October 2025. The report compares forecasts at lead times through 120 hours. [8] [9] [10]

Measures

Track forecast error, intensity forecast error, and forecast lead time. [8] [9] [10]

Results

GDMI’s track forecasts were comparable to NHC’s official forecasts through 96 hours. NHC performed slightly better at 120 hours and had lower intensity errors than every guidance model through 96 hours. [8] [9] [10]

Additional value from AI

NHC credits GDMI and regional models jointly with increasing forecasters’ confidence at unusually long lead times. It does not isolate GDMI’s effect on the official forecast. [8] [9] [10]

Limitations

The sources establish that forecasters used the guidance. They do not establish that it changed protective actions, reduced losses, or prevented deaths. [8] [9] [10]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Forecasters need timely model output, checks before operational use, and conventional guidance for comparison. Later observations are needed to assess forecast accuracy.
