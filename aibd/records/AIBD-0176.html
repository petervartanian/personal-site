What happened

Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia working paper 18–15 is by Julapa Jagtiani (Philadelphia Fed) and Catharine Lemieux (Chicago Fed), revised January 2019. Its title is “The Roles of Alternative Data and Machine Learning in Fintech Lending: Evidence from the LendingClub Consumer Platform”. It compares account-level LendingClub loans with similar loans in Y-14M reports from bank holding companies with assets of $50 billion or more. The authors report that the correlation between LendingClub's rating grades and FICO scores declined. It was about 80 percent for 2007 originations and about 35 percent for 2014–2015 vintages. The grades perform well in predicting loan performance over the two years after origination. Alternative data allowed some borrowers who would have been classified as subprime by traditional criteria to be slotted into better grades. They got lower-priced credit.

For the same default risk consumers pay smaller spreads at LendingClub than on credit cards. For 2015 loans about 8 percent of A-rated and 28 percent of B-rated borrowers had FICO scores below 680. Crossref lists a journal version in Financial Management (DOI 10.1111/fima.12295) issued 6 November 2019, which is the date used. The working paper itself carries only a month.

What it was built to do. Assign rating grades and price consumer loans on the LendingClub marketplace.

The system's part

Not assessed. The paper attributes the regrading of subprime borrowers to alternative data used by the lender. It does not establish that machine learning produced the grades. LendingClub developed and deployed its own model. The Federal Reserve authors are independent.

Replication

What produced it. Account-level LendingClub data compared with Y-14M bank data for similar loans, 2007 to 2015 vintages. Lender grading that increasingly used information not captured by FICO.

What must hold again. Alternative data that predicts repayment beyond FICO. Grades that keep predicting loan performance (the paper reports good prediction over two years after origination).
