AlpEW reportedly stops Rome–Naples trains during an earthquake

Rete Ferroviaria Italiana's AlpEW seismic early-warning system reportedly stopped Rome–Naples trains during a magnitude 4.4 earthquake; its developers describe no learned AI model.

The system’s part
The original developer study describes AlpEW as signal processing with empirical ground-motion prediction and probabilistic alert thresholds. Filter Picker detects transients, Train Marker distinguishes train vibration from seismic signals, and railway controls impose restrictions automatically. The authors discuss machine learning as a possible alternative, but do not establish a learned AI model in the deployed system. The study’s operational observations cover March 2020–January 2022 and do not verify the M 4.4 stop reported in 2025 news coverage.
Limits
The original technical study identifies AlpEW and its signal-processing methods. It does not verify the magnitude 4.4 stop described in later news coverage, identify a learned AI model or establish an accident prevented.

What happened

According to Euronews, Rete Ferroviaria Italiana's seismic early-warning system on the Rome-Naples high-speed line had been operational since 3 May 2025. Euronews reported that in the following weeks it was tested by a magnitude 4.4 earthquake, during which trains on the Rome-Naples line were stopped preventively. A magnitude 4.4 earthquake struck Campi Flegrei on 13 May 2025, and Quotidiano Nazionale reported that high-speed trains in the Naples node were suspended that day. No source says that the warning system made that stop. ANSA reported on 14 July 2025 that Ferrovie dello Stato's infrastructure company Rete Ferroviaria Italiana had activated Italy's first seismic early-warning system on the line. It was developed with the Physics Department of the University of Naples Federico II. Technical support came from the Railway Technical Research Institute of the Japanese railways. A network of latest-generation sensors detects the earliest seismic waves. Within seconds it automatically activates safety protocols to slow or stop trains. ANSA does not mention artificial intelligence. Euronews added on 15 July 2025 that the system comprises about 20 seismic stations along 200 km, on the surface and in deep boreholes. They are linked by a synchronous digital hierarchy (SDH) fiber network. According to Euronews, an advanced software intelligence instantly analyzes the data and assesses the danger of the quake. Euronews attributes the magnitude 4.4 episode to RailTech and places it within two weeks of the 3 May 2025 start. Neither article gives an exact date for that earthquake. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor lists the system as AI-powered and a protective response. In January 2026 the developers published a peer-reviewed study of the system in Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences. They describe signal processing with empirical ground-motion prediction and probabilistic alert thresholds. They describe no learned AI model in the deployed system. Their operational observations cover March 2020 to January 2022 and do not verify the magnitude 4.4 stop. The developers wrote that Rete Ferroviaria Italiana had decided to extend the system to other high-speed lines. Wired Italia reported on 24 May 2026 that the system was still in use and that its developers were studying machine learning to refine it. No report shows a second line in service by October 2026.

Details

AI system

Model

The platform was AlpEW. It combined Filter Picker and Train Marker signal processing with empirical ground-motion equations and probability thresholds; a learned AI model is not established.

Inputs and outputs

Seismic sensors detected early waves and sent measurements over fiber for software to assess earthquake danger.

Autonomy

The system automatically initiated protocols to slow or stop trains within seconds.

Workflow

Dense accelerometric stations along a high-speed line in a seismic region. Low-latency fiber links to signaling. Automatic P-wave analysis with thresholds tied to train control.

Human oversight

The system imposed emergency restrictions automatically. Maintenance staff checked the line, and the traffic controller authorized reopening.

Benefits

Duration

The system began operating on the Rome-Naples line on 3 May 2025, and Wired Italia reported on 24 May 2026 that it was still in use there. The developers wrote in January 2026 that Rete Ferroviaria Italiana had decided to extend it to other high-speed lines. No report shows a second line in service by October 2026. People and places

Dependencies

The deployment required surface and borehole seismic stations, an SDH fiber network and integration with railway safety controls.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

P-waves arrive early enough before damaging shaking to brake trains. False triggers stay rare enough to keep the automatic stop enabled.
