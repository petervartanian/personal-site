What happened

The OECD AI Incidents entry says that Israel “deployed its AI-powered Iron Beam laser defense system for the first time in combat to intercept Hezbollah missile barrages”. It separately bundles a Hankyoreh item (dated 10 October 2023) on Hamas rockets penetrating Iron Dome. The Iron Beam claim rests on social-media footage from Monday 2 March 2026, not 28 February. Ynet (Yair Kraus) reports that the footage “appeared to show the first operational use” of the system. It was delivered to the Israeli Air Force in late December 2025. Hezbollah fired rockets and drones from Lebanon after the killing of Iran's Supreme Leader, with one drone intercepted near Shlomi. The Jerusalem Post (Seth Frantzman, 2 March 2026) says that the circulated video “did not show lasers intercepting rockets”.

TimesNow (2 March 2026) says that whether this was Iron Beam's first combat use “awaits official confirmation”. Army Recognition (3 March 2026) says that officials “have not formally confirmed the specific engagements” and that no authoritative combat log names the system. The Conversation (4 March 2026) calls the reports unconfirmed. Moneycontrol (3 March 2026) sources its “first-ever use” to an “Israel War Room” social-media video. The Times of Israel reports the Defense Ministry's handover of the first operational system on 28 December 2025, after tests against rockets, mortars and drones. Times of Israel/AFP (16 March 2026) reports that the IDF “has so far refused to comment on its use during the ongoing war”. Times of Israel (Stav Levaton, 27 March 2026) reports that “military officials said it has not yet been declared operational within the IDF”.

Times of Israel (Emanuel Fabian, 30 June 2026) reports that the one deployed system “has seen limited use and has not yet been declared operational within the IDF”. A senior officer called its coverage partial. The only confirmed laser combat use is aggregate and earlier. A lower-powered, shorter-range variant shot down some 35 drones from Lebanon in 2024 (Times of Israel/AFP). The Ministry of Defense announced it in May 2025 (JPost). The MoD said that no Iron Beam components were involved (Wikipedia). No cited source describes Iron Beam as an AI system. The only AI mention found is Rafael's CEO framing machine learning as a future capability (Breaking Defense, 22 October 2025). That piece is not among the sources. Metro, Economic Times, MoneyControl's second item, Hankyoreh and others among the Monitor's 20 listed sources are syndications or explainers.

What it was built to do. Iron Beam is a high-energy laser built to intercept rockets, mortars and drones at short range. It is a low-cost complement to Iron Dome, David's Sling and Arrow. Interception of Hezbollah projectiles is its stated purpose.

The system's part

Not assessed. No cited source describes Iron Beam as an AI or machine-learning system. Those sources are Ynet, JPost, Army Recognition, TimesNow, The Conversation and Moneycontrol. They also include Times of Israel 28 December 2025, 16 Mar, 27 Mar and 30 June 2026, and Wikipedia. They describe a high-power laser with a beam director and electro-optical targeting. The Monitor's “AI-enabled” label is its own. Rafael's CEO described machine learning as a future capability (Breaking Defense, 22 October 2025). The IDF has not confirmed the engagement itself, so neither an AI role nor a contribution can be assessed.

Replication

What produced it. A ground-based high-energy laser that holds its beam on a short-range target within line of sight until it is destroyed. JPost describes the terrain, weather and dwell-time limits. Integration into the Israeli Air Force's layered air-defense array as a complement to Iron Dome.

What must hold again. IDF confirmation that Iron Beam, not Iron Dome, other interceptors or counter-UAS effects, made the 2 March 2026 interception. Line of sight and clear weather. A senior officer said in May 2026 that the system's coverage is partial and that at least 14 batteries are sought.
