Girls in Abuja schools use generative AI to make art on girls' rights

The Rising Hope Girls' Educational Foundation reports that girls in Abuja schools used generative tools such as ChatGPT and DALL-E to make art and advocacy projects.

  • 2 a person

    The foundation reports that girls created AI-assisted digital artworks, narratives and advocacy projects. Long-term effects on learning and employment are unknown.

  • Not rated an organization

    The workshops, the $750 award and the training of 312 teachers are program activities. No source reports a measured gain for the foundation that came from using AI.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
Not isolated. Students used generative tools such as ChatGPT, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion and OpenArt.ai to turn artwork into digital images and narratives. Teachers, volunteers and partner NGOs ran the workshops, and no source separates what the tools added from that teaching.

Created artworks and advocacy activities are reported outputs. They do not quantify gains in literacy, STEM learning, or lasting digital inclusion. The projected reach of more than 1,000 teachers and 7,000 students must not be treated as measured beneficiaries already helped.

What happened

The Rising Hope Girls' Educational Foundation (RHGE) runs workshops in community schools in Abuja, Nigeria. In its project account for the 2025 AI for Good report of the International Telecommunication Union, RHGE reports that girls in Nigerian community schools created AI-assisted art and advocacy projects in the workshops. RHGE says that the activities expand creative practice. No source quantifies learning effects. RHGE wrote on 6 March 2025 that students used AI to create artwork on girls' rights issues for a Ciena Solutions Challenge project. On 16 April 2025 Digital Promise listed that project among 42 teams that received a $750 Sustainability Award. On 22 September 2025 Punch reported that a grant to RHGE from the Development Research and Projects Centre was meant to set up digital labs in ten communities. RHGE later reported that it had trained 312 teachers in generative AI tools by March 2026.

The program uses pretrained generative tools to develop student art, visual stories, and STEM activities through locally adapted prompts.

Conditions that could produce the benefit are unspecified.
