A planned AI advisor would flag heat-vulnerable city areas for cooling

Christ University, Bangalore, is developing an AI-enabled urban heat advisor that would identify vulnerable areas and support targeted cooling and energy decisions.

    No heat-related illness or death is shown to have been prevented.

  • Not rated society

    Heat resilience and equitable cooling are planned benefits.

What happened

The International Telecommunication Union entry names Christ University, Bangalore, as the developer. The code repository that the entry names holds one commit, made on 17 April 2025, with one empty file. In the entry, current as of June 2025, the team describes a minimum viable product in development that uses proposed modeling and vulnerability assessment to inform heat mitigation. The intended beneficiaries include people exposed to extreme heat and city planners. Wider climate-risk integration is planned. No source reports an implemented cooling action, a measured temperature change or a health outcome.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The proposed advisor combines satellite images, connected-sensor feeds, and socioeconomic indicators to map heat and vulnerability and suggest mitigation priorities.
