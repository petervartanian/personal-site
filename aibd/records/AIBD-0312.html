APS AI camera spots Diamond Fire; crews hold it under 7 acres

depth 2 for society

contained it before it grew past 7 acres (2.8 hectares)

AP: an APS AI camera detected smoke on a March 2026 afternoon and crews contained the Diamond Fire before 7 acres; the Arizona Daily Sun's account of 26 March 2026 gives the timeline but no camera.

What happened

The Associated Press (Dorany Pineda and Brittany Peterson, 4 May 2026; read in the KPBS and Courthouse News republications) reported that on a March afternoon artificial intelligence detected something resembling smoke on a camera feed from Arizona's Coconino National Forest: one of dozens of AI cameras installed for the utility Arizona Public Service had spotted early signs of what came to be known as the Diamond Fire, and firefighters raced to the scene and contained it before it grew past 7 acres (2.8 hectares). AP says APS has nearly 40 active AI smoke-detection cameras and plans 71 by summer's end, that APS meteorologist Cindy Kobold says the technology notifies the utility about 45 minutes faster on average than the first 911 call, and that Pano AI, launched in 2020 and charging around $50,000 a year per camera, counts APS among its customers and says it detected 725 wildfires in the US last year; AP does not state outright which company made the camera that spotted the Diamond Fire. The Arizona Daily Sun (staff report, 26 March 2026) reports that a small fire later named the Diamond Fire began shortly after 2 p.m. on Thursday just north of Lake Mary Road, about three miles south-east of Lake Mary Road and Beulah Boulevard, south of Pine Canyon Golf Club and east of Flagstaff Pulliam Airport, reached seven acres by 3:16 p.m. according to WatchDuty, had its forward progress stopped at 3:37 p.m., about 80 minutes after the first report, and was 100% contained at 6:09 p.m.; it does not mention a camera, the Coconino National Forest or who made the first report. The two accounts are matched by the fire's name, month and size; no official incident record naming the camera was found (InciWeb's 'Diamond Fire' is a June 2023 Scottsdale fire). The date observed is the Daily Sun's publication date, the Thursday of the fire.

What it was built to do. Built to detect smoke on camera feeds and notify the utility and fire agencies earlier than 911 calls; this detection was that purpose.

The system's part

AP reports that the camera's AI detected something resembling smoke and spotted early signs of the fire; it does not say whether the alert was the first report or what the fire agencies did with it, and the Daily Sun's timeline (first report, 7 acres by 3:16 p.m., progress stopped 3:37 p.m., contained 6:09 p.m.) does not mention the camera. The attribution to AI rests on one news report, and the camera's maker is implied rather than stated; no official record naming the camera was found.

Replication

What produced it. An AI smoke-detection camera network installed for a utility covering national-forest land near Flagstaff. Human review of the AI detection (AP: human intervention keeps false positives low) and notification to the utility and fire agencies. Crews able to reach a small fire on a March afternoon within about 80 minutes of the first report.

What must hold again. The camera has line of sight to the ignition and the detection reaches a responding agency before the fire grows. Fire weather and fuels that allow containment at a few acres; the sources give no conditions for this fire.
