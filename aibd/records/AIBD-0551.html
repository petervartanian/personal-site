Kingston reports shorter social-care documentation with AI

Kingston reports that Magic Notes cuts documentation time by 50–60%, with supervision write-ups falling from 40 minutes to under 10.

ⓘ

What happened

Kingston piloted Beam Magic Notes to transcribe and summarize adult-social-care conversations, assessments, and supervision. The council reports 50–60% less time completing notes and assessments, and supervision write-ups falling from 40 minutes to under 10 minutes. The source reports limitations for people with low verbal skills or mental capacity and with poor audio quality.

Purpose. Reduce social-care documentation work by transcribing and summarizing conversations into usable notes and assessments.

AI’s contribution

Magic Notes transcribes and summarizes care and supervision conversations. Staff review, amend, and supplement outputs. The council attributes documentation savings to this assisted workflow, without establishing improved service-user outcomes.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    Reported reductions in case-note, assessment, and supervision writing are task-time savings.

  • a person

    Workers report less documentation effort and time. Improvements in service users’ health or care are unmeasured.

Further evidence

Setting. The pilot began with a small social-worker group and expanded, using templates adapted with council feedback. The tool supports mobile recording and produces material for the existing case-management workflow.

Conditions. Practitioners are expected to obtain participants’ consent for recording. The source reports that poor audio and limited verbal detail can reduce transcript quality, and staff retain responsibility for editing outputs.
