Smartwatch alert under 40 bpm reveals ciprofloxacin bradycardia

by the next day her heart rate was 60-70 bpm

A smartwatch alert for a heart rate under 40 bpm two days after discharge on ciprofloxacin led to an ED visit; telemetry showed 30-50 bpm, the antibiotic was switched and the rate normalised.

What happened

Cordova Sanchez, Chohan, Olatunde and White of SUNY Upstate Medical University, Syracuse, reported in the Journal of Investigative Medicine High Impact Case Reports (published 24 January 2022; the event date is not given, so the publication date is used) a 32-year-old woman seen two days after discharge from a hospital stay for cholangitis, during which she had an ERCP and was sent home on ciprofloxacin and metronidazole. She developed dizziness on minimal exertion, and her smartwatch triggered an alert for a sustained heart rate below 40 beats per minute, prompting her to attend an outside emergency department, where telemetry confirmed 30-50 bpm; she was transferred to the authors' hospital for cardiology evaluation. Her ECG showed sinus bradycardia at 51 bpm, against 66 bpm before the antibiotics; the procedural drugs given two days earlier have short half-lives and other work-up (blood tests, thyroid function, Lyme serology, drug screen, echocardiogram) was unremarkable. Ciprofloxacin was replaced with ceftriaxone and by the next day her heart rate was 60-70 bpm; she was discharged on cefpodoxime and metronidazole. The authors conclude ciprofloxacin was the likely cause and that early recognition of the bradycardia prevented a potential adverse event. The watch's brand and model are not stated; the authors describe wearable heart-rate detection generically as photoplethysmography.

What it was built to do. Smartwatch low-heart-rate alerts are built to prompt the wearer to seek review; this alert was that purpose. The authors add that consumer wearables may surface less commonly reported medication side effects.

The system's part

The watch's sustained low-heart-rate alert, combined with her symptoms, is what the authors say prompted the hospital visit; telemetry confirmed the rate and clinicians identified ciprofloxacin by exclusion and re-challenge-free withdrawal. The threshold alert is a simple rule on a photoplethysmographic heart-rate estimate, and the brand is unknown, so no learned model can be credited.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • A wearer on a newly prescribed drug whose smartwatch has a low-heart-rate alert enabled
  • Symptoms vague enough that the alert, not the symptoms alone, prompted presentation
  • Clinicians who excluded other causes and withdrew the suspected drug

What must hold again

  • The bradycardia is sustained long enough to trip the watch's threshold
  • The wearer treats the alert as a reason to seek care
