Smartwatch ECG captures 165 bpm tachycardia; Emory diagnoses VT

A smartwatch ECG recorded a 165 bpm wide-complex tachycardia during palpitations; VT was confirmed on telemetry, an ICD placed and later shocked VT, and ablation stopped the VT (JACC Case Rep 2025).

Workflow

A wearer who activates the watch's ECG recording during a transient symptomatic episode. A clinician who reviews the single-lead trace rather than dismissing consumer data. Access to telemetry, cardiac MRI, electrophysiology study, ICD implantation and ablation.

Relation to AI

The device's contribution was recording a user-triggered single-lead ECG and issuing a generic high-heart-rate warning. It did not classify the rhythm as VT, which the authors say no wearable algorithm is validated to do. Clinicians read the QRS morphology on the smartwatch trace and on the 12-lead ECG, and made every treatment decision. The brand is unknown.

Field

Healthcare (cardiology; electrophysiology). Consumer wearables.

Purpose

Smartwatch ECG apps are built to record a single-lead trace and warn of high heart rates. Capturing the VT during symptoms was that function. The authors note no smartwatch algorithm is validated to detect VT and the diagnosis came from clinicians reading the trace.
