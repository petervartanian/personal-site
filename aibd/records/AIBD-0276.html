Bradford gallery shows a painting that software matched to a Raphael

Comparing faces, a University of Bradford deep-learning model found 97% facial similarity with the Sistine Madonna in the de Brecy Tondo; Cartwright Hall showed it, and scholars called it a copy.

What happened

Hassan Ugail (University of Bradford) led a facial-recognition deep-learning comparison with University of Nottingham colleagues. It was reported on 26 January 2023 (Artforum). It found 97% similarity between the Madonna of the de Brecy Tondo and Raphael's Sistine Madonna, and 86% for the child. Ugail said that the two “are undoubtedly by the same artist” (BBC, 25 July 2023). The painting, owned by the de Brecy Trust, went on public display at Cartwright Hall Art Gallery, Bradford, from July 2023. The Art Newspaper (26 July 2023) quoted Raphael scholars rejecting the attribution. An anonymous specialist said “The picture is clearly a copy from the 19th century”. Angelamaria Aceto (Ashmolean) would not support an AI-based assessment. Artnet (18 September 2023) reported that Art Recognition's model found with 85% probability that the Tondo was not painted by Raphael. The same company had earlier asserted with 92% certainty that the National Gallery's Samson and Delilah might not be by Rubens. In a release of 21 December 2023 the University of Bradford restated that the computer had determined the Tondo to be a Raphael. The release says that this result built on earlier pigment analysis by Howell Edwards. The display was extended “due to popular demand” to 3 January 2024, according to the Bradford Museums page. That page calls it “a mystery painting found to be undoubtedly by Raphael”. Art Recognition's case study states that the Bradford group later trained a second model on 49 images, which again returned an authentic result. No source on record shows a museum or auction house accepting an AI attribution.
