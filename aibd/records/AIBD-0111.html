Google alerts Alanya phones seconds before a M5.1 quake

Android users near Alanya, Turkey, got Google's earthquake alert seconds before a M5.1 (AFAD 5.3) offshore quake on 5 January 2022.

ⓘ

What happened

At 06:21 local time on 5 January 2022 a magnitude 5.1 (USGS; AFAD 5.3) earthquake struck the Mediterranean about 57 km off Alanya, Antalya, Turkey. Depth: roughly 40 km. Yeni Şafak and DonanımHaber reported the same day on Google's Android Earthquake Alerts System, introduced in Turkey in June 2021. It sent an audible and visual alert for a magnitude 5.3 quake to Android users seconds before the shaking. Hürriyet put the lead at about 10 seconds, while NTV and İndigo Dergisi claimed about a minute. AFAD and the Alanya district governor reported no damage or injuries. On 3 February 2022 Google said the system had alerted users to two January quakes centered on Alanya and Balıkesir.

Purpose. Built to detect earthquakes from Android phones' accelerometers and alert users in the expected shaking zone seconds before the slower destructive waves arrive. It covers quakes of magnitude 4.5 and above. This alert was that intended function.

AI’s contribution

The alert was produced end to end by Google's system. Accelerometer triggers from many phones were combined on Google's servers to detect the quake and estimate its size. Alerts were pushed to phones in the expected shaking zone ahead of the slower S-waves. Google's own descriptions of this process do not use the words AI or machine learning. The OECD Monitor classifies it as an AI system doing event detection and forecasting, and the 2025 Science paper calls it smartphone-based earthquake detection algorithms. The reported lead time varies by outlet (seconds, about 10 seconds, about one minute); Google promises seconds.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    An unstated number of Android users near Alanya received a few seconds' audible and visual notice of the quake. AFAD and the district governor said it caused no damage or injuries. No protective action by a recipient is documented. Hürriyet reported on 7 January 2022 that most users who posted about the alert said they panicked.

Further evidence

Setting. A magnitude 5.1 (AFAD 5.3) quake above the 4.5 alert threshold, centered about 57 km offshore and roughly 40 km deep. So the push alert could outrun the slower S-waves to the coast. Enough Android phones near the epicenter with Earthquake Alerts on to trigger Google's server-side detection. Per Google's 3 February 2022 presentation it runs on Android 5.0 and later and users can switch it off. Crowdsourced phone detection was the only input: Google partners with the ShakeAlert seismic network only in California, Oregon and Washington.

Conditions. The server's magnitude and location estimate must be right and early. The 2025 Science paper reports only 36% of alert recipients got the alert before shaking. Recipients must know what to do with seconds of notice. Hürriyet reported on 7 January 2022 that most users who posted about the Alanya alert said they panicked. The phone must have location and the Earthquake Alerts setting enabled.
