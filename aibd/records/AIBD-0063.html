Lunit INSIGHT CXR flags 13 resectable lung cancers on routine X-rays

Lunit INSIGHT CXR flagged nodules on chest radiographs taken for other reasons in 13 patients at Yongin Severance Hospital; all 13 had their lung cancer removed, and seven were stage I.

The system’s part
The software flagged all 13 nodules and its results were visible to ordering clinicians on PACS immediately after the radiograph. The authors assume from medical-record timestamps that the AI results aided the same-day consultations in 8 of 13 patients. They acknowledge that whether clinicians acted on the AI result or on the radiologist's report cannot be settled from the study. Radiologists also read the films. In 12 of 13 cases the radiologist's report described an abnormality at the site. There the contribution is earlier attention to findings nobody was looking for. In one (patient 12) the report read “No active lung disease”, so the AI flagged a cancer that the formal report missed.

What happened

At Yongin Severance Hospital, Yongin, South Korea, the deep-learning software Lunit INSIGHT CXR (versions 2 and 3, Lunit Inc.) was applied to chest radiographs. Kwak and colleagues reported in PLOS ONE on 10 March 2023 on 75 patients with resectable lung cancer between March 2020 and February 2022. Of these, 13 (17.3%) had cancers found incidentally because the software flagged a nodule on a radiograph taken for an unrelated reason. One was a 60-year-old man, a current smoker, imaged before cholecystectomy, whose left upper lobe adenocarcinoma was stage IA1. He saw a pulmonologist three days later, after the radiologist's report. The paper reports that eight of the 13 saw a pulmonologist the same day, before the radiologist's official report. Its Table 1 is not fully consistent with this figure. All 13 had resection, and seven were stage I and needed no further treatment. The authors declare no competing interests and report university and government funding. In Frontiers in Medicine on 7 August 2024, the same hospital group reports that the software stayed in use on every chest radiograph. From March 2021 to January 2023, it flagged nodules in 1,754 patients seen in non-respiratory outpatient clinics. Of these, 512 had computed tomography, and 36 were diagnosed with lung cancer. In 2026, physicians at Hacettepe University and in Chennai reported five similar incidental early lung cancers flagged by a different product, qXR.

Details

AI system

Release

Lunit INSIGHT CXR versions 2 and 3.

Role

The software flagged suspicious nodules on chest X-rays for clinicians to review.

Configuration

The studies used versions 2 and 3 of the system.

Inputs and outputs

Lunit analyzed chest radiographs and displayed nodule flags to ordering clinicians through PACS immediately after imaging.

Workflow

Routine AI analysis of chest radiographs with results visible to ordering clinicians in real time. A prompt pulmonology referral pathway for AI-flagged nodules.

Human oversight

Ordering clinicians could refer flagged patients to pulmonology, while radiologists still read the films. Subsequent investigation and surgery established the cancer diagnoses.

AI contribution

Lunit INSIGHT CXR flagged all 13 nodules and made the results available to clinicians immediately after the chest X-rays. In one patient, it flagged a nodule that proved cancerous where the radiologist had reported no active lung disease.

Benefits

Reach

Thirteen patients had lung cancer found incidentally after the software flagged a nodule, and all 13 had surgery. The 2024 study reports 36 lung cancer diagnoses among 512 patients who had computed tomography after a flag in non-respiratory clinics. [1] [2]

Duration

The same hospital group reports that the software stayed in use on all chest radiographs from March 2021. Its flags in non-respiratory clinics led to 36 lung cancer diagnoses through January 2023. No source follows the 13 patients after surgery. [1] [2]

Measurement

Measurement

Retrospective review of medical records, radiology reports, AI abnormality scores, consultation timestamps and pathology. [1]

Setting and sample

75 patients with surgically resected lung cancer at Yongin Severance Hospital from March 2020 to February 2022, of whom 13 had cancers found incidentally on radiographs taken for other reasons. [1]

Measures

Number of incidental resectable cancers flagged by the software, stage at resection, and timing of pulmonology consultation relative to the radiologist's report. [1]

Results

All 13 nodules were flagged; 8 patients saw a pulmonologist on the day of the radiograph before the official report; 7 were stage I. [1]

Additional value from AI

Not measured; the authors assume from timestamps that the AI results prompted the same-day consultations. [1]

Limitations

Small single-hospital case series with no control group and no survival data; clinicians may have acted on the radiologist's report. [1]

Denominators

AI analysis of every chest radiograph, with results shown in the image archive immediately. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Clinicians review AI flags on films ordered for non-pulmonary reasons. CT follow-up and tissue diagnosis for flagged nodules.

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.
