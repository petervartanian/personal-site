Lunit INSIGHT CXR finds 13 resectable lung cancers incidentally

13 (17.3%) had cancers found incidentally because the software flagged a nodule

AI on routine chest X-rays flagged nodules in 13 patients not suspected of lung cancer; all had resectable cancer, 7 stage I, and surgery.

What happened

At Yongin Severance Hospital, Yongin, South Korea, the deep-learning software Lunit INSIGHT CXR (versions 2 and 3, Lunit Inc.) was applied to chest radiographs. Kwak and colleagues reported in PLOS ONE on 10 March 2023 that among 75 patients with resectable lung cancer between March 2020 and February 2022, 13 (17.3%) had cancers found incidentally because the software flagged a nodule on a radiograph taken for an unrelated reason, for example a 60-year-old man, a current smoker, imaged before cholecystectomy, whose left upper lobe adenocarcinoma was stage IA1 (he saw a pulmonologist three days later, after the radiologist's report). The paper reports that eight of the 13 saw a pulmonologist the same day, before the radiologist's official report (its Table 1 is not fully consistent with this figure); all 13 had resection and seven were stage I needing no further treatment.

What it was built to do. Lunit INSIGHT CXR is built to detect and localize abnormalities, including nodules, on chest radiographs; incidental detection of lung cancer is its intended function.

The system's part

The software flagged all 13 nodules and its results were visible to ordering clinicians on PACS immediately after the radiograph; the authors assume from medical-record timestamps that the AI results aided the same-day consultations in 8 of 13 patients, and acknowledge that whether clinicians acted on the AI result or on the radiologist's report cannot be settled from the study. Radiologists also read the films. In 12 of 13 cases the radiologist's report described an abnormality at the site, so there the contribution is earlier attention to findings nobody was looking for; in one (patient 12) the report read 'No active lung disease', so the AI flagged a cancer that the formal report missed.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • Routine AI analysis of chest radiographs with results visible to ordering clinicians in real time
  • A prompt pulmonology referral pathway for AI-flagged nodules

What must hold again

  • Clinicians review AI flags on films ordered for non-pulmonary reasons
  • CT follow-up and tissue diagnosis for flagged nodules

Harm on record

AI Incident Database 81, Researchers find evidence of racial, gender, and socioeconomic bias in chest X-ray classifiers. Same system class and task, AI classification of routine chest radiographs: the classifiers studied showed gender, racial and socioeconomic bias that underdiagnoses patients, the group Lunit's incidental nodule flags benefited.
