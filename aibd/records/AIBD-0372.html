A muscle-signal “smart hand” returns daily tasks to Wang Bing

What happened

A Guangming Daily feature on 13 August 2024 on Chongqing's Liangjiang New Area opens with Wang Bing, who lost both hands in an accident a few years earlier, leaving half of each forearm. He says he now uses the “smart hand” to eat with a spoon, drink from a cup, shake hands and rotate his wrist, manages daily life on his own, and can grip a pen and write his name. Xie Haiqiong, director of the Chongqing Bio-Intelligent Manufacturing Research Institute at Mingyue Lake, says researchers devised a scheme that detects and identifies electrical signals at suitable muscle sites and processes them through algorithmic analysis, so the user controls the hand by intention rather than passive motion.

Fiscal funds arriving since 2023 under the district's grant-to-equity model supported the project, and the institute formed a company to incubate the exoskeleton project (Guangming Daily via Haiwainet and NetEase). The fitting date is not given; the report date is used.

What it was built to do. Built to let a person with amputated hands control a prosthetic hand through muscle signals; the daily-task function Wang Bing describes is that purpose. The feature frames the project as an example of the district's policy for commercializing research.

The system's part

Not assessed. The only description of the technology is institute director Xie Haiqiong's, as quoted by Guangming Daily: a scheme that detects and identifies electrical signals at suitable muscle sites and processes them through algorithmic analysis, letting the user control the hand by intention. No source names a machine-learning method, model, training procedure or accuracy figure, and the Monitor's “AI-driven” label rests on that description. The functional gains come from Wang Bing's own account in a newspaper feature about the district's commercialization policy; no clinician or independent evaluation is cited.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Electrical signals detectable at suitable muscle sites on the residual forearms. An algorithmic scheme that identifies those signals and maps them to hand and wrist movements. Institute researchers fitting and tuning the device for one user, with district fiscal grants converted to equity.

What must hold again. Enough residual muscle to produce usable signals. Training time for the user to control the hand by intention. Continued funding and a company to maintain and produce the device.
