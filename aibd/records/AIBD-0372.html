A man without hands eats and writes with a muscle-signal “smart hand”

Guangming Daily reports that Wang Bing, who lost both hands, eats, drinks and writes his name with a Chongqing institute's “smart hand”, which decodes muscle signals by algorithm.

  • 3 a person

    Per Guangming Daily, Wang Bing, who lost both hands, says he eats with a spoon, drinks from a cup, shakes hands and rotates his wrist. He writes his name and manages daily life alone with the device. A capability restored, reported in his words without clinical assessment.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

What happened

In 2021 the Chongqing Bio-Intelligent Manufacturing Research Institute at Mingyue Lake received requests from clinicians at several large hospitals for a prosthetic arm that patients could move by intention. Xie Haiqiong, the institute's director, says that researchers devised a scheme. It detects and identifies electrical signals at suitable muscle sites and processes them by algorithmic analysis. The scheme lets the user control the hand by intention, without passive motion. Fiscal funds arriving since 2023 under the district's grant-to-equity model supported the project. The institute formed a company to incubate the exoskeleton project. A Guangming Daily feature of 13 August 2024 on Chongqing's Liangjiang New Area opens with Wang Bing (Guangming Daily via Haiwainet and NetEase). He lost both hands in an accident a few years earlier, leaving half of each forearm. He says that he now uses the “smart hand” to eat with a spoon, drink from a cup, shake hands and rotate his wrist. He manages daily life alone and can grip a pen and write his name. The feature writes his name as 王炳. The fitting date is not given.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Residual-muscle electrical signals were analyzed and converted into movements of the prosthetic hand.

Autonomy

Wang Bing controlled the hand through his muscle signals and intended movements.

Workflow

Electrical signals detectable at suitable muscle sites on the residual forearms. An algorithmic scheme that identifies those signals and maps them to hand and wrist movements. Institute researchers fitting and tuning the device for one user, with district fiscal grants converted to equity.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Wang Bing reported greater independence in eating, drinking and writing after losing both hands.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Enough residual muscle to produce usable signals. Training time for the user to control the hand by intention. Continued funding and a company to maintain and produce the device.
