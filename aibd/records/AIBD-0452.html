A map-based digital twin aims to cut energy use at 5G base stations

Amrita University proposes a network digital twin that combines geographic data with AI-supported radio adjustments to run 5G and 6G base stations on less energy; no measured result is reported.

  • Not rated an organization

    No source reports a measured change in energy use or network quality at a base station in operation.

  • Not rated society

    Reduced carbon emissions and better smart-city connectivity remain intended effects.

What happened

The proposal connects Open Radio Access Network (OpenRAN) information with geographic data and AI-supported radio adjustments. Better responsiveness, coverage, and energy efficiency are intended outcomes. The source does not report a measured change in an operating network. The team names Amrita University, ESRI ArcGIS, the Indian Institute of Science and the Indian Open Source Mobile Congress as partners. Its future work lists the integration of ArcGIS into the 5G core and a digital twin proof of concept. No later report of either appears.
