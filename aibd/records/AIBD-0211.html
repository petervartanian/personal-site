o3-assisted reanalysis yields 11 new diagnoses and seven rediscoveries

AI-assisted reanalysis of 376 locally unresolved cases yielded 18 confirmed diagnoses. Eleven were new, while seven had already been established elsewhere.

ⓘ

What happened

Boston Children’s Hospital and OpenAI researchers used o3 Deep Research to reassess 376 rare-disease cases that remained unresolved in their local research records. They reported 18 confirmed local diagnoses, or 4.8%: 11 newly identified diagnoses and seven rediscoveries of diagnoses already made elsewhere. Clinicians reviewed candidates, arranged laboratory confirmation, and returned results to families. The paper’s discussion explains that some findings depended on later gene discoveries, revised classifications, previously uncalled variants, or information that was unavailable to earlier analyses. The researchers did not compare the model with a simultaneous expert reanalysis using the same current evidence. They also did not measure improvements in treatment, cost, or clinician time.

NBC News separately interviewed Kyra Benton about receiving a diagnosis and sought comment from outside clinicians. The study was retrospective and involved OpenAI employees and funding, so it does not establish an independent estimate of the model’s added benefit.

Purpose. OpenAI o3 Deep Research is a general-purpose reasoning and research model, not a diagnostic device. The Manton Center team built an explanation-first reanalysis workflow around it to propose candidate molecular diagnoses for unsolved cases. These diagnoses were the workflow's intended output. OpenAI states that the study does not describe or endorse a customer use of o3 for diagnosis. Brownstein said that she was shocked that a commercial system could find new diagnoses in genomes analyzed many times.

AI’s contribution

o3 Deep Research read each de-identified case packet and proposed the most plausible molecular explanation with its supporting evidence. It diagnosed no one. Humans did the rest. At least two reviewers adjudicated each candidate under ACMG/AMP criteria, a CLIA-certified laboratory confirmed each variant, and the clinical team returned results to families. Its distinct contribution was surfacing candidates in cases that commercial and institutional analyses and multidisciplinary teams had already reviewed. In one early-psychosis case it inferred a 22q11.2 deletion absent from the input data, which genome sequencing then confirmed. Seven of the 18 were rediscoveries already established externally. The study was retrospective, and reviewers were not blinded to model confidence. The authors call for prospective evaluation with comparator baselines, so the gain over renewed human reanalysis is unmeasured.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Investigators returned 18 confirmed local diagnoses to families, including 11 new diagnoses and seven rediscoveries. NBC’s direct patient interview describes the value of receiving an explanation after years of uncertainty. Changes to treatment or health outcomes were not measured.

  • an organization

    The team resolved 18 cases in its local research records, including seven diagnoses already established elsewhere. No contemporaneous human-reanalysis comparison or measured savings in time, cost, or clinician effort establish the additional organizational benefit of AI.

Further evidence

Setting. Already-sequenced unsolved cases, mostly child-parent trios, assembled into a de-identified packet. Each packet held Human Phenotype Ontology terms, any clinician notes, age and sex. It also held a filtered variant table with rarity, predicted effect, ClinVar class and family signal quality. A general-purpose reasoning model asked for the single most plausible molecular explanation and its evidence, not just a ranked gene. The prompt was refined first on cases of known diagnosis. Time elapsed since the original analysis, so that newly published gene-disease links and reclassified variants existed to be found. Expert adjudication under ACMG/AMP criteria by at least two reviewers, CLIA-laboratory confirmation, and return of results by the clinical team.

Conditions. Human geneticists review every candidate and a model output is never treated as a diagnosis. Confirmatory testing in a certified laboratory before any result reaches a family. De-identified data handled inside approved environments. Yield expectations calibrated to the cohort: single-digit gains in heavily reviewed cases, and seven of the 18 here were rediscoveries.
