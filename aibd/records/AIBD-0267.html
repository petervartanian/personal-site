Med-Gemini-3D app leads to CDC42BPB diagnosis in a 22-month-old

depth 3 for a person

Independently studied, 1 source.

A mother ran a 3D-CT image through a smartphone app the authors say is powered by Google's Med-Gemini-3D; it suggested enlarged parietal foramina the physician had missed, prompting genetic testing.

What happened

Hajebian and Friel (Journal of Craniofacial Surgery, published 16 January 2026) describe a 22-month-old girl with persistent bilateral parietal skull defects and global developmental delay. The abstract, from Europe PMC, is among the sources. The full text is paywalled. Her computed tomography (CT) showed symmetric ossification defects beside the sagittal suture that the treating physician did not initially recognize. The mother used “a smartphone application powered by Med-Gemini-3D” on a 3D-CT reconstruction. It suggested “Catlin mark skull”, the historical name for enlarged parietal foramina. Genetic evaluation then identified a CDC42BPB variant associated with Chilton-Okur-Chung neurodevelopmental syndrome. The authors state that establishing the diagnosis facilitated earlier therapeutic interventions.

The abstract notes that Google introduced Med-Gemini-3D in 2024 and that it is not approved for independent clinical use. A consumer app running Med-Gemini-3D cannot be verified from the abstract. The date is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Consumer use of a CT-interpreting model to obtain a diagnosis for an unexplained skull finding

The system's part

The authors attribute the diagnostic suggestion to a smartphone application they say is powered by Med-Gemini-3D. Only the abstract could be read and the app could not be identified. So the AI role rests on the authors' description alone. Authors are not affiliated with the developer.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A caregiver submitting a 3D-CT image to a multimodal model. A rare finding unrecognized by the treating physician. Follow-up genetic evaluation.

What must hold again. Access to a model able to interpret 3D CT images. Clinicians act on a consumer-obtained suggestion.
