Med-Gemini-3D app leads to CDC42BPB diagnosis in a 22-month-old

depth 3 for a person

A mother ran a 3D-CT image through a smartphone app the authors say is powered by Google's Med-Gemini-3D; it suggested enlarged parietal foramina the physician had missed, prompting genetic testing.

What happened

Hajebian and Friel (Journal of Craniofacial Surgery, published 16 January 2026; abstract fetched through Europe PMC, full text paywalled) describe a 22-month-old girl with persistent bilateral parietal skull defects and global developmental delay whose CT showed symmetric ossification defects beside the sagittal suture that the treating physician did not initially recognize. The mother used 'a smartphone application powered by Med-Gemini-3D' on a 3D-CT reconstruction, which suggested 'Catlin mark skull', the historical name for enlarged parietal foramina; genetic evaluation then identified a CDC42BPB variant associated with Chilton-Okur-Chung neurodevelopmental syndrome, and the authors state that establishing the diagnosis facilitated earlier therapeutic interventions. The abstract notes that Google introduced Med-Gemini-3D in 2024 and that it is not approved for independent clinical use; the earlier verifier held the case because a consumer app running Med-Gemini-3D could not be verified from the abstract. The date is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Consumer use of a CT-interpreting model to obtain a diagnosis for an unexplained skull finding

The system's part

The authors attribute the diagnostic suggestion to a smartphone application they say is powered by Med-Gemini-3D; only the abstract could be read and the app could not be identified, so the AI role rests on the authors' description alone. Authors are not affiliated with the developer.
