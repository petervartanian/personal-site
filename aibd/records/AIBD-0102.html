What happened

Similipal Tiger Reserve in Mayurbhanj district, Odisha, runs a network of about 220 TrailGuard AI trail cameras. The cameras classify humans, animals and vehicles and send images to a control room. Late on Wednesday 18 February 2026 the cameras detected a group of armed intruders entering the reserve. Field Director Prakash Chand Gogineni said the real-time alerts mobilized the Similipal South Division, three ranges and three Special Tiger Protection Force units. After about 24 hours of combing, at least 39 poachers surrendered. Nine guns, gunpowder and more than 20 bows and arrows were recovered. A case was registered at Jenabil Wildlife Range. Later detections on 11 April, around 24 July and 23 September 2026 led to arrests. Courts refused bail to the July and September groups. The camera count varies by source.

The Field Director gave “around 220” in February 2026 and The Hans India “over 220” in July 2026. The Times of India in April 2026 reported 50 AI cameras in use, up from an initial 10, with 300 planned.

What it was built to do. Detect human intruders and poachers entering the reserve in near real time so rangers can intercept them before wildlife is killed.

The system's part

The AI cameras' real-time detection of the armed group is the stated trigger for the mobilization. That operation ended in surrender and seizure. The park authority's statement is the Field Director's on-record account, quoted by The New Indian Express and the Times of India. The court remands are reported in the press by PTI, Hans India and Ommcom. The cited account lacks corroboration from an available official press note or court order. The account rests on press-quoted forest-department statements.

Replication

What produced it. Dense camera coverage of entry trails (about 220 units). On-device classification that transmits only human detections. A staffed control room and armed response units able to deploy within hours. Prosecution follow-through under the Arms Act and wildlife law.

What must hold again. Cameras remain concealed and powered. Alerts reach field teams in near real time. Response teams can reach remote detection points. Courts continue to accept camera evidence.
