TrailGuard AI alerts lead to arrest of 39 poachers in Similipal

TrailGuard AI cameras detected armed intruders in Similipal Tiger Reserve on 18 February 2026; the Field Director reports that at least 39 poachers surrendered and that nine guns were seized.

What happened

Similipal Tiger Reserve in Mayurbhanj district, Odisha, runs a network of TrailGuard AI trail cameras. The cameras classify humans, animals and vehicles and send images to a control room. The New Indian Express reports that camera monitoring in the reserve began in October 2024 with about 50 cameras. In May 2025 Ommcom News reported that TrailGuard photographs helped track three poachers, whom a court sent to judicial custody after it rejected their bail. Late on Wednesday 18 February 2026 the cameras detected a group of armed intruders entering the reserve. Field Director Prakash Chand Gogineni said that the real-time alerts mobilized the Similipal South Division, three ranges and three Special Tiger Protection Force units. After about 24 hours of combing, at least 39 poachers surrendered. Nine guns, gunpowder and more than 20 bows and arrows were recovered. A case was registered at Jenabil Wildlife Range. On 27 February 2026 The New Indian Express reported that the network had grown to more than 220 cameras. The newspaper reported that the cameras had helped the reserve detect more than 110 intrusions and arrest nearly 300 poachers and wildlife criminals, and that the reserve was buying 450 more cameras. Gogineni said that the reserve planned to install cameras in at least half of its 1,375 monitoring grids. Later detections on 11 April, around 24 July and 23 September 2026 led to arrests. Courts refused bail to the July and September groups. The camera count varies by source. The Field Director gave “around 220” in February 2026, and The Hans India gave “over 220” in July 2026. The Times of India reported in April 2026 that 50 AI cameras were in use, up from an initial 10, with 300 planned.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The cameras detected people entering the reserve and sent images to the control room, giving staff a location and a reason to dispatch protection teams.

Workflow

Dense camera coverage of entry trails (about 220 units). On-device classification that transmits only human detections. A staffed control room and armed response units able to deploy within hours. Prosecution follow-through under the Arms Act and wildlife law.

Human oversight

Forest officers assessed the alerts and coordinated about 24 hours of searching across three ranges before the group surrendered.

Benefits

Reach

The benefit falls on the reserve's wildlife, and no source counts animals protected. The February operation ended with at least 39 poachers surrendered, and the reserve management reports nearly 300 poachers and wildlife criminals arrested since camera monitoring began. People and places

Dependencies

Useful deployment required cameras covering entry routes, a working alert channel to the control room, and field teams able to act on the images.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Cameras remain concealed and powered. Alerts reach field teams in near real time. Response teams can reach remote detection points. Courts continue to accept camera evidence.
