Spotlight may have led to a missing child's recovery in weeks

In April 2019 Thorn's Spotlight, using Amazon facial recognition, matched a missing child's photo to online sex ads; Thorn's chief executive said that she was recovered within weeks.

Brief from Peter

This case rests on one speaker. In a WIRED report of 19 June 2019, Thorn's chief executive, Julie Cordua, said that a California officer had run a missing child's photo through Spotlight. In April 2019 the officer had seen the photo in a post by the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children. Using Amazon's facial recognition, Spotlight returned a list of online sex ads featuring the girl, who had been sold for weeks. More traditional police work followed, and Cordua said that the child was recovered within weeks.1 No police or court record of the case is among the sources. The gap matters to anyone who cites the story, because the figures in Thorn's 2017 impact report came from surveys of its police users and viral claims have treated identification as rescue.2 Amazon Rekognition, which Spotlight used for the face match, has falsely matched members of Congress to mugshots.

  1. 1 Tom Simonite, WIRED, June 19, 2019. https://www.wired.com/story/how-facial-recognition-fighting-child-sex-trafficking/
  2. 2 Snopes reports that Thorn's 2017 impact report claimed that its tools helped identify 5,791 child victims and rescue 103. Dan MacGuill, Snopes, September 4, 2018. https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/kutcher-software-child-trafficking/
