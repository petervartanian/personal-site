Thorn says Spotlight led to a missing child's recovery in weeks

claimed its tools helped identify 5,791 child victims and rescue 103

In April 2019 a California officer ran a NCMEC missing-child photo through Thorn's Spotlight; Rekognition matching returned sex ads with the girl and, per Thorn's CEO, she was recovered within weeks.

What happened

The date is WIRED's publication; the source places the case in April 2019 without a day. According to Thorn CEO Julie Cordua, speaking at an Amazon conference and quoted by WIRED's Tom Simonite, a California law enforcement officer saw a National Center for Missing and Exploited Children post with a missing child's picture, screenshotted it and ran it through Spotlight, Thorn's investigator tool that matches faces and other clues in online sex ads. Using Amazon's facial recognition, Spotlight returned a list of ads featuring the girl, who had been sold for weeks; more traditional police work followed and, Cordua said, the child was recovered within weeks. Snopes notes Thorn's 2017 impact report claimed its tools helped identify 5,791 child victims and rescue 103, figures from Thorn's surveys of police users, and that viral claims conflated identification with rescue. No police or court record of this case was fetched.

What it was built to do. Help law-enforcement investigators find underage sex-trafficking victims by matching faces and text across online escort advertisements.

The system's part

The face match that surfaced the ads is attributed to Amazon Rekognition inside Spotlight; the recovery itself came from conventional police work. Everything about this case rests on Thorn's CEO, and Snopes documents that Thorn's aggregate figures derive from user surveys and were widely misreported, so the record is admitted only under the relaxed rule.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • A reference photo of the missing child
  • An archive of scraped escort ads with faces indexed for similarity search
  • An officer with Spotlight access who follows the matches with investigation

What must hold again

  • Ads with usable face images must exist for the victim
  • Legal access to the ad archive and face matching
  • Investigative capacity to act on matches quickly
