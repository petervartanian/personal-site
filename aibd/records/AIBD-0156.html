thynC flags a pre-arrest heart rhythm, and ward staff revive a patient

On 14 January 2026 the thynC monitoring system flagged ventricular tachycardia in an 85-year-old on a general ward in Gwangju, and staff resuscitated the patient.

  • 4 a person

    The hospital reports that the alarm brought staff to a patient in ventricular tachycardia who had stopped breathing, and that resuscitation within the golden time restored heartbeat and circulation before the patient went to intensive care. That is a serious condition resolved in time. Survival to discharge and neurological outcome are not reported, so the sources do not establish that a life was preserved.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The system's contribution was detection and summons. The wearable ECG stream showed the heart rate climbing to 192 beats per minute. The remote central monitor identified ventricular tachycardia and raised an alarm. The attending director says that is how staff learned of it at once and reached the bedside to begin CPR within the golden time. The resuscitation itself was done by people. The counterfactual rests on the hospital's framing that general-ward patients are not under continuous observation and that deterioration can be noticed late. How long this patient would otherwise have gone unobserved is not recorded. No medical record or independent assessment has been published. The case reached the press through a Daewoong Pharmaceutical announcement of 11 February 2026.
Limits
The supplier-mediated account does not include a clinical record or an independently assessed response time. Staff provided resuscitation, and the time that would have elapsed without the alert is unknown. Long-term recovery is not established by restored circulation.

What happened

On 9 January 2026 thynC began monitoring an 85-year-old patient on a general ward of Gwangju Hanguk Hospital (광주한국병원) in Gwangju, South Korea, because of cardiovascular risk factors at admission. On 14 January 2026 at about 3:10 p.m., the patient's heart rate climbed to 192 beats per minute. thynC's remote central monitor flagged ventricular tachycardia, a precursor sign of cardiac arrest, and alarmed. Staff who ran to the room found seizure-like movements, reduced consciousness and no spontaneous breathing. They performed cardiopulmonary resuscitation and artificial respiration. The heartbeat and circulation returned, and the patient was moved to intensive care. Daewoong Pharmaceutical, which supplies thynC, announced the case on 11 February 2026 with statements from the hospital's directors and nursing manager. The ThynC patch is made by Seers Technology of Seongnam. A study published in March 2024 by Seers staff and hospital cardiologists found that the patch matched conventional telemetry in 80 cardiology inpatients with less signal loss. Every article of 11 February 2026 on the Gwangju case repeats the Daewoong announcement. No other account of the case has appeared. On 19 June 2026 Asia Economy reported that Daewoong had completed thynC installations covering 558 beds at Chonnam National University Hospital and Hwasun Chonnam National University Hospital.

Details

AI system

Application developer

Not stated in the read reports, which describe thynC as the smart bed monitoring system that Daewoong Pharmaceutical supplies (자사 공급)

Inputs and outputs

The wearable ECG stream fed a central monitor that identified ventricular tachycardia and raised an alarm when the patient's heart rate reached 192 beats per minute.

Workflow

ThynC wearable monitoring of ECG, heart rate and respiratory rate, linked to a remote central monitor on a general ward. It started on 9 January 2026 because the patient had cardiovascular risk factors at admission. An alarm raised the moment the heart rate climbed to 192 beats per minute and the monitor identified ventricular tachycardia. Ward staff who went to the bedside on the alarm and performed CPR and artificial respiration within the golden time. The reports put that time at four minutes for acute cardiac arrest, citing National Fire Agency data.

Human oversight

Ward staff responded to the alarm, assessed the patient, performed CPR and assisted breathing, and transferred the patient to intensive care after circulation returned.

AI contribution

The thynC system detected ventricular tachycardia in a wearable ECG feed and raised an alarm. The hospital reported that staff reached the bedside promptly and began CPR.

Entry details

Sector

Healthcare (inpatient monitoring / cardiac arrest). Hospital general-ward remote patient monitoring.

Purpose

The hospital and Daewoong Pharmaceutical describe thynC as an AI-based smart bed monitoring system. It measures the electrocardiogram, heart rate and respiratory rate through wearable devices. It sends an alarm to a central monitor when an abnormal sign appears. Patients on a general ward, where staff cannot stay at every bedside, are then watched in real time as in an intensive care unit. Staff can respond early to deterioration. Daewoong's hospital-division head describes thynC as built to recognize changes in a patient's condition early and help medical staff respond quickly. Flagging a pre-arrest arrhythmia and summoning staff is that designed use.
