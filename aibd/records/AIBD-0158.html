What happened

Paul Conyngham, a Sydney machine-learning engineer, used ChatGPT to plan treatment for his dog Rosie. Rosie is an eight-year-old Staffy-Shar Pei cross with mast cell cancer diagnosed in 2024. He used AlphaFold to model mutated proteins from tumor sequencing he paid $3,000 for at UNSW's Ramaciotti Centre for Genomics. He chose neoantigen targets and confirmed them “again using AI”. Pall Thordarson's UNSW RNA Institute made the mRNA vaccine in under two months. Rachel Allavena of the University of Queensland injected it at Gatton in December 2025, with a checkpoint inhibitor, and gave a booster in February 2026. The tennis-ball-sized tumor on Rosie's hock halved, and she was chasing rabbits by late January 2026. Some tumors did not respond. By August 2026 the cancer had returned after a later surgery.

What it was built to do. ChatGPT is a general-purpose assistant and AlphaFold a protein-structure predictor. Conyngham used the first to plan and navigate the veterinary and genomic work. He used the second to model mutated proteins as candidate targets. Both are uses within what each tool was built to do. Neither was built to treat Rosie. The vaccine was designed by Conyngham, made by UNSW and given by a veterinary school. The therapeutic response is a downstream use of the tools rather than their product purpose.

The system's part

The AI tools worked at the design stage. ChatGPT suggested immunotherapy, pointed Conyngham to UNSW's genomics center and helped him plan the analysis. AlphaFold modeled mutated proteins. He says he “confirmed, again using AI, that those targets were good cancer candidates”. Humans sequenced the genome (Martin Smith), made the mRNA and nanoparticles (Pall Thordarson) and injected it (Rachel Allavena). A checkpoint inhibitor was given at the same time. UNSW says “We don't know how much of the response came from the checkpoint inhibitor and how much from the mRNA vaccine”. The AI's share of the outcome therefore cannot be isolated. It is a single uncontrolled case, and no outcome data have been published outside interviews and statements.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. Whole-genome sequencing of tumor and healthy tissue, about 150 billion DNA letters for $3,000, from which neoantigen targets could be chosen with AI help. An academic RNA facility, the UNSW RNA Institute, willing to make a custom mRNA vaccine from an owner-designed sequence in under two months. A veterinary school with ethics cover and an immunotherapy specialist to administer it, after about three months of ethics paperwork. An owner with 17 years of machine-learning experience able to run the process, pay for it and drive ten hours for each dose.

What must hold again. The response cannot be credited to the vaccine alone. A checkpoint inhibitor was given at the same time, and UNSW says the split is unknown. One uncontrolled animal. Mast cell tumors behave unpredictably, and some of Rosie's tumors did not respond. The benefit was time, not cure. The cancer returned after a later surgery, and the company founded on the case wrote in August 2026 that her story may have ended.

    Conditions needed to repeat the benefitRecorded; review pending

    Replication
    Produced by
    • Whole-genome sequencing of tumor and healthy tissue, about 150 billion DNA letters for $3,000, from which neoantigen targets could be chosen with AI help.
    • An academic RNA facility, the UNSW RNA Institute, willing to make a custom mRNA vaccine from an owner-designed sequence in under two months.
    • A veterinary school with ethics cover and an immunotherapy specialist to administer it, after about three months of ethics paperwork.
    • An owner with 17 years of machine-learning experience able to run the process, pay for it and drive ten hours for each dose.
    Must hold
    • The response cannot be credited to the vaccine alone. A checkpoint inhibitor was given at the same time, and UNSW says the split is unknown.
    • One uncontrolled animal. Mast cell tumors behave unpredictably, and some of Rosie's tumors did not respond.
    • The benefit was time, not cure. The cancer returned after a later surgery, and the company founded on the case wrote in August 2026 that her story may have ended.
