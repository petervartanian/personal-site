Owner designs dog's cancer vaccine with AI; tumor halves before relapse

Paul Conyngham used ChatGPT and AlphaFold to choose targets for an mRNA vaccine; with a checkpoint inhibitor, it halved Rosie's largest tumor by early 2026 before the cancer returned.

  • 2 a person

    Rosie had been given months to live after surgery and chemotherapy. Her largest tumor halved, and she regained mobility by late January 2026. ABC News reported on 22 June 2026 that she was still running and playing. A checkpoint inhibitor given at the same time confounds the partial response. The cancer later returned, and Conyngham chose to have her euthanized.

  • 1 an organization

    The UNSW RNA Institute made what Thordarson called the first personalized cancer vaccine designed for a dog, from sequencing by the Ramaciotti Centre for Genomics. They describe the case as a milestone. Conyngham's spin-out GAMGEE raised a $4 million seed round in August 2026 on the strength of the case.

The system’s part
Not isolated. The AI tools worked at the design stage. ChatGPT suggested immunotherapy, pointed Conyngham to UNSW's genomics center and helped him plan the analysis. AlphaFold modeled mutated proteins. He says he “confirmed, again using AI, that those targets were good cancer candidates”. Humans sequenced the genome (Martin Smith), made the mRNA and nanoparticles (Pall Thordarson) and injected it (Rachel Allavena). A checkpoint inhibitor was given at the same time. UNSW says “We don't know how much of the response came from the checkpoint inhibitor and how much from the mRNA vaccine”. The AI's share of the outcome therefore cannot be isolated. It is a single uncontrolled case, and no outcome data have been published outside interviews and statements.
Limits
The vaccine was given with a checkpoint inhibitor, so the tumor shrinkage cannot be credited separately to the vaccine or to the AI-assisted choice of targets. Some tumors did not respond. The cancer returned after a later surgery, and no source gives the date of Rosie's death. The case does not establish a cure or general clinical effectiveness.
Costs and harms
Tumor shrinkage cannot be credited to the vaccine alone because Rosie also received a checkpoint inhibitor. The improvement was temporary: The cancer returned after a later surgery, and Rosie was euthanized. The treatment required $3,000 of sequencing, about three months of ethics paperwork and a ten-hour drive for each dose.
Later
Reversed, 24 August 2026. 机器之心 reported on 24 August 2026, as republished by 36Kr, that Conyngham wrote on X in June 2026 that a later surgery took the cancer out of remission, that the tumors spread within four weeks, and that he chose to have Rosie euthanized. No source gives the date of her death. The GAMGEE release of 17 August 2026 writes of her in the past tense.

What happened

Paul Conyngham, a Sydney machine-learning engineer, used ChatGPT to plan treatment for his dog Rosie. Rosie is an eight-year-old Staffy-Shar Pei cross with mast cell cancer diagnosed in 2024. He paid $3,000 for tumor sequencing at UNSW's Ramaciotti Centre for Genomics, and he used AlphaFold to model mutated proteins from the results. He chose neoantigen targets and confirmed them “again using AI”. Pall Thordarson's UNSW RNA Institute made the mRNA vaccine in under two months. Rachel Allavena of the University of Queensland injected it at Gatton in December 2025, with a checkpoint inhibitor, and gave a booster in February 2026. The tennis-ball-sized tumor on Rosie's hock halved, and she was chasing rabbits by late January 2026. Some tumors did not respond. The Verge reported on 18 March 2026 that two outside researchers, David Ascher and Alvin Chan, judged that the AI's role had been overstated. The reporter noted that the checkpoint inhibitor given with the vaccine makes the vaccine's own effect unclear. Medical Daily reported on 18 August 2026 that Rosie had entered remission and that her cancer had returned after a later surgery. According to a 机器之心 report of 24 August 2026, Conyngham wrote on X in June 2026 that the surgery took the cancer out of remission and that he chose to have Rosie euthanized.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The tools helped translate tumor and healthy-tissue sequencing into candidate targets and a vaccine design. The physical sequencing, manufacture, and administration were performed by specialist teams.

Workflow

Whole-genome sequencing of tumor and healthy tissue, about 150 billion DNA letters for $3,000, from which neoantigen targets could be chosen with AI help. An academic RNA facility, the UNSW RNA Institute, willing to make a custom mRNA vaccine from an owner-designed sequence in under two months. A veterinary school with ethics cover and an immunotherapy specialist to administer it, after about three months of ethics paperwork. An owner with 17 years of machine-learning experience able to run the process, pay for it and drive ten hours for each dose.

Human oversight

Conyngham coordinated the analysis, while genomics researchers, the RNA institute, and veterinary clinicians supplied the laboratory and treatment expertise.

AI contribution

Conyngham used ChatGPT to plan the work and AlphaFold to model mutated proteins when he chose targets for Rosie's vaccine, and Grok was reported for the final mRNA construct. A checkpoint inhibitor given at the same time means that the vaccine's own share of the response cannot be isolated. Two outside researchers told The Verge that the AI's role had been overstated. Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The response cannot be credited to the vaccine alone. A checkpoint inhibitor was given at the same time, and UNSW says the split is unknown. One uncontrolled animal. Mast cell tumors behave unpredictably, and some of Rosie's tumors did not respond. The benefit was time, not cure. The cancer returned after a later surgery, and the company founded on the case wrote in August 2026 that her story may have ended.

Availability

This was a bespoke experimental treatment requiring sequencing, an academic manufacturing facility, and veterinary oversight. The consumer AI tools were accessible, but the complete treatment was not a routine service.

Entry details

Sector

Veterinary oncology / personalized mRNA vaccine design. Companion animal with mast cell cancer.

Purpose

ChatGPT is a general-purpose assistant, and AlphaFold predicts protein structures. Conyngham used ChatGPT to plan and navigate the veterinary and genomic work. He used AlphaFold to model mutated proteins as candidate targets. Both uses fall within what each tool was built to do. Neither tool was built to treat Rosie, and the therapeutic response is a downstream use of both. Conyngham designed the vaccine. UNSW made it, and a veterinary school gave it.
