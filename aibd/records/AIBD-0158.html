ChatGPT and AlphaFold pick vaccine targets; dog's tumour halves

halved Rosie's largest tumour by early 2026

AI-chosen targets, a UNSW-made mRNA vaccine and a checkpoint inhibitor halved Rosie's largest tumour by early 2026; partial, not a cure.

What happened

Paul Conyngham, a Sydney machine-learning engineer, used ChatGPT to plan treatment for Rosie, his eight-year-old Staffy-Shar Pei cross with mast cell cancer diagnosed in 2024, and AlphaFold to model mutated proteins from tumour sequencing he paid $3,000 for at UNSW's Ramaciotti Centre for Genomics. He chose neoantigen targets, confirmed 'again using AI', and Pall Thordarson's UNSW RNA Institute made the mRNA vaccine in under two months. Rachel Allavena of the University of Queensland injected it at Gatton in December 2025, with a checkpoint inhibitor, and a booster in February 2026. The tennis-ball-sized tumour on Rosie's hock halved and she was chasing rabbits by late January 2026; some tumours did not respond, and by August 2026 the cancer had returned after a later surgery.

What it was built to do. ChatGPT is a general-purpose assistant and AlphaFold a protein-structure predictor; Conyngham used the first to plan and navigate the veterinary and genomic work and the second to model mutated proteins as candidate targets, uses within what each was built to do. Neither was built to treat Rosie: the vaccine was designed by Conyngham, made by UNSW and given by a veterinary school, so the therapeutic response is a downstream use of the tools rather than their product purpose.

Who benefited

  • -2
    personal

    Rosie, given months to live after surgery and chemotherapy, had her largest tumour halve, regained mobility by late January 2026 and, per ABC on 22 June 2026, was still running and playing; the response was partial, confounded by a checkpoint inhibitor, and her cancer later returned, so -3 is not supported.

  • -1
    organizational

    UNSW's RNA Institute and Ramaciotti Centre got what Thordarson called the first personalised cancer vaccine designed for a dog and describe the case as a milestone; Conyngham's spin-out GAMGEE raised a $4 million seed round in August 2026 on the strength of it.

The system's part

The AI tools worked at the design stage: ChatGPT suggested immunotherapy, pointed Conyngham to UNSW's genomics centre and helped him plan the analysis; AlphaFold modelled mutated proteins; he says he 'confirmed, again using AI, that those targets were good cancer candidates'. Humans sequenced the genome (Martin Smith), made the mRNA and nanoparticles (Pall Thordarson) and injected it (Rachel Allavena). Because a checkpoint inhibitor was given at the same time, UNSW says 'We don't know how much of the response came from the checkpoint inhibitor and how much from the mRNA vaccine', so the AI's share of the outcome cannot be isolated; it is a single uncontrolled case, and no outcome data have been published outside interviews and statements.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • Whole-genome sequencing of tumour and healthy tissue, about 150 billion DNA letters for $3,000, from which neoantigen targets could be chosen with AI help.
  • An academic RNA facility, the UNSW RNA Institute, willing to make a bespoke mRNA vaccine from an owner-designed sequence in under two months.
  • A veterinary school with ethics cover and an immunotherapy specialist to administer it, after about three months of ethics paperwork.
  • An owner with 17 years of machine-learning experience able to run the pipeline, pay for it and drive ten hours for each dose.
What must hold again
  • The response cannot be attributed to the vaccine alone: a checkpoint inhibitor was given at the same time and UNSW says the split is unknown.
  • One uncontrolled animal; mast cell tumours behave unpredictably, and some of Rosie's tumours did not respond.
  • The benefit was time, not cure: the cancer returned after a later surgery, and the company founded on the case wrote in August 2026 that her story may have ended.
