Alice alerts lead to corrected tender notices worth R$91.7 million

The Minas Gerais court of accounts screened 2,297 tenders from August 2022 with Alice, a system that analyzes tender notices, and obtained corrections of notices worth R$91,723,011.31.

What happened

The Tribunal de Contas do Estado de Minas Gerais (TCE-MG), the state court of accounts, is one of more than ten Brazilian courts of accounts that adopted Alice. Alice (Análise de Licitações e Editais) was created by the Controladoria-Geral da União (CGU) and the Tribunal de Contas da União (TCU). In December 2022 the court published an audit of 2,297 municipal and state tenders held in a single month, August 2022. The exact day is not given. The court's own news item of 27 June 2023, reproduced by Atricon on 28 June 2023, gives the results. The work detected 60 irregularities involving R$113,713,128.26. Of the 60 alerts sent to the audited bodies, 53 received answers that allowed control verification. Irregularities were fixed in republished notices or through measures adopted for future tenders. As a result, the audit achieved corrections of notices worth R$91,723,011.31. The figures are aggregates presented by analysts Maria Aparecida Aiko Ikemura and Leonardo Alves Mateus to about 60 people from courts of accounts across Brazil. No individual tender is named. On 17 January 2024 Atricon reported that TCE-MG presented Alice and Solaris to the Espírito Santo court of accounts (TCE-ES). No adoption is reported. On 23 July 2025, O Fator reported that TCE-MG had built two tools of its own on the basis of Alice. Solaris screens purchase notices and Apolo screens engineering budgets. The court says that the two tools analyzed and helped correct notices worth more than R$8.1 billion in 2025. One alert led the court to suspend a tender in Montezuma for R$4.3 million of alcoholic drinks. All of these figures come from the court.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Alice analyzed tender notices for possible inconsistencies or irregularities and presented flags for auditors to examine. The court then sent alerts to the public bodies responsible.

Workflow

Alice flagged tender irregularities, auditors sent alerts, and public bodies replied with corrections or plans for future procurement.

Human oversight

Court analysts forwarded 60 of Alice's alerts to the audited bodies, which made the corrections. Answers to 53 alerts allowed the court to verify the controls. The court cites internal resistance among auditors as a challenge to the tool. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The court reports one audit of tenders from a single month, August 2022. In 2025 the court credited its own tools Solaris and Apolo, built on Alice, with analyzing and helping to correct notices worth more than R$8.1 billion. [1] [2] [3] [4]
