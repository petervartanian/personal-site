SARUAV detects an unconscious man in tall grass, and rescuers save him

SARUAV, software that uses convolutional neural networks, detected an unconscious 65-year-old man in tall grass on drone images after more than 24 hours, and mountain rescuers retrieved him alive.

  • 4 a person

    A 65-year-old man with Alzheimer's and a stroke a day earlier lay unconscious in two-meter grass after over 24 hours outdoors. He was found and handed to the ambulance service alive.

  • 2 an organization

    GOPR Bieszczady closed a search that police, dogs and ground teams had not resolved, in what it called the first such find in Poland.

The system’s part
The CNN detector returned the man's coordinates after police and tracking dogs had failed. Rescuers then retrieved him. Three of the five case-report authors work for SARUAV Ltd., so the independent confirmation is the GOPR Bieszczady account quoted in the Polish press.
Limits
The report documents one successful detection and rescue. It does not measure how much earlier the man was found than under an otherwise identical search without SARUAV. Its authors include developers of SARUAV and a member of the Bieszczady Mountain Rescue Service. Texas A&M researchers wrote in 2023 that SARUAV gives no details on its training data or model architecture.
Costs and harms
Two authors from the Main School of Fire Service in Warsaw wrote that each use of SARUAV needs spatial data prepared for the area and that detection works only in the area that the recipient of the system has bought. The detection also required that enough of the man was visible from above in the photographed area.
Later
Scaled up, 1 August 2024. The University of Wrocław, under whose auspices the developer operates, reports that a South Tyrol mountain rescue team used SARUAV to find a missing person at the beginning of August 2024. The person had died. The university calls this the second automatic detection in a real search and states that services in Poland, other European countries and Asia have adopted the system. Only the developer's side reports this.

Brief from Peter

The Bieszczady Mountain Rescue Service called this the first find in Poland of a missing person using a drone and SARUAV.1 A 65-year-old man with Alzheimer's disease had left home in Cergowa a day after a stroke, and police with tracking dogs did not find him after a storm. On 29 June 2021 the rescue service made four drone flights and ran the SARUAV software on 782 images. After 4 hours and 31 minutes of analysis the software returned his coordinates. Rescuers found him unconscious in grass over two meters tall, after more than 24 hours outdoors, and handed him to the ambulance service. Independent authors wrote that each use of the system needs map data prepared for the area and that SARUAV gives no details on its training data or model architecture.2 Three of the five authors of the case report work for SARUAV Ltd., and the Polish press quotes the rescue service's own account of the find and describes the failed ground search.3

Details

AI system

Role

The detector located a person in aerial photographs and returned his coordinates.

Configuration

SARUAV analyzed photographs from DJI Matrice 300 drones.

Inputs and outputs

SARUAV searched 782 downward-looking drone images for a person and returned coordinates that rescuers could inspect.

Workflow

Nadir drone imagery at close range over the search area. CNN detector run during the search, with rescuers able to verify hits and reach the coordinates.

Human oversight

Mountain rescuers flew the search, checked the detection, retrieved the unconscious man, and transferred him to the ambulance service.

Benefits

Reach

One man benefited in this case. The University of Wrocław reports that rescue services in Poland, other European countries and Asia use SARUAV, and it gives no count of people found. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Duration

The rescue was a single event on 29 June 2021, and the sources report no follow-up on the man's health. The University of Wrocław reports that SARUAV detected a second missing person, who had died, in South Tyrol in August 2024. It also reports that services in Poland, other European countries and Asia have adopted the system. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The person is within the imaged area and visible from above. Processing completes while the person can still be helped.
