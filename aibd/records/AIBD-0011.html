SARUAV finds an unconscious 65-year-old alive in tall grass

After 4 h 31 min of analysis the system detected him and returned his coordinates

29 Jun 2021: SARUAV CNN flagged a 65-year-old man unconscious in tall grass after 24+ h; GOPR Bieszczady retrieved him alive

What happened

On Monday 28 June 2021 at about 4 pm a 65-year-old man with Alzheimer's disease, who had suffered a stroke the previous day, left his home in Cergowa, south-east Poland; police and tracking dogs did not find him after a storm. On Tuesday 29 June the Bieszczady Mountain Rescue Service (GOPR Bieszczady) flew four drone flights and ran SARUAV, software from University of Wrocław researchers that uses convolutional neural networks to find people in nadir aerial images, on 782 images. After 4 h 31 min of analysis the system detected him and returned his coordinates; he was lying unconscious in grass over two metres tall after more than 24 hours outdoors. GOPR rescuers carried him out and handed him to the ambulance service. No cited source gives the day of the find outright; 29 June follows from the 28 June 4 pm departure and from the GOPR communique quoted by Świat Dronów, which opens 'Od wczoraj – 28 czerwca' ('since yesterday, 28 June') and says that the program flagged him after almost 24 hours.

What it was built to do. Built to detect people automatically in drone imagery for search and rescue; this benefit is that purpose.

The system's part

The CNN detector returned the man's coordinates after police and tracking dogs had failed; rescuers then retrieved him. Three of the five authors of the case report work for SARUAV Ltd., so the independent confirmation is the GOPR Bieszczady account quoted in the Polish press.

Replication

What produced it
  • Nadir drone imagery at close range over the search area
  • CNN detector run during the search, with rescuers able to verify hits and reach the coordinates
What must hold again
  • The person is within the imaged area and visible from above
  • Processing completes while the person can still be helped
