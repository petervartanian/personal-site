Double neural bypass restores Keith Thomas's movement and touch

Keith Thomas, paralyzed since 2020, regained arm movement and touch after a 15-hour brain-implant operation in Manhasset, New York.

ⓘ

AI system

System

Double neural bypass (Bidirectional Neural Bypass System): intracortical brain-computer interface with an LSTM decoder and deep reinforcement-learning agent, patterned spinal cord stimulation and intracortical microstimulation

Workflow

Chronic complete cervical spinal cord injury (C4 sensory/C5 motor) with movement intentions still readable from motor cortex. Five microelectrode arrays placed in motor and somatosensory cortex during a 15-hour mapped operation with the participant partly awake. An LSTM plus deep reinforcement-learning decoder paired with patterned spinal cord stimulation and intracortical microstimulation. Thirty-five weeks of supervised laboratory sessions inside an approved early-feasibility trial (NCT03680872).

Human oversight

Not assessed

Relation to AI

The decoder turns motor-cortex activity into intended hand movements. A reinforcement-learning agent regulates grasp force: figure 4c of the original paper reports 87% task success with the agent and 27% without it. The separate 86% and 62% arm-strength gains occurred during spinal stimulation and activity-based training before the hand neuroprosthesis. The full system combines AI with surgery, stimulation, an orthosis, and rehabilitation. The paper anonymizes its participant; Northwell identifies him as Keith Thomas.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Decoder accuracy sustained over months (84.6% over five months) so intended movements reliably trigger stimulation. Neurosurgical, engineering and rehabilitation teams present throughout. The gains were measured in the laboratory. Follow-up with the system switched off to confirm that strength and sensation gains persist.

Replication attempts

Not assessed

Later findings

16 July 2026. The Feinstein Institutes' July 2026 release on the Nature Medicine paper reports that the strength and sensation gains were still present at a follow-up more than two years later.

Purpose

ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03680872 describes an early feasibility trial of the Bidirectional Neural Bypass System. It is meant to restore movement and sensation in the hand and wrist of up to seven people with tetraplegia. This benefit is that purpose.
