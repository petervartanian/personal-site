A brain implant restores arm movement and touch to a paralyzed man

Keith Thomas, paralyzed since 2020, regained arm movement and touch with the Feinstein Institutes’ double neural bypass, and its AI agent raised grasp-force success to 87% from 27%.

  • 3 a person

    In the laboratory, the participant controlled grasping, including self-feeding, through AI decoding of his movement intentions, alongside spinal stimulation and sensory feedback. This restored meaningful abilities in one person. The study does not isolate the contribution of every component.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The decoder turns motor-cortex activity into intended hand movements. A reinforcement-learning agent regulates grasp force: figure 4c of the original paper reports 87% task success with the agent and 27% without it. The separate 86% and 62% arm-strength gains occurred during spinal stimulation and activity-based training before the hand neuroprosthesis. The full system combines AI with surgery, stimulation, an orthosis, and rehabilitation. The paper anonymizes its participant; Northwell identifies him as Keith Thomas.
Limits
The study does not isolate the contribution of each component. The arm-strength gains came before the hand neuroprosthesis. The text of the paper calls the grasp-force comparison an unpaired t-test, and the caption of figure 4c calls it a paired one. The report that the gains lasted more than two years comes from a Feinstein Institutes release and has no independent evaluation.
Costs and harms

What happened

A diving accident on 18 July 2020 paralyzed Keith Thomas from the chest down. On 9 March 2023, surgeons led by Ashesh Mehta and Netanel Ben-Shalom implanted five microelectrode arrays in his brain; he was 45. The 15-hour operation took place at North Shore University Hospital. The Feinstein Institutes' double neural bypass decodes his movement intentions with recurrent neural networks and reinforcement learning. It pairs the decoded intentions with patterned spinal cord stimulation. It restores touch by cortical microstimulation. By 28 July 2023 he could move his arms at will and feel his sister's hand. In a Patch report of 9 August 2023 he said, “Mobility in my right arm has increased by 110% compared to my baseline that I started with.” The Nature Medicine paper and the Feinstein release on it appeared on 16 July 2026. The release reports that his right arm strength rose by 86% and his left arm strength by 62% over 35 weeks. It also reports self-feeding. It quotes Chad Bouton, who said that the gains were still present after more than two years. The original paper places those strength gains in the earlier phase of spinal stimulation and training, before the AI-controlled hand tasks. Its grasp-force comparison reports 87% success with reinforcement learning and 27% without it.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Cortical decoders translated intended hand movements into control commands, while reinforcement learning regulated grasp force and stimulation supplied movement and touch feedback.

Workflow

Chronic complete cervical spinal cord injury (C4 sensory/C5 motor) with movement intentions still readable from motor cortex. Five microelectrode arrays placed in motor and somatosensory cortex during a 15-hour mapped operation with the participant partly awake. An LSTM plus deep reinforcement-learning decoder paired with patterned spinal cord stimulation and intracortical microstimulation. Thirty-five weeks of supervised laboratory sessions inside an approved early-feasibility trial (NCT03680872).

Human oversight

Neurosurgical, engineering, and rehabilitation teams supported implantation, calibration, and the supervised laboratory sessions.

Benefits

Reach

One person benefited, the only participant whom the sources describe. The trial registration allows up to seven participants with tetraplegia. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

The Feinstein Institutes reported on 16 July 2026 that the strength and sensation gains were still present at a follow-up more than two years later. No independent source has checked that follow-up. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Decoder accuracy sustained over months (84.6% over five months) so intended movements reliably trigger stimulation. Neurosurgical, engineering and rehabilitation teams present throughout. The gains were measured in the laboratory. Follow-up with the system switched off to confirm that strength and sensation gains persist.

Availability

This was an early-feasibility research system whose measured gains required laboratory support.
