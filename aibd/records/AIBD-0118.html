What happened

On 9 March 2023, surgeons led by Ashesh Mehta and Netanel Ben-Shalom implanted five microelectrode arrays in the brain of Keith Thomas, 45. The 15-hour operation was at North Shore University Hospital. A diving accident on 18 July 2020 had paralyzed him from the chest down. The Feinstein Institutes' double neural bypass decodes his movement intentions with recurrent neural networks and reinforcement learning. It pairs them with patterned spinal cord stimulation and restores touch by cortical microstimulation. By 28 July 2023 he could move his arms at will and feel his sister's hand. The Feinstein release of 16 July 2026 on the Nature Medicine paper reports 86% and 62% arm-strength gains, self-feeding and gains persisting beyond two years.

What it was built to do. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03680872 describes an early feasibility trial of the Bidirectional Neural Bypass System. It is meant to restore movement and sensation in the hand and wrist of up to seven people with tetraplegia. This benefit is that purpose.

The system's part

The AI is the decoder: recurrent neural networks (an LSTM) and a deep reinforcement-learning agent that turn motor-cortex activity into stimulation patterns. It reached up to 84.6% decoding accuracy sustained over five months without retraining (Feinstein release, 16 July 2026). Restored touch came from patterned intracortical microstimulation. The authors attribute the lasting strength gains to neuroplasticity from pairing the two. The decoder is necessary for real-time movement but is one part of a surgical, stimulation and rehabilitation package. The Nature Medicine paper does not name its participant. The Feinstein Institutes identify him as Thomas.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. Chronic complete cervical spinal cord injury (C4 sensory/C5 motor) with movement intentions still readable from motor cortex. Five microelectrode arrays placed in motor and somatosensory cortex during a 15-hour mapped operation with the participant partly awake. An LSTM plus deep reinforcement-learning decoder paired with patterned spinal cord stimulation and intracortical microstimulation. Thirty-five weeks of supervised laboratory sessions inside an approved early-feasibility trial (NCT03680872).

What must hold again. Decoder accuracy sustained over months (84.6% over five months) so intended movements reliably trigger stimulation. Neurosurgical, engineering and rehabilitation teams present throughout. The gains were measured in the laboratory. Follow-up with the system switched off to confirm that strength and sensation gains persist.
