Double neural bypass restores Keith Thomas's movement and touch

Keith Thomas, paralyzed since 2020, regained arm movement and touch after a 15-hour brain-implant operation in Manhasset, New York.

ⓘ

What happened

On 9 March 2023, surgeons led by Ashesh Mehta and Netanel Ben-Shalom implanted five microelectrode arrays in the brain of Keith Thomas, 45. The 15-hour operation was at North Shore University Hospital. A diving accident on 18 July 2020 had paralyzed him from the chest down. The Feinstein Institutes' double neural bypass decodes his movement intentions with recurrent neural networks and reinforcement learning. It pairs them with patterned spinal cord stimulation and restores touch by cortical microstimulation. By 28 July 2023 he could move his arms at will and feel his sister's hand. The Feinstein release of 16 July 2026 on the Nature Medicine paper reports 86% and 62% arm-strength gains, self-feeding and gains persisting beyond two years. The original paper distinguishes the earlier arm-strength gains from the AI-controlled hand tasks.

Its grasp-force comparison reports 87% success with reinforcement learning and 27% without it.

Purpose. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03680872 describes an early feasibility trial of the Bidirectional Neural Bypass System. It is meant to restore movement and sensation in the hand and wrist of up to seven people with tetraplegia. This benefit is that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The decoder turns motor-cortex activity into intended hand movements. A reinforcement-learning agent regulates grasp force: figure 4c of the original paper reports 87% task success with the agent and 27% without it. The separate 86% and 62% arm-strength gains occurred during spinal stimulation and activity-based training before the hand neuroprosthesis. The full system combines AI with surgery, stimulation, an orthosis, and rehabilitation. The paper anonymizes its participant; Northwell identifies him as Keith Thomas.

Further evidence

Setting. Chronic complete cervical spinal cord injury (C4 sensory/C5 motor) with movement intentions still readable from motor cortex. Five microelectrode arrays placed in motor and somatosensory cortex during a 15-hour mapped operation with the participant partly awake. An LSTM plus deep reinforcement-learning decoder paired with patterned spinal cord stimulation and intracortical microstimulation. Thirty-five weeks of supervised laboratory sessions inside an approved early-feasibility trial (NCT03680872).

Conditions. Decoder accuracy sustained over months (84.6% over five months) so intended movements reliably trigger stimulation. Neurosurgical, engineering and rehabilitation teams present throughout. The gains were measured in the laboratory. Follow-up with the system switched off to confirm that strength and sensation gains persist.
