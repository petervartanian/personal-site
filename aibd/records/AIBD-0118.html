Double neural bypass restores Keith Thomas's movement and touch

depth 3 for a person

86% and 62% arm-strength gains

Keith Thomas, paralyzed since 2020, regained arm movement and touch after a 15-hour brain-implant operation in Manhasset, New York.

What happened

On 9 March 2023, surgeons led by Ashesh Mehta and Netanel Ben-Shalom implanted five microelectrode arrays in the brain of Keith Thomas, 45, in a 15-hour operation at North Shore University Hospital; a diving accident on 18 July 2020 had paralyzed him from the chest down. The Feinstein Institutes' double neural bypass decodes his movement intentions with recurrent neural networks and reinforcement learning, pairs them with patterned spinal cord stimulation, and uses cortical microstimulation to restore touch. By 28 July 2023 he could move his arms at will and feel his sister's hand; the Feinstein release of 16 July 2026 on the Nature Medicine paper reports 86% and 62% arm-strength gains, self-feeding, and gains persisting beyond two years.

What it was built to do. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03680872 describes an early feasibility trial of the Bidirectional Neural Bypass System meant to restore movement and sensation in the hand and wrist of up to seven people with tetraplegia; this benefit is exactly that purpose.

Who benefited

    personal

    Thomas regained voluntary arm movement, touch in a hand insensate since 2020, self-feeding and drinking from a cup, with strength gains persisting more than two years; life-changing for one identifiable person, not life-saving.

The system's part

The AI is the decoder: recurrent neural networks (an LSTM) and a deep reinforcement-learning agent that turn motor-cortex activity into stimulation patterns, reaching up to 84.6% decoding accuracy sustained over five months without retraining (Feinstein release, 16 July 2026). Restored touch came from patterned intracortical microstimulation, and the authors attribute the lasting strength gains to neuroplasticity from pairing the two. The decoder is necessary for real-time movement but is one part of a surgical, stimulation and rehabilitation package. The Nature Medicine paper does not name its participant; the Feinstein Institutes identify him as Thomas.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Chronic complete cervical spinal cord injury (C4 sensory/C5 motor) with movement intentions still readable from motor cortex. Five microelectrode arrays placed in motor and somatosensory cortex during a 15-hour mapped operation with the participant partly awake. An LSTM plus deep reinforcement-learning decoder paired with patterned spinal cord stimulation and intracortical microstimulation. Thirty-five weeks of supervised laboratory sessions inside an approved early-feasibility trial (NCT03680872).

What must hold again. Decoder accuracy sustained over months (84.6% over five months) so intended movements reliably trigger stimulation. Neurosurgical, engineering and rehabilitation teams present throughout; the gains were measured in the laboratory. Follow-up with the system switched off to confirm that strength and sensation gains persist.
