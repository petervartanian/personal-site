Seeing AI reads recipes aloud for a woman with retinitis pigmentosa

A 62-year-old woman with retinitis pigmentosa used the Microsoft app Seeing AI, which turns printed material into auditory output, to access printed recipes in her kitchen.

What happened

A case report in Case Reports in Ophthalmological Medicine (author at Vanderbilt University Medical Center; published 1 September 2025) describes comprehensive low-vision rehabilitation. The patient was a 62-year-old woman with autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa, best-corrected acuity 20/40–2 and severely constricted visual fields in both eyes. The rehabilitation included optical aids, white cane training and counseling. After the evaluation visit she was referred to Tennessee's blind and independent living skills training program. At the insistence of her independent living skills trainer she met a Technology Access Program specialist. She downloaded free iPhone apps, among them volunteer-based Be My Eyes and Seeing AI. Seeing AI uses the phone camera to turn printed material into auditory output. The report does not name Seeing AI's maker. The Microsoft attribution rests on the app's App Store listing. At a later meeting of the clinic's bimonthly support group, she reported using both applications in the kitchen. Seeing AI helped her access printed recipes. The author declares no conflicts of interest. Visit dates are not given. The journal received the case report on 18 November 2024, so the reported use of Seeing AI in her kitchen took place by that date.
