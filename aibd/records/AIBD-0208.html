IMIE intelligent retina restores partial vision to a blind woman

she could identify E optotypes (acuity 0.03, peak 0.1)

Blind over a decade, a 61-year-old woman read E optotypes and walked indoors unaided after a 256-channel IMIE retinal implant in Changsha.

What happened

On 23 April 2026 Prof. Xu Huizhuo's team at Xiangya Hospital, Central South University, Changsha, implanted the IMIE intelligent retina, a 256-channel epiretinal array driven by camera glasses and an external video processor, in a 61-year-old woman known as Auntie Chen, diagnosed with retinitis pigmentosa at 40 and blind for over ten years; Mark Humayun advised in theater; no serious complications occurred. On 5 June 2026 the hospital announced that after over a month of rehabilitation she could identify E optotypes (acuity 0.03, peak 0.1) and walk indoors and through doorways without an escort. She is the first subject of a Phase I trial; the team says she lacks full self-care ability and will be observed for a year.

What it was built to do. Built to restore partial sight to people blinded by photoreceptor degeneration by converting camera images into electrical stimulation of surviving retinal ganglion cells; the benefit recorded here is exactly that intended function.

The system's part

The system is the whole substitute visual pathway: camera glasses capture the scene, an external processor converts each frame in real time into stimulation patterns across 256 electrodes, and the implant stimulates surviving ganglion cells whose signals reach the visual cortex. The patient had no vision before, so the benefit is attributable to the system together with structured rehabilitation. The 2026 reports describe the conversion only as an algorithm inside an 'intelligent' retina; the 2021 IMIE 256 paper describes a video processing unit with a per-electrode threshold map and 15 gray levels and no learned model, so how much of the pipeline is AI in a machine-learning sense is not established by these sources.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • Surviving retinal ganglion cells and optic nerve in end-stage retinitis pigmentosa, so that epiretinal stimulation can reach the visual cortex
  • A 256-channel flexible array fixed on the macula with 250-micrometre tacks and per-patient fitting of stimulation currents
  • Camera glasses and an external processor converting live video into stimulation patterns in real time
  • More than a month of structured visual rehabilitation after implantation
  • A surgical team with prior IMIE 256 experience (five implants reported in TVST 2021) and on-site guidance from Mark Humayun

What must hold again

  • The implant stays positioned on the macula without serious complications; the 2021 series saw one array movement and one hypotony
  • Rehabilitation continues; the hospital's one-year observation period is still running
  • The reported acuity (0.03, peak 0.1) is phosphene vision, not natural sight; the hospital says the patient lacks full self-care ability
