IMIE intelligent retina restores partial vision to a blind woman

After Xiangya Hospital implanted the IMIE intelligent retina, a 61-year-old woman blind for over ten years could identify E optotypes and walk indoors without an escort.

  • 3 a person

    One identifiable person, totally blind for more than ten years, regained enough vision to identify E optotypes (0.03, peak 0.1). She can walk indoors and through doorways without an escort. The hospital cautions that she does not yet have full self-care ability and needs long-term rehabilitation.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

Status
Reported benefit. The hospital reported partial visual function after implantation and rehabilitation. The role of machine learning remains unresolved.

The system’s part
Not isolated. The system is the whole substitute visual pathway. Camera glasses capture the scene. An external processor converts each frame in real time into stimulation patterns across 256 electrodes. The implant stimulates surviving ganglion cells whose signals reach the visual cortex. The patient had no vision before, so the benefit is attributable to the system together with structured rehabilitation. The 2026 reports describe the conversion only as an algorithm inside an “intelligent” retina. The 2021 IMIE 256 paper describes a video processing unit with a per-electrode threshold map and 15 gray levels and no learned model. How much of that processing is AI in a machine-learning sense is not established by these sources.
Limits
The reports describe one early trial participant and a combined prosthesis-rehabilitation intervention. They do not establish that the image processing uses a learned model, durability of the gains, or restoration of full independence.
Costs and harms
The reported gain was basic visual recognition and indoor navigation. It depended on surgery and training and did not establish natural vision or full independent living.
Later
Nothing later is known. The June 2026 reports mention plans to expand the subject range and move toward registration review, but no later development was found.

What happened

On 23 April 2026, Professor Xu Huizhuo's team implanted the IMIE intelligent retina in a 61-year-old woman known as Auntie Chen. The team is at Xiangya Hospital, Central South University, Changsha. The implant is a 256-channel epiretinal array driven by camera glasses and an external video processor. She was diagnosed with retinitis pigmentosa at 40 and had been blind for over ten years. Mark Humayun advised in the operating room, and no serious complications occurred. On 5 June 2026, the hospital announced that after over a month of rehabilitation she could identify E optotypes (acuity 0.03, peak 0.1). She could also walk indoors and through doorways without an escort. She is the first subject of a Phase I trial. The team says that she lacks full self-care ability and that she will be observed for a year. In a review published on 27 July 2026, researchers at Ewha Womans University wrote that IMIE 256 remains an early-stage clinical platform. They said that long-term adoption, multicenter validation and definitive regulatory translation have not been established. Their review covers the 2021 results and does not discuss the 2026 Xiangya case.

Details

AI system

Configuration

The participant’s retinal implant had 256 channels and followed the IMIE 256 device described in 2021.

Inputs and outputs

Camera glasses captured the scene, and an external processor converted it into electrical stimulation across the retinal array. The patient learned to interpret the resulting visual sensations.

Workflow

Surviving retinal ganglion cells and optic nerve in end-stage retinitis pigmentosa, so that epiretinal stimulation can reach the visual cortex. A 256-channel flexible array fixed on the macula with 250-micrometer tacks and per-patient fitting of stimulation currents. Camera glasses and an external processor converting live video into stimulation patterns in real time. More than a month of structured visual rehabilitation after implantation. A surgical team with prior IMIE 256 experience (five implants reported in TVST 2021) and on-site guidance from Mark Humayun.

Human oversight

A specialist team implanted and calibrated the device, then supervised repeated visual rehabilitation. The patient had to practice interpreting the new signals.

People and places

Dependencies

The pathway required implanted electrodes, camera glasses, an external video processor, functioning downstream visual pathways, and rehabilitation support.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The implant stays positioned on the macula without serious complications. The 2021 series saw one array movement and one hypotony. Rehabilitation continues; the hospital's one-year observation period is still running. The reported acuity (0.03, peak 0.1) is phosphene vision, not natural sight. The hospital says the patient lacks full self-care ability.

Availability

The woman was the first participant in this clinical trial. The hospital described broader enrollment and registration review as future work.
