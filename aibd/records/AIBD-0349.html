Google's AEA alerts 16 million+ users to Marmara Ereğlisi quake

AEA detected the 23 April 2025 Mw 6.2 Marmara Ereğlisi quake in 5.31 s and alerted 16 million+ users (median 56 s warning for weak shaking); Google-authored evaluation; inland Take Action shortfall.

ⓘ

What happened

On 23 April 2025 the Mw 6.2 Marmara Ereğlisi earthquake struck offshore in the Sea of Marmara, Türkiye. Initial reports cited by the evaluation put shaking in Istanbul at MMI V. At least 359 people were injured, mostly from panic. Google's Android Earthquake Alerts (AEA) system uses the accelerometers of online Android phones. An on-phone detection algorithm flags sudden changes in acceleration. The system detected the event 5.31 seconds after its origin. It issued a first “Be Aware” alert 0.43 seconds later on an initial magnitude estimate of Mw 4.83. “Take Action” alerts followed as the estimate rose. Alerts reached more than 16 million Android users in Türkiye, Greece, Romania and Bulgaria. Warning times for weak shaking (MMI III) reached up to 150 seconds with a median of 56 seconds. The authors report 90% true positives and 99% precision.

The final magnitude estimate saturated at Mw 6.04, so the “Take Action” polygon was too small. Inland populations that felt MMI V shaking received only the “Be Aware” warning before stronger shaking arrived. The evaluation (Nature Communications, 11 August 2026) and the system description (Science, 17 July 2025) are written by Google employees. So is the user-feedback data. The same system underestimated the 6 February 2023 Türkiye earthquakes. The BBC reported on 27 July 2025 that only 469 “Take Action” alerts were sent for the M7.8 event. 10 million people could have received one.

Purpose. Crowd-sourced earthquake detection and early warning delivered to Android phones in countries without dense seismic networks. The alerts were the intended use.

AI’s contribution

The system itself generated the detection and the alerts. A few hundred phones triggered on the P wave. The system detected the event 5.31 s after origin and issued alerts within 0.43 s. Magnitude estimates were updated over the next 12 s. All performance figures and user-response data come from Google-authored papers (Mousavi et al. 2026; Allen et al. 2025). That is said plainly here. The only independent cited source, the BBC, concerns the 2023 underestimation. The read papers describe the detection as an on-phone algorithm that analyzes acceleration time series for sudden changes. They do not use the terms machine learning or artificial intelligence. Whether the system uses AI is not established by the sources.

Further evidence

Setting. Dense network of online Android phones (about 66,000 used for characterization) around an offshore epicentre. On-phone trigger on P-wave acceleration and server-side magnitude estimation updated over 12 s. Two-tier alerting (Be Aware, Take Action) delivered through the operating system. A population with high earthquake awareness after the 2023 Kahramanmaraş disaster, per the authors.

Conditions. Enough stationary, charging phones near the epicentre to trigger early. Magnitude estimates that do not saturate below the true magnitude. This failed here at Mw 6.04 and failed badly in 2023. Alerts that outrun S-wave arrival at the populated distance. Users who recognize and act on the alert.
