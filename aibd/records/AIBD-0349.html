Google's AEA alerts 16 million+ users to Marmara Ereğlisi quake

1 a personunrated an organization2 society

alerted 16 million+ users (median 56 s warning for weak shaking)

AEA detected the 23 Apr 2025 Mw 6.2 Marmara Ereğlisi quake in 5.31 s and alerted 16 million+ users (median 56 s warning for weak shaking); Google-authored evaluation; inland Take Action shortfall.

What happened

On 23 April 2025 the Mw 6.2 Marmara Ereğlisi earthquake struck offshore in the Sea of Marmara, Türkiye; initial reports cited by the evaluation put shaking in Istanbul at MMI V with at least 359 people injured, mostly from panic. Google's Android Earthquake Alerts (AEA) system, which uses the accelerometers of online Android phones and an on-phone detection algorithm that flags sudden changes in acceleration, detected the event 5.31 seconds after its origin and issued a first 'Be Aware' alert 0.43 seconds later on an initial magnitude estimate of Mw 4.83, followed by 'Take Action' alerts as the estimate rose; alerts reached more than 16 million Android users in Türkiye, Greece, Romania and Bulgaria. Warning times for weak shaking (MMI III) reached up to 150 seconds with a median of 56 seconds, and the authors report 90% true positives and 99% precision. Because the final magnitude estimate saturated at Mw 6.04, the 'Take Action' polygon was too small and inland populations that felt MMI V shaking received only the 'Be Aware' warning before stronger shaking arrived. The evaluation (Nature Communications, 11 August 2026) and the system description (Science, 17 July 2025) are written by Google employees, as is the user-feedback data. The same system underestimated the 6 February 2023 Türkiye earthquakes: the BBC reported on 27 July 2025 that only 469 'Take Action' alerts were sent for the M7.8 event when 10 million people could have received one.

What it was built to do. Crowd-sourced earthquake detection and early warning delivered to Android phones in countries without dense seismic networks; the alerts were the intended use.

The system's part

The system itself generated the detection and the alerts: a few hundred phones triggered on the P wave, the system detected the event 5.31 s after origin and issued alerts within 0.43 s, with magnitude estimates updated over the next 12 s. All performance figures and user-response data come from Google-authored papers (Mousavi et al. 2026; Allen et al. 2025) and are said so plainly here; the only independent source fetched, the BBC, concerns the 2023 underestimation. The fetched papers describe the detection as an on-phone algorithm that analyzes acceleration time series for sudden changes and do not use the terms machine learning or artificial intelligence, so whether this is an AI system in the database's sense is not established by the sources.

Replication

What produced it

  • Dense network of online Android phones (about 66,000 used for characterization) around an offshore epicentre
  • On-phone trigger on P-wave acceleration and server-side magnitude estimation updated over 12 s
  • Two-tier alerting (Be Aware, Take Action) delivered through the operating system
  • A population with high earthquake awareness after the 2023 Kahramanmaraş disaster, per the authors

What must hold again

  • Enough stationary, charging phones near the epicentre to trigger early
  • Magnitude estimates that do not saturate below the true magnitude, which failed here at Mw 6.04 and failed badly in 2023
  • Alerts that outrun S-wave arrival at the populated distance
  • Users who recognize and act on the alert
