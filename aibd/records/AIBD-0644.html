Brief from Peter

PRAIM is the largest of the non-randomized implementation studies in a 2026 meta-analysis of AI in screening mammography, and a second 2026 meta-analysis cites its safety-net result.1 Across 12 German screening sites, radiologists who read with Vara MG found 6.7 cancers per 1,000 women against 5.7 per 1,000 without it, and the recall rate did not rise. The safety net led to 204 cancer diagnoses that the study's authors judge would otherwise have been missed.2 The study was observational: Radiologists chose when to use the AI, and the authors report a reading-behavior bias. Vara funded the study and employed three of its authors.3

    The largest by far is PRAIM, a prospective evaluation across twelve German screening sites, in which 463,094 women were screened and radiologists chose voluntarily whether to use AI support; detection was 6.7 versus 5.7 per 1000, a ratio of 1.18 (1.06-1.31), with recall slightly lower and non-inferior

  3. Peer-reviewed study, by the makers.

    Eisemann N, Bunk S, Mukama T, et al. Nationwide real-world implementation of AI for cancer detection in population-based mammography screening. Nat Med. 2025;31:917-924. doi:10.1038/s41591-024-03408-6

    Radiologists in the AI-supported screening group achieved a breast cancer detection rate of 6.7 per 1,000, which was 17.6% (95% confidence interval: +5.7%, +30.8%) higher than and statistically superior to the rate (5.7 per 1,000) achieved in the control group.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Radiologists read in an AI-supported viewer that tagged likely normal examinations. A safety-net alert asked radiologists to look again at examinations that the AI judged suspicious. Final recall decisions stayed with radiologists in a double-reading program.

Measurement

Measurement

Screen-detected cancers and recalls from program records. [3]

Setting and sample

463,094 women aged 50 to 69 at 12 sites, July 2021 to February 2023; 119 radiologists. [3]

Measures

Breast cancer detection rate and recall rate per 1,000. [3]

Results

6.7 vs 5.7 per 1,000 (+17.6%); recall 37.4 vs 38.3 per 1,000. [3]

Additional value from AI

1.0 more cancer per 1,000 women. [3]

Limitations

No randomization; reading-behavior bias reported; Vara funded and co-wrote the study. [3]

Denominators

260,739 women read with AI support. [3]

Materials for replication

Anonymized dataset on Dryad; protocol online. [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Radiologists must accept a share of safety-net alerts and review them. The normal tag must not lead radiologists to miss cancers in tagged examinations. The AI must perform on the local population as it did in Germany.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2.
