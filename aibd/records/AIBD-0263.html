Neuralink N1 lets Noland Arbaugh control a cursor by thought

First PRIME-study participant, implanted at Barrow in January 2024, controls a computer by thought; threads retracted after a month, software fixes restored use; a second user drives a robot arm.

ⓘ

What happened

Noland Arbaugh, paralyzed after a diving accident, received the Neuralink N1 implant at Barrow Neurological Institute, Phoenix, in January 2024 (ABC15, 2 May 2024). He demonstrated online chess and cursor movement by thought. ABC News (17 May 2024) reported that threads retracted from his brain and removal was considered. It quoted him: “I can control a computer just like anyone else can”. Neuralink said it modified the recording algorithm and the translation of signals into cursor movements. Tech Times (28 May 2026) reported roughly 85% of threads retracted about a month after surgery and recovery through software refinement. It gave about 10 hours of daily use for chess, coursework, scheduling and communication at 28 months. Barrow (29 April 2026) reported the second participant, Alex Conley (C4-C5 injury, CONVOY study), controlling a robotic arm to operate light switches and doors.

The Debrief (7 July 2025) counted 7 users, 6 implanted at Barrow. Nature news (6 August 2024) noted the device “has been adjusted in a bid to prevent glitches”. No peer-reviewed report of participant outcomes was found in Europe PMC. Neuralink's own blog could not be opened. The implant was in January 2024.

Purpose. Restore digital autonomy (cursor control, later robotic-arm control) to people with quadriplegia through an implanted brain-computer interface

AI’s contribution

The decoding software that maps neural signals to cursor and arm movement is Neuralink's. Its role is described only in company statements quoted by news outlets (recording algorithm modified, signal-to-cursor translation improved). Outcomes rest on the participant's own account, Barrow's patient story and press reports, not on a peer-reviewed report.

Further evidence

Setting. Implanted electrode threads in motor cortex. Company decoding software translating neural signals into cursor or robotic-arm commands. Software recalibration after thread retraction.

Conditions. Electrode threads remain sufficiently in place or software compensates. Daily use continues to be reported by the user. Peer-reviewed outcome reporting (absent so far).
