What happened

The editorial of Newsletter 177 (Autumn 2023) of the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) confirms that GraphCast, Pangu-Weather and FourCastNet were made available on ECMWF's public charts pages from ECMWF's own initial conditions. Google DeepMind's blog post of 14 November 2023 states that in September 2023 a live version of its publicly available GraphCast model, deployed on the ECMWF website, predicted about nine days in advance that Hurricane Lee would make landfall in Nova Scotia. DeepMind dates the forecast only as “September”. Lee, by then post-tropical, made landfall on Long Island in southwestern Nova Scotia around 2000 UTC on 16 September 2023. Its maximum sustained winds were about 55 knots. On 14 November 2023 MIT Technology Review relayed the nine-day claim from Rémi Lam, a staff research scientist at DeepMind. It added that traditional forecasting models pinpointed Nova Scotia only six days in advance. The article quotes no ECMWF staff on Lee. The tropical cyclone report on Lee by the National Hurricane Center (NHC), dated 21 March 2024, records the landfall and mentions no AI model. It reports that official NHC track errors were exceptionally low through 72 hours and below five-year means through 120 hours. The NHC verification report for 2023 contains no entry for an AI model, and no ECMWF document recording the Lee forecast is on record. A preprint by researchers at the Cooperative Institute for Research in the Atmosphere (CIRA) at Colorado State University, who are independent of DeepMind, mentions Lee only for its intensity and structure. In October 2025 researchers at CIRA published a verification of AI weather models for the 2023 cyclone season, run from initial conditions of the Global Forecast System. They found that GraphCast had the lowest mean track errors from 96 hours onward, by up to 13% against the ECMWF model, although the differences were not statistically significant. They do not report a track result for Lee. No cited source documents a forecaster or authority acting on the GraphCast Lee run.

Details

AI system

Model

GraphCast.

Role

GraphCast generated weather forecasts, including the projected path of Hurricane Lee.

Configuration

Researchers used the live GraphCast forecasts available through ECMWF.

Inputs and outputs

ECMWF initial weather conditions were processed by GraphCast to generate an experimental forecast of Lee’s path.

Workflow

GraphCast trained on decades of ERA5 reanalysis and run live from ECMWF initial conditions on ECMWF's experimental charts. A long-lived Cabo Verde hurricane whose track was, per NHC, easier than normal to forecast (low climatology-persistence errors). Vendor retrospective comparison of its own run against conventional models.

Human oversight

No cited source documents a forecaster or authority acting on the GraphCast Lee run. ECMWF ran GraphCast experimentally on its public charts pages, and the National Hurricane Center's report on Lee mentions no AI model. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Benefits

Reach

No source counts beneficiaries or documents anyone acting on the GraphCast Lee run. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Duration

The claimed benefit is a single forecast made in September 2023. In October 2025 CIRA researchers reported that GraphCast had the lowest mean track errors from 96 hours onward among the models they verified for the 2023 season. The differences were not statistically significant, and the researchers give no track result for Lee. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Measurement

Measurement

NHC verification procedures applied to 7-day track and intensity forecasts, compared with NHC and JTWC best tracks. [7]

Setting and sample

Northern Hemisphere tropical cyclones, May to November 2023; four open AI models run four times a day from GFS initial conditions, with a 2022 to 2023 comparison from IFS initial conditions. [7]

Measures

Mean track error, detection rate, intensity error and bias. [7]

Results

GraphCast had mean track errors up to 13% smaller than the next best physics-based model (ECMWF) at 96 hours and later. [7]

Additional value from AI

Not statistically significant at long lead times; GraphCast errors were larger than ECMWF's at some early lead times. [7]

Limitations

The sample is one season; the runs used GFS initial conditions and not the ECMWF-hosted live run, and the authors report that IFS initial conditions improved AI track forecasts significantly; the authors do not verify the Lee landfall forecast. [7]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Independent verification of the specific nine-day run against the official track record (not found in any ECMWF or NHC document among the sources). Access to ECMWF initial conditions for the live run. A forecaster or authority actually consulting the AI run for it to become an attributable benefit.
