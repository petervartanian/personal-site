GraphCast may have forecast Lee's landfall nine days ahead

DeepMind says a live GraphCast run on ECMWF's site forecast Lee's Nova Scotia landfall about nine days ahead; NHC records landfall 16 September 2023; no forecaster use of the run is documented.

ⓘ

What happened

Google DeepMind's blog post of 14 November 2023 describes a live version of its publicly available GraphCast model, deployed on the ECMWF website. In September 2023 it predicted about nine days in advance that Hurricane Lee would make landfall in Nova Scotia. MIT Technology Review (14 November 2023) relayed the claim from DeepMind staff research scientist Rémi Lam. It added that traditional forecasting models pinpointed Nova Scotia only six days in advance. The article quotes no ECMWF staff on Lee. ECMWF's Newsletter 177 editorial (Autumn 2023) confirms that GraphCast, Pangu-Weather and FourCastNet were made available on ECMWF's public charts pages from ECMWF's own initial conditions. The NHC tropical cyclone report on Lee (21 March 2024) records the landfall. Lee, by then post-tropical, made landfall on Long Island in southwestern Nova Scotia around 2000 UTC on 16 September 2023.

Maximum sustained winds were about 55 kt. NHC official track errors were exceptionally low through 72 h and below five-year means through 120 h. The report mentions no AI model. No ECMWF document recording the Lee forecast is on record, and the NHC 2023 verification report contains no AI-model entry. It found only an intensity-structure mention of Lee in the independent CIRA preprint. The forecast itself is dated only as “September” by the vendor. No cited source documents a forecaster or authority acting on the GraphCast Lee run.

Purpose. Machine-learning global medium-range weather forecasting (10 days, 0.25 degree), including severe-event prediction such as tropical cyclone tracks. This forecast was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The only sources for the nine-day Lee forecast are the maker's own blog and a news report quoting the maker's scientist. ECMWF's editorial confirms only that GraphCast ran experimentally on its charts. NHC's Lee report records exceptionally low official track errors without mentioning AI guidance. The AI output is therefore documented as a forecast, not as an input to any decision.

Since then
An independent Uppsala University evaluation published 7 November 2024 confirmed GraphCast's general advantage over ECMWF's IFS HRES, including on near-surface temperature and wind extremes; it does not examine the Hurricane Lee forecast, and the NHC's March 2024 report on Lee names no AI model.Both data-driven models perform significantly better than ECMWF's IFS HRES globally and in most regions. Most impressively, GraphCast significantly outperforms IFS HRES across all regions and lead times. source

Further evidence

Setting. GraphCast trained on decades of ERA5 reanalysis and run live from ECMWF initial conditions on ECMWF's experimental charts. A long-lived Cabo Verde hurricane whose track was, per NHC, easier than normal to forecast (low climatology-persistence errors). Vendor retrospective comparison of its own run against conventional models.

Conditions. Independent verification of the specific nine-day run against the official track record (not found in any ECMWF or NHC document among the sources). Access to ECMWF initial conditions for the live run. A forecaster or authority actually consulting the AI run for it to become an attributable benefit.
