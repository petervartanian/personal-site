Saronic Corsair sea drone picks up two Apache crew off Oman

depth 3 for a person, depth 2 for an organization

Independently reported, 6 sources.

Task Force 59's Corsair sea drone picked up two Apache crew shot down off Oman and took them to a helicopter hoist within about two hours.

What happened

At 7:33 p.m. EDT on 8 June 2026 (9 June in Oman's time zone), a U.S. Army AH-64 Apache patrolling near the Strait of Hormuz went down off Oman; President Trump said Iranian forces shot it down. U.S. Central Command said a Navy Corsair unmanned surface vessel, built by Saronic and fielded by Task Force 59 since late March, located the two crew, who boarded it, and carried them to a point on the water where a helicopter hoisted them for onward transport. CENTCOM said both were rescued within approximately two hours and were in stable condition, and that the Corsair was the closest and quickest asset. Officials called it the first real-world aircrew recovery by an unmanned surface vessel.

Not what it was built to do. Saronic describes the Corsair as a 24-foot multi-mission autonomous surface vessel, and gCaptain lists its design missions as maritime security, surveillance, force protection and logistics; Task Force 59 fielded it from late March 2026 to extend CENTCOM's maritime domain awareness. Recovering downed aircrew is not a stated mission: CENTCOM's spokesman said such sea drones had been used in exercises but not in real life, and that the Corsair was chosen because it was the closest and quickest asset, so the benefit was an improvised use of a surveillance and logistics platform rather than its built purpose.

The system's part

Not assessed. Saronic and gCaptain describe the Corsair as an autonomous surface vessel with autonomous navigation software that can run under remote supervision or autonomously, and Task Force 59 is the Navy's unit for unmanned systems and artificial intelligence. CENTCOM says the drone located and picked up the crew and carried them to a hoist point, but no source on file states whether it navigated autonomously or was remotely supervised during the recovery, how it found the two men, or what its sensors contributed; DefenseScoop's remark that the 360-degree sensing likely helped is the reporter's inference. The hoist and onward transport were done by a crewed helicopter, and Nextgov reports that U.S. Naval Forces Central Command, the 82nd Airborne Division and Air Force and Navy units assisted in the rescue.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A Corsair unmanned surface vessel already fielded in theater by Task Force 59 since late March 2026 and close enough to the crash site to be, in CENTCOM's words, the closest and quickest asset. A crewed helicopter able to hoist the crew from the vessel at a second point on the water and carry them onward. Two aviators who survived the shoot-down uninjured and could board a 24-foot vessel from the water.

What must hold again. The vessel must reach the crew before exposure or hostile forces do; here recovery took about two hours and the sources do not say how close Iranian forces were. Someone must still complete the rescue chain, here a helicopter hoist and onward transport. The vessel's navigation and sensing must work in a contested strait where, as the House Armed Services Committee noted the same week, navigation jamming and cut communications with crewed ships are to be expected.
