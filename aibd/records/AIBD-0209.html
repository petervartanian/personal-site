Saronic's Corsair sea drone picks up two Apache crew members off Oman

A remotely piloted Saronic Corsair sea drone carried two Apache crew off Oman to a helicopter hoist; Central Command reported that both were rescued within approximately two hours.

What happened

U.S. Central Command reported that two Army AH-64 Apache crew members were rescued at 7:33 p.m. Eastern time on 8 June 2026, or 3:33 a.m. on 9 June in Oman. That time marks the rescue and is distinct from the time that the helicopter went down. Central Command said that both were rescued within approximately two hours and that they were stable. Its release said that the cause of the helicopter’s loss was under investigation. In a direct interview with DefenseScoop, Captain Tim Hawkins, a spokesperson for Central Command, identified the vessel as a Saronic Corsair operated by the Navy’s Task Force 59. He said that it picked up the crew and carried them to another point on the water for a helicopter hoist. Al-Monitor reported on 10 June 2026 that military officials told U.S. media that an operator piloted the Corsair from afar. Stars and Stripes reported the same day that the vessel was guided to the site, located the soldiers and carried them to safer waters. Mark Cancian of the Center for Strategic and International Studies told the paper that a drone gives the same capability at less risk than a crewed platform. The sources do not identify which functions of the vessel ran on their own during the rescue. On 18 September 2026, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth told Saronic employees that the pilots are alive because of the vessel. No finding on the cause of the helicopter's loss has been published.
