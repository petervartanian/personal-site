Saronic Corsair sea drone picks up two Apache crew off Oman

A Navy Corsair sea drone picked up two Apache crew off Oman and carried them to a helicopter hoist; CENTCOM reported that both were rescued within about two hours.

ⓘ

What happened

U.S. Central Command reported that two Army AH-64 Apache crew members were rescued at 7:33 p.m. Eastern time on June 8, 2026, or 3:33 a.m. on June 9 in Oman. That is the rescue time, not the time that the helicopter went down. CENTCOM said that recovery took approximately two hours and that both crew members were stable. In a direct interview with DefenseScoop, Capt. Tim Hawkins identified the vessel as a Saronic Corsair operated by the Navy’s Task Force 59. He said that it picked up the crew and carried them to another point on the water for a helicopter hoist. The official release said that the cause of the helicopter’s loss was under investigation. The sources do not identify which autonomous functions were used during the rescue.

Unexpected use. The Corsair is an autonomous maritime platform that Task Force 59 had been fielding since late March 2026. In this case, the military used it to carry stranded crew members to a helicopter recovery point.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. Hawkins identified an unmanned Corsair as the recovery vessel. DefenseScoop describes the platform’s autonomous capabilities, but its suggestion that passive sensing helped locate the crew is an inference. The official sources do not identify the navigation mode, human supervision, or sensor contribution during this rescue. A crewed helicopter completed the recovery.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Two aviators were recovered from the water and transferred to a helicopter within approximately two hours, according to CENTCOM. Their condition was stable. The sources do not establish the counterfactual risk of death or whether another rescue asset could have reached them.

  • an organization

    The military recovered two crew members using an unmanned vessel and a helicopter. The sources describe a successful operational recovery but do not isolate the AI’s contribution or quantify an organizational saving.
