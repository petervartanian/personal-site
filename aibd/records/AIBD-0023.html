Observed 1 January 2026

An Apple Watch called for help after a runner’s heart stopped

Fall Detection on his Apple Watch Series 10 called emergency services, and he recovered fully after CPR and hospital treatment.

ⓘ

What happened

A case report published on 12 August 2026 describes a 69-year-old man who collapsed from ventricular fibrillation while running alone. He was on a dead-end road, hidden behind a hedge. The paper gives no date or place. The authors' hospitals are Radboud University Medical Center, Nijmegen, and Slingeland Hospital, Doetinchem. His Apple Watch (reported as Apple Watch 10) with Fall Detection enabled detected the fall. It automatically alerted his predefined emergency contacts and the emergency medical services by calling 112. Family members reached him, found him unresponsive and began cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) about 6–7 minutes after the collapse. An ambulance arrived about 1.5 minutes later, defibrillated him and restored circulation. He underwent coronary artery bypass grafting and defibrillator implantation, was discharged on day 29 and made a full neurological recovery.

Two interviews identify the patient as Alwi Siebers, 69, of Varsseveld, and date the collapse to 1 January 2026. Men's Health NL (28 April 2026) opens “1 januari 2026 begint voor de 69-jarige Alwi Siebers als een gewone hardloopdag. De fanatieke loper uit Varsseveld is in training voor de halve marathon van Montferland” (1 January 2026 begins for 69-year-old Alwi Siebers as an ordinary running day; the keen runner from Varsseveld is training for the Montferland half marathon). His son Tristan says “De locatie was heel precies. Hij lag achter een heg op een afgelegen plek. Zonder die melding had ik hem niet zo snel gevonden” (the location was very precise; he was lying behind a hedge in a remote spot; without that alert I would not have found him so quickly).

WANT (1 May 2026) quotes Siebers, “Ik weet via mijn activiteitenlog dat ik om twaalf over twaalf aan mijn trainingsrondje begon” (I know from my activity log that I started my training loop at twelve past twelve). It reproduces the alert his son received, headed “do 1 jan, 12:12”. The interviews match the paper on the man's age and the Apple Watch Series 10 with fall detection. They also match on the automatic alert to 112 and to his emergency contacts, the hedge and the remote spot. They match on the son starting CPR and the daughter giving mouth-to-mouth, and a history of asthma and bronchiectasis. They match on six days in intensive care, four bypass grafts, an ICD and a full recovery. No source names both the paper and Siebers, so the identification is inferred from these details.

Men's Health says that Siebers also pressed the SOS button himself and that a pulse returned within about three minutes. The paper's timeline does not support that: CPR after 6–7 minutes, four shocks, adrenaline and amiodarone before circulation returned. The street is probable but inferred. A fire-brigade pager message of 1 January 2026 at 12:18:59 records a resuscitation on the Oude Doetinchemseweg in Varsseveld. That is a dead-end road at the western edge of the village. The map in the alert screenshot shows the pin on a side road off the Doetinchemseweg. No source names the street in connection with Siebers.

Purpose. Fall Detection is built to recognize a hard fall and, if the wearer stays immobile, to call emergency services and notify contacts with the location. That is what it did.

AI’s contribution

The paper reports that the watch recognized the fall and automatically called 112 and alerted emergency contacts. Those alerts brought responders to the man behind the hedge. Humans provided resuscitation, defibrillation, and surgery. The watch's contribution is documented in the clinical narrative, but the time to rescue or outcome without it cannot be measured from this case. Apple's documentation describes the detection and notification sequence without disclosing the classifier or model version.

Further evidence

Setting. Fall Detection is enabled on a worn Apple Watch with cellular or phone connectivity. The wearer collapses with an impact the classifier recognizes and then stays immobile. Emergency services and contacts can reach the shared location quickly.

Conditions. The watch has connectivity at the scene. The collapse produces a detectable fall signature. Responders arrive within the window in which resuscitation can succeed.
