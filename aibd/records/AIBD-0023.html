Apple Watch Fall Detection calls 112 after runner's VF collapse

CPR at 6-7 min, full neurological recovery

Apple Watch Fall Detection called 112 and family after a runner's VF collapse; CPR at 6-7 min, full neurological recovery (NL).

What happened

In a case report published on 12 August 2026 (event date not stated; publication date used), a 69-year-old man in the Netherlands (the country is not stated in the text; the authors' hospitals are Radboud University Medical Center, Nijmegen, and Slingeland Hospital, Doetinchem) collapsed from ventricular fibrillation while running alone on a dead-end road, hidden behind a hedge. His Apple Watch (reported as Apple Watch 10) with Fall Detection enabled detected the fall and automatically alerted his predefined emergency contacts and the emergency medical services by calling 112. Family members reached him, found him unresponsive and began CPR about 6-7 minutes after the collapse; an ambulance arrived about 1.5 minutes later, defibrillated him and restored circulation. He underwent coronary artery bypass grafting and defibrillator implantation, was discharged on day 29 and made a full neurological recovery.

What it was built to do. Fall Detection is built to recognise a hard fall and, if the wearer stays immobile, to call emergency services and notify contacts with the location; that is what it did.

The system's part

The watch's fall-detection classifier recognised the collapse and, with no response from the wearer, placed the 112 call and contact alerts that brought responders; defibrillation and surgery were delivered by humans. The counterfactual is strong because nobody else could see him.

Replication

What produced it

  • Fall Detection is enabled on a worn Apple Watch with cellular or phone connectivity
  • The wearer collapses with an impact the classifier recognises and then stays immobile
  • Emergency services and contacts can reach the shared location quickly

What must hold again

  • The watch has connectivity at the scene
  • The collapse produces a detectable fall signature
  • Responders arrive within the window in which resuscitation can succeed
