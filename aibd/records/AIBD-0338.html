IRS applies AI to select large partnerships for examination

What happened

On 8 September 2023 the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) announced (IR-2023–166) a compliance effort funded by the Inflation Reduction Act. It would use improved technology and artificial intelligence to help compliance teams detect tax cheating, identify emerging compliance threats and improve case-selection tools. It described data scientists and tax-enforcement experts collaborating to apply machine learning to compliance risk in partnership, income and international tax. It planned to open examinations of 75 of the largest US partnerships, each averaging more than $10 billion in assets. These fall under the expanded Large Partnership Compliance program launched in 2021. A US Government Accountability Office (GAO) WatchBlog post of 6 June 2024 adds detail. The IRS already uses AI models to select a representative sample of returns for its annual audits.

It uses two AI models to prioritize partnership returns for audit. It is piloting a new model for refundable-credit audits that IRS officials said finds risky taxpayers more effectively than the previous model. GAO recommended documenting the models and noted design weaknesses that may limit their ability to identify the highest-risk returns objectively. No cited source attributes a recovered amount to the AI selection.

What it was built to do. Built to select high-risk returns for examination and to detect compliance threats. The claimed benefit is that purpose, with no attributable outcome documented.

The system's part

The models prioritize or select returns for human examiners. Examinations and any assessments are human decisions. The IRS release is the deployer's own account of a system it builds in-house. The GAO post is an independent government source that relays IRS officials' claims about the pilot model and adds caveats. Neither reports an outcome attributable to the AI.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. Inflation Reduction Act funding for IRS compliance work. Collaboration between IRS data scientists and tax-enforcement experts. Partnership return data for the Large Partnership Compliance program launched in 2021.

What must hold again. Sustained funding and staffing for the examinations the models select. Documentation of the models, which GAO found lacking in 2024. Correction of the design weaknesses GAO said may limit objective identification of high-risk returns.
