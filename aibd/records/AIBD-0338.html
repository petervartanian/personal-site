IRS applies AI to select large partnerships for examination

The IRS Large Partnership Compliance Model, an AI model, flagged 150 large partnership returns for review in April 2023; no examination that it selected had closed by May 2025.

The system’s part
GAO reports that the Large Partnership Compliance Model scores returns on risk indicators drawn from accounting rules, tax law and a machine learning algorithm, and that IRS experts weight the scores. Classifiers then review the flagged returns, and examiners decide on any assessment. GAO found that the partnership models rest on untested assumptions and that the IRS has no formal process for using audit results to improve them. No source compares the yield of model-selected audits with that of other selection methods.
Limits
Model adoption does not establish that the IRS recovered additional tax or selected returns more fairly. TIGTA reported in May 2025 that no examination selected by the Large Partnership Compliance Model had closed. No source compares the yield of model-selected audits with that of other selection methods.
Costs and harms
The 2025 inspector-general audit found that the IRS lacked a process to show whether AI selection improved examination results. Poor selection can waste examiner time and burden compliant taxpayers.
Later
Persisted, 19 May 2025. TIGTA's May 2025 audit found the Large Partnership Compliance model still in production, with the first 82 high-risk TY 2021 returns under examination, no closed examination results yet, and uncertainty over reduced staffing after the end of the contractor's work.

What happened

In April 2023 the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) used its new Large Partnership Compliance Model for the first time. It flagged 150 tax year 2021 returns for review. A US Government Accountability Office (GAO) report of 27 July 2023 describes this first use. GAO found that the partnership models rest on untested assumptions. It also found that more than 80 percent of earlier large partnership audits had closed with no change. On 8 September 2023 the IRS announced (IR-2023–166) a compliance effort funded by the Inflation Reduction Act. It would use improved technology and artificial intelligence to help compliance teams detect tax cheating, identify emerging compliance threats and improve case-selection tools. It described data scientists and tax-enforcement experts who collaborate to apply machine learning to compliance risk in partnership, income and international tax. It planned to open examinations of 75 of the largest US partnerships, each averaging more than $10 billion in assets. These fall under the expanded Large Partnership Compliance program launched in 2021. A GAO WatchBlog post of 6 June 2024 adds detail. The IRS already uses AI models to select a representative sample of returns for its annual audits. It uses two AI models to prioritize partnership returns for audit. IRS officials said that a new model in pilot for refundable-credit audits finds risky taxpayers more effectively than the previous model. GAO recommended that the IRS document the models. It noted that design weaknesses may limit the ability of the models to identify the highest-risk returns objectively. The Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration (TIGTA) reported in May 2025 that the first 82 high-risk tax year 2021 returns selected by the model were under examination. It reported that no examination selected by the model had yet closed. No cited source attributes a recovered amount to the AI selection.

AI system

Model

The Large Partnership Compliance model for tax year 2021.

Role

The models prioritized tax returns for human examiners.

Configuration

The IRS used its Large Partnership Compliance model for tax year 2021.

Autonomy

Models prioritized returns for examination, while IRS examiners investigated and decided any tax adjustments.

Workflow

Inflation Reduction Act funding for IRS compliance work. Collaboration between IRS data scientists and tax-enforcement experts. Partnership return data for the Large Partnership Compliance program launched in 2021.

Human oversight

GAO reports that IRS experts weight the risk scores of the Large Partnership Compliance Model and that classifiers review the returns that it flags. Examiners decide on any assessment. GAO found that the IRS has no formal process for using audit results to improve the models. [1] [2] [3] [4]
