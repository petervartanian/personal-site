IRS applies AI to select large partnerships for examination

IRS (8 Sep 2023): AI to help detect cheating and select cases; exams planned for 75 partnerships averaging over $10bn in assets. GAO (Jun 2024): two AI models prioritise partnership audits.

What happened

On 8 September 2023 the Internal Revenue Service announced (IR-2023-166) a compliance effort funded by the Inflation Reduction Act that would use improved technology and artificial intelligence to help compliance teams detect tax cheating, identify emerging compliance threats and improve case-selection tools. It described a collaboration between data scientists and tax-enforcement experts applying machine learning to identify compliance risk in partnership, income and international tax, and said it planned to open examinations of 75 of the largest US partnerships, each averaging more than $10 billion in assets, under the expanded Large Partnership Compliance program launched in 2021. A US Government Accountability Office WatchBlog post of 6 June 2024 reports that the IRS already uses AI models to select a representative sample of returns for its annual audits, uses two AI models to prioritise partnership returns for audit, and is piloting a new model for refundable-credit audits that IRS officials said finds risky taxpayers more effectively than the previous model; GAO recommended documenting the models and noted design weaknesses that may limit their ability to identify the highest-risk returns objectively. No fetched source attributes a recovered amount to the AI selection, so this is recorded as a general benefit without an attributable event; the date is the IRS release date.

What it was built to do. Built to select high-risk returns for examination and to detect compliance threats; the claimed benefit is that purpose, with no attributable outcome documented.

The system's part

The models prioritise or select returns for human examiners; examinations and any assessments are human decisions. The IRS release is the deployer's own account of a system it builds in-house (marked independent by the database's convention for public bodies), and the GAO post is an independent government source that relays IRS officials' claims about the pilot model and adds caveats; neither reports an outcome attributable to the AI.

What must hold again

  • Sustained funding and staffing for the examinations the models select
  • Documentation of the models, which GAO found lacking in 2024
  • Correction of the design weaknesses GAO said may limit objective identification of high-risk returns
