UK model flags risky benefit advances, saving an estimated £4.4 million

A Department for Work and Pensions machine learning model flags high-risk Universal Credit advance requests for caseworkers; the department estimates that it saved £4.4 million in three years.

  • 2 an organization

    The National Audit Office reports the department's finding that the model is about three times better at identifying fraud risk than a random control sample, a measured gain in capability. The saving of £4.4 million over three years is small. Civil Service World reports that it rests on about 7,000 rejected requests out of more than a million advances a year.

  • Not rated society

    No source reports an outcome for claimants or the public beyond the department's estimated saving. Referred claimants face added evidence demands. The department's fairness analyses showed that older claimants and claimants who are not UK nationals were over-referred.

The system’s part
The NAO describes a model trained on historical fraud and error data that scores advance requests and refers those above a threshold. Every payment decision is made by a caseworker, so the model's contribution is prioritization. Developer and deployer are the same public body (DWP). The NAO, an independent auditor, wrote the report.
Limits
The estimate is not net savings because DWP cannot separate model development and operation costs. Human caseworkers decide payment eligibility. A median one-day delay and over-referral of older and non-UK claimants qualify the benefit, while incomplete protected-characteristic data limit the fairness assessment.
Costs and harms
More targeted reviews came with payment delays and uneven referral rates across demographic groups. The £4.4 million estimate cannot establish net savings because the model's development and operating costs were not separately identified.

What happened

Since May 2022 the Department for Work & Pensions (DWP) has used a machine learning model. The model flags potentially fraudulent claims for Universal Credit advances. It scores new claims and refers those above a threshold to a caseworker. DWP staff make the decisions. On 6 December 2024 the Guardian reported documents released under the Freedom of Information Act. They show a statistically significant disparity by age, disability, marital status and nationality in the model's February 2024 fairness analysis. The National Audit Office (NAO) published “Tackling benefit overpayments due to fraud and error” on 22 October 2025. It reports that DWP estimates that the model saved £4.4 million from 2022–23 to 2024–25. DWP cannot separate the model's costs from wider spending on data analytics. The NAO reports DWP's finding that the model is around three times more effective at identifying fraud risk than a randomized control group sample. It reports a median payment delay of one day for referred requests. It reports that DWP's July 2025 fairness analysis for 2024–25 showed that older claimants (age groups 45 to 54 and above) and non-UK nationals were over-referred for review. Fairness analysis was possible on only one of nine protected characteristics. Four further models were in development. On the same day Civil Service World (Jim Dunton) reported the NAO's warnings on data infrastructure. It quoted Transformation Minister Andrew Western, who said that “our one machine learning model has made us significantly more effective”. On 16 April 2026 Civil Service World reported a letter in which DWP's permanent secretary told the Public Accounts Committee that the model had directly saved less than £5 million in three years by flagging about 7,000 requests that staff rejected. It also reported that the department admits that the model needs retraining. On 9 July 2026 DWP published an effectiveness assessment for April 2025 to March 2026. DWP reported that the retrained model was 2.5 times more effective at identifying high-risk advances than a random control group. It concluded that the model should keep operating.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The model scored new Universal Credit advance requests for fraud or error risk. Requests above a threshold were referred for review, and payment waited for a caseworker's response.

Workflow

A model trained on historical fraud and error data in advance claims. Referrals above a threshold are reviewed by caseworkers alongside control-group referrals with scores removed.

Human oversight

Caseworkers decided whether to pay. They received both model-selected and control-group referrals without the risk scores, which reduced the risk that the score would predetermine their judgment.
