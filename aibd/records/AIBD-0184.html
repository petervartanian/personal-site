What happened

The National Audit Office (NAO) published “Tackling benefit overpayments due to fraud and error” on 22 October 2025. It states that since May 2022 the Department for Work & Pensions (DWP) has used a machine learning model. The model flags potentially fraudulent claims for Universal Credit advances. DWP estimates it saved £4.4 million from 2022–23 to 2024–25. The model scores new claims and refers those above a threshold to a caseworker. Decisions are made by DWP staff. DWP cannot disaggregate the model's costs from wider spending on data analytics. The NAO reports DWP's finding that the model is around three times more effective at identifying fraud risk than a randomized control group sample. It reports a median payment delay of one day for referred requests.

DWP's July 2025 fairness analysis for 2024–25 showed older claimants (age groups 45 to 54 and above) and non-UK nationals over-referred for review. Fairness analysis was possible on only one of nine protected characteristics. Four further models were in development. Civil Service World (Jim Dunton, 22 October 2025) reported the NAO's warnings on data infrastructure. It quoted Transformation Minister Andrew Western saying that the one machine learning model has made the department significantly more effective. The date used is the NAO publication date.

What it was built to do. Assess the fraud risk of Universal Credit advance requests and refer high-risk requests to a caseworker for review.

The system's part

The NAO describes a model trained on historical fraud and error data that scores advance requests and refers those above a threshold. Every payment decision is made by a caseworker, so the model's contribution is prioritization. Developer and deployer are the same public body (DWP). The NAO, an independent auditor, wrote the report.

Replication

What produced it. A model trained on historical fraud and error data in advance claims. Referrals above a threshold are reviewed by caseworkers alongside control-group referrals with scores removed.

What must hold again. A human decision on every advance. Regular fairness assessments (age is the only protected characteristic in the model). Feedback of review outcomes into the model.
