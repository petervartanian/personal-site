Lynn White says ChatGPT and Perplexity won her eviction appeal

3 a person

avoided roughly $55,000 in penalties and over $18,000 in overdue rent

Long Beach tenant Lynn White used ChatGPT and Perplexity to overturn her eviction on appeal, avoiding about $55,000 in penalties, per NBC.

What happened

Lynn White, a small music production business owner living in a Long Beach, California, trailer park, was allegedly behind on rent and facing eviction. Represented by a lawyer from a local tenant advocacy network, she lost a jury trial. To appeal, arguing that Los Angeles Covid-era rules allowed a 12-month rent deferral, she turned to ChatGPT and the AI search engine Perplexity, gave them her documents, and had them find procedural errors by the judge, research the law and draft her court responses. NBC News reported on 8 October 2025 that, after several months of litigation, she overturned the eviction and avoided roughly $55,000 in penalties and over $18,000 in overdue rent. No court, docket or ruling date is given.

What it was built to do. ChatGPT is a general-purpose assistant for answering questions and drafting text and Perplexity an AI search engine; using them to research law, spot procedural errors and draft filings is within that general purpose, though neither is built as a legal service, and the same NBC report documents litigants sanctioned for AI-invented case citations.

The system's part

not assessed White told NBC News that she gave ChatGPT her documents and case details, that it and Perplexity identified procedural errors by the judge, researched the applicable law and drafted her responses to the court, and that she could never have won the appeal without AI. No court document, opposing-party or judicial account of what decided the appeal is cited, so the tools' contribution relative to the Covid-era rent-deferral rules she invoked and her own advocacy is unassessed.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • A litigant with time, her case documents and $40 a month for paid ChatGPT and Perplexity Pro, feeding the tools the record and detailed case facts
  • A substantive ground to find, here Los Angeles Covid-era rules allowing a 12-month rent deferral, and procedural errors at trial that an appeal could reach
  • A court system in which a self-represented tenant can appeal an eviction judgment

What must hold again

  • The tools' legal citations and procedural claims are checked before filing; the same NBC article documents litigants sanctioned for AI-invented cases
  • The litigant reads and verifies what the tools draft rather than filing it as produced
  • The underlying legal position is sound; the tools surfaced rules and errors, they did not create the rent-deferral protection
