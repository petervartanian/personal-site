Los Angeles police robot forms a barrier in a Hollywood bus standoff

A Los Angeles police drone observed a sleeping man with a suspected gun on a bus; officers then sent the Boston Dynamics Spot robot in as a barrier, and the gun proved to be a BB gun.

  • 2 an organization

    A drone observed that the man was asleep. By the department's own report, the robot then stood between him and the gun. The two-hour standoff ended in an arrest with no injury reported. No source shows that officers would otherwise have been hurt.

  • Not rated a person

    The passengers had been evacuated before the robot was used, and the man was taken into custody unharmed. None of the reports ties either outcome to the robot. They describe the man waking and leaving the bus.

What happened

Just before 4 a.m. on 8 November 2023, a passenger on a Metro bus in Hollywood reported an armed man aboard. The bus was at Melrose and Western avenues. The driver pulled over and evacuated the other passengers. Officers got no response from the man, and a two-hour standoff followed (Los Angeles Times via Police1, 9 November 2023). The quarterly report of the Los Angeles Police Department (LAPD) says that patrol officers tried to talk with the man for about 75 minutes. They then declared an armed barricade and called the special weapons and tactics (SWAT) team. The LAPD sent in its Boston Dynamics Spot quadruped. The report states that a drone observed the sleeping man and the suspected gun before the quadruped entered. It adds that SWAT chose the quadruped because a tracked robot could not have climbed the bus steps. The report states that the quadruped climbed the bus steps and formed a barrier between the man and the suspected firearm. It does not describe the robot removing the weapon. The man woke, left the bus and was taken into custody about 6:40 a.m. The weapon was a BB gun (Police1). Police1 attributes the initial observation to the quadruped’s camera. International Business Times' October 2024 account says that the robot removed the gun. Deputy Chief David Kowalski approves each use. The robot cost nearly $300,000 (NBC). By 1 January 2025 SWAT had deployed the robot at least seven times, Spectrum News reported.

Details

AI system

Role

The robot climbed the bus steps and formed a barrier between the man and the BB gun after a drone had observed his condition.

Autonomy

Officers remotely deployed the robot and used its camera. No autonomous tactical decision is documented in this incident.

Workflow

An armed or apparently armed person unresponsive in a confined space that officers cannot safely approach. A tactical unit with a camera-and-arm ground robot on call and a deputy chief's approval for each use.

Human oversight

Deputy Chief David Kowalski approved each deployment, with uses reported to the Police Commission and City Council.

Duration

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Officers position the robot and watch its camera before any approach rather than acting on its own motion. Each deployment is approved in advance and reported to the Police Commission and City Council.
