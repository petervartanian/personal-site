Researchers found 47 known gravitational-wave signals

Researchers used MADGRAV-2D to recover 47 cataloged gravitational-wave signals from LIGO data.

    Recovery of 47 known events, 44 of them shared with coherent WaveBurst, demonstrates the method. No new event, catalog adoption or operational use is documented.

What happened

On 30 September 2026, Gianluca Inguglia, Huw Haigh and Ulyana Dupletsa posted a paper on arXiv. They are at the Marietta Blau Institute for Particle Physics (Austrian Academy of Sciences, Vienna). Alessandro Longo (University of Urbino and INFN Florence) is a co-author. The paper is titled “MADGRAV: a multilevel anomaly-detection pipeline for gravitational-wave searches applied to LIGO data”. Version 2 followed on 19 October 2026, and the paper is submitted to the European Physical Journal C. The pipeline is “a series of sequential convolutional neural networks that perform anomaly detection, glitch classification, coherence testing, and signal ranking”. It is applied to one-second Q-transform windows of data from the Hanford and Livingston detectors in the observing runs O3a, O3b, O4a and O4b. That is 442.9 days of coincident livetime in total. The abstract reports 47 detections with a false alarm rate below one per year and p_astro above 0.9, where p_astro is the probability of astrophysical origin. Of those, “44 are shared with the minimally modelled coherent WaveBurst search”. The three others are cataloged events, and the authors state that “no candidate without a catalog counterpart survives the analysis”. Recovery rises with mass. It reaches 8.1% of confident catalog events below 30 solar masses and 39.8% between 30 and 100. It reaches 53.3% above 100 for a network signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) above 10. The authors conclude that anomaly-detection pipelines “can serve as an independent detection channel complementary to matched filtering in the high-mass high-SNR regime”. The paper confirms no new event independently. The authors identify the two-detector configuration as MADGRAV-2D in their code repository. Its changelog dates the final pipeline configuration to 14 September 2026, and its README states that this configuration produced the reported 47 detections. The repository provides the trained weights and frozen calibration. It already records the 47 detections in a commit dated 1 September 2026. The final configuration keeps the same 47, and 42 of them reach a p_astro of 0.99 or more. As of 29 October 2026 the paper is still under submission, and INSPIRE-HEP lists no paper that cites it.

Details

AI system

Model

BaselineCAE, a convolutional autoencoder, together with convolutional networks that classify glitches and signals. [1] [2]

Reported configuration

MADGRAV final configuration of 14 September 2026 (glitch-arm gate added; repository snapshot acb66841)

Provider

Gianluca Inguglia and the MADGRAV research team [1] [2]

Role

Convolutional autoencoder for anomaly detection in the MADGRAV-2D cascade.

Configuration

MADGRAV-2D combined anomaly detection with classifiers for glitches and low- and high-frequency signals. It analyzed observations from the Hanford and Livingston detectors during LIGO’s O3 and O4 runs. [1] [2]

Inputs and outputs

The pipeline analyzed one-second time-frequency images from both LIGO detectors, rejected likely instrumental glitches, checked agreement between sites, and ranked surviving candidates.

Workflow

Sequential CNNs for anomaly detection, glitch classification, coherence testing and signal ranking on one-second Q-transform windows of Hanford and Livingston data. 442.9 days of coincident livetime across O3a, O3b, O4a and O4b.

Benefits

Duration

The record has no follow-up on the result. As of 29 October 2026 the preprint remains under submission to the European Physical Journal C, and INSPIRE-HEP lists no paper that cites it. [1] [2]

Measurement

Measurement

Retrospective search of archival data; detections are counted at a calibrated false alarm rate below one per year with p astro above 0.9 and matched to published catalog events and to coherent WaveBurst results.

Setting and sample

LIGO Hanford and Livingston data from O3a, O3b, O4a and O4b, 442.9 days of coincident livetime.

Measures

Number and fraction of confident catalog events recovered, by mass bin.

Results

47 detections, 17% of the catalog population and 39% of the coherent WaveBurst count; 44 shared with coherent WaveBurst; no candidate without a catalog counterpart.

Additional value from AI

None over coherent WaveBurst: the detections are nearly a subset of its events.

Limitations

Written and run by the pipeline's developers; not peer reviewed; recovery falls to 8.1% of confident events below 30 solar masses.

Materials for replication

Code, trained weights and frozen calibration in the public GitHub repository.

People and places

Dependencies

The analysis required public strain data from Hanford and Livingston, trained networks, and computational background estimation using time-shifted detector data.
