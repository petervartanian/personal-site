What happened

A paper was posted on arXiv on 30 September 2026 by Gianluca Inguglia, Huw Haigh and Ulyana Dupletsa. They are at the Marietta Blau Institute for Particle Physics (Austrian Academy of Sciences, Vienna). Alessandro Longo (University of Urbino and INFN Florence) is a co-author. It is titled “MADGRAV: a multilevel anomaly-detection pipeline for gravitational-wave searches applied to LIGO data” (version 2, 19 October 2026; submitted to the European Physical Journal C). The pipeline is “a series of sequential convolutional neural networks that perform anomaly detection, glitch classification, coherence testing, and signal ranking”. It is applied to one-second Q-transform windows of Hanford and Livingston data from O3a, O3b, O4a and O4b. That is 442.9 days of coincident livetime in total. The abstract reports 47 detections with a false alarm rate below one per year and p_astro above 0.9.

Of those, “44 are shared with the minimally modeled coherent WaveBurst search”. The three others are cataloged events, and the paper states that “no candidate without a catalog counterpart survives the analysis”. Recovery rises with mass. It reaches 8.1% of confident catalog events below 30 solar masses and 39.8% between 30 and 100. It reaches 53.3% above 100 for network SNR above 10. The authors conclude that anomaly-detection pipelines “can serve as an independent detection channel complementary to matched filtering in the high-mass high-SNR regime”. The paper confirms no new event independently. The date observed is the preprint date.

What it was built to do. MADGRAV was built to search LIGO data for high-mass compact binary coalescences by anomaly detection. Recovering cataloged events was that purpose.

The system's part

The convolutional networks performed the whole detection chain, from anomaly detection to ranking, and the authors are the developers. Every detection matches a published catalog event. The authors state that no candidate without a catalog counterpart survives, so the search added no new discovery in this analysis.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Sequential CNNs for anomaly detection, glitch classification, coherence testing and signal ranking on one-second Q-transform windows of Hanford and Livingston data. 442.9 days of coincident livetime across O3a, O3b, O4a and O4b.

What must hold again. High-mass, high-SNR sources: the recovered fraction rises from 8.1% below 30 solar masses to 53.3% above 100. Two-detector coherence between Hanford and Livingston.
