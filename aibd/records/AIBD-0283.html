MADGRAV recovers 47 cataloged LIGO events; no new candidate

47 detections with a false alarm rate below one per year and p_astro above 0.9

Inguglia et al. (arXiv:2609.39583, 30 Sep 2026; v2 19 Oct 2026): sequential CNNs recover 47 cataloged high-mass events over 442.9 days of coincident LIGO data; no uncatalogued candidate survives.

What happened

Gianluca Inguglia, Huw Haigh and Ulyana Dupletsa of the Marietta Blau Institute for Particle Physics (Austrian Academy of Sciences, Vienna) and Alessandro Longo (University of Urbino and INFN Florence) posted 'MADGRAV: a multilevel anomaly-detection pipeline for gravitational-wave searches applied to LIGO data' on arXiv on 30 September 2026 (version 2, 19 October 2026; submitted to the European Physical Journal C). The pipeline is 'a series of sequential convolutional neural networks that perform anomaly detection, glitch classification, coherence testing, and signal ranking' applied to one-second Q-transform windows of Hanford and Livingston data from O3a, O3b, O4a and O4b, 442.9 days of coincident livetime in total. The abstract reports 47 detections with a false alarm rate below one per year and p_astro above 0.9, of which '44 are shared with the minimally modeled coherent WaveBurst search'; the three others are cataloged events, and the paper states that 'no candidate without a catalog counterpart survives the analysis'. Recovery rises with mass: 8.1% of confident catalog events below 30 solar masses, 39.8% between 30 and 100, and 53.3% above 100 for network SNR above 10. The authors conclude that anomaly-detection pipelines 'can serve as an independent detection channel complementary to matched filtering in the high-mass high-SNR regime'. The earlier verifier noted the paper confirms no new event independently; the date observed is the preprint date.

What it was built to do. MADGRAV was built to search LIGO data for high-mass compact binary coalescences by anomaly detection; recovering cataloged events was that purpose.

The system's part

The convolutional networks performed the whole detection chain from anomaly detection to ranking, and the authors are the developers. Every detection matches a published catalog event; the authors state that no candidate without a catalog counterpart survives, so the pipeline added no new discovery in this analysis.
