DGFiP's Foncier innovant leads to 20,000+ pools taxed for 2022

DGFiP's Foncier innovant deep-learning tool finds pools in aerial images; it led to more than 20,000 pools being taxed for 2022, close to €10 million for communes

The system’s part
Not isolated. Official sources describe the chain (AI detection, automated file check, human verification, owner contact) and attribute the taxed pools to it. No evaluation isolates the algorithm's contribution from the human verification and declaration campaign. The Assemblée nationale written answer of 14 December 2021 confirms DGFiP's use of AI on IGN imagery and that contractors do not access tax data.
Limits
Gross additional revenue should not be treated as net economic value or solely attributed to AI. Program costs, human verification, taxpayer transfers, and the distinction between potentially taxable pools and taxes collected remain material.
Costs and harms
The Cour des comptes put the project's cost at 25.9 million euros over three years, and the Sénat report puts it at €24 million. Owners of the detected pools pay more land tax. No cited source reports false detections or other harms to owners.
Later
Scaled up, 2 April 2024. The Sénat's April 2024 report records the generalization to all of metropolitan France in 2023, for about 150,000 potentially taxable pools, and the DGCL page dates an industrialization phase from June 2024.

What happened

France's Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP) runs “Foncier innovant”. A deep-learning algorithm finds building and pool outlines in public IGN aerial images. An automated check compares them to land-tax files. A tax agent verifies every anomaly before the owner is contacted. DGFiP's October 2021 note says that Capgemini provided project-management and build assistance and that Google provided the cloud infrastructure and services for developing the artificial-intelligence models. The 2022 experiment in nine departments led to more than 20,000 pools being taxed for 2022, close to €10 million of extra revenue for the communes concerned (DGFiP, 2 June 2023). The sources date the result only by the 2022 tax year. After generalization in 2023, the first pass covered metropolitan France, excluding Paris and the overseas departments (DROM). It detected 150,000 untaxed taxable pools. In its report of 15 November 2023 the Cour des comptes put the project's cost at 25.9 million euros over three years. It restated that the 2022 experiment in nine departments identified 20,000 undeclared pools and brought in 10 million euros for the communes. The court wrote that the scheme mainly demonstrates the capacity of the DGFiP to detect undeclared buildings. The Sénat's April 2024 report puts the cost at €24 million and calls the detected pools “potentiellement taxables”. Industrialization began in June 2024.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The model detected pool and building outlines in IGN aerial imagery, and an automated comparison checked them against land-tax records.

Workflow

Deep-learning segmentation of IGN orthophotos producing pool and building outlines. Automated cross-check against cadastral and land-tax files. Systematic verification by a tax agent and a declaration campaign to owners.

Human oversight

A tax agent verified every anomaly before contacting the property owner.

People and places

Dependencies

The deployment used public IGN aerial images, cadastral records, and Google cloud services for model development, with Capgemini providing development and project assistance.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Regular high-resolution public aerial imagery (IGN triennial orthophotos). Reliable cadastral records to compare against. Legal basis to tax detected constructions and agent capacity for verification.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 1. Outcome unresolved: 1.
