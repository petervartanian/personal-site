DGFiP's Foncier innovant leads to 20,000+ pools taxed for 2022

off the scale

more than 20,000 pools being taxed for 2022, close to €10 million of extra revenue

DGFiP deep-learning pool detection: 20,000+ pools taxed for 2022 (~€10m for communes); 150,000 taxable pools found across metropolitan France excluding Paris and overseas departments

What happened

France's Direction générale des Finances publiques (DGFiP) runs 'Foncier innovant': a deep-learning algorithm extracts building and pool outlines from public IGN aerial images, an automated check compares them with land-tax files, and a tax agent verifies every anomaly before the owner is contacted. DGFiP's October 2021 note says Capgemini provided project-management and build assistance and Google the cloud infrastructure and services for developing the AI models. The 2022 experiment in nine departments led to more than 20,000 pools being taxed for 2022, close to €10 million of extra revenue for the communes concerned (DGFiP, 2 June 2023); the sources give no other date for this result, so the date observed is the publication date of that announcement. The Sénat's April 2024 report puts the cost at €24 million. After generalization in 2023, the first pass across metropolitan France, excluding Paris and the overseas departments (DROM), detected 150,000 untaxed taxable pools, which the Sénat report calls 'potentiellement taxables'; industrialization began in June 2024.

What it was built to do. Detect undeclared pools and buildings from aerial imagery to bring them into the local property-tax base

The system's part

not assessed Official sources describe the pipeline (AI detection, automated file check, systematic human verification, owner contact) and attribute the taxed pools to it, but no evaluation isolates the algorithm's contribution from the human verification and declaration campaign. The AN written answer of 14 Dec 2021 confirms DGFiP's use of AI on IGN imagery and that contractors do not access tax data.

Replication

What produced it. Deep-learning segmentation of IGN orthophotos producing pool and building outlines. Automated cross-check against cadastral and land-tax files. Systematic verification by a tax agent and a declaration campaign to owners.

What must hold again. Regular high-resolution public aerial imagery (IGN triennial orthophotos). Reliable cadastral records to compare against. Legal basis to tax detected constructions and agent capacity for verification.
