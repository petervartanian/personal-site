What happened

Amazon Mining Watch is a public platform built by the Pulitzer Center's Rainforest Investigations Network, Amazon Conservation Association and Earth Genome. In its own words, it leverages artificial intelligence to map the impact of gold mining. Its code repository describes the detector as an ensemble of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) trained on hand-labeled Sentinel-2 patches. The detector is run over the 8.2 million km2 Amazon basin, with detections recomputed yearly for 2018–2025 and quarterly from 2025. The repository reports specificity 0.976 and sensitivity 0.991 on a Venezuelan geographic holdout for the cumulative 2024 detections. Mongabay reported on 24 April 2026, on the platform's quarterly report. That report found about 6,000 hectares of mining deforestation in protected areas and Indigenous territories in the last three months of 2025.

It found 496,000 hectares affected between 2018 and 2025. The enforcement operations on record are documented by the same tool maker without any AI role. MAAP #104 (4 August 2019) attributes a 92% fall in gold-mining deforestation at La Pampa to Peru's Operation Mercury of February 2019. That analysis used satellite imagery (ESA, Maxar, Sentinel-1) with no mention of machine learning. No cited source documents an enforcement action taken on an Amazon Mining Watch detection.

What it was built to do. Automated detection and mapping of artisanal gold-mining scars in satellite imagery. The users are journalists, researchers, civil society and policymakers. The mapping is the intended use.

The system's part

The AI system produces the maps and area statistics themselves. The makers' repository describes an ensemble of convolutional neural networks on Sentinel-2 patches, with reported holdout specificity 0.976 and sensitivity 0.991. Mongabay independently describes the platform as training AI algorithms to recognize mining scars. No cited source links a detection to an enforcement action. Mongabay remarks that the platform is useful for cross-border work, citing 2025 Brazil-Colombia operations on the Pure River. It does not state that those operations used its detections. MAAP's Operation Mercury analysis, by the same maker, used satellite imagery without machine learning.

Replication

What produced it. Free Sentinel-2 imagery composited on Google Earth Engine. Hand-labeled training set of 23,463 patches built from earlier human surveys with journalists. Ensemble CNN detector run over the whole basin yearly and quarterly. Public release of detections and code for journalists and NGOs.

What must hold again. Cloud-free composites over the period monitored. Mining scars visually distinct from sandbars, braided rivers and aquaculture ponds. Continued labeling and corroboration across years to limit false detections. A user, agency or newsroom willing to act on detections, which no cited source documents.
