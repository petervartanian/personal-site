Amazon Mining Watch maps 496,000 hectares of mining on protected lands

Amazon Mining Watch uses an AI detector to map gold mining, and its report shows 496,000 hectares affected between 2018 and 2025 in protected areas and Indigenous territories of the Amazon.

  • Not rated a person

    No individual beneficiary is documented.

  • 2 an organization

    Mongabay reports that the Panorama report found new mining scars in 10 Indigenous territories or protected areas in the last three months of 2025. In one of them, the Charip territory in Ecuador, no mining had been detected since the data series began in 2018. This is a realized gain in monitoring capability for the newsrooms and organizations that use the maps. No organization is documented as having changed a decision because of a detection.

The system’s part
The convolutional networks produce the maps and area statistics. People hand-label the training patches, and Mongabay reports that partners on the ground feed information back into the platform. LatAm Journalism Review reports that journalists and NGO observers supply the context needed to interpret the output. The makers report holdout specificity of 0.976 and sensitivity of 0.991. No cited source compares the maps with field surveys of the same area. No cited source links a detection to an enforcement action; Mongabay's mention of the 2025 Brazil and Colombia operations on the Puré River does not state that those operations used the detections.
Limits
Mining maps and area estimates do not establish reduced mining or enforcement attributable to the model. Geographic holdout metrics need denominators and transfer checks across the basin. The 2019 Operation Mercury reduction predates the cited AI analysis and cannot serve as its outcome.
Costs and harms
A map can reveal a probable mine without establishing whether it is illegal or whether authorities acted on it. Cloud cover, small operations, and terrain that resembles mines can also limit detection.
Later
Persisted, 1 August 2026. Earth Genome published a full rebuild of the Amazon Mining Watch models and data in August 2026, continuing the platform after the April 2026 report.

What happened

Amazon Mining Watch is a public platform built by the Pulitzer Center's Rainforest Investigations Network, Amazon Conservation Association and Earth Genome. In its own words, it leverages artificial intelligence to map the impact of gold mining. Its code repository describes the detector as an ensemble of convolutional neural networks trained on hand-labeled Sentinel-2 patches. The detector is run over the Amazon basin, an area of 8.2 million square kilometers, with detections recomputed yearly for 2018–2025 and quarterly from 2025. The repository reports specificity 0.976 and sensitivity 0.991 on a Venezuelan geographic holdout for the cumulative 2024 detections. On 24 April 2026 Mongabay reported on the platform's quarterly report. According to that report, the platform detected about 6,000 hectares of mining deforestation in protected areas and Indigenous territories in the last three months of 2025. It reports 496,000 hectares affected between 2018 and 2025. The enforcement operations on record are documented by the same tool maker without any AI role. MAAP #104 of Amazon Conservation (4 August 2019) attributes a 92% fall in gold-mining deforestation at La Pampa to Peru's Operation Mercury of February 2019. That analysis used satellite imagery from the European Space Agency, Maxar and Sentinel-1, with no mention of machine learning. No cited source documents an enforcement action taken on an Amazon Mining Watch detection. Nieman Lab reported on 27 April 2026 that Amazon Mining Watch launched in 2022 after reporters had used Earth Genome models to map illegal mines; one of them found 3,718 sites in Venezuela. LatAm Journalism Review reported on 26 August 2026 that the platform covers all nine Amazon countries and that its technology now also underlies Africa Mining Watch. Neither outlet documents an enforcement action that rests on a detection. In August 2026 Earth Genome published a full rebuild of the Amazon Mining Watch models and data.

Details

AI system

Release

48px_v4.10b-18d-20g-21a-22bc-ensemble.

Role

The models detected mining activity in satellite images and calculated its extent.

Configuration

The repository identified the July 2026 production model as 48px_v4.10b-18d-20g-21a-22bc-ensemble.

Inputs and outputs

Multispectral Sentinel-2 image patches are scored for mining scars. Postprocessing combines neighboring detections and checks persistence across time to produce maps and area estimates.

Workflow

Free Sentinel-2 imagery composited on Google Earth Engine. Hand-labeled training set of 23,463 patches built from earlier human surveys with journalists. Ensemble CNN detector run over the whole basin yearly and quarterly. Public release of detections and code for journalists and NGOs.

Human oversight

People hand-label the training patches. Mongabay reports that partners on the ground feed information back into the platform. LatAm Journalism Review reports that journalists and observers from non-governmental organizations supply the context needed to interpret the output. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Cloud-free composites over the period monitored. Mining scars visually distinct from sandbars, braided rivers and aquaculture ponds. Continued labeling and corroboration across years to limit false detections. A user, agency or newsroom willing to act on detections, which no cited source documents.
