Glider software detects right whales, and NOAA asks mariners to slow

The Low-Frequency Detection and Classification System on a glider detected right whales east of Portland, Maine, on 27 December 2023, and NOAA declared a voluntary Slow Zone.

What happened

On 19 October 2023 the ocean advocacy group Oceana reported that, in its analysis of vessel tracking data from November 2020 to July 2022, 82% of boats ignored the voluntary 10-knot limit in Slow Zones. On 27 December 2023 the Gulf of Maine Winter Slocum glider acoustically detected North Atlantic right whales east of Portland, Maine. Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution and NOAA's Northeast Fisheries Science Center operate the glider. Such gliders carry a DMON instrument running the Low-Frequency Detection and Classification System (LFDCS). The system matches pitch-tracked calls against a call library using quadratic discriminant function analysis. Shore-based analysts validate detections. On 29 December 2023 NOAA Fisheries announced a voluntary Right Whale Slow Zone bounded by 43°49'N, 43°09'N, 67°02'W and 68°57'W. It ran from 27 December 2023 through 11 January 2024. NOAA asked mariners to route around the area or transit at 10 knots or less. No source reports how vessels behaved inside this Slow Zone. On 20 January 2024 a glider made the first definite glider-based acoustic detection in southeast United States waters. In 2025 Indeck and colleagues, including the system's developer, reported that gliders had triggered or extended 51 of the 154 Slow Zones that acoustic detections triggered or extended in the program's first four years.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The glider's acoustic system matched pitch-tracked calls against a whale-call library and transmitted candidate right-whale detections for review.

Workflow

Glider or buoy carrying DMON/LFDCS in right whale habitat. Shore-based analyst validation protocol. NOAA Slow Zone program (established late 2020) accepting acoustic triggers.

Human oversight

Shore-based analysts validated the detections before NOAA announced a voluntary Slow Zone and asked mariners to avoid it or travel at ten knots or less.
