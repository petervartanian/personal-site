LFDCS glider detections trigger a NOAA Right Whale Slow Zone

depth 2 for society

mariners route around the area or transit at 10 knots or less

WHOI/NOAA glider's automated call classifier detected right whales on 27 Dec 2023; NOAA declared a Slow Zone east of Portland, ME.

What happened

On 27 December 2023 the Gulf of Maine Winter Slocum glider, operated by the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution and NOAA's Northeast Fisheries Science Center, acoustically detected North Atlantic right whales east of Portland, Maine. Such gliders carry a DMON instrument running the Low-Frequency Detection and Classification System, which matches pitch-tracked calls against a call library using quadratic discriminant function analysis; shore-based analysts validate detections. On 29 December 2023 NOAA Fisheries announced a voluntary Right Whale Slow Zone bounded by 43°49'N, 43°09'N, 67°02'W and 68°57'W, effective 27 December 2023 through 11 January 2024, requesting that mariners route around the area or transit at 10 knots or less.

What it was built to do. LFDCS was built to detect and classify baleen whale calls in near real time for management use; the Slow Zone trigger was that purpose.

The system's part

Automated classification produced the candidate detections; analysts validated them on shore before NOAA acted; the resulting Slow Zone is voluntary.

Replication

What produced it. Glider or buoy carrying DMON/LFDCS in right whale habitat. Shore-based analyst validation protocol. NOAA Slow Zone program (established late 2020) accepting acoustic triggers.

What must hold again. Whales vocalize within detection range. Mariners comply voluntarily for any protective effect to materialize.
