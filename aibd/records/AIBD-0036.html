What happened

On 27 December 2023 the Gulf of Maine Winter Slocum glider acoustically detected North Atlantic right whales east of Portland, Maine. Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution and NOAA's Northeast Fisheries Science Center operate it. Such gliders carry a DMON instrument running the Low-Frequency Detection and Classification System (LFDCS). It matches pitch-tracked calls against a call library using quadratic discriminant function analysis. Shore-based analysts validate detections. On 29 December 2023 NOAA Fisheries announced a voluntary Right Whale Slow Zone bounded by 43°49'N, 43°09'N, 67°02'W and 68°57'W. It ran 27 December 2023 through 11 January 2024. NOAA asked mariners to route around the area or transit at 10 knots or less.

What it was built to do. LFDCS was built to detect and classify baleen whale calls in near real time for management use. The Slow Zone trigger was that purpose.

Basis
The source measured it.Gliders triggered more slowdowns than aerial surveillance by a factor of two to five during fall and summer but were less effective during spring, as whales migrating into the GoSL tend to call at lower rates and occur at lower densities than during other behavioral states (Parks et al., 2011; Matthews and Parks, 2021). source
Since then
Scaled on 21 January 2025: By early 2025 gliders had triggered or extended 51 of the 154 acoustically triggered Slow Zones, and on 20 January 2024 a glider made the first definite glider-based acoustic detection in southeast US waters.In the four years since its inception, 154 Slow Zones have been triggered or extended by acoustic detections of NARWs, and 51 (33%) of those Slow Zones were triggered or extended by gliders operated by the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, Rutgers University, Stony Brook University, and The University of Maine during 57 separate glider missions. source

The system's part

Automated classification produced the candidate detections. Analysts validated them on shore before NOAA acted. The resulting Slow Zone is voluntary.

Replication

What produced it. Glider or buoy carrying DMON/LFDCS in right whale habitat. Shore-based analyst validation protocol. NOAA Slow Zone program (established late 2020) accepting acoustic triggers.

What must hold again. Whales vocalize within detection range. Mariners comply voluntarily for any protective effect to materialize.
