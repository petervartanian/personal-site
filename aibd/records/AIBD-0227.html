What happened

The Odessa American (reproduced by GovTech) reports, from a ZeroEyes news release, on an alert at 6:35 p.m. MDT on 30 November 2023. ZeroEyes alerted the Hobbs Police Department and the Eagle I.C. dispatch center. A gun had been detected on a security camera in Del Norte Pool's parking lot. Those involved were shooting sporting clays close to North Grimes Street. Responding officers detained them and charges were filed against one. Hobbs Police Chief August Fons is quoted saying detection and rapid human verification helped stop the activity before anyone was hurt. Lubbock Lights later reported ZeroEyes co-founder Sam Alaimo showing images from the incident. He said it resulted in an arrest with no one hurt and listed the City of Hobbs and its school system among local clients. No court record is among the sources.

What it was built to do. Detect visible firearms in existing security-camera feeds, verify with human analysts and alert police within seconds to reduce response times to potential shootings.

The system's part

The chain (AI detection, human verification, dispatch) is described only in ZeroEyes' release and the vendor's later interview. The chief's endorsement is quoted in that release. No police report, court filing or independent reporter observation is among the sources. The AI's necessity (whether anyone else reported the shooting) is thus undocumented.

Replication

What produced it. A gun visible to a municipal security camera connected to the detector. Human verification at the vendor's operations center. Dispatch integration with the local police department.

What must hold again. Camera coverage of the location. Officers available to respond while the activity continues.
