What happened

ScamShield is a Singapore government app by Open Government Products, a unit of GovTech. It was developed with the National Crime Prevention Council (NCPC) and the Singapore Police Force. GovInsider (June 2021) described an on-device machine-learning model that files suspected scam SMS into a junk folder. It also blocks calls from numbers on a police blocklist. It reported 722,865 SMS reported and 5,537 numbers blocked since launch, with a 95% detection accuracy claim. It quoted product manager Lennard Lim on the model's need to adapt as scammers change their attacks. Minister of State Sun Xueling gave an oral reply to parliamentary questions on 22 November 2023. She said ScamShield had about 750,000 downloads as of October 2023. About 80,000 unique scam-tainted phone numbers had been blacklisted and blocked. About 5 million SMSes had been flagged to users as potential scams.

She said it has effectively protected users. She also cited about 180 million suspected scam calls blocked at network level in the first half of 2023. That is a telco measure separate from the app. No victim-level evaluation appears in the sources.

What it was built to do. Filter scam SMS on-device and block calls from police-blacklisted numbers to protect users from scams.

The system's part

GovInsider attributes SMS filtering to an on-device machine-learning model and call blocking to a police-maintained blocklist. So only the SMS figures involve the model. The parliamentary reply gives counts without attributing them to AI. Developer (Open Government Products, a public body) and deployer (the Police and NCPC) are both government bodies. The reply was given by their ministry.

Replication

What produced it. On-device SMS classification combined with a police-maintained blocklist of scam numbers. User reports feeding the blocklist (722,865 SMS reported by mid-2021).

What must hold again. Adoption at scale (about 750,000 downloads by October 2023). Police intelligence to keep the blocklist current. Model updates as scam wording changes.
