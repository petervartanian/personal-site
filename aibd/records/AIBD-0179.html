ScamShield flags about 5 million suspected scam texts in Singapore

ScamShield, a Singapore government app whose on-device machine-learning model filters text messages, flagged about 5 million of them to users as potential scams from March 2022 to November 2023.

  • Not rated society

    The sources attribute no measured change in scam losses to the app.

What happened

ScamShield is a Singapore government app by Open Government Products, a unit of GovTech. It was developed with the National Crime Prevention Council and the Singapore Police Force. In June 2021 GovInsider described an on-device machine-learning model that files suspected scam text messages into a junk folder. The app also blocks calls from numbers on a police blocklist. GovInsider reported that users had reported 722,865 text messages and that 5,537 numbers had been blocked since launch. It also reported a claim of 95% detection accuracy. It quoted the product manager, Lennard Lim, on the model's need to adapt as scammers change their attacks. According to The Straits Times, the police had counted 300,500 downloads on iOS in August 2022. On 22 November 2023 the Minister of State Sun Xueling gave an oral reply to parliamentary questions. She said that ScamShield had about 750,000 downloads as of October 2023. She said that about 80,000 unique scam-tainted phone numbers had been blacklisted and blocked. The reply also states that about 5 million text messages had been flagged to users as potential scams. The ministry counts both figures from March 2022. She said that ScamShield had been effective in protecting users. She also cited about 180 million suspected scam calls blocked at network level in the first half of 2023. That figure is a measure by the telecommunications operators and is separate from the app. The Straits Times reported on the same day that the minister said that the app cannot work well unless users set its permissions. The Ministry of Digital Development and Information reported on a page updated on 18 August 2026 that the enhanced app, launched in August 2024, had grown its user base from 944,000 to 1.53 million users. The sources include no evaluation of losses that victims avoided.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

The on-device model filtered suspected scam texts into a junk folder. Call blocking used a police-maintained number list rather than the text classifier.

Workflow

On-device SMS classification combined with a police-maintained blocklist of scam numbers. User reports feeding the blocklist (722,865 SMS reported by mid-2021).

Human oversight

Users contributed reports, and police maintained the blocklist. Developers needed to update the classifier as scam wording changed.

Reach

By October 2023 the app had about 750,000 downloads, and by August 2026 the enhanced app had 1.53 million users. Downloads, users and flagged messages do not count people whose financial losses were prevented. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The app remained in use through 18 August 2026, when the Ministry of Digital Development and Information reported 1.53 million users of the enhanced app. The sources give no dated measure of losses avoided over that period. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Adoption at scale (about 750,000 downloads by October 2023). Police intelligence to keep the blocklist current. Model updates as scam wording changes.
