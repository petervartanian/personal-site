Apple Watch alarms lead to an AF diagnosis and anticoagulation

Apple Watch heart-rate alarms led to an ECG-confirmed AF diagnosis and anticoagulation (UK, 2019); 16 echoes from 15 published case reports to 2026.

System

Apple Watch heart-rate notifications, Irregular Rhythm Notification and ECG app

Release

Exact release not established.

Application developer

Apple

Role

The watch analyzed heart-rate signals and generated notifications about abnormal readings.

Configuration

The initial report concerned an Apple Watch Series 2. Later reports involved several other watch generations.

Workflow

The wearer owns an Apple Watch with heart-rate notifications, Irregular Rhythm Notification or the ECG app enabled. An arrhythmia occurs while the watch is worn and the alert or recording leads the wearer to seek care. A clinician confirms the rhythm on a 12-lead ECG or monitoring and treats it.

Relation to AI

The watch's heart-rate algorithm generated the overnight alarms that brought the patient to hospital. Clinicians made the diagnosis on an ECG and chose treatment. In the echoes the notification or the ECG app's single-lead trace likewise triggered evaluation, with clinicians confirming the rhythm.

Conditions for replication

The alert or trace reaches a clinician rather than being dismissed. Confirmatory ECG or electrophysiology testing is available. A treatment pathway (anticoagulation, cardioversion, ablation, device) exists.

Replication attempts

16 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 15. Outcome unresolved: 1.

Later findings
