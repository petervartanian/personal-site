Apple Watch alarms lead to an AF diagnosis and anticoagulation

her pulse exceeded 120 beats per minute while she was inactive

Apple Watch heart-rate alarms led to an ECG-confirmed AF diagnosis and anticoagulation (UK, 2019); 16 echoes from 15 published case reports to 2026.

What happened

In a case report published on 29 March 2019 (event date not stated; publication date used), a 59-year-old woman in the United Kingdom attended an emergency department and was seen by the acute medicine service (the report does not name the hospital; the author is at Ipswich Hospital) because her Apple Watch Series 2 had alarmed several times overnight that her pulse exceeded 120 beats per minute while she was inactive; she had suffered a transient ischaemic attack two months earlier. An ECG showed atrial fibrillation with a fast ventricular response. She was started on bisoprolol and a direct oral anticoagulant, and clopidogrel was stopped. Later case reports describe the watch's heart-rate alerts, irregular-rhythm notification or ECG app leading to confirmed atrial flutter, supraventricular and ventricular tachycardia and heart block, treated by cardioversion, ablation, pacemaker or defibrillator.

What it was built to do. Apple Watch high/low heart-rate notifications, the Irregular Rhythm Notification and the ECG app are built to flag possible arrhythmias so the wearer seeks medical review; this benefit was that purpose.

Who benefited

  • -2
    personal

    One woman with a recent TIA received an ECG-confirmed AF diagnosis, rate control and anticoagulation; a material benefit to one person. Several echoes (VT ablation with defibrillator, pacemaker for heart block) are deeper but are not the event.

The system's part

The watch's heart-rate algorithm generated the overnight alarms that brought the patient to hospital; clinicians made the diagnosis on an ECG and chose treatment. In the echoes the notification or the ECG app's single-lead trace likewise triggered evaluation, with clinicians confirming the rhythm.

Replication

What produced it

  • The wearer owns an Apple Watch with heart-rate notifications, Irregular Rhythm Notification or the ECG app enabled
  • An arrhythmia occurs while the watch is worn and the alert or recording leads the wearer to seek care
  • A clinician confirms the rhythm on a 12-lead ECG or monitoring and treats it

What must hold again

  • The alert or trace reaches a clinician rather than being dismissed
  • Confirmatory ECG or electrophysiology testing is available
  • A treatment pathway (anticoagulation, cardioversion, ablation, device) exists
