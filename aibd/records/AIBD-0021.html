Apple Watch heart-rate alarms lead doctors to find atrial fibrillation

Overnight heart-rate alarms from an Apple Watch Series 2 prompted a woman to visit a United Kingdom hospital, where an electrocardiogram showed atrial fibrillation and she began an anticoagulant.

Later case reports describe deeper benefits, such as ablation with a defibrillator for ventricular tachycardia and a pacemaker for heart block. This rating covers the woman's case alone.

What happened

On 11 September 2018, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration granted De Novo authorization to the Irregular Rhythm Notification feature. Its reviewers found that the probable benefits outweigh the probable risks. The authorization does not cover the high heart-rate alert. A case report of 29 March 2019 describes a 59-year-old woman who attended a United Kingdom emergency department, where the acute medicine service saw her. The report does not name the hospital or give the date of the event. The author is at Ipswich Hospital. The woman had had a transient ischemic attack two months earlier. After it, she had been in sinus rhythm at the stroke team review and throughout a 24-hour outpatient electrocardiogram, and she was taking clopidogrel. She attended because her Apple Watch Series 2 had alarmed several times overnight that her pulse exceeded 120 beats per minute while she was inactive. An electrocardiogram showed atrial fibrillation with a fast ventricular response. She started bisoprolol and a direct oral anticoagulant, and clopidogrel was stopped. Later case reports from 2019 to 2026 describe the watch's heart-rate alerts, irregular-rhythm notification or ECG app leading to confirmed atrial flutter, supraventricular and ventricular tachycardia and heart block. Treatment was by cardioversion, ablation, pacemaker or defibrillator. A Mayo Clinic review published on 25 September 2020 found that only 11.4% of 264 patients seen after an abnormal Apple Watch pulse received an actionable cardiovascular diagnosis. Its authors warn that false positives may lead to overuse of care. A systematic review published on 25 April 2024 found 44 patients with smartwatch-detected arrhythmias across 33 case reports and case series, and the Apple Watch was used in 30 cases. An Italian prospective study published on 19 April 2026 found that half of 59 smartwatch owners with palpitations documented an arrhythmia within three months. Its results are pooled across four brands.

Details

AI system

Role

The watch analyzed heart-rate signals and generated notifications about abnormal readings.

Configuration

The initial report concerned an Apple Watch Series 2. Later reports involved several other watch generations.

Inputs and outputs

The Series 2 watch measured the woman's pulse and generated repeated alerts when it exceeded 120 beats per minute while she was inactive.

Workflow

The wearer owns an Apple Watch with heart-rate notifications, Irregular Rhythm Notification or the ECG app enabled. An arrhythmia occurs while the watch is worn and the alert or recording leads the wearer to seek care. A clinician confirms the rhythm on a 12-lead ECG or monitoring and treats it.

Human oversight

The wearer decided to attend the emergency department. Clinicians confirmed atrial fibrillation on an ECG and chose the medication changes.

Measurement

Measurement

Retrospective chart review of patients whose Mayo Clinic notes mentioned an Apple Watch and an abnormal pulse. [19]

Setting and sample

264 patients at Mayo Clinic sites; 41 had an explicit alert documented. [19]

Measures

Share of patients with a new clinically actionable cardiovascular diagnosis. [19]

Results

30 of 264 (11.4%) received an actionable diagnosis, 6 of 41 (15%) among those with an explicit alert; number needed to evaluate about 7. [19]

Limitations

Retrospective review with data extracted by one reviewer, no standard workup, short follow-up that may miss paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, and no claims data on costs. [19]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The alert or trace reaches a clinician rather than being dismissed. Confirmatory ECG or electrophysiology testing is available. A treatment pathway (anticoagulation, cardioversion, ablation, device) exists.
