Apple Watch alarms lead to an AF diagnosis and anticoagulation

Apple Watch heart-rate alarms led to an ECG-confirmed AF diagnosis and anticoagulation (UK, 2019); 16 echoes from 15 published case reports to 2026.

ⓘ

What happened

In a case report of 29 March 2019 (event date not reported), a 59-year-old woman attended a United Kingdom emergency department. The acute medicine service saw her (the report does not name the hospital; the author is at Ipswich Hospital). She attended because her Apple Watch Series 2 had alarmed several times overnight that her pulse exceeded 120 bpm while she was inactive. A transient ischemic attack had occurred two months earlier. An electrocardiogram (ECG) showed atrial fibrillation with a fast ventricular response. She started bisoprolol and a direct oral anticoagulant, and clopidogrel was stopped. Later case reports describe the watch's heart-rate alerts, irregular-rhythm notification or ECG app leading to confirmed atrial flutter, supraventricular and ventricular tachycardia and heart block. Treatment was by cardioversion, ablation, pacemaker or defibrillator.

Purpose. Apple Watch high/low heart-rate notifications, the Irregular Rhythm Notification and the ECG app are built to flag possible arrhythmias so the wearer seeks medical review. This benefit was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The watch's heart-rate algorithm generated the overnight alarms that brought the patient to hospital. Clinicians made the diagnosis on an ECG and chose treatment. In the echoes the notification or the ECG app's single-lead trace likewise triggered evaluation, with clinicians confirming the rhythm.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    One woman with a recent TIA received an ECG-confirmed AF diagnosis, rate control and anticoagulation. A material benefit to one person. Several echoes (VT ablation with defibrillator, pacemaker for heart block) are deeper but are not the event.

Most of the reports come from the United States (n = 12). In the selected articles, the youngest subject was 10 days old, and the oldest was 72 years old. In most cases (n = 30), the Apple Watch was used, while Samsung Galaxy Fit was used for arrhythmia registration in one patient. source

Further evidence

Setting. The wearer owns an Apple Watch with heart-rate notifications, Irregular Rhythm Notification or the ECG app enabled. An arrhythmia occurs while the watch is worn and the alert or recording leads the wearer to seek care. A clinician confirms the rhythm on a 12-lead ECG or monitoring and treats it.

Conditions. The alert or trace reaches a clinician rather than being dismissed. Confirmatory ECG or electrophysiology testing is available. A treatment pathway (anticoagulation, cardioversion, ablation, device) exists.
