ChatGPT hints help adults learn algebra and statistics online

In a UC Berkeley experiment with 274 Mechanical Turk workers in 2023, ChatGPT-written math hints gave a 17% pre-to-post gain, the only gain significant against a no-hint control.

What happened

Zachary Pardos and Shreya Bhandari of the University of California, Berkeley ran an online experiment from 28 February to 10 May 2023 with 274 Amazon Mechanical Turk workers who held at least a high school degree. The authors randomly assigned each participant to one of four subjects drawn from OpenStax textbooks, elementary, intermediate or college algebra or statistics, and to one of three hint conditions in the open-source OATutor system: hints written by human tutors, worked-solution hints generated by the 15 December version of ChatGPT 3.5, or no hints. A three-item pre-test and post-test measured learning. The ChatGPT group showed a 17.00 percent learning gain from pre-test to post-test. The authors report that only the ChatGPT condition produced gains significant against the no-hint control, and that gains did not differ significantly between ChatGPT and human-tutor hints. ChatGPT hints failed the authors' quality checks on 32 percent of problems, a rate that a self-consistency technique cut to nearly zero for algebra. The learners were adult crowd workers in short sessions, and problems with figures were left out. PLOS ONE published the study on 24 May 2024.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

ChatGPT 3.5, "Dec 15 Version"

Workflow

ChatGPT wrote worked solutions that served as hints on textbook problems. Learners saw the hints in a tutoring system during short practice sessions.

Measurement

Measurement

Three-item pre-test and post-test on the assigned skill. [1]

Setting and sample

274 Amazon Mechanical Turk workers with at least a high school degree, 28 February to 10 May 2023. [1]

Measures

Percentage-point learning gain from pre-test to post-test. [1]

Results

17.00 percent gain with ChatGPT hints against 1.85 percent with no hints and 11.62 percent with human-tutor hints. [1]

Additional value from AI

ChatGPT hints produced a gain significant against no hints and similar to human-tutor hints. [1]

Limitations

Adult crowd workers; short sessions; three-item tests; 32 percent of ChatGPT hints failed quality checks before mitigation. [1]

Denominators

One session of about 10 to 20 minutes. [1]

Materials for replication

Participant data at doi:10.6084/m9.figshare.23935269. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Hints must be checked or generated several times to catch errors. Problems must be text only.

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit did not recur: 1.

Entry details

Sector

Education.

Purpose

ChatGPT is a general-purpose chatbot. The authors used it to write hints for tutoring software, and learning from those hints was the purpose of the study.
