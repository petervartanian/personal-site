Abridge scribe pilot trimmed EHR time for UChicago clinicians

In a 2024 University of Chicago pilot, 125 Abridge scribe users spent 15.9% less time in notes and 8.5% less time in the EHR than 478 weighted nonusers.

What happened

In July 2024 the University of Chicago Medicine offered the Abridge AI scribe to its ambulatory clinicians as a three-month pilot that ran to 30 September 2024. After patients consented, clinicians recorded visits with an Abridge app on their personal devices, then reviewed the drafted note before moving it into the record. Researchers at the university compared 125 scribe users with 478 nonusers, using propensity score weighting to balance the groups. Scribe use was associated with 8.5% less EHR time per appointment (about 2.4 minutes) and 15.9% less time in notes (about 1.8 minutes). After-hours documentation time, appointment length and visit volume did not change significantly. Clinicians volunteered for the pilot. The authors used the weighting to address selection bias, which they describe as a key limit of earlier studies. The authors report no conflicts tied to Abridge.
