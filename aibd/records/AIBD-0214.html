Cognitive Tutor Algebra I yields a 0.20 SD second-year gain

a second-year gain of about 0.20 SD (50th to 58th percentile)

RAND RCT (EEPA 2014): no first-year effect, but a second-year gain of about 0.20 SD (50th to 58th percentile) for high-school algebra students using Carnegie Learning's Cognitive Tutor Algebra I.

What happened

RAND researchers Pane, Griffin, McCaffrey and Karam ran two parallel randomized experiments of Cognitive Tutor Algebra I (CTAI), a first-year algebra course developed by Carnegie Learning that blends classroom instruction and textbook activities with computer-based tutoring software, in 147 school sites (73 high schools and 74 middle schools in 51 districts in seven states), with nearly 18,700 students in the high-school study. Schools were matched into pairs and randomly assigned to adopt CTAI for two years or keep their curriculum. The RAND research brief (18 November 2013) and the Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis article (June 2014) report no significant first-year effect; in the second year high-school students in CTAI schools scored about 0.20 SD higher, equivalent to moving from the 50th to the 58th percentile, while the middle-school effect was not statistically significant. A RAND addendum of 30 May 2014 gives 0.21 SD for high-school students in the second year. Education Week (27 January 2014) reported the study as covering 25,000 students with $6 million of U.S. Department of Education funding and quoted teachers and analysts. The date observed is the publication date of the research brief; the fetched sources do not give the school years of the trial.

What it was built to do. CTAI was built to teach first-year algebra with personalized, mastery-based software; the test-score gain is that purpose.

The system's part

The curriculum combines software with textbook and classroom instruction, so the effect is of the blended course; RAND reports the gain only in schools' second year of implementation and not for middle schools.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • A blended curriculum pairing tutoring software with textbook and classroom instruction
  • School-pair randomization across 147 sites in seven states under conditions similar to independent adoption
  • Two years of implementation per school

What must hold again

  • A second year of implementation (no first-year effect was found)
  • High-school rather than middle-school setting for a statistically significant effect
