What happened

RAND researchers Pane, Griffin, McCaffrey and Karam ran two parallel randomized experiments of Cognitive Tutor Algebra I (CTAI). CTAI, a first-year algebra course developed by Carnegie Learning, blends classroom instruction and textbook activities with computer-based tutoring software. The 147 school sites were 73 high schools and 74 middle schools in 51 districts in seven states. Nearly 18,700 students were in the high-school study. Schools were matched into pairs and randomly assigned to adopt CTAI for two years or keep their curriculum. The RAND research brief (18 November 2013) and the Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis article (June 2014) report no significant first-year effect. In the second year high-school students in CTAI schools scored about 0.20 SD higher, equivalent to moving from the 50th to the 58th percentile.

The middle-school effect was not statistically significant. A RAND addendum of 30 May 2014 gives 0.21 SD for high-school students in the second year. Education Week (27 January 2014) reported the study as covering 25,000 students with $6 million of U.S. Department of Education funding. It quoted teachers and analysts. The date observed is the publication date of the research brief. The sources do not give the school years of the trial.

What it was built to do. CTAI was built to teach first-year algebra with personalized, mastery-based software. The test-score gain is that purpose.

Since then
Persisted on 1 June 2016: The What Works Clearinghouse's June 2016 review counted the RAND trial as the one study with a statistically significant positive effect on algebra, but rated Cognitive Tutor Algebra I as having mixed effects across the five eligible studies (average improvement index +4 percentile points).Thus, for the algebra domain, one study of Cognitive Tutor® Algebra I showed a statistically significant positive effect, one study showed a substantively important positive effect, and three studies showed an indeterminate effect. This results in a rating of mixed effects, with a medium to large extent of evidence. source

The system's part

The curriculum combines software with textbook and classroom instruction, so the effect is of the blended course. RAND reports the gain only in schools' second year of implementation and not for middle schools.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A blended curriculum pairing tutoring software with textbook and classroom instruction. School-pair randomization across 147 sites in seven states under conditions similar to independent adoption. Two years of implementation per school.

What must hold again. A second year of implementation (no first-year effect was found). High-school rather than middle-school setting for a statistically significant effect.
