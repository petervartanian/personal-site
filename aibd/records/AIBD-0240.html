Anthropic flag on a shooting threat leads to a Florida arrest

Anthropic's safety monitoring flagged a chat user's threat to shoot up a Florida sheriff's office, and after human review the tip led deputies to detain her; no weapon was seized.

  • 1 an organization

    The Lee County Sheriff's Office, the threatened party, received a tip quoting a stated plan to shoot up its building and a claimed new gun. It identified the user and detained her at home without incident the day after it logged the tip. No weapon was seized, so no averted attack is shown.

  • 1 society

    Police examined a written threat of a mass shooting at a public building within days. It was at least the third Anthropic referral to police since August 2026. The benefit is limited because no weapon was found and the user described the chats as a personal diary. Experts doubt that the words were a true threat.

The system’s part
The arrest report says the platform “utilizes safety and security measures designed to monitor for key phrases and potentially threatening content”. It says the messages were “escalated to a human review team”. An automated layer therefore surfaced the two messages, and people judged their severity and made the referral. The sources do not say whether the flag came from a classifier or a keyword rule. Straight Arrow News, citing court records, says the review team escalated to the FBI, which notified the sheriff's office. Identifying the user, the home visit, the Miranda interview and the charging decision were the work of Lee County deputies and Intelligence Detective Thomas Alejo. The arrest report calls the platform “Anthropic Artificial Intelligence”. Press reports name Claude, and court records note the user told the detective she used Perplexity.

What happened

A user of Anthropic's AI chat service wrote at about 05:10 on 26 September 2026. She said that she was going to “shoot up” the Lee County Sheriff's Office in Florida. At 01:07 on 27 September she wrote that she had got a new gun. Anthropic's safety monitoring flagged the messages, and because of their severity a human review team reported them to law enforcement. Straight Arrow News, citing court records, reported that the review team escalated to the FBI, which notified the sheriff's office. The sheriff's office logged the tip on 29 September and identified the user as Carli Michelle Heller, 30, of Bonita Springs. Deputies detained her at home without incident at 00:51 on 30 September. A detective charged her under Florida Statute 836.10, written threats of violence. No weapon was seized, and she described the AI as a personal diary. The arrest report calls the platform “Anthropic Artificial Intelligence”. Press reports name Claude, and court records note that the user told the detective that she used Perplexity. The arrest form set a $10,000 bond and a 20 October 2026 appearance date. Heller pleaded not guilty, and her arraignment was set for 2 November 2026 while prosecutors had not yet filed a formal charge. The First Amendment scholar Eugene Volokh doubts that the charge will hold.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The monitoring system surfaced two messages containing violent threats. Human reviewers assessed them and passed information to law enforcement.

Autonomy

Monitoring triggered human escalation rather than an automatic arrest. The police investigator independently identified, interviewed, and charged the user.

Workflow

A vendor practice of screening consumer chats for threatening content and escalating severe cases to a human review team. That team can refer them to law enforcement, which Anthropic's consumer terms and privacy policy permit. A tip carrying enough account information for the sheriff's office to identify a named resident within days. A local agency that treated a stated threat against its own building as credible enough to send deputies the same night.

Human oversight

Reviewers assessed the messages before making the referral. Deputies identified and interviewed the user and made the charging decision.

AI contribution

The platform's monitoring flagged the two messages automatically, and a human review team judged their severity and referred them to law enforcement. The sources do not say whether a classifier or a keyword rule raised the flag. Press reports name Claude, and court records note that the user told the detective that she used Perplexity. Beneficiaries

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The referral must identify a real person and a real risk. The Lee County Sheriff's Office told reporters it does not count tips from Anthropic. No public figures exist on how often such referrals are made or prove unfounded. The disclosure must stay within the emergency exception of the Stored Communications Act. An EFF attorney told Straight Arrow News “there is always the risk of over-reporting”, and the Monitor logs reputational and psychological harm to the user. The response must remain proportionate. Here no weapon was found, and the user described a personal diary. Whether a chatbot prompt is a punishable threat under Florida Statute 836.10 is contested by the defense and by Eugene Volokh. The attribution to Claude rests on press reports and the arrest report's phrase “Anthropic Artificial Intelligence”. Court records also note the user told the detective she used Perplexity, and her attorney declined to say which.
