Anthropic flag on a shooting threat leads to a Florida arrest

What happened

At about 05:10 on 26 September 2026 a user of Anthropic's AI chat service wrote that she was going to 'shoot up' the Lee County Sheriff's Office in Florida, and at 01:07 on 27 September that she had got a new gun. Anthropic's safety monitoring flagged the messages and, because of their severity, a human review team reported them to law enforcement. The sheriff's office logged the tip on 29 September, identified the user as Carli Michelle Heller, 30, of Bonita Springs, and deputies detained her at home without incident at 00:51 on 30 September; a detective charged her under Florida Statute 836.10, written threats of violence. No weapon was seized; she described the AI as a personal diary.

What it was built to do. Anthropic's consumer terms (effective 8 October 2025) reserve the right 'at our sole discretion' to report Inputs to law enforcement, and its privacy policy (effective 10 September 2026) allows disclosure the company believes 'reasonably necessary' to 'prevent serious harm to any person or to property'; the arrest report says the platform's safety measures are 'designed to monitor for key phrases and potentially threatening content'. Flagging a stated shooting threat, reviewing it by hand and referring it to police was that purpose; of an August 2026 referral Anthropic said 'This is our safeguards process working as intended'.

The system's part

The arrest report says the platform 'utilizes safety and security measures designed to monitor for key phrases and potentially threatening content' and that the messages were 'escalated to a human review team', so an automated layer surfaced the two messages and people judged their severity and made the referral; the sources do not say whether the flag came from a classifier or a keyword rule. Straight Arrow News, citing court records, says the review team escalated to the FBI, which notified the sheriff's office. Identifying the user, the home visit, the Miranda interview and the charging decision were the work of Lee County deputies and Intelligence Detective Thomas Alejo. The arrest report calls the platform 'Anthropic Artificial Intelligence'; press reports name Claude, and court records note the user told the detective she used Perplexity.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A vendor practice of screening consumer chats for threatening content and escalating severe cases to a human review team that can refer them to law enforcement, which Anthropic's consumer terms and privacy policy permit. A tip carrying enough account information for the sheriff's office to identify a named resident within days. A local agency that treated a stated threat against its own building as credible enough to send deputies the same night.

What must hold again. The referral must identify a real person and a real risk; the Lee County Sheriff's Office told reporters it does not count tips from Anthropic, and no public figures exist on how often such referrals are made or prove unfounded. The disclosure must stay within the emergency exception of the Stored Communications Act; an EFF attorney told Straight Arrow News 'there is always the risk of over-reporting', and the Monitor logs reputational and psychological harm to the user. The response must remain proportionate; here no weapon was found, the user described a personal diary, and whether a chatbot prompt is a punishable threat under Florida Statute 836.10 is contested by the defense and by Eugene Volokh. The attribution to Claude rests on press reports and the arrest report's phrase 'Anthropic Artificial Intelligence'; court records also note the user told the detective she used Perplexity, and her attorney declined to say which.
