Anthropic flag on a shooting threat leads to a Florida arrest

Anthropic safeguards flagged a threat to shoot up a Florida sheriff's office; reviewers tipped police, who arrested the user; no gun found.

ⓘ

What happened

A user of Anthropic's AI chat service wrote at about 05:10 on 26 September 2026. She said she was going to “shoot up” the Lee County Sheriff's Office in Florida. At 01:07 on 27 September she wrote that she had got a new gun. Anthropic's safety monitoring flagged the messages, and because of their severity a human review team reported them to law enforcement. The sheriff's office logged the tip on 29 September and identified the user as Carli Michelle Heller, 30, of Bonita Springs. Deputies detained her at home without incident at 00:51 on 30 September. A detective charged her under Florida Statute 836.10, written threats of violence. No weapon was seized, and she described the AI as a personal diary.

Purpose. Anthropic's consumer terms (effective 8 October 2025) reserve the right “at our sole discretion” to report Inputs to law enforcement. Its privacy policy (effective 10 September 2026) allows disclosure the company believes “reasonably necessary” to “prevent serious harm to any person or to property”. The arrest report says the platform's safety measures are “designed to monitor for key phrases and potentially threatening content”. Flagging a stated shooting threat, reviewing it by hand and referring it to police was that purpose. Of an August 2026 referral Anthropic said “This is our safeguards process working as intended”.

AI’s contribution

The arrest report says the platform “utilizes safety and security measures designed to monitor for key phrases and potentially threatening content”. It says the messages were “escalated to a human review team”. An automated layer therefore surfaced the two messages, and people judged their severity and made the referral. The sources do not say whether the flag came from a classifier or a keyword rule. Straight Arrow News, citing court records, says the review team escalated to the FBI, which notified the sheriff's office. Identifying the user, the home visit, the Miranda interview and the charging decision were the work of Lee County deputies and Intelligence Detective Thomas Alejo. The arrest report calls the platform “Anthropic Artificial Intelligence”. Press reports name Claude, and court records note the user told the detective she used Perplexity.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    The Lee County Sheriff's Office, the threatened party, received a tip quoting a stated plan to shoot up its building and a claimed new gun. It identified the user and resolved the matter at her home within about three days without incident. No weapon was seized, so no averted attack is shown.

  • society

    A written threat of a mass shooting at a public building was examined within days rather than left unread. It was at least the third Anthropic referral to police since August 2026. The benefit is bounded by the absence of a weapon, the user's diary account, and expert doubt that the words were a true threat.

Further evidence

Setting. A vendor practice of screening consumer chats for threatening content and escalating severe cases to a human review team. That team can refer them to law enforcement, which Anthropic's consumer terms and privacy policy permit. A tip carrying enough account information for the sheriff's office to identify a named resident within days. A local agency that treated a stated threat against its own building as credible enough to send deputies the same night.

Conditions. The referral must identify a real person and a real risk. The Lee County Sheriff's Office told reporters it does not count tips from Anthropic. No public figures exist on how often such referrals are made or prove unfounded. The disclosure must stay within the emergency exception of the Stored Communications Act. An EFF attorney told Straight Arrow News “there is always the risk of over-reporting”, and the Monitor logs reputational and psychological harm to the user. The response must remain proportionate. Here no weapon was found, and the user described a personal diary. Whether a chatbot prompt is a punishable threat under Florida Statute 836.10 is contested by the defense and by Eugene Volokh. The attribution to Claude rests on press reports and the arrest report's phrase “Anthropic Artificial Intelligence”. Court records also note the user told the detective she used Perplexity, and her attorney declined to say which.
