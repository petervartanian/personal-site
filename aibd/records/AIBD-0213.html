Microsoft Copilot tutoring raises test scores in Benin City schools

In a World Bank randomized trial in Benin City, six weeks of teacher-guided Microsoft Copilot (GPT-4) tutoring raised scores 0.31 standard deviations overall and 0.23 in English.

  • 2 a person

    Treated students scored 0.31 standard deviations higher overall and 0.23 higher in English after six weeks. This is a measured gain in test scores, and independent authors who re-analyzed the trial put the overall effect at 0.310 standard deviation (95% confidence interval 0.176 to 0.444).

  • Not rated an organization

    The 1.5 to 2 years of business-as-usual schooling is a cost-effectiveness comparison made by the authors. No source reports a change that the schools themselves realized.

What happened

A six-week after-school program ran in June and July 2024 in nine public schools in Benin City, Nigeria, chosen for their computer labs. First-year senior secondary students, typically 15 years old, who expressed interest were randomized: 657 to treatment and 671 to control. Of these, 422 and 337 finished the final assessment. Treated students attended up to two 90-minute sessions a week (twelve in total). They used Microsoft Copilot, powered by GPT-4 at the time, as a virtual English tutor in curriculum-aligned activities guided by teachers. In a World Bank blog post of 18 September 2024, members of the study team report that 800 first-year senior secondary students attended the after-school classes. In a World Bank blog post of 9 January 2025, the study team describes the gain as about 0.3 standard deviation. World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 11125 (May 2025) is by De Simone, Tiberti, Barron Rodriguez, Manolio, Mosuro and Dikoru. It gives the months of the program and no exact days. The authors report a 0.31 standard deviation improvement on an assessment covering English, AI knowledge and digital skills, and 0.23 standard deviation on English. They equate the gains to 1.5 to 2 years of business-as-usual schooling. Effects were larger for female students and those with higher initial performance. In an article published on 23 October 2026 in Education Sciences, Sánchez-Trujillo and colleagues from Peru and Colombia report a secondary analysis of the trial. They have no tie to the World Bank team. They found an overall effect of 0.310 standard deviation (95% confidence interval 0.176 to 0.444) and 0.238 in English, and they report that the interaction with digital access was negative but imprecise.

Details

AI system

Model

GPT-4 within Microsoft Copilot.

Role

Copilot provided English-language tutoring in sessions supervised by teachers.

Configuration

Researchers used GPT-4 through Microsoft Copilot.

Workflow

A freely available LLM (Copilot on GPT-4) requiring only student registration. Curriculum-aligned prompts and activities guided by teachers in school computer labs. Student-level randomization among volunteers in nine schools with computer labs.

Human oversight

Teachers supplied starting prompts and kept students on task while pairs shared computers and practiced English with Copilot.

Benefits

Reach

The trial randomized 1,328 volunteer students, 657 to treatment and 671 to control. Of the treated students, 422 finished the final assessment. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Distribution and access

Female students and students with stronger initial performance had larger measured gains.

Duration

The program ran for six weeks in June and July 2024, offering up to twelve 90-minute sessions. The independent authors of 2026 describe the gains as short-term, and no source reports that the program continued after the 2024 pilot. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Measurement

Measurement

Endline assessment covering English, knowledge of AI and digital skills, plus school examination scores. [6]

Setting and sample

Nine public senior secondary schools in Benin City, Nigeria; 1,328 randomized volunteers, 657 offered the program and 671 controls. [6]

Measures

Standardized assessment score (SD). [6]

Results

0.310 SD overall (95% CI 0.176 to 0.444; n = 654); 0.238 SD in English; 0.309 SD in AI knowledge; 0.206 SD in school examinations. [6]

Additional value from AI

Not isolated: the estimate covers Copilot together with curriculum-aligned activities, pair work, teacher guidance and extra lab time. [6]

Limitations

Attrition at endline (422 treated and 337 controls finished the final assessment in the original paper); short-term outcomes only; the digital-skills effect is imprecise (p = 0.066). [6]

Denominators

Up to twelve 90-minute after-school sessions over six weeks, June and July 2024. [6]

Materials for replication

World Bank reproducibility package by the study authors, doi 10.60572/cre7-j348. [6]

People and places

Dependencies

The program required working computer labs, internet access and teachers present during the sessions.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Working computer labs and internet access. Teacher guidance in every session. Attendance (the paper reports gains rising with days attended).
