AI-drafted objection gets a Tokat bus driver's traffic fine annulled

A Tokat school bus driver used ChatGPT to write his objection to a 2,400 TL fine for dropping a student off outside a stop; the court annulled it within three weeks for lack of evidence.

The system’s part
ChatGPT, per Webtekno, drafted the objection that pointed to the absence of any image or document behind the fine. The court annulled the fine on that ground. The decisive fact was the inspector's missing evidence, which Duran could have pleaded himself. What the system contributed was a petition in legal form at no fee. Neither report quotes the petition or the ruling.
Limits
The court annulled the fine after an objection about missing evidence. That legal ground may explain the result whoever drafted the petition. Neither the petition nor the ruling is quoted. The reports do not give the dates of the fine or the ruling. Only Webtekno names ChatGPT.

What happened

Mehmet Can Duran, a school bus driver in Tokat, Turkey, was fined 2,400 TL by an honorary traffic inspector (fahri trafik müfettişi). He allegedly let a student off outside the designated stop. Webtekno says that he had stopped at a pedestrian crossing to let people cross (Webtekno, 7 February 2025). Not wanting to pay a lawyer, he had an objection petition written by artificial intelligence. Webtekno identifies it as ChatGPT. Akşam's İHA report names no tool (Akşam, 6 February 2025). The petition stressed that the inspector had submitted no image or document supporting the fine. The Tokat Civil Court of Peace (Sulh Hukuk Mahkemesi) found for Duran and annulled the fine within three weeks (Akşam; Webtekno). The reports, published on 6 and 7 February 2025, do not give the dates of the fine or the ruling.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

Duran submitted the objection, and the court decided whether to annul the fine. ChatGPT supplied drafting assistance.

Workflow

A fine issued without supporting evidence that an objection could expose. A chatbot able to produce a petition in the form the court expects. A court that accepts a self-filed written objection.

Human oversight

Duran filed the AI-drafted petition himself. The Tokat Civil Court of Peace decided the objection. Neither report says whether anyone checked the petition before he filed it. [1] [2] [3]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Duran avoided the reported 2,400-lira fine and obtained a petition without hiring a lawyer.

Reach

One driver benefited. The court annulled his 2,400 TL fine. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

The benefit was a single annulment of the fine, within three weeks of the objection. The reports of 6 and 7 February 2025 describe no later development. [1] [2] [3]
