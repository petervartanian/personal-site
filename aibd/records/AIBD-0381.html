AI-drafted objection gets a Tokat bus driver's traffic fine annulled

depth 2 for a person

Independently reported, 3 sources.

A Tokat school bus driver used ChatGPT to write his objection to a 2,400 TL fine for dropping a student off outside a stop; the court annulled it within three weeks for lack of evidence.

What happened

Mehmet Can Duran, a school bus driver in Tokat, Turkey, was fined 2,400 TL by an honorary traffic inspector (fahri trafik müfettişi). He allegedly let a student off outside the designated stop. Webtekno says he had stopped at a pedestrian crossing to let people cross (Webtekno, 7 February 2025). Not wanting to pay a lawyer, he had an objection petition written by artificial intelligence. Webtekno identifies it as ChatGPT. Akşam's İHA report names no tool (Akşam, 6 February 2025). The petition stressed that the inspector had submitted no image or document supporting the fine. The Tokat Civil Court of Peace (Sulh Hukuk Mahkemesi) found for Duran and annulled the fine within three weeks (Akşam; Webtekno). The reports, published February 6 and 7, 2025, do not give the dates of the fine or the ruling. So the first report date is used here.

What it was built to do. A general-purpose chatbot. Drafting a legal objection on request is within what it was built to do, so this benefit was expected use.

The system's part

ChatGPT, per Webtekno, drafted the objection that pointed to the absence of any image or document behind the fine. The court annulled the fine on that ground. The decisive fact was the inspector's missing evidence, which Duran could have pleaded himself. What the system contributed was a petition in legal form at no fee. Neither report quotes the petition or the ruling.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A fine issued without supporting evidence that an objection could expose. A chatbot able to produce a petition in the form the court expects. A court that accepts a self-filed written objection.

What must hold again. The objection's factual claim must be true and checkable by the court. The filer must still meet the court's deadlines and form requirements.
