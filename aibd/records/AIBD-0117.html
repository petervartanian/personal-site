Turkish Brain Project algorithm flags a glioma mid-scan at Gazi

depth 2 for a person

Independently reported, 4 sources.

Real-time MRI algorithm at Gazi University Hospital alerted doctors to a 46-year-old woman's glioma mid-scan; biopsy next day, Oct 2022.

What happened

In October 2022 (day not given; first of month used), a 46-year-old woman with a nonspecific headache had brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). This was at Gazi University Faculty of Medicine Hospital, Ankara. A region-based convolutional neural network algorithm, built within the Turkish Brain Project and running on the hospital network in real time, detected an intraparenchymal mass. It alerted the research team while she was still in the scanner. Physicians read the images, judged the finding consistent with a diffuse glioma and admitted her. A stereotactic biopsy next day confirmed an isocitrate dehydrogenase wild-type diffuse glioma; she was referred to oncology for immediate treatment. The case was published 8 May 2023 and reported by Anadolu Agency 22 May 2023.

What it was built to do. The Turkish Brain Project algorithm was built to detect abnormalities on brain MRI as images are acquired and warn the responsible physicians at once. So the days the authors say radiology typically needs to report, and the longer wait before treatment, are cut to days. Anadolu Agency reports it was trained on stroke, hemorrhage and tumor cases. Flagging this patient's tumor mid-scan is that intended use.

The system's part

The algorithm detected the mass on the real-time MRI data and raised the alert while the patient was still in the scanner. Physicians then read the images, judged the finding consistent with a diffuse glioma and admitted her. The diagnosis itself came from the pathology report after the stereotactic biopsy. What the AI contributed is detection and speed, not a different diagnosis or treatment. The counterfactual is unproven. The authors say radiology would typically take days to report and that further investigations are needed on the advantages of earlier treatment. They give no evidence of what delay this patient would otherwise have faced. The case is reported by the team that built and deployed the algorithm, and the Anadolu Agency coverage carries that team's account. No independent clinical review is cited.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A region-based convolutional neural network object-detection algorithm trained on a brain MRI data set built within the Turkish Brain Project. Anadolu Agency: six months of training on stroke, hemorrhage and tumor cases. Integration into the hospital network so MRI images are processed as they are acquired. Anadolu Agency reports evaluation within 5–10 seconds on the hospital's three MRI scanners. An alert channel to the physicians' mobile phones, reported by Anadolu Agency as a GSM operator's picture-message service carrying the segmented abnormal regions. A neurosurgery team that reads the alert at once and can admit and biopsy the patient within a day.

What must hold again. The lesion is of a kind the model was trained on (the team names stroke, hemorrhage and tumor). The alert reaches a physician who acts on it before the routine radiology report. The hospital can schedule a biopsy or operation promptly. False alerts stay low enough that physicians keep trusting the messages. Anadolu Agency quotes Sağıroğlu that the system had reported over 1,000 abnormal findings and physicians work closely with it because they trust it.
