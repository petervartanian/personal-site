Software flags a brain tumor while a woman is still in the scanner

A Turkish Brain Project algorithm on Gazi University’s hospital network alerted physicians to a mass in a woman’s brain during her scan, and a biopsy the next day confirmed a glioma.

  • 2 a person

    One identifiable patient had a diffuse glioma found during her scan. She had a biopsy the next day and was referred for immediate oncological treatment. The authors say that radiology typically needs days to report a scan and that treatment comes later still. The paper reports no follow-up and no changed outcome. The “saved a life” wording comes from the Anadolu Agency headline, and the authors make no such claim.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The algorithm detected the mass on the real-time MRI data and raised the alert while the patient was still in the scanner. Physicians then read the images, judged the finding consistent with a diffuse glioma and admitted her. The diagnosis itself came from the pathology report after the stereotactic biopsy. What the AI contributed is detection and speed, not a different diagnosis or treatment. The counterfactual is unproven. The authors say radiology would typically take days to report and that further investigations are needed on the advantages of earlier treatment. They give no evidence of what delay this patient would otherwise have faced. The case is reported by the team that built and deployed the algorithm, and the Anadolu Agency coverage carries that team's account. No independent clinical review is cited.
Limits
The patient-specific delay without the alert is unknown. The retained developer report establishes a prompt diagnostic sequence, but it does not measure the effect of that timing on survival or other clinical outcomes.
Costs and harms
No source reports the rate of false alerts or any cost or harm in this case. Anadolu Agency quotes a team member who said that physicians work closely with the system because they trust it.
Later
Scaled up, 10 June 2025. In a paper published on 10 June 2025, the Gazi team states that its models ran in real time at the Gazi, Baskent and Ankara university medical school hospitals. The same paper also says that the system is deployed to two hospitals. No independent source confirms the spread, and no outcome data from it are published.

What happened

In October 2022, a 46-year-old woman with a nonspecific headache had brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) at Gazi University Faculty of Medicine Hospital, Ankara. A region-based convolutional neural network algorithm, built within the Turkish Brain Project and running on the hospital network in real time, detected an intraparenchymal mass. It alerted the research team while she was still in the scanner. Physicians read the images, judged the finding consistent with a diffuse glioma and admitted her. A stereotactic biopsy the next day confirmed an isocitrate dehydrogenase wild-type diffuse glioma. She was referred to oncology for immediate treatment. The case was published on 8 May 2023, and Anadolu Agency reported it on 22 May 2023. In a paper published on 10 June 2025, members of the same Gazi University team state that their models later ran in real time at the Gazi, Baskent and Ankara university medical school hospitals. Elsewhere in the paper they say that the system is deployed to two hospitals. They publish no outcome data from that use.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

A region-based neural network processed MRI images as they arrived and alerted physicians to a brain mass while the patient remained in the scanner.

Workflow

A region-based convolutional neural network object-detection algorithm trained on a brain MRI data set built within the Turkish Brain Project. Anadolu Agency: six months of training on stroke, hemorrhage and tumor cases. Integration into the hospital network so MRI images are processed as they are acquired. Anadolu Agency reports evaluation within 5–10 seconds on the hospital's three MRI scanners. An alert channel to the physicians' mobile phones, reported by Anadolu Agency as a GSM operator's picture-message service carrying the segmented abnormal regions. A neurosurgery team that reads the alert at once and can admit and biopsy the patient within a day.

Human oversight

Physicians reviewed the images, admitted the patient, and arranged a stereotactic biopsy the next day. Pathology established the glioma diagnosis.

AI contribution

The Turkish Brain Project system detected a mass and alerted physicians while the patient was still undergoing an MRI scan.

Benefits

Reach

One patient, a 46-year-old woman, is reported to have benefited. Anadolu Agency quotes a team member who said that the system had reported over 1,000 abnormal findings from the hospital's three MRI scanners. That figure counts findings by the system. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The sources report no follow-up of the patient after her referral to oncology. In a paper published on 10 June 2025, the Gazi team states that its models later ran in real time at three Ankara university hospitals. It publishes no outcome data from that use. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The lesion is of a kind the model was trained on (the team names stroke, hemorrhage and tumor). The alert reaches a physician who acts on it before the routine radiology report. The hospital can schedule a biopsy or operation promptly. False alerts stay low enough that physicians keep trusting the messages. Anadolu Agency quotes Sağıroğlu that the system had reported over 1,000 abnormal findings and physicians work closely with it because they trust it.

Evidence

Original evidence

The case report by the Gazi University team that built and deployed the algorithm is the original source. The Anadolu Agency reports carried by TRT Haber and Habertürk give that team's account. Several of the same authors wrote the 2025 PeerJ Computer Science paper. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Purpose

The Turkish Brain Project algorithm was built to detect abnormalities on brain magnetic resonance images as they are acquired and to warn the responsible physicians at once. The authors say that radiology typically needs days to report a scan and that treatment comes later still. Anadolu Agency reports that the algorithm was trained on stroke, hemorrhage and tumor cases. Flagging this patient's tumor during the scan is that intended use.
