Groningen AI finds two scribes wrote the Great Isaiah Scroll

two scribes wrote the Great Isaiah Scroll, switching at columns 27–29

AI handwriting analysis showed two scribes wrote the Great Isaiah Scroll, switching at columns 27–29; published in PLOS ONE 21 April 2021

What happened

On 21 April 2021 Mladen Popović, Maruf Dhali and Lambert Schomaker of the University of Groningen published in PLOS ONE an artificial-intelligence analysis of the handwriting of the Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaa) from the Dead Sea Scrolls. Using pattern recognition on digitised images of the scroll's 54 columns, they found that the columns fall into two statistically distinct groups, with a transition in columns 27–29, and concluded that two main scribes with different writing patterns were responsible for the scroll, against the view that one scribe wrote it. Some earlier scholars had suggested a change after column 27 on other grounds; the AI analysis supplied quantitative evidence. The team is both developer of the method and author of the finding; acceptance rests on the journal's peer review.

What it was built to do. Writer identification in ancient manuscripts to distinguish individual scribes

The system's part

The AI pipeline produced the statistical separation of the columns into two groups; the scribal interpretation and the conclusion that two scribes were responsible were made by the authors. The method's developers and the finding's authors are the same team; the only non-developer source fetched is the University of Groningen's statement as carried by phys.org, and independent coverage (Smithsonian, CNN) could not be fetched.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • High-resolution digital images of all 54 columns
  • Neural-network separation of ink from background
  • Textural and allographic feature extraction and statistical comparison of column groups
What must hold again
  • Sufficient legible text per column
  • Features that separate writers rather than pens or parchment
  • Statistical significance of the separation
