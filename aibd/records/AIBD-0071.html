Groningen AI finds two scribes wrote the Great Isaiah Scroll

AI handwriting analysis showed two scribes wrote the Great Isaiah Scroll, switching at columns 27–29; published in PLOS ONE 21 April 2021

ⓘ

What happened

On 21 April 2021 Mladen Popović, Maruf Dhali and Lambert Schomaker of the University of Groningen published an artificial-intelligence analysis in PLOS ONE. It examined the handwriting of the Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaa) from the Dead Sea Scrolls. They used pattern recognition on digitized images of the scroll's 54 columns. The columns fell into two statistically distinct groups, with a transition in columns 27–29. They concluded that two main scribes with different writing patterns were responsible for the scroll, against the view that one scribe wrote it. Some earlier scholars had suggested a change after column 27 on other grounds; the AI analysis supplied quantitative evidence. The team is both developer of the method and author of the finding; acceptance rests on the journal's peer review.

Purpose. Writer identification in ancient manuscripts to distinguish individual scribes

AI’s contribution

The AI analysis produced the statistical separation of the columns into two groups. The scribal interpretation and the conclusion that two scribes were responsible were made by the authors. The method's developers and the finding's authors are the same team. The only non-developer cited source is the University of Groningen's statement as carried by phys.org. Independent coverage (Smithsonian, CNN) is not among the sources.

Further evidence

Setting. High-resolution digital images of all 54 columns. Neural-network separation of ink from background. Textural and allographic features compared statistically across column groups.

Conditions. Sufficient legible text per column. Features that separate writers rather than pens or parchment. Statistical significance of the separation.
