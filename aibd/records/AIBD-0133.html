NIO's NWM emergency pull-over stops a sleeping driver's car

depth 3 for a person

Independently reported, 6 sources.

Driver asleep at 110 km/h on a rainy highway; NIO's NWM emergency pull-over stopped the car and an SOS call woke him, 1 June 2025.

What happened

Around midnight on 1 June 2025, a NIO owner fell asleep driving at 110 km/h on a highway in rain in China. NIO's assisted driving was engaged. The NIO World Model (NWM) emergency autonomous pull-over function triggered, and an SOS call woke him. NIO had begun pushing it to more than 400,000 Banyan-platform cars on 30 May 2025. He posted a video on social media on 2 June. NIO founder William Li reposted it, describing the function (gradual deceleration, a lane change to the rightmost emergency lane, hazard lights, SOS intervention). He stressed that assisted driving is not autonomous driving. No crash or injury was reported. The vehicle model and highway are not named.

What it was built to do. NIO's first NWM release, pushed to Banyan-platform cars from 30 May 2025, added an “emergency autonomous pull-over”. It works on a highway or urban expressway when the assisted-driving system detects an unresponsive driver. The car slows gradually, moves to the rightmost emergency lane, turns on its hazard lights and triggers an SOS call. CnEVPost notes that NIO cars previously could only decelerate and stop within the current lane. This event is that function doing what it was built to do.

The system's part

The NWM-based assisted-driving system judged the driver unresponsive, ran the emergency pull-over and placed the SOS call that woke him. The car was already under assisted driving. Its specific contribution was therefore taking the car out of the live lane and rousing the driver rather than keeping it in lane. The sequence is known only from the owner's video as relayed by independent outlets. NIO's founder confirmed the function's design. No telemetry, police report or third-party verification was published.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. The car was running the first NIO World Model (NWM) release with the “emergency autonomous pull-over” function, pushed to Banyan-platform cars from 30 May 2025. Assisted driving was engaged on a highway, the function's stated operating domain (highways and urban expressways). The assisted-driving system judged the driver unresponsive. He did not respond to the escalating alarms NIO describes. The pull-over and the SOS call therefore ran to completion.

What must hold again. Driver monitoring must detect an unresponsive driver. The road must offer a rightmost emergency lane to stop in. The SOS call needs working connectivity and a NIO emergency-response channel to reach the driver or a responder. NIO and its founder present the function as a safety net within assisted driving, not autonomous driving. The driver remains responsible for staying awake.
