NIO car pulls over by itself after its driver falls asleep on a highway

The emergency pull-over function of the NIO World Model, NIO's assisted-driving software, stopped a car whose driver fell asleep at 110 km/h on 1 June 2025, and an SOS call woke him.

Workflow

The car was running the first NIO World Model (NWM) release with the “emergency autonomous pull-over” function, pushed to Banyan-platform cars from 30 May 2025. Assisted driving was engaged on a highway, the function's stated operating domain (highways and urban expressways). The assisted-driving system judged the driver unresponsive. He did not respond to the escalating alarms NIO describes. The pull-over and the SOS call therefore ran to completion.

Relation to AI

The NWM-based assisted-driving system judged the driver unresponsive, ran the emergency pull-over and placed the SOS call that woke him. The car was already under assisted driving. Its specific contribution was therefore taking the car out of the live lane and rousing the driver rather than keeping it in lane. The sequence is known only from the owner's video as relayed by independent outlets. NIO's founder confirmed the function's design. No telemetry, police report or third-party verification was published.

Purpose

NIO's first NWM release, pushed to Banyan-platform cars from 30 May 2025, added an “emergency autonomous pull-over”. It works on a highway or urban expressway when the assisted-driving system detects an unresponsive driver. The car slows gradually, moves to the rightmost emergency lane, turns on its hazard lights and triggers an SOS call. CnEVPost notes that NIO cars previously could only decelerate and stop within the current lane. This event is that function doing what it was built to do.
