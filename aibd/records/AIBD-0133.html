NIO car pulls over by itself after its driver falls asleep on a highway

The emergency pull-over function of the NIO World Model, NIO's assisted-driving software, stopped a car whose driver fell asleep at 110 km/h on 1 June 2025, and an SOS call woke him.

  • 3 a person

    One identifiable driver, asleep at 110 km/h in rain on a highway, was brought to a stop and woken without a crash. The owner's account, as relayed by the outlets, calls it a rescue (“挽救”). The account does not establish that a crash was imminent.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The NWM-based assisted-driving system judged the driver unresponsive, ran the emergency pull-over and placed the SOS call that woke him. The car was already under assisted driving. Its specific contribution was therefore taking the car out of the live lane and rousing the driver rather than keeping it in lane. The sequence is known only from the owner's video as relayed by independent outlets. NIO's founder confirmed the function's design. No telemetry, police report or third-party verification was published.
Limits
The sequence rests on an owner’s video and the founder’s description, without telemetry or police confirmation. The record does not establish that a crash was imminent or quantify safety under comparable conditions. A later software rollout is not follow-up on this driver.

What happened

On 30 May 2025 NIO began pushing the first release of its NIO World Model to more than 400,000 Banyan-platform cars. The release added an emergency autonomous pull-over function. CnEVPost reports that before this release NIO cars could only slow and stop in their own lane when a driver stopped responding. Around midnight on 1 June 2025, a NIO owner fell asleep driving at 110 km/h on a highway in rain in China. NIO's assisted driving was engaged. The emergency autonomous pull-over function triggered, and an SOS call woke him. He posted a video on social media on 2 June. NIO founder William Li reposted it and described the function: gradual deceleration, a lane change to the rightmost emergency lane, hazard lights and an SOS intervention. He stressed that assisted driving is not autonomous driving. No crash or injury was reported. The sources do not name the vehicle model or the highway. On 28 January 2026 NIO rolled out a major update of the software to more than 460,000 Banyan-system vehicles. In October 2026 Kuai Keji relayed another NIO owner's account. She said that she was taken ill on an elevated road with her daughter, and that assisted driving and the NOMI assistant got the car to the roadside and called for help.

Details

AI system

Release

The first NIO World Model release, distributed from 30 May 2025. The installed build is not identified.

Role

The system detected that the driver was unresponsive and initiated an emergency pull-over, according to the owner’s account.

Configuration

NIO released the first NIO World Model to more than 400,000 cars using its Banyan platform on 30 May 2025.

Autonomy

The reported emergency function slowed the car, moved it toward the emergency lane, and initiated an SOS call after the driver failed to respond.

Workflow

The car was running the first NIO World Model (NWM) release with the “emergency autonomous pull-over” function, pushed to Banyan-platform cars from 30 May 2025. Assisted driving was engaged on a highway, the function's stated operating domain (highways and urban expressways). The assisted-driving system judged the driver unresponsive. He did not respond to the escalating alarms NIO describes. The pull-over and the SOS call therefore ran to completion.

Human oversight

The driver was asleep while the pull-over ran, and an SOS call woke him. NIO and its founder present the function as a safety net within assisted driving, and the driver remains responsible for staying awake. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Benefits

Reach

One driver benefited: the unnamed NIO owner who fell asleep at 110 km/h. The figure of more than 400,000 Banyan-platform cars counts the cars to which NIO began pushing the software. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

The pull-over was a single event on 1 June 2025, and no source follows the driver afterwards. On 28 January 2026 NIO rolled out a major update of the software to more than 460,000 Banyan-system vehicles. In October 2026 Kuai Keji relayed another owner's account that assisted driving and the NOMI assistant got her car to the roadside when she fell ill. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Driver monitoring must detect an unresponsive driver. The road must offer a rightmost emergency lane to stop in. The SOS call needs working connectivity and a NIO emergency-response channel to reach the driver or a responder. NIO and its founder present the function as a safety net within assisted driving, not autonomous driving. The driver remains responsible for staying awake.
