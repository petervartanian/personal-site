Nabla scribe cut note time for UCLA physicians in a randomized trial

In a UCLA Health randomized trial of 238 outpatient physicians, the Nabla ambient scribe cut time-in-note by 9.5% against usual care; DAX Copilot showed no significant change.

  • 1 a person

    Physicians who used Nabla spent 9.5% less time writing notes than physicians in usual care.

  • 1 an organization

    The health system recovered documentation time across 23,653 visits in the Nabla arm without other changes to clinic work.

What happened

UCLA Health ran a pragmatic randomized trial of two ambient AI scribes from 4 November 2024 to 3 January 2025. The trial team assigned 238 outpatient physicians in 14 specialties to Microsoft Dragon Ambient eXperience (DAX) Copilot, to Nabla, or to usual care. The scribes recorded visits and drafted notes inside the Epic record. Nabla was used in 29.5% of 23,653 visits. Physicians who used Nabla cut their time-in-note by 9.5% against the control group (95% confidence interval 1.8% to 17.2%). The DAX group showed no significant change in time-in-note. Survey scores for burnout and task load suggested improvement with both scribes, but the authors write that these secondary results need confirmation in larger trials. Physicians reported clinically significant inaccuracies "occasionally", and one mild adverse event was reported. The UCLA Department of Medicine and other UCLA units funded the trial, which ran at a single academic health system over two months.

    Lukac PJ, Turner W, Vangala S, et al. Ambient AI Scribes in Clinical Practice: A Randomized Trial. NEJM AI 2025;2(12). doi:10.1056/aioa2501000. Author manuscript in PMC. Written by UCLA Health physicians and researchers, the deploying health system.

    Nabla users experienced a 9.5% (95% confidence interval [CI], -17.2% to -1.8%; P=0.02) decrease in time-in-note versus the control group, whereas DAX users exhibited no significant change versus the control group (-1.7%; 95% CI, -9.4% to +5.9%; P=0.66).

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

Nabla version 1.5

Workflow

Nabla was integrated into Epic so that drafted text filled the native note. Physicians received one hour of virtual training before the trial. Physicians chose the visits on which to use the scribe.

Measurement

Measurement

Epic Signal time-in-note (log-transformed change from baseline); surveys for burnout, task load and accuracy. [2]

Setting and sample

238 outpatient physicians in 14 specialties at UCLA Health; 23,653 Nabla-arm visits. [2]

Measures

Change in time-in-note versus control. [2]

Results

Nabla -9.5% (95% CI -17.2% to -1.8%); DAX -1.7% (95% CI -9.4% to +5.9%). [2]

Additional value from AI

9.5% less time-in-note for Nabla users. [2]

Limitations

Single system, two months, scribe used in under a third of visits, survey outcomes secondary, occasional inaccuracies. [2]

Denominators

Nabla used in 29.5% of visits in its arm. [2]

Materials for replication

ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06792890; supplementary appendix. [2]

Follow-up

Physicians use the scribe on a meaningful share of visits. Physicians review drafts for occasional inaccuracies.
