PAWS-directed patrols remove over 1,000 snares in Cambodia

PAWS-directed ranger patrols in Srepok Wildlife Sanctuary removed over 1,000 snares in December 2018, versus 200–300 in a typical month.

  • 2 an organization

    The ranger force's monthly snare removals rose from a reported two or three hundred to over one thousand. A chi-squared test confirmed that the predictions discriminated risk (p = 0.023).

  • 3 society

    Over one thousand snares were removed from a sanctuary that is identified as a site for reintroducing tigers. The paper states that each snare “represents the life of one animal potentially saved”. No source documents a change in an animal population.

The system’s part
The AI's output (risk-ranked 3 x 3 km blocks) determined where patrols went. Rangers were blinded to risk class. High-risk blocks yielded detections while low-risk blocks yielded none. The paper itself cautions that the first QENP test's increased findings “may be partially explained by factors beyond the predictive model”. The 1,000-versus-200–300 comparison relies on rangers' reported averages, not a controlled baseline. The peer-reviewed paper is written by the system's academic developers together with staff of the operating organizations (WWF, WCS, Uganda Wildlife Authority). There is no commercial vendor.
Limits
The comparison with the rangers' usual monthly total rests on their reported averages, and it does not separate the model's choice of blocks from patrol effort, seasonal conditions or reporting differences. For an earlier field test in Queen Elizabeth National Park in Uganda, the paper cautions that increased findings may be partially explained by factors beyond the predictive model. No source establishes the number of animals saved.

What happened

PAWS (Protection Assistant for Wildlife Security) is a machine-learning pipeline that predicts poaching risk and plans patrols. It was field-tested with WWF Cambodia and Ministry of Environment rangers in Srepok Wildlife Sanctuary, Cambodia, from December 2018. Rangers received center coordinates of fifteen 3 x 3 km blocks chosen from PAWS risk predictions, without being told each block's risk class. In the first month, 72 rangers in teams of eight detected poaching in 32 cells and removed over one thousand snares. They had reported removing two or three hundred in an average month. Low-risk blocks yielded no poaching. A second trial ran from February to March 2019. In that trial, high-risk blocks yielded 7 detections in 22 cells, and low-risk blocks yielded 0 detections in 34 cells. The developers and staff of the operating organizations reported both trials in a peer-reviewed paper published in 2020. On 5 November 2020, Mongabay reported that PAWS had been incorporated into the SMART conservation software, which rangers use in parks around the world. Mongabay also reported that Srepok rangers had reported finding 5 to 20 times more snares since they started using PAWS.

Details

AI system

Model

Gaussian-process models within iWare-E.

Role

The models predicted poaching risk to guide patrol locations.

Configuration

The iWare-E models were trained on dry-season data from January 2015 through April 2018.

Inputs and outputs

PAWS used previous patrol records to estimate poaching risk and supplied the center coordinates of fifteen 3-by-3-kilometer patrol blocks.

Workflow

Historical SMART patrol data (2015–2018) sufficient to train a risk model despite 3.6% positive labels. Rangers willing to patrol the suggested blocks and record findings in SMART. Dry-season deployment when rivers are passable and poaching concentrates in the north of the sanctuary.

Human oversight

Ranger teams entered the suggested blocks, searched for poaching, removed snares, and recorded what they found. They were not told each block's predicted risk class.

Benefits

Reach

No source gives a count of beneficiaries. In the first month, 72 rangers in teams of eight removed over one thousand snares, and the paper states that each snare represents one animal potentially saved. [1] [2]

Duration

In a second trial from February to March 2019, high-risk blocks yielded 7 detections in 22 cells and low-risk blocks yielded none in 34 cells. By November 2020, Srepok rangers reported finding 5 to 20 times more snares since they adopted PAWS, which had been incorporated into the SMART conservation software. [1] [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Patrol capacity exists to act on predictions (SWS ranger numbers had more than doubled since 2016). Poacher behavior remains spatially predictable from past detections. Findings are measured against a comparable baseline. The paper notes increases may partly reflect factors beyond the model.
