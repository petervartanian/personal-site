PAWS-directed patrols remove over 1,000 snares in Cambodia

PAWS-directed ranger patrols in Srepok Wildlife Sanctuary removed over 1,000 snares in December 2018, versus 200–300 in a typical month.

AI system

System

PAWS (Protection Assistant for Wildlife Security), enhanced iWare-E predictive model with Gaussian-process uncertainty and game-theoretic patrol planning

Model

Gaussian-process models within iWare-E.

Release

Exact release not established.

Application developer

Harvard University and University of Southern California (Milind Tambe and colleagues), with WWF, WCS and Uganda Wildlife Authority co-authors

Role

The models predicted poaching risk to guide patrol locations.

Configuration

The iWare-E models were trained on dry-season data from January 2015 through April 2018.

Inputs and outputs

The models used historical patrol data to estimate where poaching was likely.

Workflow

Historical SMART patrol data (2015–2018) sufficient to train a risk model despite 3.6% positive labels. Rangers willing to patrol the suggested blocks and record findings in SMART. Dry-season deployment when rivers are passable and poaching concentrates in the north of the sanctuary.

Relation to AI

The AI's output (risk-ranked 3 x 3 km blocks) determined where patrols went. Rangers were blinded to risk class. High-risk blocks yielded detections while low-risk blocks yielded none. The paper itself cautions that the first QENP test's increased findings “may be partially explained by factors beyond the predictive model”. The 1,000-versus-200–300 comparison relies on rangers' reported averages, not a controlled baseline. The peer-reviewed paper is written by the system's academic developers together with staff of the operating organizations (WWF, WCS, Uganda Wildlife Authority). There is no commercial vendor.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Patrol capacity exists to act on predictions (SWS ranger numbers had more than doubled since 2016). Poacher behavior remains spatially predictable from past detections. Findings are measured against a comparable baseline. The paper notes increases may partly reflect factors beyond the model.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2.

Later findings

5 November 2020. By November 2020 PAWS had been incorporated into the SMART conservation software used in parks worldwide, and Srepok rangers reported finding 5 to 20 times more snares since adopting it.

Depth

Reasons

Organizations: The ranger force's monthly snare removals rose from a reported two or three hundred to over one thousand. A material gain in patrol effectiveness for the operating team. A chi-squared test confirmed predictions discriminated risk (p = 0.023). Society: Over one thousand snares were removed from a sanctuary identified as a tiger-reintroduction site. The paper states each snare “represents the life of one animal potentially saved”. A material landscape-scale conservation benefit rather than a documented population-level change.
