Brief from Peter

The April 6 White House briefing documents the rescue of a downed American airman in Iran.1 The claim that an AI-assisted quantum sensor located him comes from two anonymous sources in the New York Post. President Trump subsequently told the Post that the tool was important, without explaining how it worked.2 Physicists interviewed by the Post and Scientific American questioned the described capability.3 The rescue is established; the contribution attributed to Ghost Murmur remains unverified.

What happened

The April 6 White House briefing documents the rescue of the downed F-15E weapons systems officer in Iran. The official transcript also describes watching his suspected position with a camera from 40 miles away. That distance concerns the camera, not an established heartbeat-detection range. On April 7, the New York Post reported that two anonymous sources attributed the officer's location to a CIA tool called Ghost Murmur. They described quantum magnetic sensing combined with AI that separates a heartbeat signal from background noise, and attributed its development to Lockheed Martin's Skunk Works. Lockheed Martin declined to comment. In an April 8 telephone interview with the Post, President Trump called the tool important to the rescue, but supplied no technical explanation. The Post and Scientific American separately interviewed physicists who questioned the described capability.

The official briefing does not name Ghost Murmur or establish an AI contribution. No public measurements or inspectable technical account establish that the claimed system located the officer.

What it was built to do. Per the Post's sources, it is a tool built to find a human heartbeat at distance. The uses are intelligence collection and personnel recovery, and it was tested on Black Hawk helicopters. Its stated purpose is exactly the use claimed.
