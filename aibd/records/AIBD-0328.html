NY Post says Ghost Murmur found downed F-15E officer; disputed

a camera was trained on his suspected position from 40 miles away

The rescue is official (Ratcliffe, 6 Apr 2026). The tool rests on two anonymous Post sources; Lockheed declined comment; Trump called it 'very important'; physicists say the physics fails.

What happened

The OECD AI Incidents Monitor record 2026-04-08-d905 says that the CIA deployed 'Ghost Murmur', using quantum magnetometry and AI algorithms to detect heartbeats, to locate and rescue a downed US pilot in Iran, and lists 40-odd Chinese, Korean, Italian and Spanish syndications. The rescue itself is documented: Air & Space Forces Magazine (6 Apr 2026) reports from the White House briefing that an F-15E was shot down in the early hours of 3 April local time, that the two crew ejected with rescue beacons active (Gen. Caine), that CIA Director John Ratcliffe said the agency used human assets and 'sophisticated intelligence technologies' and confirmed on the morning of 4 April that the weapons systems officer was alive in a mountain crevice, that a camera was trained on his suspected position from 40 miles away until he stood up after about 45 minutes, and that the extraction followed that night. The New York Post (Steven Nelson, 7 Apr 2026, exclusive) reports from 'two sources close to the breakthrough' that the CIA used Ghost Murmur, which 'uses long-range quantum magnetometry to find the electromagnetic signal of a human heartbeat and pairs the data with artificial intelligence software to isolate the signature from background noise', in its first field use; the sources say it was developed by Lockheed Martin's Skunk Works, 'The company declined to comment', the airman's CSEL beacon is described as giving a secondary confirmation layer, and Trump and Ratcliffe only 'alluded' to secret technology. On 8 April the Post reported that Trump told it by phone that the tool 'was very important. The CIA was fantastic', while physicists Thad Walker (Wisconsin) and others said that detection at even 100 metres with NV-diamond magnetometers 'would be astounding'. Scientific American (Deni Ellis Béchard, 8 Apr 2026) quotes Vanderbilt's John Wikswo that the heart's field is 'just barely detectable' at 10 centimetres and concludes that Ghost Murmur 'as publicly described, finds no support in decades of peer-reviewed physics, even with the help of AI'; Military.com (18 Apr 2026) says that the 40-mile heartbeat claim 'remains unverified and inconsistent with publicly documented physics' and that no agency has confirmed the capability; The Quantum Insider (8 Apr 2026) finds no published research near the described performance; Ynet and CTech (9 Apr 2026) and Diario Panorama (10 Apr 2026, relaying The Times and Scientific American) repeat the doubts. The Monitor's listed sources are derivative of the Post. No CIA, DoD, White House transcript or Lockheed confirmation of the tool exists in any fetched source.

What it was built to do. Per the Post's sources, a tool built to find a human heartbeat at distance for intelligence collection and personnel recovery, tested on Black Hawk helicopters; its stated purpose is exactly the use claimed.
