Brief from Peter

In April 2026 a United States F-15E was shot down over Iran and its weapons systems officer was found alive the next morning;1 that much is established by the White House briefing and by CIA Director Ratcliffe. The claim that a quantum sensor with AI, “Ghost Murmur”, found him is the part that traveled: it rests on two anonymous sources in a New York Post exclusive, and it spread through a presidential “very important” into forty-odd syndications in four languages.2 Scientific American and Military.com then reported that the described detection at 40 miles finds no support in published physics;3 Lockheed Martin declined to comment and no agency confirmed the tool. The case matters now because claims about AI in military operations are moving faster than the evidence for them, and this database rates them accordingly: The 5 is the officer's life, which is documented, while the AI's part is unassessed. Until an official or technical source names the tool, this is a rescue with a story attached.

What happened

The OECD AI Incidents entry says that the CIA deployed “Ghost Murmur” to locate and rescue a downed US pilot in Iran. The entry says the tool uses quantum magnetometry and AI algorithms to detect heartbeats, and it lists 40-odd Chinese, Korean, Italian and Spanish syndications. The rescue itself is documented. Air & Space Forces Magazine (6 April 2026) reports from the White House briefing. An F-15E was shot down in the early hours of 3 April local time. The two crew ejected with rescue beacons active, per Gen. Caine. CIA Director John Ratcliffe said the agency used human assets and “sophisticated intelligence technologies”. He confirmed on the morning of 4 April that the weapons systems officer was alive in a mountain crevice. A camera was trained on his suspected position from 40 miles away until he stood up after about 45 minutes. The rescue followed that night.

The New York Post (Steven Nelson, 7 April 2026, exclusive) cites “two sources close to the breakthrough”. They say the CIA used Ghost Murmur in its first field use. The Post says the tool “uses long-range quantum magnetometry to find the electromagnetic signal of a human heartbeat and pairs the data with artificial intelligence software to isolate the signature from background noise”. The sources say it was developed by Lockheed Martin's Skunk Works, and “The company declined to comment”. The airman's Combat Survivor Evader Locator (CSEL) beacon is described as giving a secondary confirmation layer. Trump and Ratcliffe only “alluded” to secret technology. On 8 April the Post reported that Trump told it by phone that the tool “was very important. The CIA was fantastic”.

Physicists Thad Walker (Wisconsin) and others said that detection at even 100 meters with NV-diamond magnetometers “would be astounding”. Scientific American (Deni Ellis Béchard, 8 April 2026) quotes Vanderbilt's John Wikswo that the heart's field is “just barely detectable” at 10 centimeters. It concludes that Ghost Murmur “as publicly described, finds no support in decades of peer-reviewed physics, even with the help of AI”. Military.com (18 April 2026) says that the 40-mile heartbeat claim “remains unverified and inconsistent with publicly documented physics”. It says that no agency has confirmed the capability. The Quantum Insider (8 April 2026) finds no published research near the described performance. Ynet and CTech (9 April 2026) and Diario Panorama (10 April 2026, relaying The Times and Scientific American) repeat the doubts.

The Monitor's listed sources are derivative of the Post. No CIA, DoD, White House transcript or Lockheed confirmation of the tool exists in any source on file.

What it was built to do. Per the Post's sources, it is a tool built to find a human heartbeat at distance. The uses are intelligence collection and personnel recovery, and it was tested on Black Hawk helicopters. Its stated purpose is exactly the use claimed.
