Brief from Peter

In a study published online on 11 June 2026, M. D. Keall of the University of Otago and S. Newstead of the Monash University Accident Research Centre analyzed injury crashes in Australia and New Zealand from 2016 to 2023. Vehicles with pedestrian autonomous emergency braking had a 17% lower rate of pedestrian or cyclist collisions (95% confidence interval 5% to 27%) than vehicles without any autonomous emergency braking (AEB). Vehicles with AEB that lacked pedestrian detection showed no reduction in these collisions, which suggests that the detection accounts for the benefit.1 The authors estimated a larger benefit at night, which differs from some other studies. The rate of collisions with motorcycles was 11% lower with pedestrian AEB, but the lower end of its 95% confidence interval is zero. The design is observational, and the authors relied on vehicle specifications to tell which vehicles had the system.2

Details

AI system

Workflow

Vehicles on Australian and New Zealand roads carried factory-fitted PAEB. Pedestrian detection made the difference: AEB without it showed no reduction in these collisions.

Measurement

Measurement

Induced exposure analysis of injury crash rates, controlling for jurisdiction, speed limit, market group, driver age and sex, weather, light and year. [1]

Setting and sample

Australian and New Zealand injury crashes, 2016 to 2023. [1]

Measures

Rate of collisions with pedestrians or cyclists, and with motorcycles. [1]

Results

17% lower pedestrian or cyclist collision rate (95% CI 5% to 27%); 11% lower motorcycle collision rate (95% CI 0% to 21%). [1]

Additional value from AI

No reduction for AEB without pedestrian detection, so the gain is tied to the detection. [1]

Limitations

Observational; fitment inferred from Redbook specifications; too few cyclists for a separate estimate. [1]

Denominators

Vehicles listed with pedestrian avoidance with braking. [1]

Materials for replication

Jurisdictional crash data and Redbook specifications; code not public. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The system must detect pedestrians, cyclists or motorcyclists in time to brake. The fitted system must include pedestrian detection.
