AI triage of abuse imagery under test to find victims faster

What happened

The Metropolitan Police Service announced that it is exploring the use of artificial intelligence to support rapid grading and triage of child sexual abuse imagery. Material possibly relating to unknown victims could then be flagged for prioritized safeguarding. The force also wants to reduce officers' manual review of distressing material. It said it is in conversations with multiple companies and testing how this could work. It described a separate tool that reviews 641,000 messages in 35 minutes, and a £10 million investment in victim interview suites. It cited more than 5,400 child sexual abuse offenses investigated in the past year and over 1,300 children safeguarded. Deputy Commissioner Matt Jukes said speed matters for safeguarding and that human judgment and oversight would remain central.

The OECD AI Incidents Monitor records it as an “AI hazard”, noting the use is under consideration and not implemented.

What it was built to do. Rapidly analyze seized imagery to grade it in categories A to C. Flag content possibly showing previously unknown victims. The aims are to prioritize safeguarding and reduce officer exposure.
