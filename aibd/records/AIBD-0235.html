What happened

On 12 July 2019 the Home Office announced an image categorization algorithm for the national Child Abuse Image Database. On 13 April 2026 the Metropolitan Police Service announced that it is exploring a similar use of artificial intelligence: rapid grading and triage of child sexual abuse imagery. Material possibly relating to unknown victims could then be flagged for prioritized safeguarding. The force also wants to reduce officers' manual review of distressing material. It said that it is in conversations with multiple companies. It said that it is testing how this could work. It described a separate tool that reviews 641,000 messages in 35 minutes. It also described a £10 million investment in victim interview suites. It cited more than 5,400 child sexual abuse offenses investigated in the past year and over 1,300 children safeguarded. Deputy Commissioner Matt Jukes said that speed matters for safeguarding. He said that human judgment and oversight would remain central. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor records the announcement as an “AI hazard” and notes that the use is under consideration and not implemented. As of 29 October 2026 neither the Metropolitan Police nor the OECD monitor had reported any use of the grading tools on live cases or any result.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The proposed tools would analyze seized images and videos, assist severity grading, and flag material that may involve previously unidentified victims.

Workflow

Large volumes of seized imagery awaiting manual grading. Vendor classifiers for severity grading and known-versus-unknown victim flagging.

Human oversight

Specialist officers would retain decision-making responsibility and oversight throughout the proposed workflow.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The intended beneficiaries were unidentified children needing safeguarding and staff exposed to distressing material during manual review. The announcement gave no count of children helped by these proposed tools.

Duration

As of 29 October 2026, neither the Metropolitan Police nor the OECD monitor had reported any use of the grading tools on live cases or any result. No source reports a benefit whose duration could be measured. [1] [2]
