Met explores AI triage of abuse imagery to find victims faster

off the scale

On 13 Apr 2026 the Met said it is exploring AI to grade and triage child sexual abuse imagery, testing with multiple companies, to flag unknown victims faster; no deployment or outcome yet.

What happened

The Metropolitan Police Service announced that it is exploring the use of artificial intelligence to support rapid grading and triage of child sexual abuse imagery, so that material possibly relating to unknown victims can be flagged for prioritized safeguarding while reducing officers' manual review of distressing material. The force said it is in conversations with multiple companies and testing how this could work, alongside a separate tool that reviews 641,000 messages in 35 minutes and a £10 million investment in victim interview suites. It cited more than 5,400 child sexual abuse offenses investigated in the past year and over 1,300 children safeguarded. Deputy Commissioner Matt Jukes said speed matters for safeguarding and that human judgment and oversight would remain central. OECD AIM records it as an 'AI hazard' (2026-04-13-83b8), noting the use is under consideration and not implemented.

What it was built to do. Rapidly analyze seized imagery to grade it (categories A to C) and flag content possibly showing previously unknown victims, prioritizing safeguarding and reducing officer exposure.
