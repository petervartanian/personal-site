IdentiFlight halts turbines for eagles; the drop in deaths is disputed

Researchers estimated an 82% (75%–89%) relative cut in eagle deaths at a Wyoming wind farm after IdentiFlight began curtailing turbines; two federal statisticians later put it at 50% (−159%, 89%).

  • 2 society

They state that deaths were not eliminated. Two U.S. Geological Survey statisticians reanalyzed the data and put the effect at 50%, with an interval that includes an increase, so the size of the effect is disputed.

What happened

McClure et al. tested an automated curtailment system at the Top of the World Windpower Facility in Wyoming, United States. The authors are from The Peregrine Fund, Western EcoSystems Technology and the U.S. Geological Survey. The system's cameras detect and classify flying objects, and the system curtails individual turbines. The design was before–after–control–impact, with a control site 15 km away. Recharge reported the claim on 28 January 2021. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor logged the 82% result as a positive record. The study appeared in the Journal of Applied Ecology (Crossref publication date 31 January 2021). Correcting for carcass detection probability, the authors estimated that fatalities fell 63% at the treatment site (95% confidence interval 59%–66%). Fatalities rose 113% at the control site. Relative to the control site, this is an 82% (75%–89%) reduction. The authors noted that fatalities were not eliminated. On 26 August 2022 the same authors reported in Ecological Solutions and Evidence that, with additional data from the same site, eagle fatalities fell by 85% (95% highest density interval 12% to 100%). Duerr et al. (PLoS One, 26 January 2023) assessed an IdentiFlight installation in California. The system correctly identified 77% of eagles and 85% of non-eagles. It issued 5,439 virtual curtailment orders for non-eagle targets against 850 for eagles. These orders did not slow the turbines. Huso and Dalthorp (Journal of Applied Ecology, 30 August 2023) reanalyzed the Wyoming data and reported four major errors. They reported that the corrected data give 50% (−159%, 89%), consistent with no effect. McClure et al. replied the same day that their data support the efficacy of automated curtailment. In its final eagle permit rule of 12 February 2024, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service answered comments that asked for IdentiFlight in permit conditions. The Service said that permittees may use such technologies but that their efficacy has not been studied enough to require them.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

The camera system classified flying objects and could order individual turbines to curtail when it identified an approaching eagle risk.

Workflow

Camera towers covering turbine airspace with per-turbine curtailment control. Carcass searches corrected for detection probability at treatment and control sites. A single post-treatment year at one site, which the reanalysis says limits causal inference.

Benefits

Reach

The sources give no count of eagles spared. They report the change in eagle fatality rates at one Wyoming wind farm relative to a control site 15 km away. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Duration

The 2021 estimate covers a single post-treatment year at one site. In 2022 the same authors reported an 85% reduction with additional data from the same site. On 30 August 2023 Huso and Dalthorp reported that the corrected Wyoming data are consistent with no effect. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Eagle abundance and flight behavior comparable to the Wyoming site. Operators willing to accept curtailment losses, including misidentified non-eagle targets. Longer, replicated monitoring to resolve the disputed effect size.
