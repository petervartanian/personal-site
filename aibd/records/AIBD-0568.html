AI summaries of planning consultations save council officers time

West Oxfordshire and Cotswold councils report that AI tagging and summaries in Konveio and Go Vocal cut planning officers' repetitive administrative time by an estimated 85%.

  • 1 an organization

    The councils report that the tagging and summary tools cut repetitive administrative time and that they made the tools available to all departments.

  • 1 a person

    Planning officers spend less time processing consultation responses by hand. The earlier rise in public participation came from digital engagement before these AI tools.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
Not isolated. Machine learning tags comments and generative AI summarizes individual or grouped responses. The councils report less repetitive administration. Earlier growth in public participation came from digital-engagement platforms and is not established as an AI effect.
Limits
The estimate concerns a subset of tasks and does not cover the entire planning process or budget. The 85 percent figure compares one West Oxfordshire consultation with one earlier Cotswold consultation, and the sources give no sample or timing method. No source assesses whether the summaries preserve minority views and important objections. More consultation responses came from the earlier engagement platform and cannot all be attributed to the AI analysis tools.

What happened

West Oxfordshire District Council and Cotswold District Council used £280,000 of PropTech funding from the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government to develop tools with Konveio and Go Vocal that tag and summarize planning-consultation feedback. A case study of 12 August 2024 on the ministry's Local Digital site compares a West Oxfordshire consultation of August to October 2023 with a Cotswold consultation of February 2022. It reports that the AI tools could save an average of up to 85 percent of officer time. After the pilot the tools were made available to all departments, and Konveio and Go Vocal added them to their standard products. In a Local Government Association case study of 19 February 2025, the councils report an estimated 85% reduction in repetitive planning-administration time and say that teams are actively using the systems. They add that full integration into routine work may take time.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

OpenAI GPT model integrated into Konveio (version not stated)

Role

The tools group consultation comments and draft summaries for planning officers. [1]

Workflow

The councils used PropTech funding and their existing partnership to develop tools with two vendors. Officers collaborated in defining needs, and teams were actively using the systems after the pilot.

Benefits

Duration

The Local Digital case study of 12 August 2024 says that the tools were available to all departments and well used. The Local Government Association account of 19 February 2025 says that teams were still using them. Neither source repeats the time measurement after the pilot. [1] [2]

Measurement

Measurement

Officer time to summarize and report on consultation feedback, compared between two consultations. [2]

Setting and sample

One West Oxfordshire Local Plan consultation (August to October 2023) against one Cotswold consultation (February 2022). [2]

Measures

Planning officer time per consultation spent summarizing and reporting feedback. [2]

Results

Average saving of up to 85 per cent of officer time. [2]

Limitations

The two consultations were held by different councils at different times; the case study says that savings would vary with the type and volume of responses. Raw hours and the method of timing are not published. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The source states that AI use was permitted case by case and prohibited for personal data. The conditions needed to reproduce the estimated time saving are unknown.

Evidence

Original evidence

The Local Government Association case study is the councils' own account. The Local Digital case study of 12 August 2024 is published by the ministry that funded the pilot and does not name its author. Neither source reports an independent evaluation. [1] [2]
