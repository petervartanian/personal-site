Whale Safe ratings support ship slow-downs; cooperation 59–85%

Launched 17 Sep 2020 by UCSB's Benioff lab; grades ships' cooperation with NOAA's 10-knot zones. KCBX: vessels slowed 59% of the time in 2023; BWBS 2024 report: 85% cooperation. No vessel outcome.

ⓘ

What happened

The Benioff Ocean Initiative (now Benioff Ocean Science Laboratory) at UC Santa Barbara built Whale Safe. Partners were Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, NTNU, UC Santa Cruz, the University of Washington, NOAA's Southwest Fisheries Science Center and Conserve.io. It was launched on 17 September 2020 (Mongabay, 23 September 2020). Its methodology page describes a moored acoustic buoy whose “tiny computer” detects and classifies blue, humpback and fin whale calls. Next, “An analyst then reviews these data and indicates for each 15-minute period whether each of the monitored species was detected”. It also describes naturalist sightings via the Whale Alert and Spotter Pro apps, NOAA aerial surveys and the WhaleWatch 2.0 blue whale habitat model. These feed a daily Whale Presence Rating.

AIS tracks are used to grade each vessel's “cooperation rate” with NOAA's voluntary 10-knot speed reduction zone. UC Santa Barbara's news office (21 September 2022) called the buoy system “AI-powered”. It quoted director Douglas McCauley: “Every year since the original launch of Whale Safe we have seen an increase in ship cooperation rates”. KCBX (11 April 2024) reported from Whale Safe data: “in 2023, large vessels only slowed down 59% of the time in designated slow speed zones”. The Santa Barbara Independent (16 July 2026) reported on a 2024 Protecting Blue Whales and Blue Skies (BWBS) report. It gave “an 85 percent cooperation rate overall by fleets” and a 50 percent reduction in fatal strikes. The BWBS site states 2025 participating vessels “posed 40% less risk to whales when compared to the baseline of transits in the area in 2016”. Nazario et al.

(Cambridge Prisms: Coastal Futures, 2026) write that the initiative “has successfully helped improve voluntary vessel speed reduction cooperation”, citing whalesafe.com. The cooperation and risk figures belong to NOAA's and BWBS's voluntary program, which predates Whale Safe (2007 onward per UCSB). No cited source isolates Whale Safe's effect on any vessel. Cascadia's John Calambokidis told Mongabay in 2020 that voluntary slow-downs “haven't been very effective”. A separate record concerns a different system (LFDCS gliders and a NOAA Right Whale Slow Zone).

Purpose. Built to tell ship operators, in near real time, when whales are likely present in the shipping lanes. It also grades their cooperation with voluntary 10-knot zones so they slow down. The published ratings and report cards are that intended use.

AI’s contribution

The automated detector on the buoy flags and classifies candidate whale calls, which an analyst confirms before they enter the daily rating. The habitat model is a statistical ensemble and the report cards are AIS arithmetic. The cooperation and risk figures belong to NOAA's and BWBS's voluntary slow-down program, which Whale Safe monitors and publicizes. No cited source separates Whale Safe's contribution from incentives, outreach and retailer pressure. The developer's own news office is the source of the claim that cooperation rose every year since launch.

Since then
Two years after the Santa Barbara Channel launch, UC Santa Barbara announced a second Whale Safe system for the San Francisco Bay area with The Marine Mammal Center, and by July 2026 the Santa Barbara Independent reported a record 60 shipping lines in the slow-down program.We are excited to partner with The Marine Mammal Center to bring the next Whale Safe system to the Bay Area — another hotspot for whale-ship collisions,” McCauley said. source

Further evidence

Setting. A moored passive acoustic buoy with automated detection and classification (Baumgartner et al. 2019 system) plus analyst review. Sightings from naturalists and NOAA aerial surveys, a daily blue whale habitat model, and AIS tracks processed by Global Fishing Watch. An existing NOAA voluntary speed-reduction zone with BWBS incentives for shipping lines.

Conditions. The acoustic buoy is online (the site reported the Santa Barbara system offline on 20 October 2026) and whales vocalize within range. Operators choose to slow in a voluntary zone. Cooperation remains incentive-driven and uneven.
