Whale Safe alerts ship operators to whales in the Santa Barbara Channel

Whale Safe, launched on 17 September 2020, uses automated whale-call detection and other data to rate whale presence daily and to grade ships' cooperation with voluntary 10-knot zones.

  • Not rated an organization

    Shipping lines' cooperation figures (59% of the time in 2023, per KCBX, and 85 percent fleet cooperation in a 2024 BWBS report) cover the whole voluntary program. No source attributes a change in any operator's behavior to Whale Safe's ratings or report cards.

  • Not rated society

    The 50 percent reduction in fatal strikes in the 2024 report and the 40% lower risk in 2025 against a 2016 baseline are modeled estimates by BWBS for participating vessels. Neither figure counts whales saved, and no source attributes either to Whale Safe.

The system’s part
The automated detector on the buoy flags and classifies candidate whale calls, which an analyst confirms before they enter the daily rating. The habitat model is a statistical ensemble and the report cards are AIS arithmetic. The cooperation and risk figures belong to NOAA's and BWBS's voluntary slow-down program, which Whale Safe monitors and publicizes. No cited source separates Whale Safe's contribution from incentives, outreach and retailer pressure. The developer's own news office is the source of the claim that cooperation rose every year since launch.
Limits
Later cooperation percentages refer to different years and vessel groups in a broader voluntary program, not an isolated Whale Safe effect. Slower transits and modeled strike-risk reductions are not direct counts of whales saved. The retrieved launch account cannot verify the later numerical outcome claims.

On 26 October 2026 the Whale Safe site showed the Santa Barbara and San Francisco acoustic systems as offline, so the AI call detector was not feeding the daily rating.

What happened

The Benioff Ocean Initiative (now the Benioff Ocean Science Laboratory) at UC Santa Barbara built Whale Safe. Its partners were Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, the Norwegian University of Science and Technology, UC Santa Cruz, the University of Washington, the Southwest Fisheries Science Center of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) and Conserve.io. Whale Safe supports NOAA's voluntary 10-knot speed reduction zone in the Santa Barbara Channel. That voluntary program predates Whale Safe (2007 onward, according to UC Santa Barbara). Whale Safe was launched on 17 September 2020 (Mongabay, 23 September 2020). Cascadia's John Calambokidis told Mongabay in 2020 that voluntary slow-downs “haven't been very effective”. Whale Safe's methodology page describes a moored acoustic buoy whose “tiny computer” detects and classifies blue, humpback and fin whale calls. Next, “An analyst then reviews these data and indicates for each 15-minute period whether each of the monitored species was detected”. The page also describes naturalist sightings through the Whale Alert and Spotter Pro apps, NOAA aerial surveys and the WhaleWatch 2.0 blue whale habitat model. These feed a daily Whale Presence Rating. Ship-tracking data from the Automatic Identification System (AIS) are used to grade each vessel's “cooperation rate” with the voluntary zone. UC Santa Barbara's news office (21 September 2022) called the buoy system “AI-powered”. It quoted director Douglas McCauley: “Every year since the original launch of Whale Safe we have seen an increase in ship cooperation rates”. KCBX (11 April 2024) reported from Whale Safe data: “in 2023, large vessels only slowed down 59% of the time in designated slow speed zones”. The Santa Barbara Independent (16 July 2026) reported on a 2024 report by Protecting Blue Whales and Blue Skies (BWBS). It gave “an 85 percent cooperation rate overall by fleets” and a 50 percent reduction in fatal strikes. The BWBS site states that participating vessels in the 2025 season “posed 40% less risk to whales when compared to the baseline of transits in the area in 2016”. On 21 May 2026 the same laboratory launched an AI thermal-camera system in San Francisco Bay. Nazario et al. (Cambridge Prisms: Coastal Futures, 24 June 2026) write that the initiative “has successfully helped improve voluntary vessel speed reduction cooperation”, citing whalesafe.com. The authors include Whale Safe's director Douglas McCauley and staff of its partner institutions, so their statement that the initiative helped improve cooperation comes from the system's makers. The cooperation and risk figures belong to the voluntary program of NOAA and BWBS. No cited source isolates Whale Safe's effect on any vessel. A separate record concerns a different system (the low-frequency detection and classification system, LFDCS, on gliders and a NOAA Right Whale Slow Zone). On 26 October 2026 the Whale Safe site listed the Santa Barbara acoustic system as offline.

Details

AI system

Role

The detector identified possible whale calls for an analyst to confirm.

Configuration

The deployment used WhaleWatch 2.0.

Autonomy

Ship operators chose whether to slow down in the voluntary zone; Whale Safe supplied information and speed report cards.

Workflow

A buoy classified possible whale calls, an analyst reviewed detections, and these results joined sightings and habitat forecasts in a daily whale-presence rating.

Human oversight

An analyst reviews the buoy's automated detections and records for each 15-minute period whether each monitored species was detected. Only confirmed detections enter the daily Whale Presence Rating, and ship operators decide whether to slow down. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Benefits

Duration

Whale Safe launched in the Santa Barbara Channel on 17 September 2020, and UC Santa Barbara added a system for the San Francisco Bay area in September 2022. By July 2026 the Santa Barbara Independent reported a record 60 shipping lines in the slow-down program. On 26 October 2026 the Whale Safe site listed the Santa Barbara and San Francisco acoustic systems as offline. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The acoustic buoy is online (the site reported the Santa Barbara system offline on 20 October 2026) and whales vocalize within range. Operators choose to slow in a voluntary zone. Cooperation remains incentive-driven and uneven.

Whale Safe grades ship cooperation with 10-knot slowdowns

Sector

Marine conservation / shipping. Endangered-species vessel-strike mitigation.

Purpose

Built to tell ship operators, in near real time, when whales are likely present in the shipping lanes. It also grades their cooperation with voluntary 10-knot zones so they slow down. The published ratings and report cards are that intended use.
