Police phone app identifies man wanted since 2012 in Cardiff

What happened

South Wales Police and Gwent Police are the first UK forces with the Operator Initiated Facial Recognition (OIFR) app. It lets an officer photograph a person on a police phone and compare the image with a reference database. It is for when the person refuses or gives false details. It launched in December 2024. More than 500 officers had been trained by July 2025 (Emergency Services Times, 15 July 2025). In one use, an officer in Cardiff identified a 38-year-old man wanted on a European arrest warrant since 2012. The warrant was for drug dealing and assaulting a police officer with a weapon in Poland. He was remanded in custody to Westminster magistrates' court (Wales Online, 14 July 2025). The forces report 50 South Wales searches producing 10 arrests, and six Gwent uses with three matches and one arrest.

They cite National Physical Laboratory testing in which the correct match came top for people in the database (Wales Online). The arrest date is not given.

What it was built to do. Built to confirm the identity of a person an officer suspects is missing, at risk or wanted when they cannot or will not give details. This identification was that use.

The system's part

The app compared a photograph taken on a police phone to the reference database and returned the match that identified the man. Officers then confirmed the warrant and detained him. The force says National Physical Laboratory testing found the correct match returned as the top result for persons in the database. It says the app returns nothing for people not in it. Neither report names the vendor, the algorithm or a match score. Both rest on the force's figures.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. A street encounter in which the person refuses or gives false details. A phone app linked to a reference database that holds the wanted person's image. A warrant record current enough for officers to act on the match.

What must hold again. The app is used after an attempted engagement and only where necessary and proportionate, per the force's policy. The match is confirmed against the warrant before detention.
