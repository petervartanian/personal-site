Delhi Police facial recognition leads to Rs 79.5 lakh recovery

Facial recognition on CCTV named a Chandni Chowk robber; two arrested within about a day and Rs 79.5 lakh of the Rs 80 lakh recovered.

ⓘ

What happened

On the evening of 17 March 2025, cash courier Ajmal Bhai Ganesh, 49, of R K Enterprises was robbed at gunpoint of Rs 80 lakh. It was near his Fatehpuri home, Chandni Chowk, Delhi. Delhi Police's North district analyzed some 500 closed-circuit television (CCTV) cameras' footage. Israeli facial recognition software matched a frame of a man who had followed the victim to a record at Kotwali police station. That gave Mohammad Ali, 21, of Daryaganj. Ali was arrested early on 19 March (the Times of India says Tuesday) and named Samir, 19, arrested in Daryaganj. A raid on Samir's home found Rs 79.5 lakh and a semi-automatic pistol, which police called the entire amount.

Purpose. The software was deployed to identify people in CCTV footage by matching faces against police databases. Naming a robbery suspect from a frame is that function.

AI’s contribution

By DCP (North) Raja Banthia's account, teams took a screengrab of a man seen following the victim. The Israeli software matched it to a prior record from Kotwali police station with a similarity score of 32.09 percent. That gave them Ali's name and his Daryaganj address. A score that low is a lead, not an identification. Police confirmed it by laying a trap and interrogating Ali. The auto-rickshaw trace, the reconnaissance evidence and Samir's arrest came from conventional work. The reports name neither the software nor its vendor.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Ajmal Bhai Ganesh and his employer R K Enterprises recovered Rs 79.5 lakh of the Rs 80 lakh taken at gunpoint. Police described that as the entire amount. A large material loss reversed within two days.

  • an organization

    Delhi Police's North district cleared an armed robbery whose video had gone viral within about 24 hours. Both accused are in judicial custody under the BNS and Arms Act and the pistol recovered.

  • society

    Two men who had surveilled cash couriers and used a firearm were taken off the street. The reports give no evidence of further robberies prevented, so the public benefit is small.

Further evidence

Setting. Dense CCTV coverage (about 500 cameras across five areas) from which a usable frame of the suspect's face could be taken. A police facial recognition database that already held the suspect's photograph from a prior arrest at Kotwali police station.

Conditions. A low-similarity match (32.09 percent) is only a lead and must be confirmed by conventional investigation before arrest. The suspect must already be in the database; a first-time offender would not have been matched.
