Face matching flags a suspect, and Delhi Police recover Rs 79.5 lakh

Israeli facial recognition software, unnamed in the reports, matched a robbery suspect's camera image to a police record; two arrested within about a day and Rs 79.5 lakh of the Rs 80 lakh recovered.

  • Not rated a person

    The recovered Rs 79.5 lakh belonged to the courier's employer, R K Enterprises, and the reports describe no outcome for Ajmal Bhai Ganesh himself.

  • 2 an organization

    R K Enterprises got back Rs 79.5 lakh of the Rs 80 lakh taken at gunpoint, which police described as the entire amount. Delhi Police's North district cleared, within about 24 hours, an armed robbery whose video had gone viral. Both accused are in judicial custody under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita and the Arms Act, and police recovered the pistol.

  • Not rated society

    Two men who had watched cash couriers and used a firearm were arrested. The reports give no evidence that further robberies were prevented, so the extent of any public benefit cannot be established.

The system’s part
By DCP (North) Raja Banthia's account, teams took a screengrab of a man seen following the victim. The Israeli software matched it to a prior record from Kotwali police station with a similarity score of 32.09 percent. That gave them Ali's name and his Daryaganj address. A score that low is a lead, not an identification. Police confirmed it by laying a trap and interrogating Ali. The auto-rickshaw trace, the reconnaissance evidence and Samir's arrest came from conventional work. The reports name neither the software nor its vendor.
Limits
The accounts give no calibrated probability of identification, no error rate for the software and no estimate of the time that the face match saved. No source names the software or its vendor, or says whether Delhi Police's 80 percent rule applies to it. Arrest and police attribution do not by themselves establish guilt or a contribution from AI that a court has confirmed.
Costs and harms
The similarity score of 32.09 percent was a search signal and gives no probability that Ali was the offender. A match that low falls below the 80 percent line that Delhi Police set out in 2022. A linked incident in the AI Incident Database describes a man in India who was reportedly misidentified by facial recognition and allegedly died after torture in police custody.

Nothing later is known. No chargesheet, trial or other development after the 19-20 March 2025 arrest reports was found; the reports say only that both accused were booked.

What happened

In replies under the Right to Information Act dated 25 July 2022, Delhi Police said that it treats face matches below 80 percent similarity as false positives that need corroborative evidence. Those replies do not name the software. On the evening of 17 March 2025, cash courier Ajmal Bhai Ganesh, 49, of R K Enterprises was robbed at gunpoint of Rs 80 lakh near his home in Fatehpuri, Chandni Chowk, Delhi. Delhi Police's North district analyzed footage from some 500 closed-circuit television cameras. Israeli facial recognition software matched a frame of a man who had followed the victim to a record at Kotwali police station, with a similarity of 32.09 percent. The match gave police the name of Mohammad Ali, 21, of Daryaganj. No source says whether the 80 percent rule applies to the Israeli system, and this match falls below that line. Police confirmed Ali's role by arresting and questioning him. He was arrested early on 19 March (the Times of India says Tuesday) and named Samir, 19, who was arrested in Daryaganj. A raid on Samir's home found Rs 79.5 lakh and a semi-automatic pistol, and police called the cash the entire amount.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Police submitted a CCTV screengrab to facial recognition software, which returned a possible match to an earlier police record.

Workflow

Dense CCTV coverage (about 500 cameras across five areas) from which a usable frame of the suspect's face could be taken. A police facial recognition database that already held the suspect's photograph from a prior arrest at Kotwali police station.

Human oversight

Investigators used the 32.09 percent match as a lead and confirmed it by arresting and questioning Ali. They then recovered the cash in a raid on Samir's home. In replies dated 25 July 2022, Delhi Police said that it treats matches below 80 percent similarity as false positives that need corroborative evidence. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

A low-similarity match (32.09 percent) is only a lead and must be confirmed by conventional investigation before arrest. The suspect must already be in the database; a first-time offender would not have been matched.
