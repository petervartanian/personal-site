What happened

Chicago's Department of Innovation and Technology and Department of Public Health, with pro bono data scientists from Allstate Insurance and the Civic Consulting Alliance, built a model that assigns each of the city's 15,000+ food establishments a likelihood of a critical violation from predictors such as previous critical or serious violations, three-day average high temperature, nearby garbage and sanitation complaints, facility type, nearby burglaries, licenses, time since last inspection, length of operation and the inspector assigned. In a double-blind retrodiction, the Department of Public Health inspected 1,637 establishments in its normal order during September and October 2014; 258 (almost 16%) had at least one critical violation, 141 (55%) found in the first month.

Re-ordering the same inspections by the model's probabilities would have found 178 (69%) in the first half of the work, on average 7.5 days earlier, with an additional 37 establishments cited in the first month. The Harvard Ash Center's Data-Smart City Solutions wrote up the pilot on 19 May 2015, which is the date used here because the city page carries no date; the code is open source in R.

What it was built to do. Forecast which Chicago food establishments are most likely to have critical sanitation violations so that inspectors visit them first.

The system's part

Developer and deployer are the same body: the City of Chicago built the model in-house with pro bono partners and the Department of Public Health is the user. The evaluation re-scored inspections after they were completed, so the model did not change any inspection in the pilot. Model type is not stated in the sources.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Open inspection, 311 complaint, business license and weather data on the city's data portal. Fewer than three dozen inspectors for 15,000+ establishments, so ordering matters. A fixed two-month inspection record to re-score.

What must hold again. The predictor set (prior violations, nearby sanitation complaints, facility type, days since last inspection, inspector) remains available. A critical-violation base rate near 15%.
