Chicago's model finds critical violations 7.5 days earlier

Double-blind retrodiction of 1,637 Chicago inspections (Sept-Oct 2014): the model would have found 69% of critical-violation sites in the first half of the work versus 55%, 7.5 days earlier.

ⓘ

What happened

Chicago's Department of Innovation and Technology and Department of Public Health built a model. Pro bono data scientists from Allstate Insurance and the Civic Consulting Alliance helped. It assigns each of the city's 15,000+ food establishments a likelihood of a critical violation. Predictors include previous critical or serious violations, three-day average high temperature, nearby garbage and sanitation complaints and facility type. Others are nearby burglaries, licenses, time since last inspection, length of operation and the inspector assigned. In a double-blind retrodiction, the Department of Public Health inspected 1,637 establishments in its normal order during September and October 2014. Of these, 258 (almost 16%) had at least one critical violation, 141 (55%) found in the first month.

Re-ordering the same inspections by the model's probabilities would have found 178 (69%) in the first half of the work, on average 7.5 days earlier. Another 37 would have been cited in the first month. The Harvard Ash Center's Data-Smart City Solutions wrote up the pilot on 19 May 2015. The city page is undated. The code is open source in R.

Purpose. Forecast which Chicago food establishments are most likely to have critical sanitation violations so that inspectors visit them first.

AI’s contribution

Developer and deployer are the same body. The City of Chicago built the model in-house with pro bono partners and the Department of Public Health is the user. The evaluation re-scored inspections after they were completed, so the model did not change any inspection in the pilot. Model type is not stated in the sources.

Further evidence

Setting. Open inspection, 311 complaint, business license and weather data on the city's data portal. Fewer than three dozen inspectors for 15,000+ establishments, so ordering matters. A fixed two-month inspection record to re-score.

Conditions. The predictor set (prior violations, nearby sanitation complaints, facility type, days since last inspection, inspector) remains available. A critical-violation base rate near 15%.
