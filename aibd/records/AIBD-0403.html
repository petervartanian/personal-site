An Emirati health agency adds a chatbot and sign language to its website

Emirates Health Services runs Ask EHS, an AI chatbot, alongside multilingual and accessibility tools on its website; no source reports a measured change in access or workload.

  • Not rated a person

    The website offers accessibility functions, and no source reports a measured change in use, understanding or health for its users.

  • Not rated an organization

    The submission asserts that the chatbot reduces staff workload. It gives no comparison of workload before and after the launch.

  • Not rated society

    Universal access and lower emissions are intended effects that no source measures.

What happened

Emirates Health Services runs a platform that combines an assistance chatbot called Ask EHS, accessibility tools, multilingual support and digital healthcare services. In its submission to the International Telecommunication Union, current as of June 2025, the agency describes features that are intended to reduce access barriers and administrative work. The submission names Mahmoud Alaa Omran and Amal Karmostaje of Emirates Health Services as contacts. Its partners include UAE Pass, the Telecommunications and Digital Government Regulatory Authority and the Ministry of Health and Prevention. The agency notes that the AI infrastructure is costly to set up and that the platform depends on internet access, so it built a lightweight version for slow connections. The submission does not quantify a change in staff workload, patient access or health. Its claims about carbon emissions and universal coverage describe broader intended effects. The agency's accessibility statement shows that the sign language and speech features were still offered in October 2026. The agency has published no usage or outcome figures.
