Ghana credits Publican AI with over US$300 million in added duties

Ghana's Finance Minister reported that the Publican AI Trade Solution raised assessed customs collections by over US$300 million from January to 17 July 2026, a 17.5 percent uplift.

The system’s part
Not isolated. The Finance Ministry counts the system's contribution as the gap between importers' declared values and the assessed values. The system analyzed about 366,000 declarations, and 24 percent triggered more than one valuation risk indicator for review by customs officers. Officers review its suggested values, and importers who object with documents such as bank records or contracts can have them set aside. No source separates the system's flags from the officers' decisions, and the count is the deployer's own.
Limits
The launch report attributes GH¢15 million in recovered revenue from five companies to the pilot and publishes no recovery ledger. The US$300 million figure counts assessed collections, and no source reports how many suggested values officers set aside. No source separates the system's flags from the officers' decisions or from changes in trade.
Costs and harms
Some importers reported duty assessments rising from about GH¢900,000 to over GH¢2.1 million, and a Member of Parliament called for the system's suspension. A Ghana Revenue Authority official said that officers set the suggested values aside when importers prove their prices.
Later
Scaled up, 9 August 2026. After full implementation from April 2026, GRA said customs collections rose from about GH¢4 billion a month to GH¢6.1 billion in July 2026.

What happened

The OECD AI Incidents Monitor entry dated 16 January 2026 summarizes Ghanaian reports that the Ghana Revenue Authority launched Publican AI. The platform analyzes trade data to identify customs fraud, undervaluation and falsified import documentation. MyJoy’s original launch report attributes GH¢15 million in recovered revenue from five companies to the pilot. The reports publish no recovery ledger. The entry is classified as an AI Incident with the harm type economic harm to property interests. The Ghanaian Times (Kingsley Asare, 19 January 2026) reported remarks by Commissioner-General Dr Anthony Sarpong. He said that the Publican AI system would give 100 percent coverage of assessment. He said that assessments that took about two hours could be completed within five minutes. He said that pilots showed 40–45% revenue gains. He said that rollout would begin at Tema Port on 1 February 2026. That article cites no realized recovery. It describes the vendor only as a Cyprus-based firm. On 7 April 2026 the Member of Parliament for New Juaben South called for the system's suspension, and MyJoyOnline reported that a coalition of traders and freight forwarders demanded the same. Some importers reported duty assessments rising from about GH¢900,000 to over GH¢2.1 million. On 22 April 2026 a welfare officer of the Ghana Union of Traders Association said that some traders were reportedly paying up to 300% more in duties than before. The next day a Customs Laboratory official of the authority said that officers review the system's suggested values and set them aside when importers prove their prices. In the 2026 Mid-Year Fiscal Policy Review of 23 July 2026, Finance Minister Cassiel Ato Forson reported that Publican raised assessed customs collections by over US$300 million from January to 17 July 2026, a 17.5 percent uplift over declared values. On 9 August 2026 MyJoyOnline reported that the Commissioner-General said that monthly customs revenue had averaged about GH¢4 billion before the full implementation of the system and reached GH¢6.1 billion in July 2026.

Details

AI system

Application developer

A Cyprus-based firm, unnamed in the read article

Role

Publican was intended to flag undervalued or misclassified imports so that customs officials could reassess declarations and collect the correct duties.

Workflow

Trade data analytics applied to every import declaration rather than sampled assessments.

Human oversight

A Ghana Revenue Authority official said that officers review the system's suggested values and set them aside when importers prove their prices. About 24 percent of the declarations that the system analyzed triggered more than one valuation risk indicator for review by customs officers. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

AI contribution

The Finance Ministry counts the system's contribution as the gap between importers' declared values and the assessed values. Customs officers review the system's suggested values, and importers who prove their prices can have them set aside.
