Tesla Autopilot stops on I-75 shoulder; drunk driver charged

depth 2 for a person, depth 1 for society

Ouest-France (3 May 2026, citing ABC 7): around 2 a.m. on 24 Apr 2026 police found a 37-year-old woman unconscious in her stopped Tesla on Interstate 75; blood alcohol over twice the limit; charged...

What happened

According to a report by Alexandre Chauvel in Ouest-France on 3 May 2026, which relays the Sarasota station ABC 7, officers were alerted at about 2 a.m. on Friday 24 April 2026 to a car stopped on the emergency lane of Interstate 75 at Sarasota, Florida, and found a 37-year-old woman unconscious at the wheel of a Tesla. Local police, quoted without naming the agency or officer, said she should not have been driving, that her blood alcohol was more than twice the legal limit and that she had assumed the Tesla would bring her home safely on Autopilot. The article explains that cabin sensors check that the driver is looking ahead and warn them, and that if the driver does not reopen their eyes after the first warning the vehicle stops. She was arrested and is being prosecuted for driving under the influence. The Tesla model is not stated. The article was read through the Wayback Machine because the publisher blocks automated fetching; no police report or the ABC 7 story was fetched. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor lists the event (2026-05-03-516b) as a protective response with French-language sources.

What it was built to do. Autopilot's driver-attention monitoring is built to warn an inattentive driver and bring the car to a stop if the driver does not respond; it is not designed to drive an impaired person home.

The system's part

Per the report, the attention-monitoring logic issued warnings and then stopped the vehicle on the emergency lane; the agency, the vehicle data and the duration of the stop are not given, and the police framed the episode as misuse rather than a rescue.

Replication

What produced it. Autopilot engaged by an impaired driver on a limited-access highway. Cabin camera attention monitoring with escalating warnings. A stop-in-lane or shoulder-stop fallback when warnings go unanswered.

What must hold again. The driver does not respond to warnings. The road has a shoulder or lane where a stopped car is not struck. Police attend before another vehicle collides with the stationary car.
