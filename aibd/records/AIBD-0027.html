Fitbit shows a rising resting heart rate, and doctors find vasculitis

The Fitbit Alta HR app showed a woman in Zurich that her resting heart rate had risen from about 55 to about 75 beats per minute, and the trend led to an early diagnosis of large vessel vasculitis.

  • 4 a person

    A 57-year-old woman received an earlier diagnosis of large vessel vasculitis, which the authors call a potentially severe disease, after a chest CT had been read as normal. Treatment normalized her resting heart rate within ten days and her inflammatory markers within two weeks, and four weeks after treatment began she reported that she was well. This is a serious condition resolved in time.

What happened

A 57-year-old woman in Zurich, Switzerland, noticed in her Fitbit Alta HR application that her resting heart rate had risen persistently from about 55 to about 75 beats per minute over three months, alongside non-specific constitutional symptoms. Two months before she came to University Hospital Zurich, her general practitioner ordered a chest computed tomography (CT) scan that was read as normal, although it showed a mild thickening of the aortic wall. Her general practitioner then treated her for polymyalgia rheumatica, and she was referred to Zurich when her symptoms returned on a lower steroid dose. The elevated resting heart rate led her to seek advice at University Hospital Zurich. Fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography with CT (FDG-PET/CT) showed inflammation of the aorta and both subclavian and axillary arteries, consistent with large vessel vasculitis. Prednisone 50 mg daily rapidly improved her symptoms and normalized her resting heart rate within ten days. Tocilizumab was introduced as a steroid-sparing treatment two weeks later. Inflammatory markers normalized within two weeks. Four weeks after treatment began she reported that she was well, with a normal resting heart rate. A case report appeared in BMJ Case Reports on 28 February 2022, and it does not report the event dates.

Details

AI system

Role

The device calculated resting heart rate over time.

Configuration

The participant used a Fitbit Alta HR.

Inputs and outputs

The app displayed a rise in resting pulse from about 55 to about 75 beats per minute over three months.

Autonomy

The Fitbit recorded a resting-heart-rate trend; it did not issue a diagnostic alert. The patient noticed the change and sought care.

Workflow

The wearer tracks resting heart rate over months with a Fitbit. A systemic inflammatory illness raises resting heart rate persistently. The wearer brings the trend to a clinician who pursues imaging.

Human oversight

Clinicians interpreted the trend alongside symptoms, ordered PET/CT imaging, diagnosed vasculitis, and prescribed treatment.
