What happened

In a case report published in BMJ Case Reports on 28 February 2022 (event dates not stated; publication date used), a 57-year-old woman in Zurich, Switzerland, noticed in her Fitbit Alta HR application that her resting heart rate had risen persistently from about 55 to about 75 beats per minute over three months, alongside non-specific constitutional symptoms. The elevated resting heart rate led her to seek medical advice at University Hospital Zurich. FDG-PET/CT showed inflammation of the aorta and both subclavian and axillary arteries, consistent with large vessel vasculitis. Treatment with prednisone 50 mg daily and tocilizumab produced rapid improvement; her resting heart rate returned to normal within 10 days and inflammatory markers normalized within two weeks.

Not what it was built to do. The Fitbit Alta HR computes resting heart rate for fitness tracking; it was not built to detect vasculitis, so this benefit was not its purpose.

The system's part

The device's resting-heart-rate algorithm produced the trend the patient noticed; there was no alert, and the patient and clinicians drew the inference. The authors credit the elevated resting heart rate with an earlier diagnosis.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. The wearer tracks resting heart rate over months with a Fitbit. A systemic inflammatory illness raises resting heart rate persistently. The wearer brings the trend to a clinician who pursues imaging.

What must hold again. A stable personal baseline exists. The clinician treats the trend as a signal rather than noise. Diagnostic imaging and immunosuppressive therapy are available.
