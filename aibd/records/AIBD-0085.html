The system’s part
The President's manifestation of 8 August 2023 states that the court's precedents office used VitórIA to identify and monitor the 247 appeals by semantic-similarity clustering. The President then judged that the question had strong potential for repetition and proposed that the Plenary find no general repercussion, which it did. The STF's 2026 statement adds that finding so many similar cases did not determine the result. The court's AI advisory unit built the tool with its IT and precedents offices, so the developer and the deployer are the same body. No source measures time saved or accuracy.
Limits
The number of grouped appeals measures an operational output. The court’s account does not quantify time saved or show that AI improved legal correctness, access to justice, or fairness. Judges made the jurisdictional decision, and the published account explicitly says that the associated time gain cannot be calculated.
Costs and harms
Costs and harms in this case remain unassessed. No cited source reports what happened to the 247 grouped appeals after the Plenary found no general repercussion.
Later
Persisted, 28 September 2026. The STF's September 2026 statement reports the tool still in use after Tema 1.261, credited with 19 themes in 2024 and 11 in 2025 after four in 2023 (years only), while saying the figures do not allow computing accuracy or time saved.

What happened

Brazil's Supremo Tribunal Federal (STF) launched VitórIA on 17 May 2023 under Resolution 800/2023. The court's artificial-intelligence advisory unit built it with the information-technology and precedents offices. The STF uses VitorIA, operational since 2023, to compare the texts of incoming appeals. It groups cases that raise similar legal questions for the staff who screen candidates for repercussão geral themes. The court says that the tool does not judge cases. In her manifestation of 8 August 2023 in ARE 1441934, the leading case of Tema 1.261 on municipal teachers' personal pay advantages, President Rosa Weber wrote that the precedents office had used VitórIA to identify and monitor at least 247 similar appeals by semantic-similarity clustering. The theme record shows that the Plenary decided unanimously on 22 August 2023 that there is no general repercussion because the question is infraconstitutional. The decision became final on 23 September 2023. No cited source says what then happened to the 247 appeals. On 28 September 2026 the STF described the controversy behind Tema 1.261. It said that VitorIA identified at least 247 appeals with similar legal questions by grouping on content similarity. The STF added that finding so many similar cases did not determine the result. The STF credits VitorIA with four themes in 2023, 19 in 2024 and 11 in 2025. It says that these figures do not refer to decisions taken with AI help and do not allow calculating accuracy or time saved.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

VitorIA compared the content of incoming appeals and grouped those raising similar legal questions for staff review.

Workflow

Electronic appeals in machine-readable text for VitorIA to compare. The STF's 2021 Victor item documents same-day OCR of all electronic appeals since December 2020. No cited source ties that layer to VitorIA, so the dependency is inferred. Textual-similarity clustering by VitorIA proposing groups of similar appeals. Human screening teams and ministers deciding whether to open a theme and how to rule.

Human oversight

Court staff reviewed proposed groups, and the justices decided whether a question warranted general-repercussion treatment and how to rule.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Appeals filed electronically with machine-readable text. A procedural device (repercussão geral themes) that lets one decision govern many appeals. Staff capacity to review proposed clusters.
