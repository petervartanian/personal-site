Hanoi traffic police trace a lost 78-year-old on AI cameras

Hanoi police traced a lost 78-year-old on AI traffic cameras on 15 December 2025; a passer-by found him at 3 a.m. the next day and took him home.

ⓘ

What happened

Mr Đ.V.T., 78, of Thanh Xuân ward, Hanoi, went out to exercise at 3:30 p.m. on 15 December 2025 and did not return. At 8:15 p.m. his family reported him to Thanh Xuân Ward Police. Ward officers worked with the Hanoi Traffic Police Department. Major Nguyễn Tá Khanh's team there searched the AI traffic-camera system in the direction the family gave. It traced him along Nguyễn Tuân street toward Thanh Xuân Park and other streets. VietNamNet and Tiền Phong say the images were posted on social media. At about 3 a.m. on 16 December a passing resident found him in Dịch Vọng, hypoglycemic and close to fainting, and took him home. No source links the finder to the appeal.

Purpose. Hanoi's AI traffic-camera system has 1,837 cameras with AI-controlled signals and was due to enter official operation in December 2025, per VOV. The Traffic Police run it, in VOV's words, “to ensure security and order and support the people” as well as to manage traffic. Tracing a lost resident for a ward police search falls within that stated remit.

AI’s contribution

The system's contribution was the trace. Police searched it in the direction the family gave. It returned images of the man walking along Nguyễn Tuân street toward Thanh Xuân Park and across other streets. That gave the police a route and pictures for a public appeal. A passing resident found him in Dịch Vọng about eleven hours after the report. No source says the finder had seen the appeal or that officers reached him. So the discovery itself is not attributable to the system. The vendor is not named and every account comes from the police.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    A 78-year-old who had been out for nearly twelve hours on a December night was home by about 3 a.m. He was found hypoglycemic and close to fainting. He and his family got him back safely, though the finder was a passer-by rather than the police.

  • an organization

    Thanh Xuân Ward Police and the Traffic Police got a direction of travel and images from the camera trace instead of a blind search. The police present the case as evidence of the new system's value.

Further evidence

Setting. A dense AI camera network along the route the person walked, here 1,837 cameras across Hanoi, with footage searchable by appearance and direction of travel. A family report the same evening giving a starting point and a direction for the camera search. A ward police unit able to hand the search to the Traffic Police's camera-control team the same night.

Conditions. The missing person stays within camera coverage. Here the trace ended at a route, not at the man, and a passer-by made the find. Someone acts on the trace or the appeal. The sources do not establish that link in this case. The vendor and the matching method are undisclosed, so the trace cannot be checked beyond the police account.
