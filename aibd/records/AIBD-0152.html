Hanoi traffic police trace a lost 78-year-old on AI cameras

Hanoi police traced a lost 78-year-old on AI traffic cameras on 15 December 2025; a passer-by found him at 3 a.m. the next day and took him home.

  • 4 a person

    After nearly twelve hours out on a December night, the 78-year-old was found at about 3 a.m. in Dịch Vọng, hypoglycemic and close to fainting, and was taken home. His family said that his health was stable afterward, so a serious harm was averted in time. No source links the find to the police trace.

  • 1 an organization

    The camera trace gave Thanh Xuân Ward Police and the Traffic Police a direction of travel and images for the search. The police present the case as evidence of the new system's value.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

The system’s part
The system's contribution was the trace. Police searched it in the direction the family gave. It returned images of the man walking along Nguyễn Tuân street toward Thanh Xuân Park and across other streets. That gave the police a route and pictures for a public appeal. A passing resident found him in Dịch Vọng about six hours and 45 minutes after the report. No source says the finder had seen the appeal or that officers reached him. So the discovery itself is not attributable to the system. The vendor is not named and every account comes from the police.

What happened

Hanoi's AI traffic-camera network began official operation on 13 December 2025. Mr. Đ.V.T., 78, of Thanh Xuân ward, Hanoi, went out to exercise at 3:30 p.m. on 15 December 2025 and did not return. At 8:15 p.m. his family reported him to Thanh Xuân Ward Police. Ward officers worked with the Hanoi Traffic Police Department. Major Nguyễn Tá Khanh's team there searched the AI traffic-camera system in the direction that the family gave. It traced him along Nguyễn Tuân street toward Thanh Xuân Park and other streets. VietNamNet and Tiền Phong say that the images were posted on social media. At about 3 a.m. on 16 December a passing resident found him in Dịch Vọng, hypoglycemic and close to fainting, and took him home. No source links the finder to the camera trace or to the social-media appeal. Sức khỏe & Đời sống reports that the family said that his health was stable and that they sent thanks to the police and residents. By 29 June 2026 Hanoi had nearly 5,000 AI cameras.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Police searched traffic-camera images using the family's information about his starting point and direction. The results reconstructed part of his route and provided pictures for an appeal.

Workflow

A dense AI camera network along the route the person walked, here 1,837 cameras across Hanoi, with footage searchable by appearance and direction of travel. A family report the same evening giving a starting point and a direction for the camera search. A ward police unit able to hand the search to the Traffic Police's camera-control team the same night.

Human oversight

Thanh Xuân Ward Police worked with the Hanoi Traffic Police Department, where Major Nguyễn Tá Khanh's team searched the camera system in the direction that the family gave. A passing resident found the man and took him home. No source links the finder to the trace or the appeal. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Purpose

Hanoi's AI traffic-camera system has 1,837 cameras with AI-controlled signals and was due to enter official operation in December 2025, per VOV. The Traffic Police run it, in VOV's words, “to ensure security and order and support the people” as well as to manage traffic. Tracing a lost resident for a ward police search falls within that stated remit.
