A chatbot cuts calls and improves routing in South Cambridgeshire

South Cambridgeshire District Council reports that its EBI.AI chatbot cut calls and improved routing; councillors later said that some callers hung up without help.

What happened

South Cambridgeshire District Council deployed an EBI.AI chatbot and call-routing functions to handle routine queries. The council reports that it received fewer calls during the chatbot’s first three months than in the prior year and that calls were classified and routed better. It supplies no figure for the reduction in call volume. The 93% accuracy figure comes from a council test, and no source reports an independent check of it. The comparison of call volumes does not isolate the chatbot’s effect. The council also reports 86–90% approval from thumbs-up and thumbs-down feedback, without a response count or a satisfaction baseline. At a council scrutiny meeting reported on 9 June 2026, the head of transformation said that the system, SAM, had drawn fewer than 10 complaints in a year and 87% positive webchat feedback. One councillor said that some callers, especially older residents, hung up without an answer or a transfer. Councillors also described a dead-deer report that the system treated as fly-tipping. The officer said that the council records hang-ups but cannot tell why callers give up.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Residents ask questions that Sam answers from council information, with some service integrations. [1]

Workflow

The model was configured with council website and policy material, predefined flows, and staff testing. The rollout included chatbot, live chat, and interactive voice response, with further integrations planned.

Human oversight

The system can transfer callers to the contact center, and the council records hang-ups. The council’s account says that staff testing helped find and correct inaccurate answers. The head of transformation said that the council cannot tell why callers give up. [1] [2]
