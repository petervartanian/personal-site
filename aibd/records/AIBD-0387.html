What happened

CAFEIN is a federated learning platform developed at CERN. It uses federated averaging (FedAvg) to train models across locally held datasets, with secure aggregation and privacy protections. In a preprint of 2 October 2024, CERN staff and their co-developers at Politecnico di Milano and the Italian National Research Council describe the platform built for clinical sites in the EU TRUSTroke stroke project. They tested it on a public stroke dataset split across three simulated nodes. They report that federated training with FedAvg performed better than training on local data alone. They describe the platform as ready for deployment in hospitals. In a submission to the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), current as of June 2025, CERN describes the platform as operationally used. Its intended benefit is collaborative model-assisted analysis without centralizing sensitive records. No source quantifies an analytical gain for a partner institution or a resulting patient outcome. No source reports a clinical result or an independent evaluation.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Local data trained models at each institution. The coordinating service combined updates, allowing collaborators to build a shared model without pooling raw records.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

People and places

Dependencies

CERN hosted the coordinating infrastructure, while participating institutions retained and protected their own datasets. The platform exchanged training updates using secure aggregation.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The account does not establish requirements for reproducing the benefit in other settings.

Availability

CERN described the platform as operational and hosted as a service. Its code was not open source, and federated inference was listed as future development.
