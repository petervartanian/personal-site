CA-GPT matches expert stent choices more often than junior physicians

In a preprint on 96 patients, CA-GPT, built on DeepSeek-R1, agreed with expert stent plans more often than junior physicians and ChatGPT-5, which received only extracted measurements.

What happened

An arXiv preprint of 11 December 2025 (Fang et al., last author Yan Li) reports the single-center COMPARE study. The study was retrospective and covered patients treated at Tangdu Hospital between June 2024 and August 2025. It compared CA-GPT, “a novel large model deployed on an AI-OCT system”, with ChatGPT-5 and junior physicians in percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) guided by optical coherence tomography (OCT). The preprint states that the large model layer is built on DeepSeek-R1 with 14 billion parameters. Its Methods describe retrieval from more than 100,000 annotated PCI cases and clinical guidance. The authors thank Vivolight Medtech for interface support and training. Decisions for 96 patients were scored against an expert-derived record. In pre-PCI planning CA-GPT's median agreement was 5 against 3 for ChatGPT-5 and 4 for junior physicians. It “showed superior performance to junior physicians in stent diameter (90.3% vs. 72.2%, P<0.05) and length selection (80.6% vs. 52.8%, P<0.01)”. CA-GPT received raw OCT images through its integrated pipeline, whereas ChatGPT-5 received manually extracted measurements. The comparison therefore combines differences in models, retrieval and input processing. The OECD Monitor pairs this study with a Xinhua report of 13 December 2025 on the “Titian” ablation robot at Shaoxing People's Hospital. That report describes teleoperation and no AI function. Machine Heart (Sina, 16 December 2025) reports that Li Yan's team at Tangdu Hospital and Zhu Rui's team at Shenzhen Tsinghua University Research Institute built CA-GPT. It says that they used open-source DeepSeek with retrieval over a million cardiovascular papers, on a Vivolight OCT platform. That description of the retrieval differs from the preprint's Methods. As of 29 October 2026, no source reports a peer-reviewed version or an independent evaluation.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

CA-GPT large model layer built on open-source DeepSeek-R1 with 14 billion parameters, using retrieval over current guidelines and more than 100,000 annotated PCI cases, on a Vivolight P80 OCT system (per the preprint)

Role

CA-GPT proposed decisions before and after coronary intervention for comparison with expert assessments.

Configuration

CA-GPT combined automated OCT analysis with retrieval, using temperature 0.7 and repetition penalty 1.1 in the reported configuration.

Inputs and outputs

CA-GPT received raw OCT images, while ChatGPT-5 received manually extracted measurements and prompts for short procedural recommendations.

Autonomy

The systems’ proposed decisions were scored retrospectively against expert procedural records, without determining treatment for the 96 patients.

Workflow

A domain model built on DeepSeek with retrieval over cardiovascular literature and guidelines. Deployment on a Vivolight OCT imaging platform with the team's own image-analysis functions. An expert-derived procedural record from one center used as the reference standard. Scoring of decisions on ten pre-specified metrics across pre- and post-PCI phases.

Human oversight

The study was retrospective, and CA-GPT's decisions were not acted on for the 96 patients. Its output was scored against an expert-derived record. No source describes safeguards for clinical use. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Benefits

Reach

The study scored decisions for 96 patients against an expert-derived record. No source reports a benefit for any patient, because the decisions were not acted on. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

The study is a single retrospective comparison of patients treated between June 2024 and August 2025. As of 29 October 2026, no source reports a peer-reviewed version, an external validation or a prospective trial. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Measurement

Measurement

Decisions from CA-GPT, ChatGPT-5 and junior physicians scored for agreement with the procedural record of senior operators on ten pre-specified metrics, five before and five after PCI.

Setting and sample

96 patients with 160 lesions, from 156 consecutive patients who underwent OCT-guided PCI at Tangdu Hospital, Xi'an, between June 2024 and August 2025.

Measures

Agreement score from 0 to 5 per phase and percent agreement per metric.

Results

Median pre-PCI agreement of 5 for CA-GPT against 3 for ChatGPT-5 and 4 for junior physicians; stent diameter agreement 90.3% against 72.2% for junior physicians.

Additional value from AI

CA-GPT against junior physicians: 18.1 percentage points on stent diameter and 27.8 points on stent length; the authors did not test physicians assisted by the system.

Limitations

Single center, retrospective, no clinical outcomes, a reference standard drawn from the same hospital's senior operators, different inputs for CA-GPT and ChatGPT-5, and authors who built the system.

Denominators

None; the decisions were generated after the procedures and not acted on.

Materials for replication

The authors state that all data are in the article and its supplementary files; the preprint offers no code or model weights for CA-GPT.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

OCT image quality and lesion mix comparable to the 96-patient cohort. The expert reference record is accepted as correct. Prospective use with clinicians acting on the output, which the study did not test.

Evidence

Original evidence

The account rests on the authors' arXiv preprint, which Machine Heart's article of 16 December 2025 retells. The OECD Monitor entry pairs the study with an unrelated Xinhua report on the “Titian” ablation robot. DeepSeek's own pages document the base model. The authors give no checkpoint identifier. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-14B is the likely checkpoint, given its 14 billion parameters. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]
