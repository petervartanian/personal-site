An OCT-reading model beats ChatGPT-5 at planning coronary stents

arXiv preprint (11 December 2025): CA-GPT on an AI-OCT system agreed with expert PCI records more than ChatGPT-5 and junior physicians across 96 patients; stent diameter 90.3% vs 72.2% for juniors.

ⓘ

What happened

An arXiv preprint of 11 December 2025 (Fang et al., last author Yan Li) reports the single-center COMPARE study. It compared CA-GPT, “a novel large model deployed on an AI-OCT system”, with ChatGPT-5 and junior physicians. This was in optical coherence tomography (OCT)-guided percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). Decisions for 96 patients were scored against an expert-derived record. In pre-PCI planning CA-GPT's median agreement was 5 against 3 for ChatGPT-5 and 4 for junior physicians. It “showed superior performance to junior physicians in stent diameter (90.3% vs. 72.2%, P<0.05) and length selection (80.6% vs. 52.8%, P<0.01)”. Machine Heart (Sina, 16 December 2025) says Li Yan's team at Tangdu Hospital and Zhu Rui's at Shenzhen Tsinghua University Research Institute built it.

They used open-source DeepSeek with retrieval over a million cardiovascular papers, on a Vivolight OCT platform. The Monitor pairs this study with a Xinhua report (13 December 2025) on the “Titian” ablation robot at Shaoxing People's Hospital. It describes teleoperation and no AI function.

Purpose. CA-GPT was built to interpret intravascular OCT images and generate PCI planning and assessment decisions as expert-level support. The reported agreement with expert records is that purpose.

AI’s contribution

CA-GPT is the system under test: a large model on an AI-OCT platform producing pre- and post-PCI decisions that were scored against an expert record. Its reported advantage over ChatGPT-5 and junior physicians is the authors' own measurement in a preprint that has not been peer reviewed. The decisions were not acted on for the 96 patients.

Further evidence

Setting. A domain model built on DeepSeek with retrieval over cardiovascular literature and guidelines. Deployment on a Vivolight OCT imaging platform with the team's own image-analysis functions. An expert-derived procedural record from one center used as the reference standard. Scoring of decisions on ten pre-specified metrics across pre- and post-PCI phases.

Conditions. OCT image quality and lesion mix comparable to the 96-patient cohort. The expert reference record is accepted as correct. Prospective use with clinicians acting on the output, which the study did not test.
