Egyptian and Saudi partners plan AI to forecast wind on the Red Sea

The University of Sadat City and SAMI Advanced Electronics are developing an AI model to forecast wind on the Red Sea coast and the Gulf of Suez; operational validation is still planned.

What happened

The ITU entry, current as of June 2025, names the Environmental Research Institute of the University of Sadat City in Egypt and SAMI Advanced Electronics in Saudi Arabia as the organizations behind the project. They propose to forecast wind speed and direction with AI for wind farms, grids, and extreme-weather planning. A prototype and comparisons with historical benchmarks were in development. No gain in generation, dispatch, or safety has been quantified. In August 2025 an unrelated team reported that machine-learning models forecast wind at the Gabal El-Zayt farm on the Gulf of Suez with a close fit to historical data. No result from this project had appeared by October 2026.

  2. Peer-reviewed study, by authors with no tie to the system. Context or contrary evidence.

    Nehal Elshaboury and Haytham Elmousalami, Wind speed and power forecasting using Bayesian optimized machine learning models in Gabal Al-Zayt, Egypt, Scientific Reports, 5 August 2025. The authors work at Egypt's Housing and Building National Research Center and the University of Melbourne and have no tie to this project. They show that machine-learning forecasts at a Gulf of Suez wind farm fit historical data closely, and they say nothing about this project's model.

    For the generated models, the 200 MW onshore Gabal El-Zayt wind farm has been chosen as the case study.
