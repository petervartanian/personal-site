Huddersfield's first facial recognition vans: three arrests, six checks

West Yorkshire Police reported three arrests and six Sexual Harm Prevention Order checks in Huddersfield on August 12–14, 2026. Its register records nine alerts and an estimated 12,875 faces seen.

ⓘ

What happened

West Yorkshire Police used live facial recognition in Huddersfield on August 12–14, 2026. The force reported three arrests, involving a domestic stalking suspect, a person wanted on a warrant, and a shoplifting suspect, along with six Sexual Harm Prevention Order checks. Its deployment register records five alerts on August 12 and two on each subsequent day. All nine were recorded as confirmed true alerts. The estimated numbers of faces seen were 5,824, 2,951, and 4,100, totaling 12,875. These are the force’s operational records, not an independent accuracy study. Zero recorded false alerts does not establish that there were no missed matches. The force identifies NEC NeoFace as the matching system and states that trained officers review alerts before intervention. The reported arrests and checks do not establish a conviction or a specific harm that was prevented.

Purpose. LFR vans are deployed to match passers-by against a watchlist of wanted people and suspects so that officers can arrest or check them. The arrests and order checks are that purpose. The Examiner adds that the cameras also serve to find missing people, witnesses and victims.

AI’s contribution

NEC NeoFace compares camera images with a police watchlist and generates possible matches. Trained officers review the alerts and decide whether to act. The nine recorded alerts and resulting interventions are reported by the deployer; no independent evaluation of these deployments was found.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    The force’s own release and deployment register report three arrests and six other interventions over three days. They establish a reported operational result, without independent verification of accuracy or later case outcomes.

  • society

    The force reported detention of three suspects and six checks of order subjects. No specific prevented harm or subsequent conviction is established.

Further evidence

Setting. Two vans on the first deployment day and one on each subsequent day, with trained officers available to review alerts. A watchlist of wanted people, suspects and order subjects built from intelligence. Officers on hand to review alerts and act on them.

Conditions. Watchlist entries are current and lawful. Officers verify each alert before a stop. Non-matching faces are deleted as the force states.
