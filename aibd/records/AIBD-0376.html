Police facial recognition vans in Huddersfield lead to three arrests

West Yorkshire Police reported three arrests and six Sexual Harm Prevention Order checks after NEC NeoFace live facial recognition issued nine alerts in Huddersfield on 12 to 14 August 2026.

What happened

The National Physical Laboratory tested NEC NeoFace V4 for the Metropolitan Police and South Wales Police in 2022 and reported in March 2023. It found that at a face-match threshold of 0.64 the system produced no false positives and had a true recognition rate of 79 percent. West Yorkshire Police records the same threshold of 0.64 for its deployments. The force identifies NEC NeoFace as the matching system and states that trained officers review alerts before intervention. West Yorkshire Police used live facial recognition in Huddersfield from 12 to 14 August 2026. The force reported three arrests, involving a domestic stalking suspect, a person wanted on a warrant and a shoplifting suspect. It also reported six Sexual Harm Prevention Order checks. Its deployment register records five alerts on 12 August and two on each following day. All nine were recorded as confirmed true alerts. The estimated numbers of faces seen were 5,824, 2,951 and 4,100, which total 12,875. These figures come from the force's own operational records, and no source reports an independent study of these deployments. The absence of recorded false alerts does not establish that the system missed no matches. The reported arrests and checks do not establish a conviction or a specific harm that was prevented. The force's register shows that the vans returned to Huddersfield town center on 8, 9 and 10 September 2026, with 16 alerts, all confirmed true. They returned again on 10 October 2026 and recorded no alerts.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Camera images were compared with a police watchlist, and possible biometric matches became alerts for trained officers.

Workflow

Two vans on the first deployment day and one on each subsequent day, with trained officers available to review alerts. A watchlist of wanted people, suspects and order subjects built from intelligence. Officers on hand to review alerts and act on them.

Human oversight

Officers reviewed each alert before intervening and decided whether an arrest or an order-compliance check was warranted.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Watchlist entries are current and lawful. Officers verify each alert before a stop. Non-matching faces are deleted as the force states.
