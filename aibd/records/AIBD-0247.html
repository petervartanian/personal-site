Seibu Railway cameras detect people left in crossings and alert drivers

An AI monitoring system from OKI and Marubeni Network Solutions and a 3D stereo-camera system detect people left in Seibu Railway crossings and light lamps that warn train drivers.

What happened

In April 2021 a man in his 60s who was left inside the Ikebukuro No. 9 crossing was killed by a train. A trial of a crossing anomaly detection system (“踏切異常検知システム”) began in December 2021. Response.jp reported on 11 November 2022 that Seibu Railway would begin full operation of the system on 14 November 2022. The report was based on press releases. It said that the trial had confirmed stable operation. The rollout used two alternative technologies at different crossings. One is an AI monitoring system developed by OKI and Marubeni Network Solutions. It uses skeleton recognition to detect people and objects left in crossings. The other is a 3D image-analysis system from Computer Systems Laboratory that uses stereo-camera depth. When a detection occurs, the special signal lamps that warn drivers of danger operate. Live video of the site is sent to the operations control center. Full operation was announced for three crossings on the Ikebukuro and Shinjuku lines, with two more planned within fiscal 2022. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor lists the deployment as an aggregate benefit. Asahi reported on 16 November 2022 that the AI system covers the Ikebukuro No. 9 and Tokorozawa No. 3 crossings and that the 3D system covers the Igusa No. 2 crossing. According to the transport ministry, the 3D system was the first of its kind in Japan. Yomiuri reported on 19 September 2026 that Seibu had installed AI cameras at seven crossings in all, starting with Ikebukuro No. 9. No source reports a detection event or accident figures for Seibu.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Camera analysis detected people or objects remaining in a crossing, activated warning lamps and sent live video to the control center.

Autonomy

Detection automatically activated stop-signal lamps to warn approaching drivers. Controllers could inspect camera images remotely; the releases do not specify the complete train-braking interface.

Workflow

Camera coverage of smaller pedestrian crossings. Special signal lamps already used to warn drivers. A year-long trial before full operation.

Human oversight

The signal lamp warns the train driver, and live video of the site is sent to the operations control center. [1] [2] [3] [4]

AI contribution

At the Ikebukuro No. 9 crossing, the camera system lights the special signal lamp that warns the driver when a person or bicycle stays three seconds inside the closed crossing. Without it, a person left inside is noticed by the driver's sight or by someone pressing the crossing's emergency button. No source gives detection statistics. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Duration

Full operation began on 14 November 2022. Yomiuri reported on 19 September 2026 that Seibu had installed AI cameras at seven crossings in all. No source reports detections over that period. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Detection remains reliable across weather and lighting. Drivers can stop in time once the lamp operates.
