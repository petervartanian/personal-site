Turkmenistan fixes eight methane leaks after UN satellite alerts

UNEP lists eight methane leaks in Turkmenistan that were mitigated from November 2024 to March 2026 after alerts from its Methane Alert and Response System (MARS), which uses AI.

What happened

UNEP states that its Methane Alert and Response System (MARS) combines satellite observations, scientific review and AI to identify emissions for government and company action. UNEP’s MARS mitigation index lists eight Turkmenistan cases: two in Yelguyi in November 2024, Babarap in June 2025, Gugurtli field in July 2025, Balguyy in August 2025, two in Dauletabad in February 2026, and Lebap Province in March 2026. Each entry has a source identifier and a link to an individual case document. UNEP describes these as mitigations that its notification and monitoring system helped prompt and verify. UNEP's document for case TKM_S_406, updated on 15 April 2026, states that MARS first detected the Lebap pipeline leak on 22 June 2025. It states that MARS estimated 16,000 tonnes of methane from 47 plumes. The document reports that the operator repaired a corroded flowline and that satellites detected no further emissions after 7 March 2026. The Guardian reported on 31 August 2026 that the first Turkmen leak had been fixed in November 2024. It reported that Turkmenistan had stopped eight leaks by repairing corroded pipes, faulty wells and failing flares. The MARS program manager told the Guardian that Turkmenistan's response rate was growing month by month. She added that eight cases did not yet change the overall magnitude of Turkmen emissions. The available aggregate evidence does not isolate the contribution of AI from that of analysts, notifications and physical repairs, and it does not quantify their combined benefit.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Satellite detection and analysis, notification to government or operators, and physical mitigation that UNEP reports as verified.

Human oversight

MARS analysts reviewed satellite evidence and notified governments or operators. Operators then had to locate and repair the physical sources, and MARS monitored subsequent emissions.

Benefits

Reach

No source counts beneficiaries. UNEP lists eight mitigated leak sources in Turkmenistan. The benefit falls on society. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The eight listed mitigations occurred from November 2024 through March 2026. UNEP's case document of 15 April 2026 reports that satellites detected no further emissions at the Lebap site after 7 March 2026. Continued monitoring is needed to establish how long the reductions last. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Operators can identify and address the notified emissions source. Continued monitoring distinguishes durable mitigation from temporary non-detection. Site-level evidence is needed to establish each repair and its effects.
