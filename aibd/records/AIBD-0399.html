What happened

The Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center built ChatZOC, a specialist ophthalmology model for knowledge questions, diagnostic assistance, and teaching. From 14 November to 7 December 2023, the center's team ran a randomized trial of a digital-patient teaching system built on the ChatZOC retrieval framework with 84 medical students. In its submission to the International Telecommunication Union, current as of June 2025, the center reports pilots across regions of China. The center says that the approach can improve access and support clinicians. The submission supplies no measured clinical improvement or individual health outcome. The team reports in npj Digital Medicine (4 August 2025) that students trained with the digital-patient system scored 10.5 points higher in history taking than students trained with real patients. No study by an outside group and no patient outcome from the clinical pilots had appeared by October 2026.

Details

AI system

Model

The 2025 submission identifies Qwen 2.5 as ChatZOC’s current language-model foundation. The referenced 2024 study evaluated an earlier Baichuan-13B implementation.

Reported configuration

ChatZOC language model fine-tuned by the Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center on a Qwen 2.5 open-source base (2025); the 2024 study version used Baichuan-13B

Inputs and outputs

Clinical questions and ophthalmic images supported knowledge retrieval and diagnostic suggestions. The system combined its specialized language component with the Visionome image model.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

Human oversight

Ophthalmologists reviewed generated reports and retained final clinical authority. Expert annotation and output auditing were explicit parts of the design.

Benefits

Reach

The randomized trial involved 84 medical students, some of whom trained with the digital-patient system. No source counts the patients reached by the clinical pilots. [1] [2] [3]

Measurement

Measurement

Study 1: Standardized medical history-taking assessment scores after training. Study 2: Double-masked expert ranking of answers to 300 ophthalmology clinical questions. [3]

Setting and sample

Study 1: 84 fourth-year clinical medicine students at the Zhongshan School of Medicine, Sun Yat-sen University; single center; registered as NCT06229379. Study 2: ChatZOC and 10 other language models, including GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 Turbo; no patients involved. [3]

Measures

Study 1: History-taking assessment score. Study 2: Human ranking score and agreement with scientific consensus. [3]

Results

Study 1: 10.50-point increase (95% CI 4.66 to 16.33, p < 0.001). Study 2: Ranking score 0.60 against 0.48 for the baseline and 0.61 for GPT-4; scientific consensus 84.0 percent against 46.5 percent for the baseline. [3]

Additional value from AI

Study 1: The trial compares training with the LLM digital patient against training without it, so the difference reflects the system as used in the course. Study 2: The comparison isolates the effect of adding the ophthalmic knowledge base to the baseline model. [3]

Limitations

Study 1: Single center, small sample and author team from the developer; the outcome is a test score and does not measure later care of patients. Study 2: A benchmark by the developers without clinical patients; the authors state that real-world use needs further study. [3]

Denominators

Study 1: Training sessions with voice-enabled virtual patients built from de-identified records. [3]

Materials for replication

Study 1: Code for the LLMDP system is posted on GitHub (pangjianyu-sunivers/LLMDP). [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The account does not establish requirements for reproducing the benefit in other settings.

Availability

The submission described regional pilots within a larger research project. The specialized model and patient-derived datasets were not publicly available.

Evidence

Original evidence

All three sources come from the Zhongshan Ophthalmic Center: its submission to the International Telecommunication Union, current as of June 2025, a JAMA Ophthalmology study of 18 July 2024 and an npj Digital Medicine trial of 4 August 2025. No study by an outside group had appeared by October 2026. [1] [2] [3]
