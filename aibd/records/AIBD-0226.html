ZeroEyes alert sends FSU police to a man pointing a stolen Glock

depth 2 for society

At about 2:30 a.m. on 2 May 2026 ZeroEyes flagged a man pointing a gun in FSU's St. Augustine garage; officers found a stolen Glock 19 on Tyler Farmer and charged him, per court documents.

What happened

According to arrest papers cited by WCTV, Florida State University police responded to the St. Augustine parking garage at about 2:30 a.m. on Saturday 2 May 2026 after ZeroEyes gun detection technology detected an individual in black pointing a gun on the bottom level. Camera footage showed Tyler Farmer, 19, brandishing a loaded firearm at four other people; officers found a Glock 19 handgun, stolen from Miller County, Georgia, in his waistband. He was charged with possessing a firearm on school property, unlawful carry, improper display of a firearm and grand theft of a firearm, and bonded out on Sunday. The Tallahassee Democrat, citing court records, says police responded to a 'ZeroEyes camera notification' and that Farmer is not an FSU student. ZeroEyes layers over existing cameras and routes human-verified detections to police.

What it was built to do. Detect visible firearms in existing security-camera feeds, verify with human analysts and alert police within seconds to reduce response times to potential shootings.

The system's part

Court documents (as reported) attribute the police response to a ZeroEyes camera notification; human analysts verify detections before alerts, so the AI's contribution is the initial detection. Both sources are local news citing arrest papers; neither quantifies response time or states whether anyone else had called police.

Replication

What produced it. A firearm visibly brandished within view of a connected security camera. Human verification at the vendor's operations center before dispatch. Campus police able to reach the location while the person was still present.

What must hold again. Camera coverage and image quality sufficient for the detector. The weapon must be visible (concealed guns are not detected). A staffed verification center and a responsive police unit.
