ZeroEyes alert sends police to an armed man in a Florida State garage

On 2 May 2026 ZeroEyes gun detection flagged a man pointing a gun in a Florida State University garage, and police found a stolen Glock 19 on him and charged him, court documents say.

What happened

On 13 February 2026, Undark reported that there is little or no empirical evidence that AI weapons detection systems have prevented shootings in real settings such as schools. According to arrest papers cited by WCTV, Florida State University police responded to the St. Augustine parking garage at about 2:30 a.m. on Saturday 2 May 2026. ZeroEyes gun detection technology had detected an individual in black pointing a gun on the bottom level. ZeroEyes layers over existing cameras and routes human-verified detections to police. Camera footage showed Tyler Farmer, 19, brandishing a loaded firearm at four other people. Officers found a Glock 19 handgun, stolen from Miller County, Georgia, in his waistband. He was charged with possessing a firearm on school property, unlawful carry, improper display of a firearm and grand theft of a firearm. He bonded out on Sunday 3 May 2026. The Tallahassee Democrat, citing court records, says that police responded to a “ZeroEyes camera notification” and that Farmer is not a student at the university. FOX49 reported on 5 May 2026 that the police report credits the ZeroEyes notification with helping officers respond more quickly and know what they faced. The university declined to say when it bought the system or why.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Security-camera footage produced a candidate visible-gun detection and a camera notification that prompted police to attend the garage.

Autonomy

The system raised an alert. Officers conducted the encounter, assessed the weapon possession, and made the arrest.

Workflow

A firearm visibly brandished within view of a connected security camera. Human verification at the vendor's operations center before dispatch. Campus police able to reach the location while the person was still present.

Human oversight

The reported ZeroEyes workflow includes human verification of candidate detections. FSU officers then located the suspect and recovered the handgun.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The four people near the armed man and other garage users were the immediate intended beneficiaries of intervention. The report does not establish that a shooting would otherwise have occurred.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Camera coverage and image quality sufficient for the detector. The weapon must be visible (concealed guns are not detected). A staffed verification center and a responsive police unit.
