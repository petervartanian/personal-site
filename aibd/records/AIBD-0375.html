AI cameras identify 18 teenagers gathering with weapons in Việt Trì

On 13 June 2026 Phú Thọ police saw teenagers preparing weapons to fight in Việt Trì, and the provincial AI camera system identified all 18 faces in about 30 minutes.

  • 2 an organization

    The AI camera system identified all 18 participants for Phú Thọ provincial police within about 30 minutes, per Tuổi Trẻ and VietNamNet. Tiền Phong reports that the case was clarified in just over two hours, with all 18 summoned and admitting the violations.

  • Not rated society

    Residents of Việt Trì were spared a street fight only if one was about to happen. Thanh Niên reports that two groups fought at the Hội Thịnh roundabout that night, and no report says whether the 18 identified by camera took part.

The system’s part
Police found the gathering through camera monitoring together with tips from residents. The facial recognition of the provincial AI camera network then supplied the identities of all 18 in about 30 minutes, per the police account. Officers verified the matches and summoned and questioned the 18. No source names a vendor or an algorithm or gives an accuracy figure, and every source relays the provincial police.
Limits
The police account gives no matching logs, no independent identity verification and no handling of false matches. The summons and the reported admissions do not show that violence was prevented. Thanh Niên reports that two groups fought at the Hội Thịnh roundabout that night, and the reports do not say whether the 10 people charged are among the 18 identified by camera.
Costs and harms
The original police statement also credits public tips and investigative work. The reported 30-minute identification figure should not be treated as proof that AI alone prevented a fight.
Later
Nothing later is known. On 19 June 2026 Phú Thọ police charged 10 people aged 16 to 18 with public disorder over a fight with weapons at the Hội Thịnh roundabout around 23:30 on 13 June. One was detained, and nine were barred from leaving their place of residence. Thanh Niên describes most of the 10 as workers. Tuổi Trẻ and Tiền Phong describe many of the 18 identified by camera as pupils. The reports do not say whether the two groups overlap.

What happened

On the night of Saturday 13 June 2026, Phú Thọ provincial police detected a group of teenagers gathering in Việt Trì ward. Officers were monitoring and mining data from the camera system. Tuổi Trẻ reports that police found the group through camera monitoring and tips from residents. It reported on 14 June that the teenagers were preparing weapons and vehicles to fight and disturb public order. Within about 30 minutes the AI camera system of the provincial command center had accurately identified the faces of all 18 participants. They were aged 14 to 18. They came from Việt Trì, Vân Phú and Nông Trang wards and Phù Ninh and Bình Phú communes (Tuổi Trẻ, VietNamNet). Tiền Phong says that the whole case was clarified in just over two hours. It says that 17 of the 18 were pupils of schools including Vũ Thê Lang High School and Dữu Lâu Middle School. One had dropped out. It says that the provincial police summoned all 18, and that at the police station they admitted the violations. None of the reports of 14 June says whether a fight took place. On 19 June 2026 Phú Thọ police said that two groups had fought with knives, swords, tridents and glass beer bottles at the Hội Thịnh roundabout around 23:30 on 13 June. They charged 10 people aged 16 to 18 with public disorder (Thanh Niên). One was detained, and nine were barred from leaving their place of residence. Thanh Niên describes most of the 10 as workers. The reports do not say whether these 10 are among the 18 identified by camera.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Officers used camera imagery and reported AI identification to connect the observed gathering with named individuals for investigation.

Autonomy

The cameras supported detection and identification. Police decided whom to investigate and handled questioning and subsequent action.

Workflow

A provincial camera network with facial recognition feeding the police command center. Officers watching camera data at night who noticed the gathering. Reference images that let the system put names to 14-to-18-year-olds. Police able to summon the 18 and their families within hours.

Human oversight

Police combined camera evidence with information from residents, then verified identities, summoned the teenagers, and investigated individual conduct.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Camera coverage of the gathering point with usable faces. The subjects are enrolled in the reference database. Police verification of each match before action.
