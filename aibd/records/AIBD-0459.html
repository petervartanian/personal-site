What happened

Radiolytica combines speech recognition, language models and human review to monitor French and Swahili radio. Fondation Hirondelle's 2024 annual report states that a pilot to detect hate speech with AI on community radio in Goma and Bukavu was set up in 2024. In its June 2025 account, the foundation describes a research prototype and planned expansion. On a project page for the 2025 AI Action Summit, the foundation wrote that it monitors community radio stations in the east of the Democratic Republic of the Congo and broadcasts adapted radio content in response to the hate speech that it detects. Faster monitoring and counter-messaging are intended benefits. No source documents a response to a particular broadcast or a measured reduction in harm. The foundation's project sheet of April 2026 sets the goal of extending this monitoring to 100 radio stations in 2026 and reports no measured result.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Radiolytica transcribes French and Swahili radio and uses language models to analyze potentially harmful content.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

Human oversight

Local human reviewers validate the analysis because transcription errors and model hallucinations can misrepresent broadcasts.
