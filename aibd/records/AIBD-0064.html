Brainomix flags Jean Hines's stroke; transferred in 25 minutes

transferred in 25 minutes for successful thrombectomy

AI-supported scan identified Jean Hines's major stroke on arrival; transferred in 25 minutes for successful thrombectomy; good recovery.

What happened

Jean Hines, 83, collapsed at home and was taken to the Royal Berkshire Hospital, Reading, England, on a weekend in August (NHS England's December 2025 statement gives no year; 2025 is inferred). A scan carried out immediately on arrival, supported by the Brainomix AI imaging tool, identified a major stroke needing urgent specialist treatment. Within 25 minutes of reaching the emergency department she was on her way by ambulance to the John Radcliffe Hospital, Oxford, where she had a successful mechanical thrombectomy; she describes a very good physical recovery with no serious disabilities. NHS England states the Brainomix 360 Stroke tool is used in over 70 hospitals and was associated with a 64-minute door-in-door-out reduction at primary stroke centres.

What it was built to do. Brainomix 360 Stroke is built to analyse stroke imaging and support rapid treatment and transfer decisions, including selection for thrombectomy; this is its intended function.

Who benefited

  • -3
    personal

    Rapid transfer enabled thrombectomy for a major stroke and she reports no serious disabilities; the AI's share of this outcome is the time saved, so -3 rather than -4.

  • -2
    organizational

    NHS England reports a 25-minute door-in-door-out for this patient against a 64-minute average reduction at primary stroke centres and a doubling of thrombectomy rates (2.3% to 4.6%) at participating sites.

The system's part

The AI-supported scan read identified the major stroke immediately on arrival, enabling a 25-minute door-in-door-out; the treatment itself (thrombectomy) was delivered by the Oxford team. The stroke would very likely have been diagnosed without AI, so the AI's contribution is the speed of decision and transfer, supported at population level by the Lancet Digital Health study (which has Brainomix co-authors alongside NHS lead authors).

Replication

What produced it

  • AI stroke imaging with results available to the admitting team immediately after the scan
  • A regional transfer pathway from primary stroke centre to a thrombectomy centre

What must hold again

  • Rapid ambulance transfer and an available thrombectomy team
  • Clinicians act on the AI-supported read without waiting for a separate specialist report
