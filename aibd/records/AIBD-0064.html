Brainomix-aided scan finds a stroke; patient transferred in 25 minutes

A scan supported by Brainomix 360 Stroke identified Jean Hines's major stroke on arrival, and she was transferred in 25 minutes for successful thrombectomy and recovered well.

  • 3 a person

    An 83-year-old woman was transferred rapidly for thrombectomy of a major stroke, and she describes a very good physical recovery with no serious disabilities. The sources do not show how much of the speed came from the software.

  • 2 an organization

    NHS England associates the tool with a 64-minute reduction in door-in-door-out time at primary stroke centers. The Lancet Digital Health study reports that thrombectomy rates rose from 2.3% to 4.6% at the 26 hospitals that implemented the software.

The system’s part
The AI-supported scan read identified the major stroke immediately on arrival, enabling a 25-minute door-in-door-out. The treatment itself (thrombectomy) was delivered by the Oxford team. The stroke would very likely have been diagnosed without AI. So the AI's contribution is the speed of decision and transfer. The Lancet Digital Health study (which has Brainomix co-authors alongside NHS lead authors) supports that at population level.
Limits
A transfer within 25 minutes and a good individual recovery do not isolate the contribution of Brainomix 360 Stroke. The 64-minute reduction that NHS England reports comes from a different comparison and does not measure the time saved for this patient. NICE stated in January 2024 that it is unclear how much of the faster access to treatment comes from the software.

What happened

Jean Hines, 83, collapsed at home and was taken to the Royal Berkshire Hospital, Reading, England, on a weekend in August. National Health Service (NHS) England's December 2025 statement gives no year, so 2025 is inferred. A scan carried out immediately on arrival, supported by the Brainomix AI imaging tool, identified a major stroke needing urgent specialist treatment. Within 25 minutes of reaching the emergency department she was on her way by ambulance to the John Radcliffe Hospital, Oxford. There she had a successful mechanical thrombectomy. She describes a very good physical recovery with no serious disabilities. NHS England states the Brainomix 360 Stroke tool is used in over 70 hospitals. It associates the tool with a 64-minute door-in-door-out reduction at primary stroke centers. In January 2024 NICE allowed e-Stroke, the earlier name of the Brainomix software, to be used in the NHS while more evidence is gathered. NICE stated that it is unclear how much of the faster access to treatment comes from the software. In January 2026 researchers at the University of Rzeszow in Poland, with no tie to Brainomix, reported that the software found 83.5% of anterior large vessel occlusions in 109 past transfers, and they estimated a median saving of up to 30 minutes in door-in-door-out time.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Brainomix analyzed the emergency CT images and supported recognition of a major stroke requiring urgent transfer for thrombectomy.

Workflow

AI stroke imaging with results available to the admitting team immediately after the scan. A regional transfer pathway from primary stroke center to a thrombectomy center.

Human oversight

The hospital team assessed Jean Hines and arranged transfer. Specialists at the John Radcliffe Hospital performed the thrombectomy.

Benefits

Reach

One patient, Jean Hines, is the documented beneficiary. At population level, the Lancet Digital Health study reports that thrombectomy rates rose from 2.3% to 4.6% at the 26 hospitals that implemented the software. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

No source follows Jean Hines after the recovery that she describes in NHS England's statement of 2 December 2025. The Lancet Digital Health study of the same date reports the software implemented in 26 NHS hospitals, and NHS England states that it is used in over 70 hospitals. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Brainomix 360 was run on the original scans of past transfers, and two blinded vascular neurologists simulated transfer decisions; workflow times were compared with a center that used the software routinely. [3]

Setting and sample

109 patients transferred for thrombectomy from five low-volume primary stroke centers in Poland over 53 months, and 69 patients at a comprehensive stroke center. [3]

Measures

Sensitivity for anterior large vessel occlusion; estimated door-in-door-out saving; CTA-to-thrombectomy notification time. [3]

Results

Sensitivity 83.5% (95% CI 76.5 to 90.5); estimated median maximal DIDO saving 30 minutes (IQR 4 to 45); notification time 11 minutes with routine AI against 48 minutes at the primary centers. [3]

Limitations

Retrospective design, patients in the 6 to 24 hour window excluded, varying scan protocols, one region of Poland; the time saving is an estimate and the notification comparison is between different kinds of center. [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Rapid ambulance transfer and an available thrombectomy team. Clinicians act on the AI-supported read without waiting for a separate specialist report.

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.
