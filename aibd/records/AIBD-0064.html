Brainomix flags Jean Hines's stroke; transferred in 25 minutes

AI-supported scan identified Jean Hines's major stroke on arrival; transferred in 25 minutes for successful thrombectomy; good recovery.

ⓘ

What happened

Jean Hines, 83, collapsed at home and was taken to the Royal Berkshire Hospital, Reading, England, on a weekend in August. National Health Service (NHS) England's December 2025 statement gives no year, so 2025 is inferred. A scan carried out immediately on arrival, supported by the Brainomix AI imaging tool, identified a major stroke needing urgent specialist treatment. Within 25 minutes of reaching the emergency department she was on her way by ambulance to the John Radcliffe Hospital, Oxford. There she had a successful mechanical thrombectomy. She describes a very good physical recovery with no serious disabilities. NHS England states the Brainomix 360 Stroke tool is used in over 70 hospitals. It associates the tool with a 64-minute door-in-door-out reduction at primary stroke centers.

Purpose. Brainomix 360 Stroke is built to analyze stroke imaging and support rapid treatment and transfer decisions, including selection for thrombectomy. This is its intended function.

AI’s contribution

The AI-supported scan read identified the major stroke immediately on arrival, enabling a 25-minute door-in-door-out. The treatment itself (thrombectomy) was delivered by the Oxford team. The stroke would very likely have been diagnosed without AI. So the AI's contribution is the speed of decision and transfer. The Lancet Digital Health study (which has Brainomix co-authors alongside NHS lead authors) supports that at population level.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Rapid transfer enabled thrombectomy for a major stroke and she reports no serious disabilities. The AI's contribution was the time saved.

  • an organization

    NHS England reports a 25-minute door-in-door-out for this patient against a 64-minute average reduction at primary stroke centers. It also reports a doubling of thrombectomy rates (2.3% to 4.6%) at participating sites.

Since then
Four months after the inferred date of Jean Hines's stroke, the Lancet Digital Health study reported the same software systematically implemented in 26 NHS hospitals and associated with doubled thrombectomy rates.Stroke AI imaging software was associated with increased endovascular thrombectomy rates across the English NHS. These results support the routine use of AI imaging software in the management of patients with stroke. source

Further evidence

Setting. AI stroke imaging with results available to the admitting team immediately after the scan. A regional transfer pathway from primary stroke center to a thrombectomy center.

Conditions. Rapid ambulance transfer and an available thrombectomy team. Clinicians act on the AI-supported read without waiting for a separate specialist report.
