What happened

Jean Hines, 83, collapsed at home and was taken to the Royal Berkshire Hospital, Reading, England, on a weekend in August. National Health Service (NHS) England's December 2025 statement gives no year, so 2025 is inferred. A scan carried out immediately on arrival, supported by the Brainomix AI imaging tool, identified a major stroke needing urgent specialist treatment. Within 25 minutes of reaching the emergency department she was on her way by ambulance to the John Radcliffe Hospital, Oxford. There she had a successful mechanical thrombectomy. She describes a very good physical recovery with no serious disabilities. NHS England states the Brainomix 360 Stroke tool is used in over 70 hospitals. It associates the tool with a 64-minute door-in-door-out reduction at primary stroke centers.

What it was built to do. Brainomix 360 Stroke is built to analyze stroke imaging and support rapid treatment and transfer decisions, including selection for thrombectomy. This is its intended function.

    NHS England (2025).

    A scan carried out immediately on arrival — supported by the Brainomix AI imaging tool — identified a major stroke and the need for urgent specialist treatment.

The system's part

The AI-supported scan read identified the major stroke immediately on arrival, enabling a 25-minute door-in-door-out. The treatment itself (thrombectomy) was delivered by the Oxford team. The stroke would very likely have been diagnosed without AI. So the AI's contribution is the speed of decision and transfer. The Lancet Digital Health study (which has Brainomix co-authors alongside NHS lead authors) supports that at population level.

Replication

What produced it. AI stroke imaging with results available to the admitting team immediately after the scan. A regional transfer pathway from primary stroke center to a thrombectomy center.

What must hold again. Rapid ambulance transfer and an available thrombectomy team. Clinicians act on the AI-supported read without waiting for a separate specialist report.
