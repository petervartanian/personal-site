Houston Audubon uses BirdCast forecasts to time lights-out alerts

Houston Audubon issues Lights Out alerts when BirdCast's model forecasts high migration, and Houston proclaimed Lights Out Nights on 9 April 2021; no Houston collision reduction is documented.

The system’s part
The learned forecast model identifies the nights on which alerts are issued. The proclamation and alert program are documented by the operating partner, not the model's makers. The collision-reduction evidence comes from a separate Chicago study co-authored by BirdCast scientists and is not specific to Houston. The counterfactual for Houston is unproven.
Limits
Houston's alert program and public commitment are documented activities. Their effect on light use and bird collisions in Houston is not measured in the material reviewed. The large collision reduction estimated in Chicago cannot be transferred directly to Houston.

What happened

BirdCast is produced by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology with Colorado State University and now Purdue University and the University of Illinois. It forecasts nocturnal bird migration from a model trained on 23 years of weather-radar bird detections and reanalysis weather data. Houston Audubon states that its staff use the BirdCast forecast maps to track migration predictions. They issue Lights Out Action Alerts when a chance of high migration coincides with weather events. Alerts ask residents and businesses to turn off nonessential lights from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m. during spring and fall migration. On 9 April 2021 the City of Houston proclaimed “Lights Out Nights” to help migrating birds. The Houston Chronicle reported on 19 April 2021 that Mayor Sylvester Turner had proclaimed Lights Out Nights for 19 April to 7 May, the span that Houston Audubon recommended. The paper also reported that the Cornell Lab and Colorado State University share BirdCast maps with Houston Audubon to predict when birds will pass through. In June 2021 a separate 21-year Chicago study co-authored by BirdCast scientists estimated that halving lighted window area cuts collisions 11-fold in spring and 6-fold in fall. On 17 April 2022 Houston-area cities asked businesses and residents to go Lights Out for birds. On 25 August 2023 a statewide Lights Out gathering took place at Texas A&M University. No Houston collision reduction is documented.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

BirdCast used weather conditions and learned migration patterns to forecast nocturnal bird movement. Houston Audubon combined the forecasts with local weather to issue Lights Out alerts.

Workflow

Continental weather-radar archive to train the forecast. A local partner that translates forecasts into alerts. Municipal endorsement of lights-out practice.

Human oversight

Houston Audubon staff chose alert nights, and residents and building managers had to switch off nonessential lights from 11 p.m. To 6 a.m.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Forecast skill on peak nights. Building managers actually act on alerts. Monitoring to measure collisions before and after.
