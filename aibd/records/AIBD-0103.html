BirdCast forecasts underpin Houston's Lights Out Nights

Houston Audubon issues Lights Out alerts from BirdCast forecasts; City of Houston proclaimed Lights Out Nights on 9 April 2021.

ⓘ

What happened

BirdCast is produced by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology with Colorado State University and now Purdue University and the University of Illinois. It forecasts nocturnal bird migration from a model trained on 23 years of weather-radar bird detections and reanalysis weather data. Houston Audubon states its staff use the BirdCast forecast maps to track migration predictions. They issue Lights Out Action Alerts when a chance of high migration coincides with weather events. Alerts ask residents and businesses to turn off nonessential lights from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m. during spring and fall migration. On 9 April 2021 the City of Houston proclaimed “Lights Out Nights” to help migrating birds. No Houston collision reduction is documented. A separate 21-year Chicago study co-authored by BirdCast scientists estimated halving lighted window area cuts collisions 11-fold in spring and 6-fold in fall.

Purpose. Forecast nightly bird migration intensity from weather radar and weather models so that cities, building owners and residents can reduce lighting on high-migration nights.

AI’s contribution

The learned forecast model identifies the nights on which alerts are issued. The proclamation and alert program are documented by the operating partner, not the model's makers. The collision-reduction evidence comes from a separate Chicago study co-authored by BirdCast scientists and is not specific to Houston. The counterfactual for Houston is unproven.
