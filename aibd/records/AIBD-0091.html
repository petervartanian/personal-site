UN alert leads an Algerian operator to fix a decades-old methane leak

An alert from the UN Environment Programme's Methane Alert and Response System led an Algerian operator to fix an unlit flare at Hassi Messaoud; satellites show no emissions since 15 October 2024.

What happened

UNEP's Methane Alert and Response System (MARS) combines data from more than 30 satellite instruments with expert analysis and AI models. UNEP's case document on Hassi Messaoud, Algeria, records a Landsat 5 detection of methane at the site dated 1 July 1987. It counts 113 plumes since 1987 and estimates total emissions at 960,000 tonnes. Carbon Mapper's public catalog independently records that NASA's EMIT instrument saw methane plumes at the site on 17 dates from 17 August 2022 to 29 September 2024, at about 2,100 to 3,600 kilograms per hour. The case document attributes the emissions to an unlit flare caused by a passing valve on the flare line. The operator corrected the valve and restored normal operations. In October 2024 UNEP's International Methane Emissions Observatory confirmed mitigation of the source through MARS. UNEP states that no further emissions have been detected at the location since 15 October 2024. The Carbon Mapper catalog lists no later plume at that point. UNEP issued the case document in November 2024, according to the repository record, and the PDF's last-updated field reads 15 October 2024. UN News reported on 23 July 2026 that the Algerian alert led to the repair of a leak that had persisted for decades. On 31 August 2026 The Guardian reported that the leak, which had run for almost 40 years, was stemmed. The Guardian gave no data of its own on the site.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Satellite detection and expert quantification of a large point source, followed by a MARS notification to the government and operator. An operator able to identify a simple equipment fault (passing valve on a flare line) and correct it. Follow-up satellite monitoring confirming cessation.

Human oversight

UNEP communicated the detection, and the operator repaired the faulty valve that supplied an unlit flare.

Benefits

The benefit is a cut in methane emissions, and no source counts beneficiaries. UNEP estimates that the source emitted 960,000 tonnes in total, and no study quantifies the emissions avoided. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Duration

UNEP reports that no further emissions have been detected since 15 October 2024, and Carbon Mapper's catalog lists no plume at the site after 29 September 2024. On 31 August 2026 The Guardian reported that the leak was stemmed, without data of its own on the site. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]
