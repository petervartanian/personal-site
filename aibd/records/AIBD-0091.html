UNEP MARS alert leads to methane leak repair at Hassi Messaoud

MARS alert led an Algerian operator to fix an unlit flare at Hassi Messaoud; satellites show no emissions since 15 Oct 2024.

What happened

UNEP's Methane Alert and Response System (MARS), which combines data from more than 30 satellite instruments with expert analysis and AI models, confirmed mitigation of a methane source at Hassi Messaoud, Algeria (case DZA_S_012) in October 2024. UNEP's case document (issued November 2024 per the repository record; the PDF's last-updated field reads 15 October 2024) records a Landsat 5 detection dated 1 July 1987 and attributes the emissions to an unlit flare caused by a passing valve on the flare line. The operator corrected the valve and restored normal operations; UNEP states that no further emissions have been detected at the location since 15 October 2024. UN News reported on 23 July 2026 that the Algerian alert led to the repair of a leak that had persisted for decades.

What it was built to do. Satellite-based detection and notification of major methane emission events to governments and operators to enable mitigation.

The system's part

UNEP IMEO both developed and operates MARS (developer and deployer are the same body). UNEP's MARS page describes detection using more than 30 satellite instruments coupled with expertise and advanced AI models, the MARS FAQ says deep learning models monitor high-resolution satellite data for further emissions, and the case document's boilerplate says MARS combines 30+ satellite instruments with expert analysis and AI models; none of these states which step in this case was AI-driven, and the repair was carried out by the operator. The two case-document items are treated as a public body's official reports rather than vendor material.

Replication

What produced it

  • Satellite detection and expert quantification of a large point source, followed by a MARS notification to the government and operator
  • An operator able to identify a simple equipment fault (passing valve on a flare line) and correct it
  • Follow-up satellite monitoring confirming cessation

What must hold again

  • A responsive focal point or operator; UN News reports only 13 per cent of alerts lead to any response
  • Verification depends on later satellite coverage; the Kazakhstan case shows emissions can recur after confirmed mitigation
