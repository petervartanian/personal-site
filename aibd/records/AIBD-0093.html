Togo uses machine learning to send emergency cash to its rural poor

Machine-learning models using satellite imagery and phone metadata set eligibility for Togo's Novissi rural cash; 138,531 beneficiaries were paid from November 2020 to August 2021.

  • 2 a person

    Each of 138,531 rural beneficiaries received five mobile-money installments of 8,170 CFA (women) or 7,000 CFA (men), according to Table 1 of the World Bank study. The developers' randomized trial reports gains in food security, mental health and self-perceived economic status. A beneficiary told NPR that he paid school fees with the money.

  • 2 an organization

    The Government of Togo delivered shock-responsive rural assistance without a social registry. The World Bank documents the model as a reusable case study.

  • 3 society

    The program reached 138,531 rural beneficiaries. The authors of the Nature evaluation report that exclusion errors were 4 to 21 percent lower than with geographic targeting.

The targeting advantage depends on the comparator. The Nature study reports fewer exclusion errors than geographic targeting and more than a hypothetical comprehensive social registry. Costs and harms
People without usable mobile-phone records were excluded by design. The method reduced exclusion compared with available geographic alternatives but performed worse than hypothetical methods using a complete social registry.

What happened

The Government of Togo launched the Novissi emergency cash program on 8 April 2020. On 2 November 2020 it extended the program to rural areas with a machine-learning pipeline built with researchers at the University of California, Berkeley and Innovations for Poverty Action. Satellite imagery produced poverty estimates for every canton to rank the poorest. Within eligible cantons, a model trained on mobile-phone metadata predicted each subscriber's consumption, and the predictions determined who was paid. Beneficiaries registered themselves from their phones by USSD and received mobile-money installments of 8,170 CFA (women) or 7,000 CFA (men). NPR reported on 15 February 2021 that Eric Dossekpli, a farmer in Anfoin Avele, had received four payments and had used them for his children's school fees. In March 2022 the team that built the targeting published its evaluation in Nature. The authors found that phone-based targeting reduced exclusion errors by 4–21% relative to geographic alternatives. In a hypothetical comparison with methods requiring a comprehensive social registry, which Togo did not have, phone-based targeting increased exclusion errors by 9–35%. In September 2023 the World Bank and the Government of Togo reported 138,531 rural beneficiaries from November 2020 to August 2021, funded by GiveDirectly with US$10 million. The same team ran a randomized trial of the first rural phase, published as a working paper in October 2023 and in the Journal of Development Economics in June 2025. The authors report that the transfers improved recipients' food security, mental health and self-perceived economic status. They also report that estimates of those effects from phone data do not match the survey results.

Details

AI system

Model

LightGBM, used for gradient-boosted regression.

Role

The models estimated poverty to rank areas and determine eligibility for payments.

Configuration

Researchers implemented gradient-boosted regression with Microsoft’s LightGBM software.

Inputs and outputs

Satellite images ranked cantons by poverty, and mobile-phone metadata predicted consumption for subscribers within the selected cantons to determine eligibility.

Workflow

Satellite-imagery poverty map ranking cantons. Consumption model trained on mobile-phone metadata with a recent phone survey as ground truth. USSD self-registration and mobile-money payment rails. Government and GiveDirectly funding decision.

Human oversight

Government and funders chose the budget, thresholds, and registration rules. Eligible residents registered by USSD and received payments through mobile money.

Benefits

Reach

The rural extension paid 138,531 beneficiaries from November 2020 through August 2021.

Duration

Payments ran from November 2020 to August 2021. In January 2022 the French Development Agency signed a second €10 million agreement to extend cash transfers to 45,000 new beneficiaries. The report on that agreement does not say whether the phone-data model chose them. The developers' trial paper reports that later phases expanded to the poorest 200 cantons. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Endline phone survey of treatment and control subscribers in May 2021 by enumerators of Togo's national statistics institute (INSEED), compared with welfare predicted from mobile phone data. [5]

Setting and sample

Eligible subscribers in the first phase of the GiveDirectly Novissi program, which covered Togo's 100 poorest cantons, with treatment randomly assigned at the individual level. [5]

Measures

Food security, mental health and self-perceived economic status. [5]

Results

Survey data show gains of 0.06 standard deviations in food security (p = 0.003), 0.07 in mental health (p < 0.001) and 0.04 in self-perceived socioeconomic status (p = 0.074); phone-data estimates did not show similar effects. [5]

Additional value from AI

Not measured: the trial tests the transfers and does not compare the machine-learning targeting with another method. [5]

Limitations

Written by the team that built the targeting; effects on financial health, financial inclusion and health care access were positive but not statistically significant; phone-data impact estimates disagree with survey estimates. [5]

Denominators

Mobile-money transfers paid to registrants chosen by satellite and phone-data targeting. [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Target population owns and uses mobile phones (non-subscribers are excluded by design). Operators provide anonymized call detail records. Recent ground-truth survey available for training. Mobile-money infrastructure in place.

Replication attempts

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.
