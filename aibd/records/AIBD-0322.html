Nuru may have lifted cassava yield from 0.5 kg to 2–5 kg a plant

PlantVillage says that its Nuru app helped a Kenyan farmer select healthy cassava cuttings and that her harvest rose to 2.0–5.0 kg per plant against 0.5 kg; no independent source confirms it.

  • 2 a person

Not isolated. Only the maker's account exists for this case. PlantVillage's post attributes the yield change, in the farmer's words, to Nuru's leaf-scanning diagnosis guiding removal of diseased plants and selection of clean cuttings. The independent evidence concerns the model's accuracy (Mrisho et al. 2020, developer co-authored) and another farmer's self-report in Business Insider, not Arisat. Limits
The developer's profile combines app use with improved seed, planting practice, and market participation. It cannot isolate Nuru's yield effect. Accuracy studies and another farmer's testimonial do not independently verify this woman's harvest figures.

What happened

A PlantVillage (Pennsylvania State University) blog post dated 23 March 2020 profiles Josephine Arisat of Asing'e location, Busia County. The CGIAR Platform for Big Data in Agriculture's project page links it as a “Success story from a cassava farmer in Busia County, Kenya”. She has grown cassava since 1995 and had been using the PlantVillage Nuru smartphone app “for a period of One and a half years”. Organizations “such as SHA and Ugunja Resource Center” introduced it. She is quoted: “Initially, I used to harvest rotten cassava tubers whereas their leaves looked healthy. I never knew that Cassava leaves can showcase diseases”. With Nuru she can scan leaves for cassava mosaic disease, cassava green mite or cassava brown streak disease and get management advice. The post states that before Nuru she “would harvest just 0.5kg per Cassava plant”. Using the app to select healthy cuttings to replant, “she harvests between 2.0–5.0 kg per Cassava plant”. It also records that she switched in 2016 to certified seed (MM06 and Mygeria varieties) and joined an aggregation selling to True Trade in 2019. She recommends one-meter spacing. The post does not separate any of these from the app's effect. Per CGIAR's page, Nuru was tested in Busia County “in partnership with Self Help Africa, a charity helping 28,000 farmers in the cassava value chain”. It says that over 100 lead farmers there received smartphones at the start of 2019. Mrisho et al. (Frontiers in Plant Science, 2020), co-authored by developers at PlantVillage and the International Institute of Tropical Agriculture, measured Nuru's symptom-recognition accuracy. It was 65% in 2020 versus 40–58% for extension agents and 18–31% for farmers, including 50 farmers from Busia County. That paper reports no individual outcomes. Business Insider (10 August 2022) independently quotes a Kilifi County farmer, Caroline Dama Kitsao, on Nuru. “The levels of pests and diseases in our farms have reduced, and our crops are more healthy”, she said. It quotes PlantVillage's David Hughes, who claims yield increases “between 130% to 530%”. No peer-reviewed or agency source documents Arisat's case. Separate records cover Nuru's accuracy study and its use by the Food and Agriculture Organization against fall armyworm.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Nuru classified visible symptoms in photographed cassava leaves and returned disease or pest guidance that could inform cutting selection.

Workflow

Arisat reported using the app to select healthy cuttings and remove diseased plants before replanting.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Symptoms are visible on leaves. Mrisho et al. Report accuracy rising when six leaves per plant are scanned. The app's effect is separable from certified seed, spacing and market changes, which the post does not attempt.
