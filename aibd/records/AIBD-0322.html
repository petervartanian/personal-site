What happened

A PlantVillage (Pennsylvania State University) blog post dated 23 March 2020, profiles Josephine Arisat of Asing'e location, Busia County. The CGIAR Platform for Big Data in Agriculture's project page links it as a “Success story from a cassava farmer in Busia County, Kenya”. She has grown cassava since 1995 and had been using the PlantVillage Nuru smartphone app “for a period of One and a half years”. Organizations “such as SHA and Ugunja Resource Center” introduced it. She is quoted: “Initially, I used to harvest rotten cassava tubers whereas their leaves looked healthy. I never knew that Cassava leaves can showcase diseases”. With Nuru she can scan leaves for cassava mosaic disease, cassava green mite or cassava brown streak disease and get management advice. The post states that before Nuru she “would harvest just 0.5kg per Cassava plant”.

Using the app to select healthy cuttings to replant, “she harvests between 2.0–5.0 kg per Cassava plant”. It also records that she switched in 2016 to certified seed (MM06 and Mygeria varieties) and joined an aggregation selling to True Trade in 2019. She recommends one-meter spacing. The post does not separate any of these from the app's effect. Per CGIAR's page, Nuru was tested in Busia County “in partnership with Self Help Africa, a charity helping 28,000 farmers in the cassava value chain”. It says over 100 lead farmers there received smartphones at the start of 2019. Mrisho et al. (Frontiers in Plant Science, 2020), co-authored by PlantVillage and IITA developers, measured Nuru's symptom-recognition accuracy. It was 65% in 2020 versus 40–58% for extension agents and 18–31% for farmers, including 50 farmers from Busia County. That paper reports no individual outcomes.

Business Insider (10 August 2022) independently quotes a Kilifi County farmer, Caroline Dama Kitsao, on Nuru. “The levels of pests and diseases in our farms have reduced, and our crops are more healthy”, she said. It quotes PlantVillage's David Hughes claiming yield increases “between 130% to 530%”. No peer-reviewed or agency source documents Arisat's case. Separate records cover Nuru's accuracy study and FAO fall armyworm use.

What it was built to do. Nuru is built to recognize cassava disease and pest symptoms on leaves offline and give management advice. Selecting clean cuttings from diagnosed plants is that intended use.

The system's part

Not assessed. Only the maker's account exists for this case. PlantVillage's post attributes the yield change, in the farmer's words, to Nuru's leaf-scanning diagnosis guiding removal of diseased plants and selection of clean cuttings. The independent evidence concerns the model's accuracy (Mrisho et al. 2020, developer co-authored) and another farmer's self-report in Business Insider, not Arisat. So the AI's share of her outcome cannot be assessed.

Replication

What produced it. A smartphone with Nuru supplied through a lead-farmer scheme (Self Help Africa, Ugunja Resource Center) with training. Use of the diagnosis to uproot diseased plants and select clean cuttings for replanting.

What must hold again. Symptoms are visible on leaves. Mrisho et al. report accuracy rising when six leaves per plant are scanned. The app's effect is separable from certified seed, spacing and market changes, which the post does not attempt.
