Tesla Autopilot brakes and steers clear of a wrong-way driver

Bret Peters's Model S on Autopilot braked and moved right as a wrong-way car came at him on Highway 49 in North Carolina, and TeslaCam recorded the near miss.

  • 2 a person

    By his account and the footage as described, one driver was moved out of a wrong-way car's path at about 60 mph before he reacted. The other driver also swerved onto the median, so whether a collision would have occurred is unknown. No avoided injury or damage can be measured.

What happened

Before dawn on 27 February 2020, by the Daily Mail's date, Bret Peters was driving his 2020 Tesla Model S on Autopilot. He is a father and a former state trooper. He was on Highway 49 near Cabarrus Arena in Concord, Cabarrus County, North Carolina. A car traveling the wrong way at about 60 miles per hour came toward him in his lane. He wrote on Facebook that he did not see it until after Autopilot hit the brakes and started steering away. TeslaCam footage shows the Tesla moving into the right lane while the oncoming car swerves onto the grassy median. There was no collision. FOX 46 Charlotte first reported the near miss on 5 March 2020 from its own interview with Peters, and it aired his footage. He told the station that he drives the route for an hour each way twice a day. The station places the drive early on a Friday, a day later than the Daily Mail's 27 February. On 8 March 2020 CleanTechnica relayed a since-deleted Reddit post and the station's report. On 9 March 2020 Electrek and the Daily Mail described the footage. No police or Tesla statement is reported.

Details

AI system

Role

The driver reported that Autopilot braked and began changing lanes.

Configuration

The report identified a 2020 Tesla Model S, without an installed Autopilot release.

Autonomy

Peters reported that Autopilot braked and steered right before he reacted. The video descriptions show movement into the right lane but do not independently establish which control system initiated it.

Workflow

Autopilot engaged on a divided highway at night, with an adjacent lane the system has registered as empty. An oncoming vehicle entering the Tesla's lane within range of its forward cameras and radar. Automatic braking and a lane change executed before the driver reacts.

Human oversight

Peters was at the wheel with Autopilot engaged. He said that the car had started changing lanes before he could put his foot on the brake. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Bret Peters avoided a collision during the reported encounter. The evidence does not identify an injury that certainly would have occurred without the maneuver.

Reach

The sources describe one driver in the Tesla. They give no count of the people in the wrong-way car. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The benefit was a single near miss before dawn in late February 2020. No police, Tesla or follow-up report is recorded. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

A clear adjacent lane exists; Peters said the car knew nobody was to his right. The oncoming vehicle is detected early enough at a closing speed of around 60 mph plus the Tesla's own. The driver does not override the maneuver.
