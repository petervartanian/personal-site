Tesla Autopilot brakes and steers clear of a wrong-way driver

Bret Peters's Model S on Autopilot braked and moved right as a wrong-way car came at him on Highway 49, NC; TeslaCam recorded the near miss.

ⓘ

What happened

Before dawn on 27 February 2020, Bret Peters, a father and former state trooper, was driving his 2020 Tesla Model S on Autopilot. He was on Highway 49 near Cabarrus Arena in Concord, Cabarrus County, North Carolina. A car traveling the wrong way at about 60 mph came toward him in his lane. He wrote on Facebook that he did not see it until after Autopilot hit the brakes and started steering away. TeslaCam footage, aired by Fox 46 Charlotte and described by Electrek and the Daily Mail, shows the Tesla moving into the right lane. The oncoming car swerves onto the grassy median. There was no collision, and no police or Tesla statement is reported.

Purpose. Autopilot is a driver-assistance system that steers, accelerates and brakes within a lane and includes collision-avoidance features. Braking and moving away from an oncoming vehicle is within that intended use as the driver describes it.

AI’s contribution

By Peters's account Autopilot braked and began a lane change to the right before he had seen the oncoming car. The TeslaCam footage, as described by Electrek and the Daily Mail, shows the Tesla moving right while the wrong-way car veers off-road. The claim rests on the driver's statements and the footage. Neither Tesla nor police is quoted, and the oncoming driver's own swerve means the system's action cannot be shown to have been decisive.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    By his account and the footage as described, one driver was moved out of a wrong-way car's path at about 60 mph before he reacted. The other driver also swerved onto the median, so whether a collision would have occurred is unknown. No avoided injury or damage can be measured.

Further evidence

Setting. Autopilot engaged on a divided highway at night, with an adjacent lane the system has registered as empty. An oncoming vehicle entering the Tesla's lane within range of its forward cameras and radar. Automatic braking and a lane change executed before the driver reacts.

Conditions. A clear adjacent lane exists; Peters said the car knew nobody was to his right. The oncoming vehicle is detected early enough at a closing speed of around 60 mph plus the Tesla's own. The driver does not override the maneuver.
