Cheshire facial recognition match jails county lines dealers

Cheshire Constabulary's facial recognition matched CCTV of an unknown Northwich dealer to Keenan Hughes; he and Vincent Potter were stopped on 17 May 2024 with crack cocaine and jailed on 1 July 2024.

ⓘ

What happened

Cheshire Constabulary's release states that in April 2024 officers in Northwich learned of a phone number used by the “Kyle” county lines gang. They recovered closed-circuit television (CCTV) of an unknown man suspected of using it. The force facial recognition system matched the image to Keenan Hughes of Connah's Quay. On Friday 17 May officers on patrol spotted Hughes on Winnington Street with Vincent Potter. A search found 99 wraps of crack cocaine worth about £990 and a hunting-style knife on Hughes. Potter had two phones, a lock knife and £960. Both pleaded guilty, and on 1 July 2024 Chester Crown Court jailed Hughes (18) for 28 months and Potter (19) for 34 months. PC Dean Butterworth said Hughes was not known locally and the technology quickly identified him as a suspect. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor records the case, listing a harm type of human or fundamental rights.

Purpose. Identify unknown suspects by comparing CCTV images against police custody image databases.

AI’s contribution

The retrospective face match supplied the suspect's identity from CCTV. The arrest arose from a later patrol sighting, and the conviction rested on seized drugs and phone messages. Sources are the deploying force's release and a local newspaper quoting the named officer. The vendor, match score and any false-match safeguards are not stated.

Further evidence

Setting. A usable CCTV still of an unknown suspect. A custody-image database containing the suspect. Officers able to corroborate the match through patrol and search.

Conditions. The suspect must have a prior custody image. Lawful retrospective use of facial recognition. Corroborating evidence beyond the match.
