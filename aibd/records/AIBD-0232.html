Facial recognition identifies a Northwich drug dealer; two men jailed

Cheshire Constabulary's facial recognition system matched camera footage of an unknown Northwich dealer to Keenan Hughes; he and Vincent Potter were stopped on 17 May 2024 and jailed on 1 July 2024.

What happened

Cheshire Constabulary's release states that in April 2024 officers in Northwich learned of a phone number used by the “Kyle” county lines gang. They recovered closed-circuit television (CCTV) footage of an unknown man suspected of using it. The force's facial recognition system matched the image to Keenan Hughes of Connah's Quay. On Friday 17 May 2024 officers on patrol spotted Hughes on Winnington Street with Vincent Potter. A search found 99 wraps of crack cocaine worth about £990 and a hunting-style knife on Hughes. Potter had two phones, a lock knife and £960. Both pleaded guilty. On 1 July 2024 Chester Crown Court jailed Hughes (18) for 28 months and Potter (19) for 34 months. Police Constable Dean Butterworth said that Hughes was not known locally and that the footage and facial recognition helped officers identify him quickly as a suspect. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor records the case under a harm type of human or fundamental rights. The BBC's Local Democracy Reporting Service reported on 10 February 2026 that there were 973 positive identifications using facial recognition across Cheshire in 2025. Cheshire Police described this as a 60% rise on the year before. The Police and Crime Commissioner said that the technology has been used retrospectively in Cheshire and is not used in real time.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

A face from recorded CCTV was compared with police-held images to suggest the identity of a suspect who was not known locally.

Autonomy

Officers treated the match as an investigative lead. Later patrol recognition, searches and seized evidence supported the prosecution.

Workflow

A usable CCTV still of an unknown suspect. A custody-image database containing the suspect. Officers able to corroborate the match through patrol and search.

Human oversight

Officers corroborated the facial recognition match with a patrol sighting and a search that found drugs and knives. The sources state no match score or false-match safeguard. The Police and Crime Commissioner said that the technology is used retrospectively in Cheshire and not in real time. [1] [2] [3] [4]
