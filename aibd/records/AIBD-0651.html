What happened

Viz.ai applied to the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) for a new technology add-on payment for ContaCT, software that reads CT angiograms of the brain and alerts a stroke specialist to a suspected large vessel occlusion. ContaCT became commercially available in October 2018. In the proposed rule, CMS questioned whether the evidence showed that shorter notification times improve outcomes, and the company then submitted more data. In a retrospective test on past scans that supported its FDA authorization, the mean time from scan to specialist notification was 7.32 minutes with ContaCT and 58.72 minutes in usual care. Viz.ai reported that across 4,763 patients in six months of routine use, the median time to notification was 4.31 minutes. Unpublished single-center reviews at hospitals in Colorado, Georgia and Tennessee reported average reductions in door-to-puncture time of 24, 30 and 44 minutes, and a Mount Sinai cohort of 42 thrombectomy patients had lower stroke severity scores after implementation. In the final rule published on 18 September 2020, CMS determined that ContaCT represents a substantial clinical improvement because it shortens time to notification, and it approved add-on payments for fiscal year 2021. CMS based its finding on information that the applicant provided, and the hospital comparisons were small single-site cohorts.

Details

AI system

Workflow

The software analyzed CT angiograms of the brain and alerted specialists in parallel with the radiology read. Specialists received alerts and images through the Viz Hub and Viz View mobile tools.

Measurement

Measurement

Study 1: Hospital records of door-to-puncture times before and after ContaCT implementation, as reported by the applicant. Study 2: Comparison of ContaCT notification times with recorded usual-care notification times on past scans, as cited by CMS. [1]

Setting and sample

Study 1: SkyRidge (32 patients), Wellstar (120 patients), Methodist Le Bonheur (46 patients), Mount Sinai (42 patients). Study 2: Past scans from the study submitted for FDA authorization. [1]

Measures

Study 1: Door-to-puncture time; time to specialist notification. Study 2: Time from CT angiogram to specialist notification. [1]

Results

Study 1: Average door-to-puncture reductions of 24, 30 and 44 minutes; median notification 4.31 minutes in 4,763 patients. Study 2: 7.32 minutes against 58.72 minutes mean. [1]

Additional value from AI

Study 1: Not isolated in the hospital cohorts. Study 2: About 51 minutes earlier notification. [1]

Limitations

Study 1: Small single-site cohorts; data supplied by the applicant; CMS earlier raised confounding concerns. Study 2: Time to notification is a process measure; patient outcomes were not measured in this study. [1]

Denominators

Study 1: Stroke patients with CT angiography at hospitals using ContaCT. Study 2: One analysis per scan. [1]

Materials for replication

Study 1: None public. Study 2: None public. [1]
