Orca AI may have cut high-severity close encounters by 52%

Orca AI and NorthStandard report 52% fewer severe close encounters across 139 vessels of several types, comparing early and late use without an untreated fleet.

  • 2 an organization

    The vessels went from 2.7 to 1.3 high-severity close encounters per vessel per month between the first three months and months 10 to 12, a measured improvement in a navigation safety result for their operators.

What happened

The Maritime Executive reported on 12 March 2024 that NorthStandard helps pay for its members to install and use Orca AI. Trade reports published on 15 and 16 April 2026 describe a study by the protection and indemnity insurer NorthStandard with Orca AI. The reports are MarineLink, Riviera Maritime Media's Martyn Wingrove, and Hellenic Shipping News reproducing Orca AI's release. The study covered a globally operating cohort of 139 vessels of several types fitted with Orca AI's navigation platform. Riviera Maritime Media described the cohort as 139 container vessels. The report states that its data were collected between 2023 and 2025. Across more than 10.8 million nautical miles, the study compared the first three months after installation with months 10 to 12. It reported a 52% reduction in high-severity close encounters, with 22% within the first six months. The reduction was 36% in the North and Baltic Seas and 18% in the seas around China and Japan. Encounters were defined by closest point of approach, time to closest point of approach, interactions under the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea (COLREGs) and traffic density. Orca AI's report page states that such encounters declined from 2.7 per vessel per month early after implementation to 1.3 by months 10 to 12. NorthStandard's head of loss prevention Colin Gillespie and Orca AI's chief executive Yarden Gross are quoted. The design is a before-and-after comparison on the same vessels with no control fleet, and the report is hosted by the vendor. Because NorthStandard subsidizes its members' use of Orca AI, the insurer that co-wrote the 2026 study has a financial stake in the system.

Details

AI system

Role

The platform detected navigational hazards for the ship’s crew to assess.

Configuration

The vessel used Orca AI’s SeaPod system.

Autonomy

The studied deployment supported watchkeeping and crew decisions. The report describes greater assisted autonomy as a future direction.

Workflow

Fleet-wide installation of a sensor-and-software watchkeeping platform. Twelve months of anonymized operational data per vessel. An insurer willing to analyze member-fleet data.

Human oversight

Bridge crews used the platform’s hazard information to make navigational decisions. Fleet managers could also review event recordings and trends for training.

Benefits

Reach

The evaluated cohort contained 139 vessels. That is a count of ships observed, not a count of people protected or collisions prevented.

Duration

The comparison covered each vessel’s first 12 months after installation, using data collected between 2023 and 2025. Months 1–3 were compared with months 10–12.

Measurement

Measurement

Data from Orca AI's own platform on each vessel. Events were classed as high-severity by CPA, TCPA, COLREGs scenario, local traffic density and counterpart vessel behavior. Rates were normalized per vessel per month. [1]

Setting and sample

139 vessels of mixed types in global trade, 139 ship-years and more than 10.8 million nautical miles, data from 2023 to 2025. [1]

Measures

High-severity close encounters per vessel per month. [1]

Results

From about 2.7 to about 1.3 per vessel per month by months 10 to 12, a 52% reduction; 36% in the North and Baltic Seas and 18% in the China and Japan Seas. [1]

Limitations

No control fleet and no confidence intervals. The baseline is the first three months with the system; no period without the system is measured. Crew familiarization, season and route changes are not separated from the system's effect. The vendor measures the outcome with its own sensors, and the co-author insurer subsidizes the system. [1]

Denominators

Each vessel's first 12 months with the SeaPod installed. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Crews keep using the system after the adaptation phase. The close-encounter definition stays constant across the comparison. Routes and traffic density are comparable between periods.
