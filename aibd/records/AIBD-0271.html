GPT-5 proposes oncocytoma first in two retrospective cases

Given slit-lamp photos, ChatGPT (GPT-5) named oncocytoma first in two retrospective cases and gave a wrong leading diagnosis in three exploratory tests; patient care was not affected.

The system’s part
GPT-5 produced the correct primary hypothesis in two retrospective cases when given images and minimal clinical data. The result is a proof of concept reported by non-vendor clinicians. It had no role in the patients' management.
Limits
The original two diagnoses were known before testing. Additional tumor tests expose misclassification, but the small selected sample does not establish sensitivity or specificity. Queries were not repeated, and the study did not compare independent clinician assessments or measure prospective patient benefit.
Costs and harms
The two correct leading diagnoses are a narrow demonstration; all three exploratory examples had incorrect leading diagnoses.
Later
Nothing later is known. The report is a retrospective proof of concept on two histologically confirmed cases; the cited source describes no follow-up and the authors call for larger validation and benchmarking against human experts.

What happened

Sacchi et al. retrospectively analyzed two cases of caruncular oncocytoma. Both tumors had been excised, on 27 January and 26 March 2025, and confirmed histopathologically. On 8 September 2025, months after confirmation, the authors gave ChatGPT (GPT-5, 2025 version) only slit-lamp photographs and a brief summary (age, sex, site). They queried the model once per case and did not repeat runs. It proposed oncocytoma as the primary hypothesis in both cases. Differentials included papilloma, nevus, squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma. Perplexity proposed different leading diagnoses for the two main cases. The analysis had no effect on care. The authors published the report in Frontiers in Ophthalmology on 24 April 2026. They also report three exploratory tests using published images of other tumor types. GPT-5’s leading diagnosis was wrong in all three against the authors’ stated labels. The authors call the study a proof of concept that a general-purpose multimodal model can recognize a rare ocular surface tumor. They note that the small sample limits the finding. The authors declare no competing interests. These selected examples do not establish a rate of diagnostic accuracy. As of 29 October 2026, Europe PMC listed no article that cites the report.

Details

AI system

Model

GPT-5.

Role

GPT-5 proposed diagnoses from images and brief clinical descriptions in a retrospective test.

Configuration

The researchers submitted unedited JPEG slit-lamp images and short clinical summaries to GPT-5 in ChatGPT, once per main case, on September 8, 2025. The checkpoint and settings are unspecified. [1]

Inputs and outputs

Slit-lamp photographs and brief clinical summaries produced a leading diagnosis and alternatives.

Autonomy

The model was tested after histological confirmation and did not influence either patient’s care.

Workflow

Slit-lamp photographs plus a brief clinical summary submitted to a multimodal model.

Human oversight

Histopathology had confirmed both diagnoses months before the authors queried the model. The model's output had no role in either patient's care. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

One standardized prompt per case with slit-lamp photographs plus age, sex and lesion site; the authors recorded whether oncocytoma appeared and its rank among the hypotheses. [1]

Setting and sample

Two patients with caruncular oncocytoma confirmed by histology at the University of Sassari eye clinic, plus three published images of other caruncular tumors. [1]

Measures

Rank of the correct diagnosis among the model's hypotheses. [1]

Results

Oncocytoma ranked first in both cases; the leading diagnosis was wrong for all three published images of other tumor types. [1]

Additional value from AI

Not measured; the authors did not compare the model with clinicians. [1]

Limitations

Two cases, one query per case with no repeated runs, no clinician comparison, and selected images. [1]

Denominators

None; the analysis took place after excision and histology. [1]

Materials for replication

The prompt text and the slit-lamp figures appear in the article. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Prospective testing before any clinical use. Larger samples than two cases.

Evidence

The only source is the authors' own case report, published in Frontiers in Ophthalmology on 24 April 2026. As of 29 October 2026, Europe PMC listed no article that cites the report. [1]
