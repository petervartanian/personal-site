Lime fits Lime Vision sidewalk alerts to 3,500 Seattle scooters

Seattle Times (20 Mar 2026): Lime Vision uses cameras and AI to detect sidewalk riding and emit repeating audible alerts; 3,500 scooters by 1 June 2026, whole fleet by year-end; no outcome data yet.

What happened

Nicholas Deshais of The Seattle Times reported on 20 Mar 2026 (syndicated by The Spokesman-Review, which is the copy read here) that Lime would fit 'Lime Vision', a system of cameras and AI software developed in-house to detect sidewalk riding and emit audible alerts that repeat until the scooter leaves the sidewalk or the trip ends, to about 3,500 vehicles, half its Seattle fleet, by 1 June 2026 and to the whole fleet by year's end, with new and retrofitted scooters arriving from mid-April. Parker Dawson, Lime's regional government relations lead, said the technology had been tested in Seattle in recent months, that Seattle would get it before any other city, and that it coaches riders rather than slowing the scooter; he would not say why the scooters do not slow down on sidewalks as they do under geofencing at Seattle Center. Jon Scholes of the Downtown Seattle Association called sidewalk riding a safety issue. Lime shares data with a Seattle Department of Transportation dashboard. The article gives no sidewalk-riding or complaint figures before or after deployment; the date given is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Lime Vision was built to detect sidewalk riding and deter it through audible alerts.

Replication

What produced it

  • Camera-equipped scooters with on-device sidewalk classification
  • Audible alerts that persist until the rider leaves the sidewalk
  • A city where sidewalk riding is barred and where the operator shares data with the transport department

What must hold again

  • Classification works on Seattle's mixed sidewalk and parking-corral layout
  • Riders respond to alerts rather than ignoring them
