Lime fits Lime Vision sidewalk alerts to 3,500 Seattle scooters

Seattle Times (20 March 2026): Lime Vision uses cameras and AI to detect sidewalk riding and emit repeating audible alerts; 3,500 scooters by 1 June 2026, whole fleet by year-end; no outcome data yet.

ⓘ

What happened

Nicholas Deshais of The Seattle Times reported on 20 March 2026 that Lime would fit “Lime Vision” to its Seattle scooters. The Spokesman-Review syndicated the report. Lime Vision is a system of cameras and AI software developed in-house to detect sidewalk riding. It emits audible alerts that repeat until the scooter leaves the sidewalk or the trip ends. Lime would fit it to about 3,500 vehicles, half its Seattle fleet, by 1 June 2026, and to the whole fleet by year's end. New and retrofitted scooters were to arrive from mid-April. Parker Dawson, Lime's regional government relations lead, said the technology had been tested in Seattle in recent months. He said Seattle would get it before any other city, and that it coaches riders rather than slowing the scooter. He would not say why the scooters do not slow down on sidewalks as they do under geofencing at Seattle Center.

Jon Scholes of the Downtown Seattle Association called sidewalk riding a safety issue. Lime shares data with a Seattle Department of Transportation (SDOT) dashboard. The article gives no sidewalk-riding or complaint figures before or after deployment.

Purpose. Lime Vision was built to detect sidewalk riding and deter it through audible alerts.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. The article describes the intended function. No detection counts, accuracy or behavioral results are reported, and Lime declined to share many details.

Further evidence

Setting. Camera-equipped scooters with on-device sidewalk classification. Audible alerts that persist until the rider leaves the sidewalk. A city where sidewalk riding is barred and where the operator shares data with the transport department.

Conditions. Classification works on Seattle's mixed sidewalk and parking-corral layout. Riders respond to alerts rather than ignoring them.
