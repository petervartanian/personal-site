Lime scooters in Seattle get camera AI that alerts sidewalk riders

Lime Vision, Lime's in-house camera AI, detects sidewalk riding and sounds repeating alerts; Seattle's transport department reported its rollout on 9 June 2026 without outcome data.

What happened

Nicholas Deshais of The Seattle Times reported on 20 March 2026 that Lime would fit “Lime Vision” to its Seattle scooters. The Spokesman-Review syndicated the report. Lime Vision is a system of cameras and AI software that Lime developed in-house to detect sidewalk riding. It emits audible alerts that repeat until the scooter leaves the sidewalk or the trip ends. Lime would fit it to about 3,500 vehicles, half its Seattle fleet, by 1 June 2026, and to the whole fleet by year's end. New and retrofitted scooters were to arrive from mid-April. Parker Dawson, Lime's regional government relations lead, said that the technology had been tested in Seattle in recent months. He said that Seattle would get it before any other city. He said that it coaches riders and does not slow the scooter. He would not say why the scooters do not slow down on sidewalks as they do under geofencing at Seattle Center. Jon Scholes of the Downtown Seattle Association called sidewalk riding a safety issue. Lime shares data with a dashboard of the Seattle Department of Transportation. The article gives no figures on sidewalk riding or complaints before or after deployment. Permits that the department updated on 1 April 2026 require scooter companies to detect sidewalk riding on at least half the standing fleet within the second quarter of 2026 and on all devices by the end of 2026. The department described these permits on 14 May 2026. It said that it could add fines or account suspension once testing confirms that the sensors work as intended. On 9 June 2026 the department wrote that Lime was rolling out AI detection of sidewalk riding with audible alerts. Neither post gives figures on sidewalk riding.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Forward-facing camera images are classified for sidewalk riding. A positive classification produces repeated audible alerts until the rider leaves the sidewalk or ends the trip.

Autonomy

Lime Vision automatically issues warnings, but this deployment does not slow the scooter. Riders decide whether to respond.

Workflow

Camera-equipped scooters with on-device sidewalk classification. Audible alerts that persist until the rider leaves the sidewalk. A city where sidewalk riding is barred and where the operator shares data with the transport department.

Human oversight

Lime shares data with a dashboard of the Seattle Department of Transportation. The department said on 14 May 2026 that it could add fines or account suspension once testing confirms that the sensors work as intended. [1] [2] [3]

AI contribution

Lime's regional government relations lead described how Lime Vision is meant to work, and the Seattle Department of Transportation reported its rollout on 9 June 2026. No source gives detection counts, accuracy or results on rider behavior. The separate contribution of the AI therefore remains unassessed. [1] [2] [3]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Pedestrians, including older downtown residents, are the intended safety beneficiaries. Riders may benefit from clearer guidance, but no behavioral improvement was measured in the report.

Reach

No source reports a beneficiary count. Lime planned to fit about 3,500 scooters, half its Seattle fleet, by 1 June 2026 and the whole fleet by the end of the year. These figures count equipped vehicles, and no source counts pedestrians or riders who benefited. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

On 9 June 2026 the Seattle Department of Transportation wrote that Lime was rolling out the system as part of its permit, which requires detection on all devices by the end of 2026. No source measures an effect on sidewalk riding, so no source shows how long any effect lasted. [1] [2] [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Classification works on Seattle's mixed sidewalk and parking-corral layout. Riders respond to alerts rather than ignoring them.
