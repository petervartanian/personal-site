Locust control fed by eLocust3m may have averted USD 1.56 billion

PlantVillage's eLocust3m app gathered over 240,000 locust records, and AI removed unusable ones for the control operation that FAO credits with averting USD 1.56 billion in losses.

  • 3 society

    FAO states that East African control operations prevented the loss of 4 million tonnes of cereal and 800 million liters of milk. FAO says that they protected the food security of 36.6 million people. The Times quotes FAO's USD 1.5 billion averted since February 2020. These are estimates for the whole operation, and AI data handling was one part of it.

  • 2 an organization

    FAO received up to 2,500 records a day with nearly 25 percent unusable. PlantVillage's AI removed the unwanted data. Over 240,000 records were collected in a year from scouts and citizens who could not have used the discontinued eLocust3 tablets.

The system’s part
Not isolated. Two AI uses are documented: algorithms that double-check submitted photos (Times) and AI that filters unusable records out of FAO's daily intake (FAO). A Penn State deep-learning soil-moisture model is mentioned by the maker's university. The outcome figures belong to the entire control operation and no source isolates the AI's contribution. FAO is both co-developer and deployer, so its feature is maker-authored. The Times report is independent with named sources (Cressman, Ferrand, Hughes, Jeptoo, Owuor, Craig).

The estimated losses avoided belong to the whole response, including scouts, aircraft, spraying, satellite communication, and coordination. They cannot be assigned to the data-cleaning model alone. The model’s error rates and the counterfactual calculation behind the US$1.56 billion estimate were not verified.

What happened

In early 2020 desert locust swarms swept the Horn of Africa. FAO's senior locust forecaster Keith Cressman asked Penn State's PlantVillage to adapt its app. PlantVillage had built FAO's fall armyworm app. eLocust3M was released on Google Play on 20 February 2020. Anyone with a smartphone could record georeferenced locust sightings, and algorithms checked the photos. Penn State also extended a deep-learning soil-moisture model to the affected region. The wider operation also used Garmin satellite devices, EarthRanger (Vulcan) and 28 aircraft coordinated with 51 Degrees. On 8 April 2021 The New York Times reported that PlantVillage scouts, government personnel and citizens had collected over 240,000 records in a year. The Times quoted FAO's estimate of USD 1.5 billion averted since February 2020. It quoted FAO's Cyril Ferrand, who said that swarms were prevented from spilling into the Sahel. FAO's feature of 6 May 2021 says that FAO received up to 2,500 records a day and that nearly 25 percent were unusable or incorrect. FAO asked PlantVillage to use artificial intelligence to identify and remove the unwanted data before it shared locations with ground and air control teams. The feature says that East African control operations prevented the loss of 4 million tonnes of cereal and 800 million liters of milk. It says that they protected the food security of 36.6 million people and avoided USD 1.56 billion in losses. On 15 June 2022 FAO declared the East Africa upsurge over. It said that it had trained 3,800 people across the region to use applications such as eLocust3 to report locusts. In May 2026 Serdo and Nemeth, who have no tie to FAO or PlantVillage, named eLocust3m among the tools of the 2020 to 2021 response in a review in Frontiers in Zoology. They restate FAO's figures without new analysis.

Details

AI system

Role

The software checked submitted photographs and filtered unusable reports before FAO analysis.

Configuration

The deployment used eLocust3M.

Inputs and outputs

ELocust3M gathered georeferenced sightings and photographs. Algorithms checked images and filtered unusable records before FAO passed useful locations to control teams.

Workflow

An upsurge with swarms across Kenya, Ethiopia and Somalia in 2020 and no trained locust trackers in those countries. An existing FAO-PlantVillage app blueprint (fall armyworm) adaptable in under a month. Scouts recruited and trained by PlantVillage and governments. Aerial control assets (28 aircraft) coordinated through EarthRanger with 51 Degrees.

Human oversight

Scouts, government personnel, and citizens submitted observations. FAO specialists and coordinated ground and air teams used the information to organize locust control.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

FAO’s survey and control teams, and people whose food supplies were threatened by locusts.

Reach

FAO estimates that 36.6 million people benefited from the whole control campaign. The number attributable to AI is not established.

Duration

The Times reports FAO's estimate of USD 1.5 billion averted since February 2020. FAO's feature of 6 May 2021 gives no period for its figure of USD 1.56 billion. On 15 June 2022 FAO declared the East Africa upsurge over. It said that it had trained 3,800 people across the region to use applications such as eLocust3 to report locusts. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
