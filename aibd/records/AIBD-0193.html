FAO says locust control fed by eLocust3m averts USD 1.56 billion

protected the food security of 36.6 million people and avoided USD 1.56 billion in losses

PlantVillage's app, built with FAO in under a month in 2020, gathered 240,000+ records; AI removed unusable data; FAO credits the tech-enabled operation with averting USD 1.56 billion in losses.

What happened

When desert locust swarms swept the Horn of Africa in early 2020, FAO's senior locust forecaster Keith Cressman asked Penn State's PlantVillage, which had built FAO's fall armyworm app, to adapt it; eLocust3M was released on Google Play on 20 February 2020 so that anyone with a smartphone could record georeferenced locust sightings, with photos checked by algorithms. Penn State also extended a deep-learning soil-moisture model to the affected region. FAO reported receiving up to 2,500 records a day, nearly 25 percent unusable or incorrect, and turned to PlantVillage to use artificial intelligence to identify and remove the unwanted data before sharing locations with ground and air control teams; the New York Times reported more than 240,000 records collected in a year by PlantVillage scouts, government personnel and citizens. The wider operation also used Garmin satellite devices, EarthRanger (Vulcan) and 28 aircraft coordinated with 51 Degrees. FAO's 6 May 2021 feature says control operations in East Africa prevented the loss of 4 million tonnes of cereal and 800 million liters of milk, protected the food security of 36.6 million people and avoided USD 1.56 billion in losses; the Times quoted FAO's estimate of USD 1.5 billion averted since February 2020 and FAO's Cyril Ferrand saying swarms were prevented from spilling into the Sahel. The date is FAO's feature date.

What it was built to do. Collect georeferenced locust sightings from anyone with a smartphone and automatically check photos and remove unusable records so that FAO can direct survey and control teams.

The system's part

Two AI uses are documented: algorithms that double-check submitted photos (Times) and AI that filters unusable records out of FAO's daily intake (FAO); a Penn State deep-learning soil-moisture model is mentioned by the maker's university. The outcome figures belong to the entire control operation and no source isolates the AI's contribution. FAO is both co-developer and deployer, so its feature is maker-authored; the Times report is independent with named sources (Cressman, Ferrand, Hughes, Jeptoo, Owuor, Craig).

Replication

What produced it

  • An upsurge with swarms across Kenya, Ethiopia and Somalia in 2020 and no trained locust trackers in those countries
  • An existing FAO-PlantVillage app blueprint (fall armyworm) adaptable in under a month
  • Scouts recruited and trained by PlantVillage and governments
  • Aerial control assets (28 aircraft) coordinated through EarthRanger with 51 Degrees

What must hold again

  • Smartphone coverage among scouts, with Garmin satellite devices where there is no network
  • FAO's Desert Locust Information Service acting on records daily
  • Control teams and aircraft available to act on reported locations
