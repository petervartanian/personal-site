Wigan Council reports that Agilisys QuickAction tools saved an estimated 107 staff days on minutes and made 4,500 tenant survey responses usable for analysis.

What happened

Wigan and Agilisys developed QuickAction workflows for care assessments, minutes, audits and analysis of free-text surveys. On 2 January 2025 the council reported that automation halved the time spent on minute-taking and saved an estimated 107 days of staff work. It also reported analysis of 4,500 tenant responses that could not previously be processed in aggregate. The council's account presents reduced sickness, recruitment and overtime as projections. It provides no independent outcome evaluation. In its assessment published on 18 March 2026, the Care Quality Commission found that Wigan had introduced AI tools for note-taking, case audits and resident advice. Staff told the regulator that the note-taking tools helped them focus on people during assessments and reduced administrative burden. The regulator found that case records produced with the AI assessment tool captured detailed and personalized conversations. It gives no time or cost figures. In the same assessment the regulator rated Wigan's adult social care outstanding. Agilisys later reported £72,000 in savings from automated minutes and stated that more than 300 social workers use the tools daily. These figures come from the maker.

  3. Regulatory document, by authors with no tie to the system.

    Care Quality Commission, Wigan Council local authority assessment (March 2026), Assessing needs, 18 March 2026. The regulator reviewed case records produced with the AI assessment tool and found that they captured detailed and personalized conversations.

  4. Regulatory document, by authors with no tie to the system.

    Care Quality Commission, Wigan Council local authority assessment (March 2026), Governance, management and sustainability, 18 March 2026. The regulator describes the AI audit tool and reports what staff and leaders told assessors about it.

    Staff told us this provided detailed insights on their work which had helped them to identify areas of learning or improvement.

  5. Descriptive report, by the makers.

    Agilisys, Wigan Council: AI transforms adult social care, case study page, undated (after March 2026). Agilisys built the tools with the council, so the page is a maker account; it reports realized savings and wider use that the LGA account gave only as estimates or projections.

    The £72,000 in efficiencies breaks down to £59,000 saved by PA teams over 12 months and £13,000 saved by ASC business support staff over three months - from meeting minutes automation alone.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Recordings become draft care documents, while handwritten survey comments become text that staff can analyze. [1]

Workflow

Council staff and Agilisys co-developed several tools around specific adult-social-care administrative tasks. The reported deployments include meeting minutes and analysis of care and tenant feedback, with further refinement during pilots.

Benefits

Reach

Agilisys states that more than 300 social workers use the tools daily. No source counts the residents affected. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

In March 2026 the Care Quality Commission found that the note-taking, audit and chatbot tools were in use. Agilisys later stated that more than 300 social workers use the tools daily. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Regulatory assessment of the council's adult social care, with interviews of staff, leaders, partners and people who use services, and review of case records. [2]

Setting and sample

Wigan Council adult social care, England; assessment published 18 March 2026. [2]

Measures

Staff accounts of administrative burden and of time spent with people; assessors' review of records made with the tools. [2]

Results

Staff told assessors that AI note-taking tools helped them focus on people during assessments and reduced administrative burden. [2]

Limitations

The report gives no time or cost figures and does not name Agilisys or QuickAction. The AI tools were one part of a service that the regulator rated outstanding for many other reasons. [2]

Follow-up

Availability

The administrative tools were being piloted, while the resident chatbot remained under development. [1]
