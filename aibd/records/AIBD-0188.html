What happened

On 2 January 2015 the Southern Nevada Health District (SNHD) began a controlled experiment with nEmesis, a University of Rochester system. It applies a support-vector-machine language model to geotagged tweets to rank Las Vegas food venues by foodborne-illness risk. During the first three months environmental health specialists inspected 142 venues, half chosen by nEmesis, half under the standard protocol. Inspectors were blind to which was which. Adaptive inspections uncovered 9 demerits per inspection versus 6 (p = 0.019). They found 11 venues graded C or worse versus 7 for controls, a 64% improvement. The abstract summarizes this as 63% more effective. Side findings included an unpermitted seafood establishment and a food handler working with an influenza-like illness.

The authors estimate, from CDC outbreak averages, that adaptive inspections saved 71 infections and 4.4 hospitalizations over the three months. They estimate all of Las Vegas's 35,000+ annual inspections made adaptive could prevent over 9,126 cases and 557 hospitalizations a year. SNHD performed 5 foodborne-illness investigations between 2 January and 31 March 2015, against 11 in the same window the year before. The date given is the end of that reported window.

What it was built to do. Rank Las Vegas food venues by predicted foodborne-illness risk, inferred from Twitter posts, so that health inspectors can prioritize the riskiest venues.

The system's part

The AI chose which venues to inspect. The comparison was a controlled double-blind design with 142 venues split between nEmesis and standard protocol. So the difference in findings is attributable to the AI's venue selection. The developers (Rochester) are distinct from the deployer (SNHD), though SNHD staff co-authored the paper. The paper was peer-reviewed as an IAAI deployed paper.

Replication

What produced it. Dense geotagged social-media posting across a metropolitan area. A health department willing to assign half of a matched inspection sample to algorithm-flagged venues. A graded inspection protocol (demerits, letter grades) that makes arms comparable.

What must hold again. Access to geotagged public posts and venue locations as available through the Twitter and Google Places APIs in 2015. Inspectors blinded to the source of the assignment. Enough flagged venues per day to schedule adaptive inspections.
