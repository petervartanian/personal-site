Inspections guided by nEmesis find more venues graded C or worse

In a blinded trial in Las Vegas from 2 January 2015, inspections of venues that nEmesis flagged from tweets found 9 demerits per inspection versus 6, and 11 venues graded C or worse versus 7.

Distribution and access

The system probably favored venues whose customers posted location-linked accounts of illness, so restaurants serving less visible social-media users were less likely to receive an algorithmic priority.

Editor’s note. The ranking depended on geotagged tweets rather than a population-wide sample of diners. Unequal posting therefore provides a direct mechanism for unequal visibility, although its size was not measured.

Relation to AI

The AI chose which venues to inspect. The comparison was a controlled double-blind design with 142 venues split between nEmesis and standard protocol. Reasons

Organizations: In 142 blinded inspections the nEmesis arm found 9 versus 6 demerits per inspection (p = 0.019). Society: The 71 infections and 4.4 hospitalizations said to be saved are the authors' estimates from outbreak averages of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. The drop in foodborne-illness investigations from 11 to 5 comes from an uncontrolled comparison with the year before, so no prevented illness is measured.
