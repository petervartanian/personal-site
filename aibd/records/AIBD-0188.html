Inspections guided by nEmesis find more venues graded C or worse

In a blinded trial in Las Vegas from 2 January 2015, inspections of venues that nEmesis flagged from tweets found 9 demerits per inspection versus 6, and 11 venues graded C or worse versus 7.

What happened

On 2 January 2015 the Southern Nevada Health District began a controlled experiment with nEmesis, a University of Rochester system. It applies a language model built on a support vector machine to geotagged tweets and ranks Las Vegas food venues by their risk of foodborne illness. During the first three months environmental health specialists inspected 142 venues. Half were chosen by nEmesis and half under the standard protocol. Inspectors were blind to which was which. Inspections guided by nEmesis, which the authors call adaptive inspections, uncovered 9 demerits per inspection versus 6 (p = 0.019). They found 11 venues graded C or worse versus 7 for controls. The authors call this a 64% improvement, and their abstract summarizes it as 63% more effective. Side findings included an unpermitted seafood establishment and a food handler working with an influenza-like illness. The health district performed 5 foodborne-illness investigations between 2 January and 31 March 2015, against 11 in the same window the year before. The authors estimate, from outbreak averages of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, that adaptive inspections saved 71 infections and 4.4 hospitalizations over the three months. They estimate that making all of the more than 35,000 annual inspections in Las Vegas adaptive could prevent more than 9,126 cases and 557 hospitalizations a year. In 2016 the same health district tested FINDER, a different model built by Google. The developers published a journal version of the nEmesis study in AI Magazine on 31 March 2017 that gives the gain as 64 percent. On 6 November 2018 the FINDER authors reported that 52.3% of the 132 venues that FINDER chose in Las Vegas and Chicago were unsafe, against 24.7% of other inspections.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

NEmesis used geotagged tweets and venue locations to rank Las Vegas restaurants for inspection according to suspected foodborne illness.

Autonomy

The model prioritized inspection targets. Health specialists inspected the premises and determined their violations and grades.

Workflow

Dense geotagged social-media posting across a metropolitan area. A health department willing to assign half of a matched inspection sample to algorithm-flagged venues. A graded inspection protocol (demerits, letter grades) that makes arms comparable.

Human oversight

Environmental health specialists of the Southern Nevada Health District inspected and graded all 142 venues. They did not know which venues nEmesis had chosen. [1]

Benefits

Reach

No source counts the people who benefited. The experiment covered 142 inspected venues. The inspections that nEmesis guided found 11 venues graded C or worse versus 7 for controls. [1]

Duration

The experiment ran for three months from 2 January 2015. No source reports that the Southern Nevada Health District kept using nEmesis afterward. In 2016 the same health district tested FINDER, a different Google model. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

Demerits and letter grades recorded by SNHD environmental health specialists in standard inspections. [1]

Setting and sample

142 food venues across the Las Vegas metropolitan area, half chosen by nEmesis and half by the standard protocol, January to March 2015. [1]

Measures

Demerits per inspection; venues graded C or worse. [1]

Results

9 against 6 demerits per inspection (p = 0.019); 11 against 7 venues graded C or worse. [1]

Limitations

Small sample over three months; SNHD staff and the developers wrote the paper together; the prevented illnesses and hospitalizations are model estimates from CDC outbreak averages. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Access to geotagged public posts and venue locations as available through the Twitter and Google Places APIs in 2015. Inspectors blinded to the source of the assignment. Enough flagged venues per day to schedule adaptive inspections.

3 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 3.

Original evidence

The results come from the developers' own papers, written with staff of the health district: the AAAI conference paper of 18 February 2016 and the AI Magazine version of 31 March 2017. The FINDER paper, by the same lead author, describes a later Google system. [1]
