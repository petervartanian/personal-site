Danish fraud models flag welfare cases that end with benefits cut

Three of Udbetaling Danmark's machine-learning fraud models flagged cases that ended with benefits stopped or reduced 135, 36 and 72 times in 2023; Amnesty International questions their effectiveness.

  • Not rated a person

    No individual beneficiary of the controls is identified. The people directly affected are those investigated. By Amnesty's reading of UDK's figures, 54% to almost 90% of opened cases per model ended with no further action.

  • 1 an organization

    UDK's own 2023 figures give 135, 36 and 72 cases with revenue from three models against 412, 511 and 491 requested cases. No amounts are given, and the authority says that yearly figures are not directly comparable.

  • Not rated society

    No societal outcome is reported beyond the cases with revenue that UDK counts for itself. The 2023 figures give no amounts, so the extent of any gain to public funds cannot be established, and Amnesty's report questions whether the models are effective.

The system’s part
The models select “requested cases” that UDK's HOK control team screens and investigates. Decisions to stop or reduce benefits are taken by humans after investigation. UDK measures model quality by the share of cases the team accepts for manual processing. Every outcome figure is a UDK statement (FOI responses and a letter) as reproduced verbatim in Amnesty International's report. No official publication of them is among the sources. Amnesty contests effectiveness and alleges discrimination risks, and UDK rejected parts of Amnesty's assessment. NNIT built the models to ATP's specifications. No developer-authored source is on record. The dm.dk report and ATP's page are not among the sources.
Limits
Cases with reduced or stopped benefits include errors as well as fraud and are not a demonstrated net public benefit. UDK warns that annual outcome cases are not the same cohort as cases opened that year, so the rows cannot yield simple success rates. Financial recovery, false positives, and distributional harms remain unresolved.
Costs and harms
Amnesty International alleges discrimination risks and disproportionate surveillance. Udbetaling Danmark disputed parts of its assessment.
Later
Nothing later is known. No later development in the controls is established. The employment minister forwarded a UDK board account to Parliament (Folketinget DIU Alm.del 2024-25 bilag 32), but its contents remain unverified.

What happened

Udbetaling Danmark (UDK), the Danish public authority that has centralized welfare payments since 2012, has its benefits administered by ATP (Arbejdsmarkedets Tillaegspension). ATP designs the fraud-control components of UDK's Joint Data Unit (Den Faelles Dataenhed), which the University of Copenhagen's Offentlig AI database says was established in 2014. NNIT A/S is the supplier. The registry describes profiling and machine learning run on linked register data, and it names Naive Bayes classification and cluster analysis. Background sources give earlier yields. In July 2019 the Danish legal think tank Justitia used UDK figures to show that UDK's data-based controls led to 705 cases in 2017 and 1,094 cases in 2018 with benefits stopped, changed or repaid. Its author argues that this yield is very low for data matching that can cover up to 2.7 million recipients. The Automating Society 2020 Denmark chapter cites the July 2019 report and gives the same counts out of 2.7 million screened beneficiaries. AlgorithmWatch (2020) reports that controls in 2017 and 2018 unveiled about half a billion kroner in erroneous payments. As of 2019 UDK was reported to use up to 60 algorithms for fraud detection and other purposes. In a letter to Amnesty International before November 2024, UDK supplied its 2023 statistics for three data-driven controls. The 2023 figures carry no day. The “Really Single” model produced 412 requested cases, 292 established control cases and 135 cases with revenue (benefits stopped or reduced). “Model Abroad” (unreported departures, state and disability pension) gave 511, 351 and 36. The parental-leave controls including the “Fictitious Employment” model gave 491, 207 and 72. UDK cautioned that cases with revenue in a year are not necessarily a proportion of cases established that year. On 11 January 2024 a UDK and ATP control official told Amnesty that the team manages 5,000 to 6,000 cases a year, of which around 1,800 come from the models. A freedom-of-information report on dm.dk in October 2024 said that nearly all 1.2 million pensioners were screened and that only 36 cases in 2023 ended with a benefit stopped or reduced. That matches the Model Abroad row. The dm.dk report is not among the cited sources, and neither is ATP's page, which credits 260 million kr. a year of savings to IT modernization generally. Amnesty's report of 12 November 2024 concludes that it is questionable whether the algorithms are effective. It finds that most opened cases end with no further action. It also finds that the system risks discrimination and mass surveillance. A Copenhagen Municipality control official said that more often than not there is no fraud in the cases that UDK sends. Amnesty's report states that the Danish Data Protection Authority told it that it acts only on inquiries or complaints. No regulator finding on the models is on record.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Linked public-register information was scored or grouped to generate suspected-benefit-fraud cases for manual investigation.

Autonomy

Models proposed cases; the control team chose which to investigate and humans decided whether to change benefits.

Workflow

Register linkage of benefits data with civil, housing, business, tax, income, health and employment registers under a 2015 legal authorization. About 60 profiling and machine-learning models developed by NNIT to ATP's specifications. A UDK/ATP control team (HOK) that manually screens model output into established control cases and investigates them. A legal duty to also process all externally reported cases, which the control team says limits the share drawn from the models to about 30%.

Human oversight

Staff investigated the files and decided whether benefits should be reduced or stopped.

AI contribution

Udbetaling Danmark’s models selected benefit files for investigation. Staff screened the selections before deciding whether to investigate further.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Udbetaling Danmark is the intended institutional beneficiary. No individual beneficiary of the controls is identified.

Duration

UDK reports 135, 36 and 72 cases with benefits stopped or reduced in 2023. It cautions that these counts are not necessarily a proportion of the cases established that year. Justitia reports that UDK's data-based controls led to 705 cases in 2017 and 1,094 in 2018 with benefits stopped, changed or repaid. No later development in the controls is established.
