UDK fraud models yield 135, 36 and 72 cases; Amnesty contests

2023 figures of 135, 36 and 72 cases with revenue from three models

UDK/ATP ML fraud models (about 60 since 2019): 2023 figures of 135, 36 and 72 cases with revenue from three models; Amnesty (Nov 2024) calls their effectiveness questionable.

What happened

Udbetaling Danmark (UDK), the Danish public authority that has centralised welfare payments since 2012, has its benefits administered by ATP (Arbejdsmarkedets Tillaegspension), which designs the fraud-control components of UDK's Joint Data Unit (Den Faelles Dataenhed, established 2014 per the University of Copenhagen's Offentlig AI database) with NNIT A/S as supplier. As of 2019 UDK was reported to use up to 60 algorithms for fraud detection and other purposes; the registry describes profiling and machine learning (it names Naive Bayes classification and cluster analysis) run on linked register data. In a letter to Amnesty International before November 2024, UDK supplied its 2023 statistics for three data-driven controls: the 'Really Single' model produced 412 requested cases, 292 established control cases and 135 cases with revenue (benefits stopped or reduced); 'Model Abroad' (unreported departures, state and disability pension) 511, 351 and 36; and the parental-leave controls including the 'Fictitious Employment' model 491, 207 and 72. UDK cautioned that cases with revenue in a year are not necessarily a proportion of cases established that year. A UDK/ATP control official told Amnesty the team manages 5,000 to 6,000 cases a year of which around 1,800 come from the models. Amnesty's report of 12 November 2024 concludes that it is questionable whether the algorithms are effective, that most opened cases end with no further action, and that the system risks discrimination and mass surveillance; a Copenhagen Municipality control official said that more often than not there is no fraud in the cases UDK sends. Background sources give earlier yields: AlgorithmWatch (2020) reports that 2017 and 2018 controls unveiled about half a billion kroner in erroneous payments, and the Automating Society 2020 Denmark chapter cites a July 2019 report counting 705 cases in 2017 and 1,094 in 2018 where payments were stopped, adjusted or repaid, out of 2.7 million screened beneficiaries. The earlier verifier's dm.dk FOI report (October 2024: nearly all 1.2 million pensioners screened, only 36 cases in 2023 ending with a benefit stopped or reduced, a figure consistent with the Model Abroad row) and ATP's page attributing 260 million kr. a year of savings to IT modernisation generally were not re-fetched here. The 2023 figures carry no day; the date used is the publication date of the Amnesty report that reproduces them.

What it was built to do. Built to flag benefit recipients at elevated risk of receiving unentitled payments for manual control by UDK's control team; the recorded outcome, cases with benefits stopped or reduced, is that purpose.

The system's part

The models select 'requested cases' that UDK's HOK control team screens and investigates; decisions to stop or reduce benefits are taken by humans after investigation, and UDK measures model quality by the share of cases the team accepts for manual processing. Every outcome figure is a UDK statement (FOI responses and a letter) as reproduced verbatim in Amnesty International's report, not an official publication fetched directly; Amnesty contests effectiveness and alleges discrimination risks, and UDK rejected parts of Amnesty's assessment. NNIT built the models to ATP's specifications; no developer-authored source was fetched. The earlier verifier's dm.dk and ATP sources were not re-fetched.

Replication

What produced it

  • Register linkage of benefits data with civil, housing, business, tax, income, health and employment registers under a 2015 legal authorisation
  • About 60 profiling and machine-learning models developed by NNIT to ATP's specifications
  • A UDK/ATP control team (HOK) that manually screens model output into established control cases and investigates them
  • A legal duty to also process all externally reported cases, which the control team says limits the share drawn from the models to about 30%

What must hold again

  • Legal basis for register linkage and model use remains in force despite Amnesty's challenge and the dispute over classification under the EU AI Act
  • Control-team capacity of about 5,000 to 6,000 cases a year
  • Models continue to be monitored on their training cases, since UDK documents no evaluation metrics
  • Political tolerance of the system after the November 2024 criticism
