Taiwan AI-ECG alerts reduce mortality in a randomized trial

A Taiwanese randomized trial involving 15,965 patients found 90-day mortality of 3.6% with AI-ECG alerts versus 4.3% with usual care.

ⓘ

What happened

An academic medical center and a community hospital in Taiwan tested AI-ECG alerts with 39 physicians and 15,965 patients. Patients were randomly assigned to AI-assisted care or usual care. Ninety-day all-cause mortality, the primary endpoint, was 3.6% versus 4.3%, respectively: hazard ratio 0.83, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.70–0.99. The prespecified high-risk subgroup had a hazard ratio of 0.69 (0.53–0.90). The trial covered ECGs collected from December 15, 2021, through April 30, 2022. Longer-term survival and generalizability to other health systems remain uncertain.

Purpose. Identify patients at high risk of death and prompt timely clinical assessment.

AI’s contribution

A convolutional neural network assessed ECGs and sent high-risk alerts to physicians. Clinicians selected further assessment and treatment. The trial evaluated this combined care pathway. The precise mechanism of the survival improvement remains uncertain.

Further evidence

Setting. Routine ECGs were analyzed automatically, with warnings sent to physicians and risk reports available in the electronic health record. Physicians assessed alerted patients and selected care using their clinical judgment.

Conditions. Reliable ECG transfer and clinicians able to assess alerts are likely requirements. Effectiveness in other health systems requires evaluation.
