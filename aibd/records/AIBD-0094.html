What happened

Lacking an urban social registry, Nigeria's National Social Safety Nets Coordinating Office (NASSCO) built the Rapid Response Register for a government-funded COVID-19 cash transfer. Ward-level poverty maps came from machine learning (ML) algorithms applied to satellite imagery with household-survey labels, by Berkeley and Columbia researchers with World Bank economists. The maps selected 1,163 of the poorest urban wards, population over 20 million. Residents self-registered by USSD after SMS blasts (first wave June 2021), then in-person verification. Human Rights Watch records the program began in January 2021. The PNAS authors state disbursement began mid-January 2021 (day not given; first day of month used). World Bank monitoring reports 49,000 beneficiary households of the government's COVID-CT as of 15 October 2021.

It reports over 1.75 million registrations by November 2021, against a government target of one million households.

What it was built to do. Geographically target a COVID-19 urban cash transfer to the poorest wards where no social registry existed.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Households in selected wards were beneficiaries of the government's COVID-CT. The PNAS authors say disbursement began mid-January 2021. The World Bank ISR records 49,000 beneficiary households as of 15 October 2021 without saying how many had been paid.

  • an organization

    NASSCO stood up an urban registry within months through USSD self-registration guided by the ML poverty map. Over 1.75 million registrations succeeded (PAD, Nov 2021).

  • society

    Material to many: 1,163 wards with over 20 million residents made eligible. PNAS simulation finds the ML maps would cut inclusion errors by 61.7% versus the survey-based benchmark.

Since then
Scaled on 29 January 2025: The World Bank's 2021 scale-up project adopted the same machine-learning poverty maps to choose expansion wards for the RRR, and its January 2025 status report counts over 5 million households supported by the direct benefit transfer program after delays and a switch to biometric-verified payments, without breaking out the RRR-targeted share.The direct benefit transfer program to date has supported over 5 million households. Following the CBN circular of December 2023, mandating the use of NIN or BVN to operate an account after March 2024, and the recommendation from the presidential panel, all transfers since March 2024 are made to beneficiaries who’s NIN or BVN have been verified. source

The system's part

Decisive at the geographic stage: the ML poverty map alone chose which 1,163 wards were eligible. Individual inclusion depended on USSD self-registration and in-person verification, not AI. Payment and registration counts come from World Bank operational documents, not from the map developers. The PNAS paper and World Bank blog are co-authored by the developers.

Replication

What produced it. Deep-learning poverty map trained on geo-located household surveys. Government decision to adopt the ward ranking for eligibility. SMS blasts and USSD self-registration. In-person verification, de-duplication and payment onboarding.

What must hold again. Ground-truth survey data for training labels. Ward boundaries compatible with map resolution. Mobile-phone access in target wards. Government funding for transfers and identity verification capacity.
