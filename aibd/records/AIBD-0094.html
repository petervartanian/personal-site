AI system

System

Ward-level poverty maps from supervised machine learning (deep learning) on satellite imagery, used for geographic targeting in the Rapid Response Register

Application developer

Joshua Blumenstock (UC Berkeley) and Isabella Smythe (Columbia University), using wealth estimates by Chi et al. (2021), with World Bank economists Jonathan Lain and Tara Vishwanath, for the Government of Nigeria

Workflow

Deep-learning poverty map trained on geo-located household surveys. Government decision to adopt the ward ranking for eligibility. SMS blasts and USSD self-registration. In-person verification, de-duplication and payment onboarding.

Relation to AI

Decisive at the geographic stage: the ML poverty map alone chose which 1,163 wards were eligible. Individual inclusion depended on USSD self-registration and in-person verification, not AI. Payment and registration counts come from World Bank operational documents, not from the map developers. The PNAS paper and World Bank blog are co-authored by the developers.

Later findings

29 January 2025. The World Bank's 2021 scale-up project adopted the same machine-learning poverty maps to choose expansion wards for the RRR, and its January 2025 status report counts over 5 million households supported by the direct benefit transfer program after delays and a switch to biometric-verified payments, without breaking out the RRR-targeted share.

Depth

People: 2. Organizations: 2. Society: 3.

Reasons

People: Households in selected wards were beneficiaries of the government's COVID-CT. The PNAS authors say disbursement began mid-January 2021. The World Bank ISR records 49,000 beneficiary households as of 15 October 2021 without saying how many had been paid. Organizations: NASSCO stood up an urban registry within months through USSD self-registration guided by the ML poverty map. Over 1.75 million registrations succeeded (PAD, Nov 2021). Society: Material to many: 1,163 wards with over 20 million residents made eligible. PNAS simulation finds the ML maps would cut inclusion errors by 61.7% versus the survey-based benchmark.
