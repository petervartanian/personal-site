Treasury recovers $1 billion in check fraud flagged by machine learning

The U.S. Treasury reported that machine learning helped it recover $1 billion in check fraud in fiscal year 2024, part of over $4 billion prevented or recovered; no source names the software.

What happened

According to CNN, Treasury officials began using AI to detect financial crime in late 2022. On 17 October 2024 the U.S. Department of the Treasury reported on fraud detection at the Bureau of the Fiscal Service's Office of Payment Integrity. The enhanced processes, including machine-learning AI, prevented and recovered over $4 billion in fraud and improper payments in fiscal year 2024 (October 2023 to September 2024). The comparison figure for fiscal year 2023 was $652.7 million. Of the total, $1 billion was recovered through machine-learning identification of Treasury check fraud. Identifying and prioritizing high-risk transactions brought $2.5 billion, expanded risk-based screening $500 million and payment-processing efficiencies $180 million. CNN reported on the same day that the $1 billion recovered through machine learning was nearly triple the Treasury's recovery of the prior fiscal year. Treasury official Renata Miskell told CNN that a human is always in the loop and that federal agencies make the final determination of fraud. NBC News reported that a Treasury official declined to give specific deployment examples for security reasons. On 18 October 2024 Nextgov reported that the office uses machine learning to examine large amounts of data and flag potential fraud schemes. No source reports an independent audit of the figures, and none names a vendor or a technique. On 18 September 2026 the Bureau of the Fiscal Service reported that Treasury's payment integrity efforts prevented, detected or recovered more than $11.7 billion in fiscal year 2025, nearly three times the total for fiscal year 2024.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Centralised disbursement data at the Bureau of the Fiscal Service (Nextgov: over 1.4 billion payments and more than $6.9 trillion a year). Machine learning applied to large payment datasets to flag potential fraud schemes, combined with risk-based screening and prioritization of high-risk transactions.

Treasury official Renata Miskell told CNN that a human is always in the loop. She said that federal agencies make the final determination of fraud. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
