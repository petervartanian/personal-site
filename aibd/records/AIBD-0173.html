What happened

On 17 October 2024 the U.S. Department of the Treasury reported on fraud detection at the Bureau of the Fiscal Service's Office of Payment Integrity. The enhanced processes, including machine-learning AI, prevented and recovered over $4 billion in fraud and improper payments in FY2024 (October 2023 to September 2024). The FY2023 comparison figure was $652.7 million. Of the total, $1 billion was recovered through machine-learning identification of Treasury check fraud. $2.5 billion came from identifying and prioritizing high-risk transactions, $500 million from expanded risk-based screening and $180 million from payment-processing efficiencies. Nextgov reported that the office uses machine learning to examine large amounts of data and flag potential fraud schemes. NBC News reported that a Treasury official declined to give specific deployment examples for security reasons.

No independent audit of the figures and no vendor or technique appears in the sources. The date used is the announcement date.

What it was built to do. Detect and prevent fraud and improper payments in federal disbursements, including Treasury check fraud.

The system's part

Treasury's release attributes $1 billion of FY2024 recoveries to machine-learning AI that expedited identification of Treasury check fraud. It credits $2.5 billion to prioritizing high-risk transactions. The technique is not described and no vendor is named. So developer and deployer appear to be the same public body (the Bureau of the Fiscal Service's Office of Payment Integrity). This is not stated. The figures are not independently verified in the sources.

Replication

What produced it. Centralised disbursement data at the Bureau of the Fiscal Service (Nextgov: over 1.4 billion payments and more than $6.9 trillion a year). Machine learning applied to large payment datasets to flag potential fraud schemes, combined with risk-based screening and prioritization of high-risk transactions.

What must hold again. Access to payment and check data at scale. Operational capacity to stop or claw back flagged payments and an office that measures the results. Independent verification of the claimed figures, which the sources do not provide.
