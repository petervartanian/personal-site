Microsoft's MatterGen generates TaCr2O6, measured at 169 GPa

a bulk modulus of 200 GPa and measured at 169 GPa

Microsoft's diffusion model generated a structure conditioned on bulk modulus 200 GPa; SIAT (Shenzhen) synthesized it and measured 169 GPa, with Ta/Cr compositional disorder. Vendor co-authored proof.

What happened

Zeni et al. (Nature, 16 January 2025; Microsoft Research AI for Science with co-authors including the Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology) present MatterGen, a diffusion model over 3D crystal geometry trained on about 608,000 stable materials, whose outputs are reported as more than twice as likely to be new and stable as those of earlier generative models; as a proof of concept they synthesized one generated structure and measured its property 'within 20% of our target'. Microsoft's blog identifies the compound as TaCr2O6, generated for a bulk modulus of 200 GPa and measured at 169 GPa by Prof Li Wenjie's SIAT group, with the structure matching MatterGen's proposal 'with the caveat of compositional disorder between Ta and Cr'. The earlier verifier noted that the synthesis is vendor co-authored and that a Nature news critique of 1 October 2025 was paywalled beyond its lede; that item could not be located here, and no independent experimental replication was found. The date is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Generate novel stable inorganic materials conditioned on target properties
