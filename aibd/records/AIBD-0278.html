MatterGen proposes a material later found to be known since 1971

Researchers synthesized TaCr2O6 proposed by MatterGen and estimated its bulk modulus at 169 GPa against a 200 GPa target; a chemist later found that the compound had been known since 1971.

Limits
The reported 169 GPa bulk modulus was estimated from indentation measurements and a computed Poisson ratio, using the maximum of four estimates. One synthesized candidate does not establish that the model generates reliably in general or that it is commercially useful, and independent experimental replication is not established. Juelsholt found that the one synthesized compound had been known since 1971 and sat in the training data.
Costs and harms
Only one of four selected candidates was synthesized. Its compositional disorder and estimated modulus below target show that the model did not precisely determine the laboratory outcome.

What happened

Zeni et al. reported MatterGen in Nature on 16 January 2025. The diffusion model was trained on 607,683 stable crystal structures. For experimental validation, successive filters reduced generated candidates to 75, and experts selected four for synthesis. One produced TaCr2O6 with compositional disorder between Ta and Cr. Researchers measured Young's modulus by nanoindentation. They estimated the bulk modulus with a Poisson ratio of 0.30, computed with density functional theory. Four estimates yielded 158±11 GPa. The authors chose the maximum, 169 GPa, as their best estimate for a potentially noncompact powder sample, against a 200 GPa target. The synthesis and measurement belong to the study that Microsoft researchers co-authored, and independent experimental replication is not established. On 19 June 2025 Mikkel Juelsholt of Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin posted a crystallographic reanalysis. In October 2025 researchers at the Fritz Haber Institute and Imperial College London reported that their disorder classifier predicts that TaCr2O6 is disordered, which matches the synthesis. Juelsholt published his reanalysis in Materials Horizons on 12 June 2026. He found that the synthesized compound is identical to Ta1/2Cr1/2O2, first described in 1971 and included in MatterGen's training data.

  3. Peer-reviewed study, by authors with no tie to the system. Context or contrary evidence.

    Juelsholt M, Continued challenges in high-throughput materials predictions: MatterGen predicts compounds from the training dataset, Materials Horizons, 12 June 2026 (preprint on ChemRxiv from 19 June 2025). The author is a chemist at Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin and the Max Planck Institute for Coal Research, funded by the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation and the Carlsberg Foundation, with no tie to Microsoft or the synthesis laboratory. His crystallographic analysis finds that the synthesized compound is a known phase from 1971 that sat in MatterGen's training data.

    However, detailed crystallographic analysis presented in this paper reveals that this is not a novel compound but is identical to the previously reported Ta 1/2 Cr 1/2 O 2 , first described in 1971 and included in MatterGen's training dataset.

  4. Peer-reviewed study, by authors with no tie to the system. Context or contrary evidence.

    Jakob KS, Walsh A, Reuter K, Margraf JT, Learning Crystallographic Disorder: Bridging Prediction and Experiment in Materials Discovery, Advanced Materials, 23 October 2025. The authors work at the Fritz Haber Institute, Imperial College London and the University of Bayreuth and have no stake in MatterGen; their classifier predicts that TaCr2O6 would be disordered, which matches the synthesis.

    Another example is the case of TaCr 2 O 6 proposed in the recent MatterGen effort, [ 12 ] which is also predicted to be disordered by the RNN model (with the balanced 50% classification threshold).
