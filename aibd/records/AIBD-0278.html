Microsoft's MatterGen generates TaCr2O6, measured at 169 GPa

Microsoft's diffusion model generated a structure conditioned on bulk modulus 200 GPa; SIAT (Shenzhen) synthesized it and measured 169 GPa, with Ta/Cr compositional disorder. Vendor co-authored proof.

ⓘ

What happened

Zeni et al. (Nature, 16 January 2025) are Microsoft Research AI for Science with co-authors including the Shenzhen Institutes of Advanced Technology. They present MatterGen, a diffusion model over 3D crystal geometry trained on about 608,000 stable materials. Its outputs are reported as more than twice as likely to be new and stable as those of earlier generative models. As a proof of concept they synthesized one generated structure and measured its property “within 20% of our target”. Microsoft's blog identifies the compound as TaCr2O6, generated for a bulk modulus of 200 GPa. Prof Li Wenjie's SIAT group measured 169 GPa. The structure matched MatterGen's proposal “with the caveat of compositional disorder between Ta and Cr”. The synthesis is vendor co-authored, and a Nature news critique of 1 October 2025 was paywalled beyond its lede.

That critique is not among the sources, and no independent experimental replication was found.

Purpose. Generate novel stable inorganic materials conditioned on target properties

AI’s contribution

MatterGen generated the structure that SIAT then synthesized. Both the generation and the measurement are reported by the vendor and its co-authors in the Nature paper and the Microsoft blog. No independent confirmation of novelty or utility is among the sources.

Since then
A Materials Horizons paper by Mikkel Juelsholt (12 June 2026) reports that the synthesized disordered TaCr2O6 is identical to Ta1/2Cr1/2O2, a compound first described in 1971 and included in MatterGen's training dataset, so the proof-of-concept material was not new.However, detailed crystallographic analysis presented in this paper reveals that this is not a novel compound but is identical to the previously reported Ta1/2Cr1/2O2, first described in 1971 and included in MatterGen's training dataset. source

Further evidence

Setting. Diffusion model fine-tuned on a bulk-modulus target. Synthesis and measurement by a collaborating laboratory.

Conditions. Independent synthesis of generated structures. Confirmation that generated compounds are new rather than known phases.
