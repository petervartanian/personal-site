Waymo pulls a car over; police find a loaded ghost gun with two minors

LA Times (12 September 2026): before 4 a.m. on 3 Sep Waymo pulled a robotaxi over in the Richmond District after detecting a firearm violation; SFPD found a loaded ghost gun and booked two minors.

ⓘ

What happened

Shortly before 4 a.m. on Thursday 3 September 2026, Waymo pulled one of its driverless cars over in San Francisco's Richmond District. It had detected “a violation of our terms of service involving a firearm”. Waymo alerted emergency services and “cooperated fully” with police, a Waymo spokesperson told the Los Angeles Times (12 September). The San Francisco Police Department said officers made a “high-risk vehicle stop” and detained the two juvenile passengers. They found a loaded AR-style assault rifle, suspected marijuana and mace spray, and the two were taken to juvenile hall. The Independent, citing SFGATE, quotes SFPD spokesperson Allison Maxie calling the weapon an “AR-styled assault rifle also known as a ghost gun”. It says the boy and girl were booked into the Juvenile Justice Center on firearm-possession charges.

The California Post puts the stop at 40th Avenue and Cabrillo Street. No source says how the firearm was detected; The Independent and Xataka both say this is unclear.

Purpose. The Waymo Driver is built to provide driverless rides. The service's rider rules prohibit weapons, and Xataka says interior cameras record rides so that the company can check compliance and respond to emergencies. Pulling over and calling police on a firearm violation is that safeguard working, not a driving function.

AI’s contribution

What the system did is established only at the level of the company. Waymo says it detected a terms-of-service violation involving a firearm, pulled the driverless car over and alerted emergency services. The Independent says it is not clear how the firearm was detected. Xataka says Waymo has not explained whether detection was automatic or human. The Monitor's statement that the vehicle's AI detected the gun is not supported by any cited source. The driverless car's ability to stop on command is the only system function the accounts describe.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    SFPD took a loaded AR-style ghost gun, suspected marijuana and mace from two juveniles. It booked both on firearm-possession charges after the company's alert, per the Los Angeles Times and The Independent.

  • society

    A loaded unserialized rifle was taken out of circulation, and two minors were charged. No report shows a harm that was about to occur, so the public benefit is the seizure itself.

Further evidence

Setting. Interior cameras recording the ride, which Waymo says it may review to check rule compliance and respond to emergencies (Xataka). A company process that pulls the car over and calls emergency services on a firearm violation. Police able to reach the stopped car quickly before 4 a.m.

Conditions. The weapon is visible or otherwise detectable inside the cabin. Waymo staff or systems act on the detection in time. Riders stay in the car until police arrive. The Independent notes the doors were not locked in the July San Mateo case.
