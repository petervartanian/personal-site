Transport for NSW trials PAWS wildlife cameras; no results yet

off the scale

Transport for NSW's Protective Animal Warning System: two AI-enabled roadside cameras on a 13 km Monaro Highway stretch trigger driver warnings; trial announced 18 Jul 2026, no results yet.

What happened

About Regional reported on 18 July 2026 that Transport for NSW developed the Protective Animal Warning System (PAWS), with two AI-enabled cameras being placed along the Monaro Highway between Bredbo and Williamsdale on a 13 km stretch. Heat-sensing and optical roadside cameras detect animals on or near the road and use an AI model trained to identify common species such as kangaroos, koalas, deer and wombats; the solar-powered cameras trigger signs telling drivers to slow down. The trial was expected to start in August 2026 and, per the report, to run until 2027/2028; Member for Monaro Steve Whan welcomed it. The NSW Government media release of 18 July 2026 named in Google News could not be fetched. No detection, driver response or collision outcome has been reported; the date used is the news report's publication date.

What it was built to do. Detect wildlife near the road and warn drivers dynamically to limit wildlife-vehicle strikes; no benefit has yet been observed.

Replication

What produced it. Roadside thermal and optical cameras with an on-site AI species model. Dynamic warning signs linked to detections. Solar power at remote sites. State agency funding for a multi-year trial.

What must hold again. Trial start and continued operation to 2027/2028. Detection accuracy across target species. Driver compliance with dynamic warnings. Collision monitoring to measure effect.
