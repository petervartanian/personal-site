What happened

The New South Wales (NSW) Government announced its Protective Animal Warning System trial on 18 July 2026. The trial grew out of a 2024 Wildlife Symposium recommendation and an initial A$500,000 government commitment. Transport for NSW developed the system, which uses thermal and optical cameras with AI to recognize wildlife and trigger roadside warnings. The announcement describes training at Yennora and Symbio Wildlife Park before a live trial on the Monaro Highway. About Regional's separate interviews add the planned two-camera layout on a 13 km stretch between Bredbo and Williamsdale and the role of local wildlife carers. The intended benefit is fewer collisions after drivers receive warnings. Transport for NSW plans to monitor the speed of vehicles approaching the signs. Wildlife carers have been asked to move dead animals to the roadside so that strikes can be counted. Neither source reports a measured change in detection accuracy, driver behavior or collisions. As of 29 October 2026 no results have been reported.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Thermal and optical images were used to recognize animals near the road and illuminate warning signs. Training combined animal images and movement with time-of-day and weather information.

Autonomy

The system generated roadside warnings. Drivers remained responsible for slowing or avoiding the animal.

Workflow

Roadside thermal and optical cameras with an on-site AI species model. Dynamic warning signs linked to detections. Solar power at remote sites. State agency funding for a multi-year trial.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Drivers and wildlife on the trial section were the intended beneficiaries. No reduction in collisions was reported at launch.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Trial start and continued operation to 2027/2028. Detection accuracy across target species. Driver compliance with dynamic warnings. Collision monitoring to measure effect.
