Automated scan alerts help Houston stroke teams start treatment sooner

At four Houston comprehensive stroke centers, Viz.ai LVO detection with mobile alerts cut door-to-groin time for thrombectomy by 11.2 minutes.

What happened

Researchers at UTHealth Houston ran a cluster-randomized stepped-wedge trial from 1 January 2021 to 27 February 2022 at four comprehensive stroke centers in the Houston area. The centers switched on, in random order, cloud-based Viz.ai software that reads CT angiograms for large vessel occlusion and sends alerts to clinicians' phones within minutes of the scan. Among 243 patients with large vessel occlusion stroke who arrived through the emergency department and had thrombectomy, the time from hospital arrival to groin puncture fell by 11.2 minutes (95% CI 4.2 to 18.2) after activation, from a pre-activation median of 100 minutes. Time from CT to thrombectomy start fell by 9.8 minutes, while tPA treatment times and length of stay did not change. The senior author reported grants from Viz.AI during the study. In 2025, a meta-analysis of 12 studies with 15,595 patients, by authors who declare no competing interests, reported that Viz.ai use was associated with shorter door-to-groin times, with no significant difference in clinical outcomes.

Sources

  1. Peer-reviewed study, by the makers.

    Martinez-Gutierrez JC, Kim Y, Salazar-Marioni S, et al. Automated Large Vessel Occlusion Detection Software and Thrombectomy Treatment Times: A Cluster Randomized Clinical Trial. JAMA Neurol. 2023;80(11):1182-1190.

    In mixed-effects linear regression, implementation of the AI algorithm was associated with a reduction in DTG time by 11.2 minutes (95% CI, -18.22 to -4.2).

  2. Peer-reviewed study, by authors with no tie to the system.

    Sarhan K, Azzam AY, Moawad MHED, et al. Automated Emergent Large Vessel Occlusion Detection Using Viz.ai Software and Its Impact on Stroke Workflow Metrics and Patient Outcomes in Stroke Centers: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis. Transl Stroke Res. 2025;16(6):2258-2271.

    The pooled analysis demonstrated that the implementation of the Viz.ai algorithm was associated with lesser CT scan to EVT time (SMD -0.71, 95% CI [-0.98, -0.44], p < 0.001) and DTG time (SMD -0.50, 95% CI [-0.66, -0.35], p < 0.001)

Details

AI system

Workflow

Every CT angiogram for suspected stroke went to the cloud algorithm automatically. Alerts reached clinicians and radiologists by phone with secure group messaging.

Measurement

Measurement

Study 1: Door-to-groin time from stroke registry data; mixed-effects linear regression with center as random effect. Study 2: Systematic review and meta-analysis of before/after cohorts and one randomized trial. [1] [2]

Setting and sample

Study 1: 243 emergency department patients with large vessel occlusion stroke treated with thrombectomy at four centers, January 2021 to February 2022. Study 2: 12 studies, 15,595 patients, stroke centers in several countries. [1] [2]

Measures

Study 1: Door-to-groin time. Study 2: Door-to-groin time and other workflow times (standardized mean difference). [1] [2]

Results

Study 1: 11.2 minutes shorter (95% CI 4.2 to 18.2). Study 2: Door-to-groin SMD -0.50 (95% CI -0.66 to -0.35). [1] [2]

Additional value from AI

Study 1: 11.2 minutes. Study 2: Shorter workflow times; no significant change in clinical outcomes. [1] [2]

Limitations

Study 1: Small number of clusters; transfers excluded; senior author received grants from the developer. Study 2: Mostly observational source studies, several by developer-linked authors. [1] [2]

Denominators

Study 1: Patients treated after activation at each center. Study 2: Patients treated after implementation. [1] [2]

Materials for replication

Study 1: Data sharing statement in supplement. Study 2: Supplementary file with extracted data. [1] [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Stroke teams must act on phone alerts before the formal report. The centers must have thrombectomy capacity on site.
