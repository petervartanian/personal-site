TrailGuard AI transmits images of armed poachers in Kishanpur

A TrailGuard AI camera in Kishanpur Wildlife Sanctuary sent officials images of poachers with a gun and knives on two dates in 2022; officials identified them, and later sources claim arrests.

  • 1 an organization

    Forest officials received the images in near real time and identified the individuals. The paper documents an investigation, not an arrest or prosecution, so the recorded gain is an investigative lead.

  • Not rated society

    Protection of the reserve's wildlife from this gang is claimed through arrests only in the Clemson release, developer material and The Hindu's second-hand report. The paper notes a chital presumably poached before the second detection.

What happened

Dertien, Negi, Dinerstein and colleagues (BioScience 73(10):748–757, published online 14 September 2023) report deploying the TrailGuard AI camera-alert system. The system “runs on-the-edge artificial intelligence algorithms to detect tigers and poachers and transmit real-time images”. It was deployed in the Kanha-Pench corridor and in Dudhwa Tiger Reserve, Uttar Pradesh. The paper states that the seven Dudhwa units ran from early September 2022 to mid-December 2022. All seven transmitted images; three transmitted objects of interest to forest department officials. “One unit within Kishanpur Wildlife Sanctuary captured a group of poachers carrying a gun and knives on two separate dates, and a presumably poached chital (Axis axis) on the second detection”. The paper does not give the dates of the detections, so the two poacher detections can be placed only within the deployment period. Tigers were detected at the same spot three days before the second poacher detection. “Authorities were able to quickly identify the individuals via the transmitted images and were investigating the matter”. The authors include RESOLVE and CVEDIA staff (the system's developers) alongside Dudhwa Tiger Reserve, Wildlife Institute of India and Global Tiger Forum co-authors. A Clemson University release of 15 September 2023 carries Nightjar and RESOLVE boilerplate. It states that the alerts “led the authorities to the arrest of these individuals for poaching a few weeks later”. It quotes Sanjay Kumar Pathak, former field director of Dudhwa, as saying that the system “led to the detection and arrest of poachers in Dudhwa”. The Hindu (16 September 2023) cited a person involved in the experiment as saying that poachers caught on camera had helped forest authorities secure a conviction a year later. It quoted Piyush Yadav of Nightjar, the manufacturer: “A year down, these poachers were arrested with images from TrailGuard playing a key role”. The article's lead puts the capture in Kanha-Pench and the Yadav quote in Dudhwa. Agence France-Presse (Taipei Times, 6 October 2023) reported the study's claim of “identifying a team of poachers”. No forest-department statement or court record of an arrest was found. A Clemson University release of 6 August 2025 states that TrailGuard AI has contributed to the arrests of more than 20 poachers, but it names no reserve or case.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

An on-camera classifier selected images of people or tigers and transmitted the relevant images to officials’ phones.

Workflow

Officials identified people from the transmitted images and investigated suspected poaching. The peer-reviewed account stops before a confirmed arrest.

Human oversight

The camera's on-board classifier filtered motion triggers to people and tigers and sent the images to officials' phones. The authorities then identified the individuals from the images and were investigating the matter. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Officials act on the images while the poachers can still be found. The paper records identification and investigation only. The embedded classifier suppresses non-target triggers so that officials keep trusting the alerts.

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.
