TrailGuard AI transmits images of armed poachers in Kishanpur

depth 1 for an organization

Independently reported, 5 sources.

Dertien et al. (BioScience 2023): one TrailGuard AI unit captured poachers “carrying a gun and knives” on two dates and a poached chital; officials identified them. Arrests claimed only in releases.

What happened

Dertien, Negi, Dinerstein and colleagues (BioScience 73(10):748–757, published online 14 September 2023) report deploying the TrailGuard AI camera-alert system. That date is used because the paper does not date the detections. The system “runs on-the-edge artificial intelligence algorithms to detect tigers and poachers and transmit real-time images”. It was deployed in the Kanha-Pench corridor and in Dudhwa Tiger Reserve, Uttar Pradesh. All seven Dudhwa units transmitted images; three transmitted objects of interest to forest department officials. “One unit within Kishanpur Wildlife Sanctuary captured a group of poachers carrying a gun and knives on two separate dates, and a presumably poached chital (Axis axis) on the second detection”. “Authorities were able to quickly identify the individuals via the transmitted images and were investigating the matter”.

Tigers were detected at the same spot three days before the second poacher detection. The authors include RESOLVE and CVEDIA staff (the system's developers) alongside Dudhwa Tiger Reserve, Wildlife Institute of India and Global Tiger Forum co-authors. AFP (Taipei Times, 6 October 2023) reported the study's claim of “identifying a team of poachers”. A Clemson University release of 15 September 2023 carries Nightjar/RESOLVE boilerplate. It states that the alerts “led the authorities to the arrest of these individuals for poaching a few weeks later”. It quotes Sanjay Kumar Pathak, former field director of Dudhwa, that the system “led to the detection and arrest of poachers in Dudhwa”. The Hindu (16 September 2023) cited a person involved in the experiment. Poachers caught on camera had a year later helped forest authorities secure a conviction.

It quoted Piyush Yadav of Nightjar, the manufacturer, that “A year down, these poachers were arrested with images from TrailGuard playing a key role”. The article's lead puts the capture in Kanha-Pench and the Yadav quote in Dudhwa. No forest-department statement or court record of an arrest was found. A separate record in this database covers TrailGuard AI at Similipal, Odisha.

What it was built to do. Built to detect tigers and people on trails with embedded AI and send real-time images to forest officials. Detecting and transmitting the poachers' images was that purpose.

The system's part

The camera's on-board classifier filtered motion triggers to people and tigers and transmitted the poachers' images to officials' phones within about a minute. Identification and investigation were done by officials. The peer-reviewed account is co-authored by the developers (RESOLVE, CVEDIA) with Dudhwa and Wildlife Institute of India staff and stops at “investigating”. The arrest claim rests on a Clemson release quoting the ex-field director, developer press releases and The Hindu quoting the manufacturer and an unnamed participant.

Replication

What produced it. Camouflaged camera units on trails inside the reserve used by both tigers and poachers. Cellular coverage allowing images to reach forest officials' phones in under a minute.

What must hold again. Officials act on the images while the poachers can still be found. The paper records identification and investigation only. The embedded classifier suppresses non-target triggers so that officials keep trusting the alerts.
