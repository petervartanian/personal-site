Face2Gene's DeepGestalt changes geneticists' testing in 17 cases

17 cases involving 19 patients

Rudy et al. (Am J Med Genet A, online 4 Jul 2025) report 17 cases/19 patients in which DeepGestalt changed geneticists' testing decisions; full text inaccessible, so no per-patient detail.

What happened

Rudy, Gomes, Tkemaladze and 13 co-authors published 'Artificial Intelligence Software Changes Rare Disease Testing Strategy in Real Time: An International Case Series Using Face2Gene' online in the American Journal of Medical Genetics Part A on 4 July 2025 (volume 197, issue 11, November 2025). The abstract, read on Europe PMC, states: 'We present 17 cases involving 19 patients, including one pair of affected siblings and one case involving a child and her affected mother', for which 'DeepGestalt, the technology powering Face2Gene, changed medical geneticists' testing decisions'. Crossref lists author affiliations at the Greenwood Genetic Center, UC San Diego and Rady Children's Hospital, Tbilisi State Medical University, Weill Cornell, the University of Nebraska Medical Center, IDI-IRCCS Rome, the Hospital for Sick Children Toronto, UCSF/Fresno, the University of Alabama at Birmingham and, for one author (Nicole Fleischer), FDNA Inc, and a CC BY-NC-ND licence. Despite that licence, Wiley returned a security page to this pass (as it returned 403 to the earlier verifier), Unpaywall listed no repository copy and there is no PMC copy, so the patients' ages, features, the suggestions made and the tests ordered could not be read. The date observed is the online publication date; the consultations are undated in the abstract.

What it was built to do. Face2Gene is built to suggest candidate syndromes from facial photographs and clinical features so that geneticists can direct testing; changed testing decisions were that purpose.

The system's part

The authors state that DeepGestalt's suggestions changed geneticists' testing decisions in real time in all 17 cases; the clinicians, not the software, made the diagnoses. The assessment rests on the abstract alone because the full text was unreadable; one co-author is an FDNA employee, the other 15 are at clinical institutions.

Replication

What produced it

  • Geneticists uploading facial photographs and clinical features to Face2Gene during consultations
  • Cases where recognition was hard: atypical presentation, overlapping dysmorphology or phenotypes across ancestries, per the abstract

What must hold again

  • The true syndrome is represented in DeepGestalt's training data
  • Clinicians act on the suggestion by changing the test ordered and confirm by molecular testing
