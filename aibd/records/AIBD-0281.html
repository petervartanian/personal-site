Face2Gene's DeepGestalt changes geneticists' testing in 17 cases

Rudy et al. (Am J Med Genet A, online 4 July 2025) report 17 cases/19 patients in which DeepGestalt changed geneticists' testing decisions; full text inaccessible, so no per-patient detail.

ⓘ

What happened

Rudy, Gomes, Tkemaladze and 13 co-authors published “Artificial Intelligence Software Changes Rare Disease Testing Strategy in Real Time: An International Case Series Using Face2Gene”. It appeared online in the American Journal of Medical Genetics Part A on 4 July 2025 (volume 197, issue 11, November 2025). It states: “We present 17 cases involving 19 patients, including one pair of affected siblings and one case involving a child and her affected mother”. For those cases, “DeepGestalt, the technology powering Face2Gene, changed medical geneticists” testing decisions'. Crossref lists author affiliations at the Greenwood Genetic Center, UC San Diego and Rady Children's Hospital, Tbilisi State Medical University and Weill Cornell.

It also lists the University of Nebraska Medical Center, IDI-IRCCS Rome, the Hospital for Sick Children Toronto, UCSF/Fresno and the University of Alabama at Birmingham. It lists FDNA Inc for one author (Nicole Fleischer). It also lists a CC BY-NC-ND license. Despite that license, the full text was not available from Wiley. Unpaywall listed no repository copy and there is no PMC copy. So the sources on record do not give the patients' ages, features, the suggestions made or the tests ordered. The consultations are undated in the abstract.

Purpose. Face2Gene is built to suggest candidate syndromes from facial photographs and clinical features so that geneticists can direct testing. Changed testing decisions were that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The authors state that DeepGestalt's suggestions changed geneticists' testing decisions in real time in all 17 cases. The clinicians, not the software, made the diagnoses. The assessment rests on the abstract alone because the full text was unreadable. One co-author is an FDNA employee. The other 15 are at clinical institutions.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    DeepGestalt changed medical geneticists' testing decisions in 17 cases involving 19 patients, per the abstract read on Europe PMC. That is a measurable change in the test strategy itself. What diagnoses or treatment followed is not stated. The full text is inaccessible.

  • an organization

    The authors claim “a more efficient test strategy”. No counts of tests avoided or time saved are readable in the abstract.

Further evidence

Setting. Geneticists uploading facial photographs and clinical features to Face2Gene during consultations. Cases where recognition was hard: atypical presentation, overlapping dysmorphology or phenotypes across ancestries, per the abstract.

Conditions. The true syndrome is represented in DeepGestalt's training data. Clinicians act on the suggestion by changing the test ordered and confirm by molecular testing.
