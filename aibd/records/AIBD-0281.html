Face2Gene's DeepGestalt changes geneticists' testing in 17 cases

Face2Gene's DeepGestalt facial analysis changed geneticists' testing decisions in 17 retrospectively selected cases involving 19 patients, leading to confirmed diagnoses.

  • 1 an organization

What happened

In a series published online on 4 July 2025, Rudy and colleagues describe 17 cases involving 19 patients from seven institutions in which Face2Gene changed clinicians’ testing decisions. The full article reports that clinicians used DeepGestalt, sometimes with Feature Matcher, to analyze photographs and phenotype terms. Cases 1–3 prompted additional testing, and Cases 4–17 narrowed the testing approach. In Case 3, a strong KBG match prompted a different ANKRD11 assay after an inconclusive exome, which revealed an intragenic deletion. In Case 10, a Cornelia de Lange suggestion led to a panel that included NIPBL, which the originally considered panel omitted. Cases 5–7 illustrate targeted testing after insurance barriers to broader sequencing. The authors selected cases retrospectively from clinicians’ recollections of changed decisions, so the series does not estimate success rates across all consultations. One author was affiliated with FDNA. Independent work places the series in context. Shortly before the series appeared, Wenderott and colleagues at Bonn reported on 23 May 2025 that ten of 15 German geneticists whom they interviewed used Face2Gene in routine care, and that nine used it before molecular testing. Bruwer and colleagues reported on 27 August 2026 that DeepGestalt named the confirmed diagnosis first for only 19% of 36 South African children with a molecular diagnosis.

Details

AI system

Model

DeepGestalt analyzed facial appearance; some clinicians also used Feature Matcher to incorporate phenotype terms.

Role

The software helped clinicians choose additional or narrower tests. In one case, it prompted a different ANKRD11 test after an inconclusive exome; in another, it redirected testing to a panel that included NIPBL.

Inputs and outputs

Clinicians supplied frontal facial photographs and sometimes phenotype terms. Face2Gene returned ranked syndromes and qualitative match scores that informed targeted molecular testing.

Autonomy

Medical geneticists chose the tests and interpreted the clinical and molecular findings.

Workflow

Geneticists uploading facial photographs and clinical features to Face2Gene during consultations. Cases where recognition was hard: atypical presentation, overlapping dysmorphology or phenotypes across ancestries, per the abstract.

Human oversight

Clinicians used DeepGestalt's suggestions to add or narrow molecular tests and interpreted the results. Molecular testing confirmed the diagnoses. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Reach

The series describes 17 cases involving 19 patients, including related family members.

Distribution and access

Targeted testing was particularly useful when insurance would not cover broader sequencing. In Cases 5–7, clinicians found a narrower route to molecular diagnosis.

Duration

No follow-up on the 19 patients was found. A South African cohort study published on 27 August 2026 found low accuracy for African children. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The true syndrome is represented in DeepGestalt's training data. Clinicians act on the suggestion by changing the test ordered and confirm by molecular testing.

Wenderott and colleagues at Bonn and Bruwer and colleagues in South Africa wrote independent studies of Face2Gene in other settings, and neither examined the cases in this series. [1] [2] [3] [4]
