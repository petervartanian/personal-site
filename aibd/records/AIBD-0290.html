Smartwatch alert leads to an ICD and finds a lung adenocarcinoma

depth 3 for a person

found a 28 mm lung adenocarcinoma, resected

A 58-year-old's smartwatch flagged an irregular rate over 180 bpm; NSVT was diagnosed from watch data, an ICD placed, and the post-procedure X-ray found a 28 mm lung adenocarcinoma, resected.

What happened

Cole, Kar, Nimako and Smelt, of St George's Healthcare NHS Trust (thoracic surgery) and East Surrey Hospital (respiratory medicine) in England, reported in JRSM Open (published 4 January 2022; the event dates are not given, so the publication date is used) a previously well 58-year-old non-smoker whose smartwatch alerted him that his heart rate was irregular and exceeding 180 beats per minute while he felt nothing. His GP's 12-lead ECG showed tachycardia without arrhythmia and he was admitted; inpatient ECGs showed incomplete right bundle branch block and intermittent first-degree AV block but captured no significant arrhythmia. Using the smartwatch results, the cardiologist diagnosed non-sustained ventricular tachycardia and an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator was inserted. The post-procedure chest X-ray, taken to exclude pneumothorax, incidentally showed a right upper zone abnormality; CT found a 28 mm lesion, PET showed FDG-avid nodes, and biopsy showed lepidic adenocarcinoma, staged cT1c N0 M0. He underwent a video-assisted right upper lobectomy with node dissection, was discharged on day 10, and the 35 mm tumor was staged pT2a pN0 M0 with clear margins; the nodes showed sarcoidosis and cardiac MRI found no cardiac sarcoid. The authors call this the first reported case of a smartwatch assisting the diagnosis of both NSVT and lung adenocarcinoma. The watch's brand and model are not stated.

Not what it was built to do. The watch's heart-rate alert was its intended function; the lung cancer diagnosis was an incidental consequence of the ICD procedure that the alert set in motion, outside anything the device was built to do.

The system's part

The watch's alert in an asymptomatic man started the chain, and its stored data were what the cardiologist used to diagnose NSVT when hospital tracings captured nothing; the lung cancer was found by a routine post-ICD X-ray, not by the device. The brand is unknown, so the alerting algorithm cannot be identified; the authors describe smartwatch rhythm monitoring generically as photoplethysmography.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. An asymptomatic wearer whose smartwatch alerted to an irregular rate above 180 bpm and who took it to his GP. A cardiologist willing to diagnose from the watch's stored data when hospital tracings were silent. A procedure (ICD insertion) whose routine follow-up imaging happened to cover the lung.

What must hold again. The arrhythmia is one the watch records while hospital monitoring misses it. The incidental finding is in the field of the post-procedure X-ray and is caught early enough for curative surgery; the authors cite an estimated 1 in 25 cancers diagnosed incidentally.
