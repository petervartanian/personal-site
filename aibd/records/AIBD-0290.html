Smartwatch alert leads to an ICD and finds a lung adenocarcinoma

A 58-year-old's smartwatch flagged an irregular rate over 180 bpm; NSVT was diagnosed from watch data, an ICD placed, and the post-procedure X-ray found a 28 mm lung adenocarcinoma, resected.

Workflow

An asymptomatic wearer whose smartwatch alerted to an irregular rate above 180 bpm and who took it to his GP. A cardiologist willing to diagnose from the watch's stored data when hospital tracings were silent. A procedure (ICD insertion) whose routine follow-up imaging happened to cover the lung.

Not assessed

Relation to AI

The watch's alert in an asymptomatic man started the chain. Its stored data were what the cardiologist used to diagnose NSVT when hospital tracings captured nothing. The lung cancer was found by a routine post-ICD X-ray, not by the device. The brand is unknown, so the alerting algorithm cannot be identified. The authors describe smartwatch rhythm monitoring generically as photoplethysmography.

Conditions for replication

The arrhythmia is one the watch records while hospital monitoring misses it. The incidental finding is in the field of the post-procedure X-ray and caught early enough for curative surgery. The authors cite an estimated 1 in 25 cancers diagnosed incidentally.
