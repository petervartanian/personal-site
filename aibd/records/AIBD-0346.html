DIU says UN INSARAG teams in Adıyaman used xView2 damage maps

estimating 3,849 buildings damaged or destroyed

MIT Technology Review (20 Feb 2023), quoting DIU: xView2 used by two UN INSARAG teams in Adıyaman and by AFAD, World Bank, IFRC and WFP; Microsoft reports 3,849 damaged buildings mapped for AFAD.

What happened

MIT Technology Review reported on 20 February 2023 that xView2, an open-source building-damage assessment project sponsored and developed in 2019 by the Pentagon's Defense Innovation Unit (DIU) and Carnegie Mellon University's Software Engineering Institute with partners including Microsoft and UC Berkeley, uses machine-learning algorithms (semantic segmentation) on satellite imagery to identify and grade building damage. Ritwik Gupta, DIU's principal AI scientist, told the magazine that in Türkiye xView2 had been used by at least two ground teams of search and rescue personnel from the UN's International Search and Rescue Advisory Group in Adıyaman, that it helped workers find damaged areas they were unaware of, and that AFAD, the World Bank, the IFRC and WFP had all used the platform; Atishay Abbhi, a World Bank disaster risk management specialist, said the same degree of assessment would typically take weeks and now takes hours or minutes. The article notes that the first usable satellite images came on 9 February 2023, three days after the first quake, and that the model is up to 85 or 90% accurate. Microsoft's February 2023 'Turkey Earthquake Report' (authors including Gupta and Microsoft AI for Good staff) states that the team partnered with AFAD to deliver building-level damage estimates over four cities in southeast Türkiye from imagery of the first three days, estimating 3,849 buildings damaged or destroyed, with Marash the most affected at 7.44% of buildings. The earlier verifier found that the World Bank GRADE report does not mention xView2, AI or Microsoft, that DLR's peer-reviewed account states its own AI damage products were not part of the viewer delivered to responders, and that Direct Relief's December 2025 article (fetched here) describes a research study on Microsoft and Google models rather than a response decision. The publication date of the MIT Technology Review article is used; no responder statement describing a decision taken on xView2 output was fetched.

What it was built to do. Rapid building-damage assessment from pre- and post-event satellite imagery for humanitarian assistance and disaster response; this use was that purpose.

The system's part

The AI produced the damage classifications that responders are said to have used; which decisions changed is not documented. The sources are the developer (DIU's Gupta) quoted in one news report, the World Bank specialist quoted in the same report, and Microsoft's own report of delivering estimates to AFAD; World Bank's GRADE report and DLR's account (per the earlier verifier) do not corroborate operational use of AI products.

Replication

What produced it
  • Usable high-resolution post-event satellite imagery (first available 9 February 2023, three days after the quake)
  • A pre-trained open-source damage model (xBD/xView2) run by the developers on request of responding organizations
  • Direct contact between DIU/Microsoft researchers and responders (UN INSARAG teams, AFAD)
What must hold again
  • Cloud-free daytime imagery with a satellite overhead; the article notes far fewer images in remote or less developed areas such as Syria
  • Responder trust in an off-site AI product, which the developer describes as hard to obtain
  • Documentation by the responding agency of what the maps changed, which no fetched source provides
