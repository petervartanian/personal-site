UN INSARAG teams in Adıyaman may have used xView2 damage maps

MIT Technology Review (20 February 2023), quoting DIU: xView2 used by two UN INSARAG teams in Adıyaman and by AFAD, World Bank, IFRC and WFP; Microsoft reports 3,849 damaged buildings mapped for AFAD.

AI system

System

xView2 building damage assessment models (semantic segmentation on satellite imagery, trained on the xBD dataset)

Application developer

US Defense Innovation Unit (DIU) with Carnegie Mellon University Software Engineering Institute; research partners Microsoft AI for Good and UC Berkeley

Workflow

Usable high-resolution post-event satellite imagery (first available 9 February 2023, three days after the quake). A pre-trained open-source damage model (xBD/xView2) run by the developers on request of responding organizations. Direct contact between DIU/Microsoft researchers and responders (UN INSARAG teams, AFAD).

Relation to AI

The AI produced the damage classifications that responders are said to have used. Which decisions changed is not documented. Sources: the developer (DIU's Gupta) and the World Bank specialist, quoted in one news report, and Microsoft's own report of delivering estimates to AFAD. World Bank's GRADE report and DLR's account do not corroborate operational use of AI products.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Cloud-free daytime imagery with a satellite overhead. The article notes far fewer images in remote or less developed areas such as Syria. Responder trust in an off-site AI product, which the developer describes as hard to obtain. Documentation by the responding agency of what the maps changed, which no cited source provides.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Outcome unresolved: 2.

Later findings

Direct Relief's 10 December 2025 article describes a retrospective research study of the Microsoft and Google 2023 models that found they disagreed in significant ways; no later operational use of xView2 was found in the cited sources.

Depth

Reasons

People: No individual rescue or beneficiary is attributed to the maps in any cited source. Organizations: Responders are reported to have received building-level damage maps in hours instead of weeks and to have found damaged areas they were unaware of. The reports come from the developer and a World Bank specialist, not the responding agencies. Society: No downstream effect on rescues, shelter siting or reconstruction is documented. The maps covered part of the affected zone after imagery became usable on 9 February.

  3. News report Independent author

    Ryan-Mosley, T. (2023). How AI can actually be helpful in disaster response. MIT Technology Review, 20 Feb 2023.

    xView2 has been used by at least two different ground teams of search and rescue personnel from the UN’s International Search and Rescue Advisory Group in Adiyaman
    noting Turkey’s Disaster and Emergency Management Presidency, the World Bank, the International Federation of the Red Cross, and the United Nations World Food Programme have all used the platform
    Atishay Abbhi, a disaster risk management specialist at the World Bank, tells me that this same degree of assessment would typically take weeks and now takes hours or minutes.
    It uses machine-learning algorithms in conjunction with satellite imagery from other providers to identify building and infrastructure damage in the disaster area and categorize its severity
