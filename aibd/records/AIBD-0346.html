Rescue workers in Türkiye find damaged areas with xView2 maps

After the 2023 Türkiye earthquakes, xView2, a machine-learning model that grades building damage in satellite images, gave responders maps that UN rescue teams used in Adıyaman.

  • Not rated a person

    No individual rescue or beneficiary is attributed to the maps in any cited source.

  • 2 an organization

    The developer and a World Bank specialist report that responders received building-level damage maps within hours or minutes, where such an assessment would typically take weeks. The developer reports that teams found damaged areas that they had been unaware of. No responding agency gives its own account.

  • Not rated society

    No downstream effect on rescues, shelter siting or reconstruction is documented. The maps covered part of the affected zone after imagery became usable on 9 February 2023.

What happened

In 2019 the Pentagon's Defense Innovation Unit (DIU) and Carnegie Mellon University's Software Engineering Institute sponsored and developed xView2, an open-source building-damage assessment project. Partners included Microsoft and the University of California, Berkeley. It uses machine learning (semantic segmentation) on satellite imagery to identify and grade building damage. Microsoft's February 2023 “Turkey Earthquake Report” has authors who include Ritwik Gupta, DIU's principal AI scientist, and Microsoft AI for Good staff. It states that the team partnered with Türkiye's Disaster and Emergency Management Presidency (AFAD) to deliver building-level damage estimates over four cities in southeast Türkiye. The report’s image dates range from 7 to 9 February 2023. It estimated 3,849 buildings damaged or destroyed, with Marash the most affected at 7.44% of buildings. MIT Technology Review reported on xView2 on 20 February 2023. Gupta told the magazine that at least two ground teams of search and rescue personnel from the UN's International Search and Rescue Advisory Group (INSARAG) had used xView2 in Adıyaman. He said that it helped workers find damaged areas that they were unaware of. He said that AFAD, the World Bank, the International Federation of the Red Cross (IFRC) and the United Nations World Food Programme (WFP) had all used the platform. Atishay Abbhi, a World Bank disaster risk management specialist, said that the same degree of assessment would typically take weeks and now takes hours or minutes. The article gives 9 February 2023 as the first date with usable images. The technical report that it links includes 7 February imagery for Islahiye and 9 February imagery for the other three cities. The article says that the model is up to 85 or 90% accurate. The World Bank GRADE report does not mention xView2, AI or Microsoft. DLR's peer-reviewed account states that its own AI damage products were not part of the viewer delivered to responders. No responder statement that describes a decision taken on xView2 output is among the sources. On 27 October 2025 researchers from Stanford University and Direct Relief published in PLOS Digital Health an outside test of the Microsoft model built on xBD, the dataset behind xView2. Among 38 hospitals and dialysis centers in three cities, the model found 42.86% of the damaged facilities and correctly cleared 93.55% of the undamaged ones. The authors conclude that such models cannot yet replace ground surveys. Direct Relief's article of 10 December 2025 describes this research study on Microsoft and Google models. It does not describe a response decision.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Satellite images were converted into maps that located buildings and classified their apparent earthquake damage.

Workflow

Usable high-resolution post-event satellite imagery (first available 9 February 2023, three days after the quake). A pre-trained open-source damage model (xBD/xView2) run by the developers on request of responding organizations. Direct contact between DIU/Microsoft researchers and responders (UN INSARAG teams, AFAD).

Human oversight

Analysts supplied local training labels, and GIS specialists could adjust damage thresholds for individual buildings. The sources do not document a specific rescue dispatch made from an automated result.

Benefits

Reach

No source counts beneficiaries. Microsoft's estimate of 3,849 damaged or destroyed buildings across four cities counts buildings. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The record documents use only in February 2023, after the earthquakes of 6 February 2023. In October 2025 researchers from Stanford University and Direct Relief reported that the Microsoft model built on xBD found 42.86% of the damaged health facilities in their test. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Retrospective accuracy study. The authors overlaid each model's building damage output on geocoded health facilities and compared the result with reference damage data. For dialysis centers the reference was data from the Turkish Nephrological Institute; for hospitals it was the consensus of two reviewers who examined satellite images. [5]

Setting and sample

25 hospitals, 13 dialysis facilities and 454 pharmacies in Antakya, Kahramanmaraş and Iskenderun after the February 2023 earthquakes; sensitivity and specificity computed for the 38 hospitals and dialysis centers. [5]

Measures

Sensitivity, specificity and agreement (Cohen's kappa) for detecting damaged facilities. [5]

Results

Microsoft model: sensitivity 42.86%, specificity 93.55%; sensitivity 100% for hospitals and 0% for dialysis centers; agreement with the Google model kappa 0.32. Aggregating damage over 0.125 km² cells raised sensitivity to 71.4%. [5]

Additional value from AI

Not measured. The study compares two models with reference data and does not compare them with a human-only damage survey. [5]

Limitations

Small sample with a wide interval (95% CI 9.90% to 81.59%); hospital reference based on imagery review; no reference data for pharmacies; the study does not examine whether responders used the maps. [5]

Denominators

Model output for three cities in the February 2023 response. [5]

Materials for replication

Open-access article with methods and results tables. [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Cloud-free daytime imagery with a satellite overhead. The article notes far fewer images in remote or less developed areas such as Syria. Responder trust in an off-site AI product, which the developer describes as hard to obtain. Documentation by the responding agency of what the maps changed, which no cited source provides.
