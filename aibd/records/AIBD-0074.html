Google model predicts the lost colors of Klimt's destroyed paintings

A colorization model from the Google Arts & Culture Lab proposed colors for photographs of Klimt's destroyed Faculty Paintings, which the Belvedere showed at original size in 2025.

  • 1 an organization

    The Belvedere adopted the reconstructions and showed them in its 2025 exhibition, according to its exhibition page.

  • 1 society

    The public can view a hypothesis of the colors of lost works. In a statement that Jing Daily quotes, Smola said that the result was surprising in areas where no knowledge of the colors existed.

The system’s part
The model proposed colors for the monochrome photographs. Known colors from Smola's archival research were added directly, so the output mixes documented evidence with learned statistical guesses. The Belvedere calls it an attempt at reconstruction, and no source claims the colors are verified.
Limits
The original paintings are lost, so many reconstructed colors cannot be checked against the originals. Archival color descriptions and model-generated guesses are combined, and exhibition display does not validate historical color fidelity.

What happened

Gustav Klimt's Faculty Paintings (Philosophy, Medicine and Jurisprudence) were made for the ceiling of the University of Vienna's Great Hall. Fire destroyed them in 1945; only black-and-white photographs survive. On 7 October 2021 Google Arts & Culture unveiled the online exhibition Klimt vs. Klimt – The Man of Contradictions with the Belvedere. For it, Google Arts & Culture Lab resident Emil Wallner trained a colorization model on 91,749 artworks and then on Klimt's color paintings. Curator Franz Smola's compilation of contemporary descriptions of the colors guided him. On 24 November 2021 Wired published a feature based on its own interviews. In it Smola said that the process re-colorizes the photographs. He said that it does not recreate the original colors. The conservator Ben Fino-Radin called it a misnomer to describe such work as restoration. The Belvedere describes the result as an attempt to reconstruct the original colors in which artificial intelligence played an essential role. It showed the color reconstructions at original size in its exhibition Gustav Klimt – Pigment & Pixel (20 February to 7 September 2025).

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The colorization model proposed colors for surviving monochrome photographs, while documented color descriptions supplied fixed guidance for particular regions.

Workflow

Surviving black-and-white photographs of the destroyed paintings. A colorization model pre-trained on 91,749 artworks and fine-tuned on Klimt's color paintings. Curator-compiled written color testimony injected as fixed colors.

Human oversight

Curator Franz Smola researched historical descriptions, and Emil Wallner used that evidence to guide the model's output for institutional display.

The reconstructions went online on 7 October 2021. The Belvedere showed them at original size and in their ceiling arrangement in its exhibition Gustav Klimt – Pigment & Pixel, from 20 February to 7 September 2025. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
