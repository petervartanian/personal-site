What happened

Gustav Klimt's Faculty Paintings (Philosophy, Medicine and Jurisprudence) were made for the ceiling of the University of Vienna's Great Hall. Fire destroyed them in 1945; only black-and-white photographs survive. The online exhibition Klimt vs. Klimt – The Man of Contradictions was unveiled by Google Arts & Culture with the Belvedere on 7 October 2021. For it, Google Arts & Culture Lab resident Emil Wallner trained a colorization model on 91,749 artworks and then on Klimt's color paintings. Curator Franz Smola's compilation of contemporary descriptions of the colors guided him. The Belvedere describes the result as an attempt to reconstruct the original colors in which artificial intelligence played an essential role. It showed the color reconstructions at original size in its exhibition Gustav Klimt – Pigment & Pixel (20 February to 7 September 2025).

What it was built to do. Colorize black-and-white photographs of destroyed paintings in the artist's style, guided by archival color descriptions

The system's part

The model proposed colors for the monochrome photographs. Known colors from Smola's archival research were added directly, so the output mixes documented evidence with learned statistical guesses. The Belvedere calls it an attempt at reconstruction, and no source claims the colors are verified.

Replication

What produced it. Surviving black-and-white photographs of the destroyed paintings. A colorization model pre-trained on 91,749 artworks and fine-tuned on Klimt's color paintings. Curator-compiled written color testimony injected as fixed colors.

What must hold again. Monochrome photographs of adequate quality exist. Written color descriptions exist for key areas. An institutional partner validates and displays the result.
