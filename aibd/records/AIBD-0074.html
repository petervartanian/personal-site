Google Arts & Culture Lab recolours Klimt's destroyed paintings

ML colour reconstruction of Klimt's destroyed Faculty Paintings launched 7 Oct 2021 with the Belvedere, shown in its 2025 exhibition

What happened

Gustav Klimt's Faculty Paintings (Philosophy, Medicine and Jurisprudence), created for the ceiling of the University of Vienna's Great Hall, were destroyed by fire in 1945 and survive only in black-and-white photographs. For the online exhibition Klimt vs. Klimt – The Man of Contradictions, unveiled by Google Arts & Culture with the Belvedere on 7 October 2021, Google Arts & Culture Lab resident Emil Wallner trained a colourisation model on 91,749 artworks and then on Klimt's colour paintings, guided by curator Franz Smola's compilation of contemporary descriptions of the colours. The Belvedere describes the result as an attempt to reconstruct the original colours in which artificial intelligence played an essential role, and showed the colour reconstructions at original size in its exhibition Gustav Klimt – Pigment & Pixel (20 February to 7 September 2025).

What it was built to do. Colourise black-and-white photographs of destroyed paintings in the artist's style, guided by archival colour descriptions

The system's part

The model proposed colours for the monochrome photographs; known colours from Smola's archival research were added directly, so the output mixes documented evidence with learned statistical guesses. The Belvedere calls it an attempt at reconstruction, and no source claims the colours are verified.

Replication

What produced it
  • Surviving black-and-white photographs of the destroyed paintings
  • A colourisation model pre-trained on 91,749 artworks and fine-tuned on Klimt's colour paintings
  • Curator-compiled written colour testimony injected as fixed colours
What must hold again
  • Monochrome photographs of adequate quality exist
  • Written colour descriptions exist for key areas
  • An institutional partner validates and displays the result
