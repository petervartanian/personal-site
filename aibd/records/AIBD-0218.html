Supreme Court of India publishes TERES AI hearing transcripts

From 21 February 2023 the Supreme Court of India transcribed Constitution Bench hearings live with an AI service from TERES, and by February 2024 transcripts appeared on its website within days.

  • 1 an organization

    The court gained lawyer-verified transcripts of Constitution Bench hearings on a trial basis. It had published them for 9 of 25 hearings by May 2023 and for almost ten main matters by December 2023, and by February 2024 it published them regularly within days of a hearing.

  • 1 society

    Transcripts were published on the court's website for public and academic use, for only a minority of hearings in the first months. By February 2024 they appeared regularly within days of a hearing, and a judge reproduced part of one in the Electoral Bonds judgment.

The system’s part
The AI produced the live draft. Lawyers proofread before publication and, per the Chief Justice, people oversee overlapping speech. The published-transcript rate (36% of hearings by May 2023) shows the benefit depended as much on human vetting as on the recognizer.
Limits
Live drafting and publication are distinct outcomes. Nine published transcripts out of 25 early hearings show incomplete delivery, while later reports use main matters as their denominator. Those counts cannot be directly combined into a trend. Accuracy, accessibility gains, and time saved remain unmeasured.

What happened

On 21 February 2023 Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud's Constitution Bench heard the Maharashtra political crisis matter in Court Room 1. It used AI live transcription supplied by TERES, with screens facing the lawyers showing the text. The Chief Justice called it an experiment. He said that transcripts would go to lawyers for verification and then onto the Supreme Court website (LiveLaw, 21 February 2023). The Supreme Court Observer reported on 18 May 2023 that 25 Constitution Bench hearings had been held across four cases. Only nine transcripts had been published: six for the marriage-equality case, three for the Maharashtra case and none for the other two. None was published in March 2023. NDTV reported on 8 December 2023 a written reply in the Lok Sabha by Law Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal. He said that AI transcription had been deployed for Constitution Bench matters since February. He said that transcripts in almost ten main matters had been generated and published on the court's website. The use was on a trial basis. The Supreme Court Observer reported on 20 February 2024 that transcripts were by then regularly published on the court website within days of a Constitution Bench hearing. It also reported that Justice Sanjiv Khanna reproduced part of a hearing transcript in the Electoral Bonds judgment of 15 February 2024. A Press Information Bureau feature of 11 February 2026 states that the system is being extended to regular hearing days. It says that the extension follows successful use in Constitution Bench proceedings.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Courtroom speech produced live draft text on screens and transcripts for subsequent verification and publication.

Workflow

A speech-to-text service installed in one courtroom with screens for counsel. A verification step in which lawyers proofread transcripts before publication. Court leadership willing to run it as an experiment on Constitution Bench matters.

Human oversight

The CJI described a procedure in which lawyers would check transcripts before publication and human overseers would resolve overlapping speech. The later government account confirms that final validation remained with people.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Human vetting capacity, since unpublished transcripts accumulated in March 2023. Handling of overlapping speech in the courtroom. Continued institutional backing (the Law Ministry described it as trial use).
