Supreme Court of India publishes TERES AI hearing transcripts

depth 1 for an organization, depth 1 for society

From 21 Feb 2023 the Supreme Court of India transcribed Constitution Bench hearings live with an AI service from TERES; 9 of 25 hearings had published transcripts by May 2023, and almost 10 matters...

What happened

On 21 February 2023 Chief Justice D.Y. Chandrachud's Constitution Bench, hearing the Maharashtra political crisis matter in Court Room 1, used AI live transcription supplied by TERES, with screens facing the lawyers showing the text; the CJI called it an experiment and said transcripts would be sent to lawyers for verification and uploaded to the Supreme Court website (LiveLaw, 21 February 2023). The Supreme Court Observer reported on 18 May 2023 that 25 Constitution Bench hearings had been held across four cases but only nine transcripts had been published (six for the marriage-equality case, three for the Maharashtra case, none for the other two), with none published in March 2023.

NDTV reported on 8 December 2023 a written reply in the Lok Sabha by Law Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal that AI transcription had been deployed for Constitution Bench matters since February, that transcripts in almost ten main matters had been generated and published on the court's website, and that the use was on a trial basis. A Press Information Bureau feature of 11 February 2026 states that the system is being extended to regular hearing days after successful use in Constitution Bench proceedings.

What it was built to do. The transcription was introduced to give judges, lawyers and law colleges a record of arguments and to make the court a court of record; published transcripts are that purpose.

Who benefited

  • 1
    an organization

    The court gained lawyer-verified transcripts for part of its Constitution Bench hearings (9 of 25 by May 2023; almost ten matters by December 2023), on a trial basis.

  • 1
    society

    Transcripts were published on the court's website for public and academic use, but only for a minority of hearings in the first months.

The system's part

The AI produced the live draft; lawyers proofread before publication and, per the CJI, persons oversee overlapping speech. The published-transcript rate (36% of hearings by May 2023) shows the benefit depended on the human vetting step as much as on the recognizer.

Replication

What produced it. A speech-to-text service installed in one courtroom with screens for counsel. A verification step in which lawyers proofread transcripts before publication. Court leadership willing to run it as an experiment on Constitution Bench matters.

What must hold again. Human vetting capacity, since unpublished transcripts accumulated in March 2023. Handling of overlapping speech in the courtroom. Continued institutional backing (the Law Ministry described it as trial use).
