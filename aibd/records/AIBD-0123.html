NEO BCI lets a man quadriplegic for 14 years drink unaided

54-year-old man, quadriplegic for 14 years, drank unaided by driving a pneumatic glove through the NEO epidural BCI; announced 29 January 2024.

ⓘ

What happened

On 24 October 2023, teams led by Zhao Guoguang (Xuanwu Hospital) and Hong Bo (Tsinghua University) implanted the NEO wireless epidural brain-computer interface (BCI). The patient was a 54-year-old man with complete C4 spinal cord injury (ASIA A) from a car accident, quadriplegic for 14 years. Discharged ten days later, he trained three months at home. At a 29 January 2024 trial-phase summary meeting the team reported he could drive a pneumatic glove with his brain activity to drink water unaided. Grasp-decoding accuracy was above 90%, with improved ASIA scores and somatosensory evoked potentials. A September 2024 preprint reported nine months of home use: a 0.91 grasp-detection F1-score, eating and drinking, and a 27-point ARAT gain.

Purpose. NEO was built to let people with tetraplegia control external equipment, including a pneumatic exoskeleton glove, from epidural brain signals. The aim is assistance and hand-function rehabilitation at home. Restoring this patient's grasp so that he could drink was that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The AI contribution is the decoder, a Riemannian-geometry classifier with a hidden Markov model for temporal smoothing. Calibrated in about ten minutes on beta, low-gamma and high-gamma features of eight-channel epidural ECoG, it detects grasp intention and triggers the pneumatic glove. The preprint reports 94% calibration accuracy and a grasp-detection F1-score rising above 0.9 after three months (average 0.91 over nine months). It gives 100% object-transfer success with the BCI against 35% without. The electrodes, implant, surgery and glove are not AI. The authors attribute the later neurological recovery to repeated BCI training, not the decoder alone.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    After 14 years of complete quadriplegia the patient regained the ability to drink and eat by himself through the BCI-driven glove. The preprint reports neurological recovery: upper-limb motor score +5, ARAT +27, and the ability to hold objects without the BCI.

Further evidence

Setting. Eight chronic epidural electrodes over the sensorimotor cortex of a patient whose cortex still generated grasp-related signals after 14 years of complete C4 injury. A grasp-intention decoder calibrated in about ten minutes and robust to the signal drift and chewing artifacts of home use. A battery-free implant powered and read through the scalp, so the patient could train at home daily after discharge ten days post-surgery. A wearable pneumatic glove able to execute the decoded grasp on the patient's right hand, which retained limited forearm movement.

Conditions. The patient's motor cortex must still produce decodable grasp intention despite years of paralysis. The decoder must stay accurate without frequent recalibration across months of home use. The patient or a caregiver must be able to attach the external coil and the glove for daily sessions. Neurological recovery beyond assisted grasping is a single-patient finding and must be reproduced in further patients.
