TeleAI plans learned scheduling for greener computing clusters

TeleAI describes learned scheduling with large language model explanations for computing clusters; its reported GPU gains come from virtualization and a heuristic scheduler.

  • Not rated an organization

    Better scheduling with AI is intended. The 150% rise in GPU availability comes from virtualization and resource sharing, and the 49% cut in unallocated GPUs comes from a heuristic scheduler on an emulated cluster.

  • Not rated society

    Lower emissions from AI-assisted scheduling are intended, and no source reports an energy or carbon reduction that can be attributed to it.

What happened

In July 2023, Qizhen Weng and co-authors at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) and Alibaba presented Fragmentation Gradient Descent, a heuristic scheduler, at the 2023 USENIX Annual Technical Conference. The authors report that it cut unallocated GPUs by up to 49% against packing-based schedulers on an emulated cluster of more than 6,200 GPUs. Weng is the TeleAI contact for this use case, and this heuristic is the scheduler with published results. In June 2025, TeleAI described a system that is designed to use learned scheduling, including reinforcement-learning agents and explanations from large language models, to improve how computing resources are allocated. Its reported 150% increase in GPU availability comes from virtualization and resource sharing. Improvements in AI-assisted scheduling and energy use remain intended, with no measured gain attributed to those functions. No source through October 2026 reports that TeleAI uses a learned scheduler or one assisted by a large language model. No source reports a measured energy or carbon saving.
