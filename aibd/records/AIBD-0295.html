Dexcom G6's Urgent Low Soon alert may cut time below 54 mg/dL

Dexcom's analyses of 1,424 and 15,000 users link the G6's 20-minute Urgent Low Soon alert to less hypoglycemia; authors of an independent trial in 24 adults found a reduction only below 2.8 mmol/L.

  • 2 a person

    Dexcom's authors report that time below 54 mg/dL fell from 1.0% to 0.6% for G6 users in their own analysis. The authors of the one independent randomized trial of the alert found a difference only below 2.8 mmol/L. No source credits the alert with averting a specific serious event.

The system’s part
The alert is generated when the G6 algorithm forecasts glucose at or below 55 mg/dL within 20 minutes. Users still have to act on it. The principal outcome evidence is the maker's own real-world analyses (Puhr et al. 2019, 2020). They compare the same users before and after the G6 transition without a control arm. The independent Imperial College PACE randomized trial found no difference at 3.0 or 3.9 mmol/L after exercise and a difference only below 2.8 mmol/L. The Imperial switch study cannot separate the alert from the change of device. None of the sources describes the prediction method as machine learning.
Limits
No study isolates the alert in routine use. Dexcom's analyses compare the same users before and after the switch without a control arm, and the Imperial switch study and the ALERTT1 trial change the whole device. The 2022 authors could not test whether the alert changed outcomes, and the prediction algorithm is proprietary.
Costs and harms
Repeated or nuisance alerts can make users mute warnings, which removes the opportunity for earlier intervention.
Later
Persisted, 8 September 2022. A Dexcom analysis of users in Germany, Sweden and the United Kingdom found that hypoglycemia fell after the G6 switch and that the gains were sustained over the first 15 months. ULS use fell from 96% to 98% of users to 84% to 87% by the end of the study, and the authors could not test whether the alert itself changed outcomes.

What happened

The Dexcom G6 continuous glucose monitor was classified by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) under De Novo DEN170088 on 27 March 2018. Its “Urgent Low Soon” (ULS) alert fires when an algorithm predicts an estimated glucose value of 55 mg/dL or below within 20 minutes. The FDA decision summary describes the threshold and predictive alerts as intended to warn patients to act before a hypoglycemic event. Dexcom employees Puhr, Derdzinski, Welsh, Parker, Walker and Price compared 1,424 users before and after they moved from the G5 (no predictive alert) to the G6 between 1 May and 31 August 2018. Their anonymized comparison was in Diabetes Technology & Therapeutics (online 22 March 2019). With a 70 mg/dL low-threshold setting (n=658), time below 54 mg/dL fell from 1.0% to 0.6%. With an 80 mg/dL setting (n=766), it fell from 0.6% to 0.4% (both P<0.001). ULS fired 0.9 and 0.6 times a day. A second Dexcom analysis (Puhr et al., Journal of Diabetes Science and Technology, online 3 April 2019; abstract only) had 15,000 G6 app users. More than 93% had ULS enabled. Its use was associated with less time below 55 and 70 mg/dL regardless of how often users viewed the app. Clinicians at six Belgian hospitals ran the Dexcom-funded ALERTT1 trial and reported on 2 June 2021 that 3 of 127 adults on the G6 had severe hypoglycemia over six months, against 13 of 127 on FreeStyle Libre. They compared whole systems, so the result does not isolate the alert. In 2022 Dexcom staff reported that hypoglycemia also fell after the switch in Germany, Sweden and the United Kingdom. ULS use dropped from 96% to 98% of users to 84% to 87%, and the authors could not test whether the alert itself made the difference. Independent evidence is thinner. The PACE crossover randomized trial at Imperial College Healthcare NHS Trust had 24 adults with type 1 diabetes. Each had ULS on or off for 40 days. Its authors found no difference in hypoglycemia below 3.0 or 3.9 mmol/L in the 24 hours after exercise. Time below 2.8 mmol/L was lower with ULS on (P=0.04). In 2024 researchers in Ulm argued that outside checks of predictive alerts are hard because the algorithms are proprietary. Preechasuk et al. (Imperial College London) had 29 adults who switched from FreeStyle Libre 2 to a Dexcom G7 with ULS. They spent less time below 70 mg/dL (3.0% to 2.0%, P=0.006). That design changes the device and the alert together. Clinicians in Prague later reported a patient with impaired hypoglycemia awareness whose continuous glucose monitor with ULS on did not eliminate prolonged hypoglycemia. He sometimes muted the alerts. None of the sources calls the predictive algorithm machine learning.

Details

AI system

Configuration

The vendor analyses and PACE trial used Dexcom G6 devices. The Preechasuk study used Dexcom G7 devices.

Inputs and outputs

Continuous glucose estimates generated a warning when glucose was predicted to reach 55 mg/dL or below within 20 minutes.

Workflow

Real-time CGM with the ULS alert enabled (over 93% of G6 app users in Dexcom's sample). Users who respond to the alert by treating or adjusting insulin, which the sources assume rather than measure. A low-threshold alert also set (70 or 80 mg/dL in the Dexcom analysis).

Benefits

Reach

Dexcom's main analysis covered 1,424 users and its second analysis 15,000 G6 app users; the sources do not count how many of them avoided a low. In the ALERTT1 trial, 3 of 127 adults on the G6 had severe hypoglycemia, against 13 of 127 on FreeStyle Libre. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Duration

A 2022 Dexcom analysis of users in Germany, Sweden and the United Kingdom found that the fall in hypoglycemia after the G6 switch was sustained over the first 15 months. Use of the Urgent Low Soon alert fell from 96% to 98% of users to 84% to 87% by the end of that study. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Measurement

Measurement

Study 1: Sensor glucose and reported severe hypoglycemia events over six months. Study 2: Anonymized CGM data uploaded to Dexcom Clarity, in three-month intervals over two years. [6] [7]

Setting and sample

Study 1: Six hospitals in Belgium; 254 adults with type 1 diabetes who already used FreeStyle Libre, recruited 29 January to 30 July 2019. Study 2: Users in Germany, Sweden and the United Kingdom who switched from G5 to G6 9 to 12 months after the G6 launch in each country, followed in a two-year window from 2018 to 2020. [6] [7]

Measures

Study 1: Time below 3.0 mmol/L; participants with severe hypoglycemia. Study 2: Median percent time below 3.0 and 3.9 mmol/L; ULS use. [6] [7]

Results

Study 1: Time below 3.0 mmol/L 0.47% against 0.84% (p=0.0070); severe hypoglycemia in 3 against 13 participants (p=0.0082). Study 2: Time below 3.0 mmol/L fell 0.12 to 0.28 percentage points in the first three months, with a further 0.11 to 0.21 point fall in months three to six. [6] [7]

Additional value from AI

Study 1: Not isolated: the G6 arm received real-time readings and all G6 alerts together. Study 2: Not isolated; the authors could not link ULS use to outcomes. [6] [7]

Limitations

Study 1: Open-label; Dexcom funded the trial; the predictive alert cannot be separated from the other features of the G6. Study 2: No control group; country of residence taken from user-reported app data; written by Dexcom staff and a Dexcom-funded academic. [6] [7]

Denominators

Study 1: Six months of Dexcom G6 use. [6] [7]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Users keep the alert on rather than muting it for alarm fatigue, as the Prague patient sometimes did. Sensor accuracy sufficient to avoid nuisance alerts, which Puhr et al. Cite as a cause of CGM discontinuation. Benefit beyond a plain threshold alert, which the PACE trial found only below 2.8 mmol/L.

Replication attempts

5 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 4. Benefit did not recur: 1.

Evidence

Four of the five authors of the 2022 analysis of users in Germany, Sweden and the United Kingdom are Dexcom employees, and the fifth has received Dexcom funding. Dexcom funded the ALERTT1 trial. The PACE trial and the Preechasuk et al. Switch study at Imperial College are independent of the maker. The Ulm institute where the authors of the 2024 review work has received research support and fees from Dexcom and other makers. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]
