Google DR screening in Thailand matches specialists at 94.7%

depth 2 for a person, depth 1 for an organization

94.7% accuracy for vision-threatening DR, 2,412 referrals

At nine primary-care sites (Dec 2018-Mar 2020) Google's deep-learning system graded fundus photos in real time, 94.7% accuracy for vision-threatening DR, 2,412 referrals; a fifth of images rejected.

What happened

Ruamviboonsuk et al. (Lancet Digital Health, 7 March 2022), a prospective interventional cohort study funded by Google and Rajavithi Hospital with Google Health co-authors, deployed a deep-learning system at nine primary-care sites of Thailand's national diabetic retinopathy screening program between 12 December 2018 and 29 March 2020. Of 7,940 patients screened, 7,651 were analyzed and 2,412 (31.5%) were referred for retinopathy, macular edema, ungradable images or low acuity; for vision-threatening retinopathy the system's accuracy was 94.7%, sensitivity 91.4% and specificity 95.4%, against 93.5%, 84.8% and 95.5% for retina-specialist over-readers. MIT Technology Review (27 April 2020) reported Google's own field study of the deployment in 11 clinics: more than a fifth of images were rejected, nurses were frustrated by rejections and slow internet, and when it worked the system sped screening. The Nation Thailand (10 February 2025) reported Google and Perceptra planning AI screening for about 1 million people in Thailand over a decade, citing more than 600,000 patients screened with the model globally. The earlier verifier saw the abstract only and did not enter it to stay within the record cap. The date is the publication date.

The system's part

The deep-learning grades and referral recommendations were delivered in real time with specialist over-reading as a safety net; accuracy figures come from the vendor-funded, vendor co-authored prospective study, while an independent news account of Google's field study records substantial image rejection and nurse frustration.

Replication

What produced it. Fundus photography at primary-care sites. Real-time deep-learning grading with referral recommendations. Retina-specialist over-reading as a safety mechanism.

What must hold again. Image quality sufficient for grading (a fifth of images were rejected in the 2020 field study). Reliable connectivity. Referral pathways able to absorb referrals.
