Google software grades Thai diabetic eye photos as well as specialists

Google's deep-learning system, ARDA, graded eye photographs in real time at nine Thai primary-care sites with 94.7% accuracy for vision-threatening diabetic retinopathy; 2,412 patients were referred.

  • 2 a person

    The study analyzed 7,651 patients who received real-time grading with accuracy similar to that of retina specialists, and 2,412 of them were referred.

  • 1 an organization

    Primary-care sites received results in real time. MIT Technology Review reported that the system sped screening when it worked, and it also reported image rejections and connectivity problems.

  • Not rated society

    No source reports a vision outcome across the population.

Accuracy figures come from the vendor-funded, vendor co-authored prospective study. An independent news account of Google's field study records substantial image rejection and nurse frustration.
Limits
Diagnostic accuracy and referral counts do not measure vision preserved, and no source reports visual outcomes for the referred patients. Referrals also included ungradable images and low acuity. The plan to screen about 1 million people in Thailand over a decade is an announcement, and the figure of more than one million patients screened comes from the makers' own comment and a summary of it.
Costs and harms
Image-quality rejection required repeat photography and sometimes unnecessary referrals. Slow or failed internet connections delayed screening, despite rapid grading when connectivity worked.

What happened

Between 12 December 2018 and 29 March 2020 Google's deep-learning system graded fundus photographs in real time at nine primary-care sites of Thailand's national diabetic retinopathy screening program. On 27 April 2020 MIT Technology Review reported Google's own field study of the deployment in 11 clinics. Over a fifth of images were rejected. Nurses were frustrated by the rejections and by slow internet connections. When the system worked, it sped screening. On 7 March 2022 Ruamviboonsuk and colleagues reported the results of the deployment in The Lancet Digital Health. Google and Rajavithi Hospital funded their prospective interventional cohort study, and Google Health staff were among the co-authors. Of 7,940 patients screened, 7,651 were analyzed. Of these, 2,412 (31.5%) were referred for retinopathy, macular edema, ungradable images or low acuity. For vision-threatening retinopathy the system's accuracy was 94.7%, its sensitivity 91.4% and its specificity 95.4%. The retina specialists who over-read the images scored 93.5%, 84.8% and 95.5%. On 10 January 2025 Chotcomwongse and colleagues, two of them working for Google, reported that 89.1% of patients with a true-positive AI referral at a community hospital in Ayutthaya adhered to the referral. On 10 February 2025 The Nation Thailand reported that Google and Perceptra planned AI screening for about 1 million people in Thailand over a decade. It cited over 600,000 patients screened with the model globally. In a meta-analysis published on 17 April 2026, Oxford-led researchers reported that AI-assisted screening raised referral uptake, and they attributed much of the gain to redesigned care pathways. On 23 September 2026 Google and partner-site authors described in Nature Medicine how they scaled the same tool across India, Thailand and Australia. In October 2026 an AI Weekly alert reported that the model, ARDA, had screened more than one million patients, with Perceptra licensed to run screening in Thailand.

Details

AI system

Role

The model graded retinal images for signs of diabetic retinopathy and recommended referral.

Configuration

The deployment used the Automated Retinal Disease Assessment system, ARDA.

Inputs and outputs

Retinal photographs produced disease grades and real-time referral recommendations, with specialists reading the images as a safety check.

Workflow

Fundus photography at primary-care sites. Real-time deep-learning grading with referral recommendations. Retina-specialist over-reading as a safety mechanism.

Human oversight

Retina specialists over-read the system's grades and referral recommendations as a safety net, and the study compared their accuracy with that of the system. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Patients with diabetes at participating primary-care sites received screening, and 2,412 analyzed patients were referred for disease, image-quality or visual-acuity concerns.

Reach

Of 7,940 patients screened in the cohort, 7,651 were analyzed and 2,412 were referred. No source counts patients whose vision the screening preserved. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Duration

The cohort ran from 12 December 2018 to 29 March 2020. In 2025 researchers reported that 89.1% of patients with a true-positive AI referral at a community hospital in the same program adhered to it. By October 2026 the model had screened more than one million patients in three countries, according to an AI Weekly alert. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Image quality sufficient for grading (a fifth of images were rejected in the 2020 field study). Reliable connectivity. Referral pathways able to absorb referrals.
