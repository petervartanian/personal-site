NHC's 2025 verification: Google DeepMind ensemble mean beat official Atlantic track forecasts at 12-72 h; first AI models in NHC operations.

What happened

The National Hurricane Center's Forecast Verification Report for the 2025 season, dated 30 March 2026, states that 2025 marked the first time NHC incorporated AI-based models into real-time operations. In the Atlantic track comparison, the Google DeepMind ensemble mean (GDMI) slightly outperformed the official forecast at lead times from 12 to 72 h, while official forecasts outperformed the consensus aids TVCA, HCCA and FSSE. In the eastern North Pacific, GDMI performed considerably better than every other individual model and beat the official forecast and all consensus aids at 48-120 h; for intensity it was the top individual model from 12 to 48 h. GDMI was not available early in the season, so the first storms are excluded from these comparisons.

What it was built to do. Experimental AI tropical cyclone forecast model providing ensemble track and intensity guidance to NHC forecasters.

The system's part

NHC's own homogeneous verification isolates GDMI's track and intensity performance relative to official forecasts, consensus aids and other models; the sample excludes early-season storms when GDMI was unavailable.

Replication

What produced it. Real-time availability of the Google DeepMind ensemble to NHC for most of the 2025 season. NHC's standard homogeneous verification procedure applied to early-model versions of the guidance.

What must hold again. Consistent timely delivery of the AI guidance (NHC notes some AI systems were not consistently available). Results from one season; NHC's Science Operations Officer says it takes a long time to fully evaluate new models.
