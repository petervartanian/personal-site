DeepMind's AI predicts 2025 hurricane tracks better than US forecasters

In 2025, its first season with AI models in operations, the US National Hurricane Center found that Google DeepMind's ensemble mean beat its official Atlantic track forecasts at 12 to 72 hours.

    NHC's verification measures lower Atlantic track errors for the DeepMind ensemble than for NHC's official forecast at 12 to 72 hours, and an independent analysis found that it was the most accurate guidance of the season, a measurable gain in a tool that NHC used in operations.

Limits
The retained verification excludes early storms when GDMI was unavailable. Forecast accuracy does not establish fewer casualties or lower losses. The undated NWS interview does not provide an independent performance evaluation.
Costs and harms
The results support GDMI's value as additional guidance, but performance depends on basin, lead time, and timely delivery. Better average forecast skill does not establish a measured reduction in deaths or damage.
Later
Persisted, 10 September 2026. Lowry reported on 10 September 2026, from Franklin's season-to-date verification, that DeepMind again beat all other publicly available track guidance and NHC's official track forecasts in 2026, in the Atlantic, eastern Pacific and central Pacific.

What happened

James Franklin, a former branch chief at the US National Hurricane Center (NHC), shared his own verification of the 2025 season in late October 2025. Michael Lowry relayed it on 3 November 2025, and CNN reported it on 3 December 2025. Franklin found that the DeepMind ensemble was the most accurate track and intensity guidance of the 2025 season. CNN added that it did not perform evenly with each storm. NHC's Forecast Verification Report for the 2025 season is dated 30 March 2026. It states that 2025 marked the first time that NHC incorporated AI-based models into real-time operations. In the Atlantic track comparison, the Google DeepMind ensemble mean (GDMI) slightly outperformed the official forecast at lead times from 12 to 72 hours. Official forecasts outperformed the consensus aids TVCA, HCCA and FSSE. In the eastern North Pacific, GDMI performed considerably better than every other individual model. It beat the official forecast and all consensus aids at 48 to 120 hours. For intensity it was the top individual model from 12 to 48 hours. GDMI was not available early in the season, so the first storms are excluded from these comparisons. On 10 September 2026 Lowry reported that Franklin's numbers showed DeepMind ahead of NHC's official track forecasts for a second year.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Real-time availability of the Google DeepMind ensemble to NHC for most of the 2025 season. NHC's standard homogeneous verification procedure applied to early-model versions of the guidance.

Human oversight

NHC forecasters retained responsibility for official forecasts. GDMI was one source of operational guidance and was evaluated separately against the resulting forecasts.

Benefits

Reach

No source counts people who benefited. NHC's verification measures forecast errors across the 2025 season, without the early storms for which the DeepMind ensemble was not available. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

Lowry reported on 10 September 2026, from Franklin's verification of the 2026 season through 6 September, that DeepMind again beat NHC's official track forecasts at most lead times in the Atlantic, eastern Pacific and central Pacific. That result covers part of a season and comes from one independent analyst. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Consistent timely delivery of the AI guidance (NHC notes some AI systems were not consistently available). Results from one season. NHC's Science Operations Officer says it takes a long time to fully evaluate new models.
