See & Spray saves 28.4-62.4% of postemergence herbicide

off the scale

on average saved 28.4% to 62.4% of postemergence herbicide

Arkansas-Mississippi trials (2021-2022) with Blue River co-authors: targeted sprays matched broadcast weed control within 1% while saving 28.4% to 62.4% of postemergence herbicide.

What happened

University of Arkansas weed scientists and Blue River Technology agronomists ran four site-years of randomized complete block trials in 2021 and 2022 at the Northeast Research and Extension Center in Keiser, Arkansas, and Stoneville R&D in Greenville, Mississippi, comparing John Deere See & Spray machine-vision targeted applications with traditional broadcast programs in glyphosate/glufosinate/dicamba-resistant soybean, using a preemergence residual followed by early-postemergence dicamba and mid-postemergence glufosinate. Targeted applications gave control comparable to broadcast, with at most a 1% decrease in efficacy and overall control of 93% or more for Palmer amaranth, broadleaf signalgrass, morningglory and purslane species, reduced soybean injury by at most 5 percentage points without a yield increase, and on average saved 28.4% to 62.4% of postemergence herbicide, with narrower nozzle angles spraying less area. The paper was published online on 15 November 2024, the date used here; it is a research trial, not a commercial farm deployment.

What it was built to do. Detect weeds with machine vision and apply herbicide only where weeds are present, reducing herbicide use while maintaining control.

The system's part

The machine-vision sprayer decided where to spray; the comparison with broadcast programs is a randomized trial with consistent herbicides and rates. Savings depended on nozzle angle and machine settings. Co-authored by the maker's agronomists with university weed scientists.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Herbicide-resistant soybean with a PRE residual, EPOST dicamba and MPOST glufosinate. Four site-years (2021-2022) in a randomized complete block design.

What must hold again. The specific machine settings and nozzle angles used. Weed species and pressure comparable to the trial sites.
