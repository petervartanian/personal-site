See & Spray saves 28.4–62.4% of postemergence herbicide

Arkansas-Mississippi trials (2021–2022) with Blue River co-authors: targeted sprays matched broadcast weed control within 1% while saving 28.4% to 62.4% of postemergence herbicide.

ⓘ

What happened

University of Arkansas weed scientists and Blue River Technology agronomists ran four site-years of randomized complete block trials in 2021 and 2022. Sites were the Northeast Research and Extension Center in Keiser, Arkansas, and Stoneville R&D in Greenville, Mississippi. They compared John Deere See & Spray machine-vision targeted applications with traditional broadcast programs in glyphosate/glufosinate/dicamba-resistant soybean. They used a preemergence residual followed by early-postemergence dicamba and mid-postemergence glufosinate. Targeted applications gave control comparable to broadcast, with at most 1% less efficacy. Overall control was 93% or more for Palmer amaranth, broadleaf signalgrass, morningglory and purslane species. Soybean injury fell by at most 5 percentage points without a yield increase.

On average they saved 28.4% to 62.4% of postemergence herbicide, with narrower nozzle angles spraying less area. The paper appeared online on 15 November 2024. It is a research trial, not a commercial farm deployment.

Purpose. Detect weeds with machine vision and apply herbicide only where weeds are present, reducing herbicide use while maintaining control.

AI’s contribution

The machine-vision sprayer decided where to spray. The comparison with broadcast programs is a randomized trial with consistent herbicides and rates. Savings depended on nozzle angle and machine settings. Co-authored by the maker's agronomists with university weed scientists.

Since then
John Deere reported that customers ran See & Spray on more than five million acres in the 2025 season, cutting non-residual herbicide use by nearly half.John Deere’s See & Spray technology was used across more than five million acres of farmland during the 2025 growing season – a land area larger than the state of New Jersey. In 2025, John Deere customers reduced non-residual herbicide use by an average of nearly 50% — saving nearly 31 million gallons of herbicide mix—demonstrating the power of targeted application, even in a season marked by elevated weed pressure and frequent rains. source

Further evidence

Setting. Herbicide-resistant soybean with a PRE residual, EPOST dicamba and MPOST glufosinate. Four site-years (2021–2022) in a randomized complete block design.

Conditions. The specific machine settings and nozzle angles used. Weed species and pressure comparable to the trial sites.
