What happened

Virginia’s Office of Regulatory Management commissioned a 2025 pilot of Vulcan’s AI-assisted regulatory analysis. Vulcan describes a system that maps legal provisions and drafts potential changes. Vulcan's chief executive told Reason that the pilot ran from 1 May to 1 August 2025. VPM’s interviews with the company and the office’s director document a three-month, $150,000 contract and a process in which agencies review the suggestions that the AI makes. VPM also distinguishes the state’s already-announced 25% regulatory reduction from the further reduction that officials intended AI to assist. Researchers interviewed by VPM questioned whether reduced counts of words or requirements demonstrated an economic benefit. The public reporting does not establish how much AI contributed to any economic savings. Statewide figures for savings and housing costs cannot establish money that Vulcan or AI saved. On 30 June 2026 Governor Abigail Spanberger signed Executive Order 17. It rescinded Executive Order 51 and all guidance on the AI Regulatory Reduction Pilot, effective 1 July 2026. No public source reports what the pilot achieved. The date is the first day of the pilot.

Details

AI system

Workflow

The pilot combined AI analysis with government staff review of suggested regulatory changes.

Human oversight

According to VPM's interviews, state agencies review the suggestions that the AI makes. Executive Order 17 ended the pilot and its guidance from 1 July 2026. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

The intended beneficiaries were Virginia's Office of Regulatory Management and the state's executive agencies. No source counts the agencies that used the suggestions that the AI made. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Vulcan's chief executive told Reason that the pilot ran from 1 May to 1 August 2025. Executive Order 17 rescinded all guidance on the pilot from 1 July 2026. No source reports a benefit that lasted beyond the pilot. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
