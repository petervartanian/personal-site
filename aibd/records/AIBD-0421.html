Predicted cloud images improve solar forecasts in a developer's test

Open Climate Fix reports that predicted satellite images from its Cloudcasting model raised the accuracy of its PVNet solar forecasts by 5% at two hours ahead.

  • Not rated an organization

    Open Climate Fix reports a 5% accuracy gain at a two-hour horizon in its own test on past data, and no source reports that live forecasts improved after Cloudcasting went into production.

  • Not rated society

    The annual saving of 100,000 tons of emissions is a projection, and no source reports that the saving occurred.

What happened

Cloudcasting began as a 2024 Manchester Prize entry by Open Climate Fix with the Alan Turing Institute. As a finalist, the project received £100,000 in the prize, which the UK Department for Science, Innovation and Technology runs. On 20 February 2025, Open Climate Fix published an early evaluation of the model. It reported that Cloudcasting input gave a 5% accuracy gain at a two-hour horizon when integrated into PVNet, its solar forecasting model. It also reported that cloud forecasts from the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) added 2.5% when integrated into PVNet. Open Climate Fix planned to add Cloudcasting to Quartz Solar in the United Kingdom by summer 2025. Its release of 6 November 2025 on the adoption of Quartz Solar in the control room of the National Energy System Operator does not mention Cloudcasting. The developer's code repository describes the model as running live in production, and the repository received updates in August 2026. No source reports a measured effect of Cloudcasting in live forecasts. The annual carbon savings remain projected.

Details

AI system

Role

A simVP model predicts satellite imagery across 11 spectral channels, and a second model converts those predictions into solar-power forecasts.

Inputs and outputs

The cloudcasting component uses satellite imagery to forecast cloud movement and improve solar-power forecasts.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

Measurement

Measurement

Developer's comparison of PVNet solar forecasts with and without Cloudcasting predicted satellite images, and with ECMWF cloud forecasts as an alternative input. [2]

Setting and sample

UK solar forecasts using EUMETSAT satellite imagery in 11 spectral channels; period not stated. [2]

Measures

Solar forecast accuracy at a two-hour horizon. [2]

Results

5% improvement in solar forecast accuracy at two hours ahead. [2]

Additional value from AI

5% from the Cloudcasting input, against 2.5% from ECMWF input. [2]

Limitations

The developer gives no test period, error metric or uncertainty range, and the result has not been repeated by an independent party. [2]

Materials for replication

Open-source code at https://github.com/openclimatefix/sat_pred, https://github.com/openclimatefix/cloudcasting-app and https://github.com/alan-turing-institute/cloudcasting; EUMETSAT SEVIRI data on Google Cloud public datasets. [2]
