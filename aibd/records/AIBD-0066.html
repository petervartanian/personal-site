Confirmed TREWS sepsis alerts are linked to 3.3% lower mortality

off the scale

3.3% absolute (18.7% relative) reduction in in-hospital mortality

Prospective five-hospital study: sepsis alerts confirmed within 3 h had 3.3% absolute lower in-hospital mortality (CI 1.7-5.1).

What happened

The Targeted Real-time Early Warning System (TREWS), a machine-learning sepsis alert developed at Johns Hopkins University, was deployed in five US hospitals including Johns Hopkins, Howard County General Hospital and Suburban Hospital. In a prospective multi-site cohort study published in Nature Medicine on 21 July 2022, 590,736 patients were monitored and the analysis focused on 6,877 patients with sepsis identified by the alert before antibiotics were started. After adjustment for presentation and severity, patients whose alert was confirmed by a provider within three hours had a 3.3% absolute (18.7% relative) reduction in in-hospital mortality, with less organ failure and shorter stays, compared with patients whose alert was not confirmed within three hours. No individual patient is described; depth is not rated.

What it was built to do. TREWS was built to identify patients developing sepsis early so that treatment can start sooner; the mortality association is its intended benefit.

The system's part

The study is observational: it compares patients whose TREWS alert was confirmed within 3 hours with those confirmed later or not at all, adjusting for presentation and severity. It supports an association, not causation; a medRxiv critique titled 'Randomized Clinical Trials or Convenient Controls: TREWS or FALSE?' (Nemati et al., 2022) questions the comparison design. Only the abstract was retrieved, so the paper's competing-interests statement was not read.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. EHR-integrated deployment with alerts presented to bedside providers. A provider confirmation workflow linked to sepsis treatment.

What must hold again. Providers evaluate alerts promptly (the benefit was seen only for alerts confirmed within 3 hours). Comparable patient mix and alert thresholds.
