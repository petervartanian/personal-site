Confirmed TREWS sepsis alerts are linked to 3.3% lower mortality

In a five-hospital study by the TREWS developers, patients whose sepsis alert was confirmed within three hours had 3.3% absolute lower in-hospital mortality, a link that outside researchers question.

The system’s part
TREWS flagged likely sepsis, and providers decided whether to confirm the alert and treat. The study compares patients whose alert was confirmed within 3 hours with those whose alert was not, with adjustment for presentation and severity but not for provider factors. Kennedy and Rudd (2022) note that faster confirmation may mark better care teams, and Nemati and colleagues (2022) argue that the result is likely to be severely biased. The sources include no randomized comparison of TREWS against usual care.
Limits
Earlier provider confirmation of TREWS alerts is associated with lower mortality in a selected sepsis cohort. Clinician responsiveness and patient differences may influence both confirmation and outcomes. Adjustment does not establish the mortality reduction that deployment itself caused.

What happened

The Targeted Real-time Early Warning System (TREWS) is a machine-learning sepsis alert developed at Johns Hopkins University. It was deployed in the electronic health record at five hospitals in the United States, including Johns Hopkins, Howard County General Hospital and Suburban Hospital. A prospective multi-site cohort study by its developers, published in Nature Medicine on 21 July 2022, monitored 590,736 patients. The analysis focused on 6,877 patients with sepsis identified by the alert before antibiotics were started. After adjustment for presentation and severity, patients whose alert a provider confirmed within three hours had 3.3% absolute (18.7% relative) lower in-hospital mortality. They also had less organ failure and shorter stays than patients whose alert was not confirmed within three hours. No individual patient is described. Outside researchers questioned the result. In August 2022 Nemati and colleagues at the University of California San Diego wrote that the comparison is likely to be severely biased. In September 2022 Kennedy and Rudd of the University of Pittsburgh wrote that prompt confirmation may mark better care teams. Bayesian Health, which holds a license agreement with Johns Hopkins, received 510(k) clearance from the Food and Drug Administration for its sepsis flagging device on 30 April 2026. The Food and Drug Administration made no finding on mortality.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

TREWS analyzed information in the electronic health record and presented a sepsis warning for bedside providers to evaluate and confirm.

Workflow

EHR-integrated deployment with alerts presented to bedside providers. A provider confirmation workflow linked to sepsis treatment.

Human oversight

Providers assessed the warning and decided on sepsis care. The study grouped patients according to whether a provider confirmed the alert within three hours.

Benefits

Reach

The study monitored 590,736 patients and analyzed 6,877 patients with sepsis identified by the alert before antibiotics were started. The mortality difference is an adjusted association in that group, and no source counts individual patients who benefited. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Duration

The study measured outcomes during the hospital stay, and no source reports whether the mortality difference persisted. Bayesian Health, which holds a license agreement with Johns Hopkins, received 510(k) clearance from the Food and Drug Administration for its sepsis flagging device on 30 April 2026. No source reports an independent outcome study. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Measurement

Measurement

Adjusted in-hospital mortality, change in organ failure score and length of stay, by whether a provider confirmed the alert within 3 hours. [3]

Setting and sample

Five Johns Hopkins hospitals, 2018 to September 2020; 590,736 encounters monitored; 6,877 sepsis encounters analyzed (4,220 confirmed within 3 hours, 2,657 not). [3]

Measures

Adjusted absolute difference in in-hospital mortality. [3]

Results

Unadjusted mortality 14.6% against 19.2%; adjusted difference -3.3% (CI -5.1 to -1.7). [3]

Limitations

Observational comparison within alerted patients, with no adjustment for provider-level features. A reanalysis by University of California San Diego researchers argues that confounding by processes of care biases the result. [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Providers evaluate alerts promptly (the benefit was seen only for alerts confirmed within 3 hours). Comparable patient mix and alert thresholds.

Evidence

Original evidence

The benefit rests on one prospective cohort study by the developers of TREWS, who compared confirmation within three hours with later or absent confirmation among 6,877 sepsis patients out of 590,736 monitored. Researchers at the University of California San Diego, in a preprint, and at the University of Pittsburgh, in a commentary, question that comparison. The Food and Drug Administration, which cleared Bayesian Health's sepsis flagging device on 30 April 2026, made no finding on mortality. [1] [2] [3] [4]
