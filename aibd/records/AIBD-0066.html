What happened

The Targeted Real-time Early Warning System (TREWS) is a machine-learning sepsis alert developed at Johns Hopkins University. It was deployed in the electronic health record (EHR) at five US hospitals including Johns Hopkins, Howard County General Hospital and Suburban Hospital. A prospective multi-site cohort study (Nature Medicine, 21 July 2022) monitored 590,736 patients. The analysis focused on 6,877 patients with sepsis identified by the alert before antibiotics were started. After adjustment for presentation and severity, patients whose alert a provider confirmed within three hours had 3.3% absolute (18.7% relative) lower in-hospital mortality. They had less organ failure and shorter stays than patients whose alert was not confirmed within three hours. No individual patient is described; depth is not rated.

What it was built to do. TREWS was built to identify patients developing sepsis early so that treatment can start sooner; the mortality association is its intended benefit.

The system's part

The study is observational. It compares patients whose TREWS alert was confirmed within 3 hours with those confirmed later or not at all, adjusting for presentation and severity. It supports an association, not causation. A medRxiv critique titled “Randomized Clinical Trials or Convenient Controls: TREWS or FALSE?” (Nemati et al., 2022) questions the comparison design. The sources include only the paper's abstract, not its competing-interests statement.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. EHR-integrated deployment with alerts presented to bedside providers. A provider confirmation workflow linked to sepsis treatment.

What must hold again. Providers evaluate alerts promptly (the benefit was seen only for alerts confirmed within 3 hours). Comparable patient mix and alert thresholds.
