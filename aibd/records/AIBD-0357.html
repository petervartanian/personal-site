What happened

Citizen Digital reported on 21 September 2024 that the Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS) had installed FLIR thermal cameras at three black rhino sites. The sites are Lake Nakuru National Park, Ol Pejeta Conservancy and Solio Game Reserve, which was the latest of the three. Meru National Park was named as the next site. The US Department of State Bureau of International Narcotics and Law Enforcement Affairs and WWF supported the installations. Geoffrey Chege (WWF-Kenya) said that the cameras “have inbuilt analytics that send out automated alerts every time they detect an intrusion from humans, vehicles or wildlife”. Colby Loucks of WWF-US said that the cameras “can tell you when a human is in the picture or an animal” and alert rangers. KWS Director General Dr Erustus Kanga said: “I am aware that over the last six years, we have not lost a single black rhino in this sanctuary”. That record predates the Solio installation. The article reports no arrest, interception or prevented poaching event attributable to the cameras.
