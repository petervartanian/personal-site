What happened

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services' report “Fraud Prevention System Return on Investment, Fourth Implementation Year” is undated. The Wayback Machine archived it on 24 August 2016. It states the FPS helped identify or prevent $654.8 million in inappropriate payments during calendar year 2015, about 44% more than the previous year. That counts actions taken due to the FPS and investigations it expedited, augmented or corroborated. The corrected ROI is $11.5 to $1, after a rounding error in the originally reported $11.6. Table 1 trends identified savings from $115.4 million in the first year (July 2011 to June 2012) through $250.1 million and $454.0 million. The cumulative total is $1,474.3 million. Savings are split across pre-payment edit denials, payment suspensions, projected cost avoidance from revoking billing privileges and overpayments referred for recovery.

The value of law-enforcement referrals was $49.2 million in 2015. The report says the methodology was certified by the HHS Office of Inspector General. It puts 45 providers on active payment suspension at year end. Costs were an estimated $57.1 million, including $19.5 million for a developing contractor and $4.8 million for a modeling contractor. The report does not describe the system's technique.

What it was built to do. Identify or prevent inappropriate Medicare payments by generating leads that program-integrity contractors investigate and act on.

The system's part

Not assessed. The report counts savings from actions taken due to the FPS and from investigations the FPS expedited, augmented or corroborated. So the system's own contribution cannot be separated from investigators' work. The technique is not described. CMS deployed the system and paid unnamed developing and modeling contractors, so developer and deployer are not the same body.

Replication

What produced it. Claims data run through FPS edits and models that generate leads for Zone Program Integrity Contractors. Administrative levers listed in Table 1 (pre-payment edits, payment suspensions, revocation of billing privileges, overpayment referrals, law-enforcement referrals).

What must hold again. Statutory basis and funding (the Small Business Jobs Act of 2010 defines the implementation years). Contractor capacity to investigate leads (investigation costs of $28.9 million in 2015). A savings methodology certified by HHS-OIG.
