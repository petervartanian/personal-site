What happened

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services' undated report “Fraud Prevention System Return on Investment, Fourth Implementation Year” (archived by the Wayback Machine on 24 August 2016) states that the FPS helped identify or prevent $654.8 million in inappropriate payments during calendar year 2015 through actions taken due to the FPS or through investigations expedited, augmented or corroborated by it, about 44% more than the previous year, for a corrected ROI of $11.5 to $1 after a rounding error in the originally reported $11.6.

Table 1 trends identified savings from $115.4 million in the first year (July 2011 to June 2012) through $250.1 million and $454.0 million to a cumulative $1,474.3 million, split across pre-payment edit denials, payment suspensions, projected cost avoidance from revoking billing privileges, overpayments referred for recovery and the value of law-enforcement referrals ($49.2 million in 2015). The report says that the methodology was certified by the HHS Office of Inspector General, that 45 providers were on active payment suspension at year end, and that costs were an estimated $57.1 million, including $19.5 million for a developing contractor and $4.8 million for a modeling contractor. The read document does not describe the system's technique; the date used is the end of the reporting period.

What it was built to do. Identify or prevent inappropriate Medicare payments by generating leads that program-integrity contractors investigate and act on.

The system's part

Not assessed. The report counts savings from actions taken due to the FPS and from investigations the FPS expedited, augmented or corroborated, so the system's own contribution cannot be separated from investigators' work; the technique is not described. CMS deployed the system and paid unnamed developing and modeling contractors, so developer and deployer are not the same body.

Replication

What produced it. Claims data run through FPS edits and models that generate leads for Zone Program Integrity Contractors. Administrative levers listed in Table 1 (pre-payment edits, payment suspensions, revocation of billing privileges, overpayment referrals, law-enforcement referrals).

What must hold again. Statutory basis and funding (the Small Business Jobs Act of 2010 defines the implementation years). Contractor capacity to investigate leads (investigation costs of $28.9 million in 2015). A savings methodology certified by HHS-OIG.
