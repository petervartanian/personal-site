Screening Medicare claims helps flag or prevent $654.8 million in 2015

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services count $654.8 million in inappropriate Medicare payments identified or prevented in 2015 with help from their Fraud Prevention System.

  • 1 an organization

    The Medicare program saved money through denied claims, payment suspensions and referred overpayments. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services count $654.8 million identified or prevented in 2015. They reported to the Government Accountability Office that the system's edits denied nearly 169,000 claims and saved $11.3 million in fiscal 2015. These are cost savings for the Medicare program.

  • Not rated society

    Savings to the Medicare program are stated only as yearly totals that mix denied claims with projected avoidance, and no source traces an effect on beneficiaries or taxpayers.

The system’s part
GAO found that FPS leads started about 20 percent of contractor investigations in fiscal 2015 and 2016. Contractor officials told GAO that FPS sped up the finding and triage of suspect providers, but that it generally did not speed up the investigations themselves. Investigators did the case work, and the FPS prepayment edits apply Medicare rules without scoring risk, so the dollar totals combine model-generated leads, rule-based edits and human investigation.
Limits
The $654.8 million total combines denied claims, payment suspensions, projected cost avoidance and overpayments referred for recovery, so it counts more than cash recovered. The Government Accountability Office notes that the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services estimated actual savings of $133 million for 2014 against $454 million identified. The agency's report does not describe the technique of the system. No source separates the contribution of the system from the work of investigators.
Costs and harms
Costs for 2015 were an estimated $57.1 million, including $19.5 million for a developing contractor and $4.8 million for a modeling contractor. No source reports a burden on providers or beneficiaries from denied claims or suspended payments.
Later
Persisted, 30 August 2017. GAO reports that CMS kept using FPS through fiscal 2016 and had 24 prepayment edits in place by May 2017. Edit denials nearly doubled from fiscal 2015 to fiscal 2016.

What happened

The report “Fraud Prevention System Return on Investment, Fourth Implementation Year” of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services (CMS) is undated. The Wayback Machine archived it on 24 August 2016. It states that the Fraud Prevention System (FPS) helped identify or prevent $654.8 million in inappropriate payments during calendar year 2015, about 44% more than the previous year. That total counts actions taken due to the FPS and investigations that it expedited, augmented or corroborated. The report gives a corrected return on investment of $11.5 to $1. The originally reported $11.6 contained a rounding error. Table 1 shows identified savings of $115.4 million in the first year (July 2011 to June 2012), $250.1 million in the second and $454.0 million in the third. The cumulative total is $1,474.3 million. The savings come from claims denied by pre-payment edits, payment suspensions, projected cost avoidance from revoking billing privileges and overpayments referred for recovery. The value of referrals to law enforcement was $49.2 million in 2015. The report says that the Office of Inspector General of the Department of Health and Human Services certified the methodology. It counts 45 providers on active payment suspension at year end. Costs were an estimated $57.1 million. They include $19.5 million for a developing contractor and $4.8 million for a modeling contractor. The report does not describe the system's technique. In a report of 30 August 2017, the Government Accountability Office (GAO) found that FPS leads started about 20 percent of contractor fraud investigations in fiscal 2015 and 2016. GAO found that these investigations led to corrective actions and savings. Contractor officials told GAO that FPS generally did not speed up investigations once they began. GAO notes that CMS estimated actual savings of $133 million for 2014 against $454 million identified. CMS reported to GAO that FPS edits denied nearly 169,000 claims and saved $11.3 million in fiscal 2015. In fiscal 2016 the edits denied nearly 324,000 claims and saved $20.4 million. CMS had 24 prepayment edits in place by May 2017.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

FPS processed Medicare claims through edits and models to produce payment checks and investigative leads for program-integrity contractors.

Workflow

Claims data run through FPS edits and models that generate leads for Zone Program Integrity Contractors. Administrative levers listed in Table 1 (pre-payment edits, payment suspensions, revocation of billing privileges, overpayment referrals, law-enforcement referrals).

Human oversight

Program-integrity contractors investigated the leads that the system generated, and their investigators did the case work. The system's prepayment edits apply Medicare rules and deny claims before payment. [1] [2]

Benefits

Duration

Identified savings rose each year, from $115.4 million in the first implementation year to $654.8 million in 2015. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services reported to the Government Accountability Office that the edits denied nearly 169,000 claims in fiscal 2015 and nearly 324,000 in fiscal 2016. The agency had 24 prepayment edits in place by May 2017. [1] [2]
