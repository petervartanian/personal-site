Upstart's model approves 27% more applicants, CFPB reports

approving 27% more applicants at 16% lower APRs than a traditional model

CFPB's 2019 update reported Upstart's model approving 27% more applicants at 16% lower APRs than a traditional model; a 2024 independent monitor found approval disparities for Black applicants.

What happened

In a blog post of 6 August 2019 by Patrice Alexander Ficklin and Paul Watkins, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau reported results from the compliance plan attached to its first no-action letter, granted to Upstart Network in September 2017. Comparing Upstart's tested model with a hypothetical traditional model using conventional variables, the CFPB reported that the tested model approves 27% more applicants and yields 16% lower average APRs for approved loans, with acceptance rates up 23-29% and APRs down 15-17% across racial, ethnic and sex categories; near-prime applicants (FICO 620-660) were approved approximately twice as frequently, applicants under 25 were 32% more likely to be approved and those with incomes under $50,000 13% more likely. The post states that the results for minority, female and 62-and-older applicants show no disparities requiring further fair-lending analysis and that they reflect the net effect of both the alternative data and the machine-learning methodology; the analysis was provided by Upstart, and the CFPB does not say that it replicated it. On 8 June 2022 the CFPB terminated the 2020 no-action letter at Upstart's request after Upstart planned to expand the variables in its model. On 27 March 2024 the NAACP Legal Defense Fund and the Student Borrower Protection Center announced the final report of an independent monitorship by Relman Colfax, which found no proxies for protected classes and no pricing disparities but identified approval disparities for Black applicants, with Upstart rejecting the monitor's proposed less discriminatory alternative model.

What it was built to do. Underwrite and price unsecured consumer loans using alternative data and machine learning.

The system's part

The CFPB states that the reported gains reflect the net effect of both the alternative data and the machine-learning methodology, so the model's contribution cannot be separated from its data; Upstart developed and deployed the model and supplied the comparison. The independent monitor later found approval disparities for Black applicants and disagreed with Upstart about a less discriminatory alternative.

Replication

What produced it
  • Alternative data combined with machine-learning underwriting, compared against a hypothetical traditional model on Upstart's own applicant pool
  • A regulatory compliance plan requiring fair-lending testing and reporting
What must hold again
  • An applicant pool and comparison model similar to the 2019 test
  • Fair-lending testing that measures approval as well as pricing disparities
  • Independent monitoring, which in 2024 found approval disparities the 2019 test did not report
