Apollo Go robotaxis add voice controls for visually impaired riders

Baidu reports that Apollo Go robotaxis give visually impaired passengers audible arrival alerts, automatic unlocking, and voice controls, and that these passengers took more than 200 rides in Wuhan.

  • 2 a person

    The limited public rollout gives visually impaired passengers audible arrival alerts, automatic unlocking, and voice-first controls, and the company said that they took more than 200 rides in Wuhan. No source measures whether the passengers gained lasting independent mobility.

Depth runs from 1, a convenience or time saved, to 5, a life or a livelihood preserved.

What happened

Five visually impaired people first tested the features on 26 November 2024. In its project account, current as of June 2025, Baidu reports a limited public robotaxi rollout with audible arrival alerts, automatic unlocking, and voice controls for visually impaired passengers. In a sponsored item of 15 October 2025, the company said that visually impaired users had taken more than 200 rides in Wuhan. It also said that the service was being extended to Hainan. On 5 December 2025, Wuhan named the service among ten technology cases for people with disabilities. On the night of 31 March 2026, several Apollo Go vehicles stalled in Wuhan traffic and stranded riders. Police attributed the stall to a system malfunction. Chinese regulators then paused new robotaxi permits from late April to July 2026. Long-term mobility effects are unmeasured.
