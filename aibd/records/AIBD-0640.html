An automated diabetes prevention app did as well as human coaches

In a 368-person Johns Hopkins trial, the Sweetch AI-led diabetes prevention program met the risk-reduction goal as often as human coaching (31.7% vs 31.9%).

What happened

Researchers at Johns Hopkins Medicine ran a randomized noninferiority trial from 11 October 2021 to 16 December 2024 at sites in Baltimore, Maryland, and Reading, Pennsylvania. They referred 368 adults with prediabetes and overweight or obesity either to an AI-led diabetes prevention program from Sweetch Health or to one of four human-led programs recognized by the CDC. The Sweetch app used a reinforcement learning algorithm to time and tailor push messages on weight, activity and food. After 12 months, 31.7% of the AI group and 31.9% of the human-coaching group met the combined goal for weight loss, physical activity or HbA1c reduction. That result met the prespecified noninferiority margin. More people started the AI program (93.4% against 82.7%) and more completed it (63.9% against 50.3%). The National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases funded the trial. Sweetch was paid to deliver the program and took no part in the analysis. The human programs ran remotely because of COVID-19, and the trial measured risk factors in place of new cases of diabetes.

Details

AI system

Workflow

Participants received a Bluetooth scale and the app within 8 to 12 days of referral. A reinforcement learning algorithm chose the timing and content of messages from logged and passive data. No human coach took part in the AI arm.

Measurement

Measurement

Composite of at least 5% weight loss, at least 4% weight loss with 150 minutes of weekly activity by actigraphy, or HbA1c drop of 0.2 points at 12 months. [1]

Setting and sample

368 adults with prediabetes and overweight or obesity at two US sites. [1]

Measures

Share meeting the composite outcome; program initiation and completion. [1]

Results

31.7% against 31.9% (noninferior); initiation 93.4% against 82.7%; completion 63.9% against 50.3%. [1]

Additional value from AI

Equal outcome to human coaching with higher uptake. [1]

Limitations

Unmasked; surrogate outcomes; human programs delivered remotely; app updates during the trial. [1]

Denominators

12 months. [1]

Materials for replication

ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05056376; protocol in supplements. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Participants own a smartphone and keep using the app for months. Clinicians refer adults with prediabetes to the program.
