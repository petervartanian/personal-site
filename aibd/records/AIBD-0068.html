Imagene AI infers EGFR from H&E; osimertinib 2 days post-biopsy

What happened

In July 2022 (day not given; first day used) a healthy woman in her early 40s was admitted to Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center, Israel. She had a month of worsening headaches. Imaging showed a 2.6 cm left apical lung adenocarcinoma with leptomeningeal spread; biopsy tissue was scarce. To guide gene testing without exhausting tissue, the team ran Imagene's deep-learning algorithm on the H&E slide. Within 48 hours of biopsy it inferred EGFR mutation positivity. A rapid Idylla PCR assay confirmed the L858R exon 21 mutation; osimertinib started two days after biopsy. The authors say that was one week earlier than routine practice and avoided whole-neuraxis radiotherapy. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) at two months showed the enhancement gone.

What it was built to do. Imagene's algorithm is built to infer genomic alteration status from routine pathology slides in order to direct molecular testing; this benefit is its intended function.

The system's part

The AI inference directed which rapid test to run; treatment began only after PCR confirmation. The AI therefore changed the sequence and speed of testing rather than the final diagnosis. Authors are Tel Aviv Sourasky / Tel Aviv University clinicians declaring no competing interests. A second patient in the report (February 2023) followed the same path.

Replication

What produced it. Scanning the diagnostic H&E slide and running the inference within 48 hours of biopsy. A rapid single-gene PCR assay available for confirmation.

What must hold again. PCR or sequencing confirmation before treatment. A tumor with a targetable driver the model can infer.
