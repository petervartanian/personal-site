GNoME predicts 2.2 million crystal structures, reviewers dispute

2.2 million crystal structures (380,000 stable)

Google DeepMind's GNoME proposed 2.2 million crystal structures (380,000 stable); independent reviews (Cheetham & Seshadri 2024, Kurlin 2026) find duplicates and scant evidence of useful compounds.

What happened

On 29 November 2023 Google DeepMind published GNoME in Nature, reporting 2.2 million structures below the convex hull, calling it 'an order-of-magnitude expansion in stable materials known to humanity' and stating that 736 of the stable structures had already been independently realized; the company blog adds that the Berkeley Lab A-Lab synthesized more than 41 materials from the predictions. Cheetham and Seshadri (UC Santa Barbara, Chem. Mater., 8 April 2024) examined the listings and reported 'scant evidence for compounds that fulfill the trifecta of novelty, credibility, and utility'; 404 Media (11 April 2024) quoted Cheetham that the paper 'falls way short' as a practical contribution and DeepMind that it stands by all claims. C&EN (15 October 2026) reported an arXiv analysis by Vitaliy Kurlin (Liverpool) finding more than 15% near-duplicates within GNoME, about 24% duplicating Materials Project entries and about 17% close to ICSD entries, with Leslie Schoop (Princeton) calling some entries 'completely unrealistic'. The earlier verifier could not read the Chem. Mater. critique (HTTP 403); its abstract was fetched here through Europe PMC (PMC11044265). The date is the Nature publication date.

What it was built to do. Predict stable inorganic crystal structures at scale to accelerate materials discovery

The system's part

not assessed The graph-network model is the vendor's; its predictions are documented in a vendor-authored Nature paper and blog. Independent reviewers (Cheetham & Seshadri 2024; Kurlin 2026 as reported by C&EN) report near-duplicates, unrealistic compounds and little evidence of useful novel materials. Benefit therefore rests on the vendor's account and is contested.

Replication

What produced it

  • Graph neural network trained on existing crystal databases with iterative active learning
  • High-throughput DFT stability checks of candidates
  • Open release of the predicted structures

What must hold again

  • Predicted structures are genuinely novel rather than near-duplicates of known entries
  • Predicted compounds are synthesisable and have useful properties
  • Independent experimental confirmation of utility
