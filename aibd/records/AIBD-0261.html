Relation to AI

The makers report that the graph networks filtered candidate structures and that DFT calculations then checked their stability. Over six rounds of active learning, the share of filtered structural candidates that DFT confirmed as stable rose from under 6% to over 80%. Candidates came from substitutions of atoms in known crystals and from random structure search. Widdowson and Kurlin trace near-duplicates of A-Lab products in older databases to the substitution step. No source isolates a contribution of the model to any material that was made and used.

Citable summary

Google DeepMind's GNoME proposed 2.2 million crystal structures (380,000 stable); independent reviews (Cheetham & Seshadri 2024, Kurlin 2026) find duplicates and scant evidence of useful compounds. Counts of predicted stable structures do not establish distinct, useful, synthesizable materials. Concurrent experimental realizations are not necessarily caused by GNoME, and the retained critiques raise duplication and plausibility concerns that require direct reconciliation. The graph-network model is the vendor's. Its predictions are documented in a vendor-authored Nature paper and blog. Independent reviewers (Cheetham & Seshadri 2024; Kurlin 2026 as reported by C&EN) report near-duplicates, unrealistic compounds and little evidence of useful novel materials. Benefit therefore rests on the vendor's account and is contested.
