Lunit INSIGHT CXR spots subpulmonic effusion; ultrasound agrees

At a basic emergency service in the Algarve, the AI marked bilateral effusion (97% left, 77% right); screening ultrasound confirmed a right subpulmonic effusion; the patient refused transfer and left.

What happened

Miravent et al. (Cureus, 7 February 2026) report an 87-year-old man with three days of dyspnoea at a basic emergency service of the Algarve Local Health Unit. Lunit INSIGHT CXR (Lunit Inc., Seoul) flagged bilateral pleural effusion on the chest radiograph with higher confidence on the left (97%) than the right (77%), because the right-sided subpulmonic distribution mimicked hemidiaphragm elevation; screening thoracic ultrasound by the radiographer confirmed a right subpulmonic effusion, which the authors say informed management and prevented misclassification as diaphragmatic elevation. He received nebulised bronchodilators, but despite advice and an indication for transfer to a referral hospital he refused, signed a discharge against medical advice and left. The authors declare no financial support. The earlier verifier set the case aside because no beneficial outcome was observed. The date is the publication date.
