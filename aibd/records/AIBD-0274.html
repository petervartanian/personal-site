Lunit INSIGHT CXR flags a subpulmonic effusion that ultrasound confirms

At an emergency service in the Algarve, Lunit INSIGHT CXR flagged bilateral pleural effusion on a chest radiograph; ultrasound confirmed a right subpulmonic effusion, and the patient refused transfer.

The system’s part
The AI flagged the effusion correctly but with lower confidence on the subpulmonic side. Ultrasound confirmed it. The source is a non-vendor case report with no financial support declared. The outcome did not follow because the patient declined care.
Limits
Correct identification does not establish subsequent clinical improvement. The patient's refusal limits the observed outcome, and the case does not isolate what the radiographer would have recognized without the AI output.
Costs and harms
The lower right-sided confidence did not mean that clinically relevant fluid was absent. Its atypical subpulmonic distribution required ultrasound confirmation, and better detection did not ensure that the patient accepted further care.
Later
Nothing later is known. The patient refused transfer and left against medical advice. The case report describes no follow-up, and no radiologist read the image.

What happened

On 29 September 2023, Tejani and colleagues at the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center published a study that checked chest-radiograph readings against computed tomography. They found that Lunit INSIGHT CXR detected pleural effusion about as well as second-year residents, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.82 against 0.86. Miravent et al. (Cureus, 7 February 2026) report an 87-year-old man with three days of dyspnea. He attended a basic emergency service of the Algarve Local Health Unit. Lunit INSIGHT CXR (Lunit Inc., Seoul) flagged bilateral pleural effusion on his chest radiograph. Its confidence was higher on the left (97%) than on the right (77%), because the right-sided subpulmonic distribution mimicked hemidiaphragm elevation. The radiographer performed a screening thoracic ultrasound, which confirmed a right subpulmonic effusion. The authors say that this informed management and prevented misclassification as diaphragmatic elevation. He received nebulized bronchodilators. Despite advice and an indication for transfer to a referral hospital, he refused, signed a discharge against medical advice and left. The authors declare no financial support. The case report was accepted and published on 7 February 2026 and gives no date for the visit.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

A chest radiograph produced probability scores, ellipse overlays, and heatmaps for suspected abnormalities. Effusion confidence was 97% on the left and 77% on the right.

Autonomy

The software automatically analyzed the acquired radiograph but did not confirm the effusion or control treatment and transfer.

Workflow

AI chest-radiograph triage at a peripheral emergency service. Radiographer-performed screening ultrasound to confirm.

Human oversight

The attending physician ordered imaging, and a radiographer checked the suspected right-sided fluid with focused ultrasound. The patient declined the recommended transfer.
