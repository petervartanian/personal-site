What happened

Miravent et al. (Cureus, 7 February 2026) report an 87-year-old man with three days of dyspnea. He attended a basic emergency service of the Algarve Local Health Unit. Lunit INSIGHT CXR (Lunit Inc., Seoul) flagged bilateral pleural effusion on the chest radiograph. Its confidence was higher on the left (97%) than the right (77%), because the right-sided subpulmonic distribution mimicked hemidiaphragm elevation. Screening thoracic ultrasound by the radiographer confirmed a right subpulmonic effusion. The authors say this informed management and prevented misclassification as diaphragmatic elevation. He received nebulized bronchodilators. Despite advice and an indication for transfer to a referral hospital he refused, signed a discharge against medical advice and left. The authors declare no financial support. The date is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Flag abnormalities on chest radiographs at an emergency service without on-site radiology

The AI flagged the effusion correctly but with lower confidence on the subpulmonic side. Ultrasound confirmed it. The source is a non-vendor case report with no financial support declared. The outcome did not follow because the patient declined care.
