GraphCast matches best operational cyclone track models in 2023

adding all four improved the NHC track consensus by 11% at 120 h

CIRA/Colorado State evaluation: GraphCast and three AI models match top operational cyclone track models in 2023; adding all four cut consensus error 11% at 120 h.

What happened

Google DeepMind's GraphCast was run live on ECMWF's public charts from 2023. DeepMind's blog of 14 November 2023 states that the live model predicted about nine days in advance that Hurricane Lee would make landfall in Nova Scotia in September 2023 (the blog says only 'In September'; the year is implied by its 14 November 2023 post date). The ECMWF publications fetched do not document the case; MIT Technology Review quoted ECMWF's Peter Dueben saying the models are so good they cannot be avoided. An independent CIRA/Colorado State evaluation applying NHC verification to May-November 2023 cyclones found GraphCast-operational and three other AI models' track errors comparable to the best operational models, and adding all four improved the NHC track consensus by 11% at 120 h.

What it was built to do. Medium-range global weather forecasting by machine learning, including severe-event prediction such as tropical cyclone tracks.

The system's part

The CIRA preprint isolates the AI models' contribution via NHC verification and consensus experiments; the nine-day Hurricane Lee landfall forecast rests on vendor statements and press quotes of ECMWF staff, and ECMWF documents fetched (Newsletters 177 and 178, science blog, Severe Event Catalogue) do not record it.

Replication

What produced it

  • AI models initialised from operational NWP analyses and run alongside physics-based models
  • Verification using the NHC procedure, including early-model conversion and consensus experiments

What must hold again

  • Track skill does not extend to intensity: the CIRA preprint reports a substantial low intensity bias
  • Independent case evaluation (Storm Ciaran) found peak winds underestimated by all AI models tested
