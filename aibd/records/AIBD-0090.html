GraphCast matches the best operational cyclone track models in 2023

Independent researchers at Colorado State University found that DeepMind's GraphCast and three other AI weather models matched the best operational models on 2023 cyclone tracks.

What happened

Google DeepMind's GraphCast was run live on the public charts of the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) from 2023. DeepMind's blog of 14 November 2023 says that the live model predicted Hurricane Lee's landfall in Nova Scotia in September 2023 about nine days in advance. The blog says only “In September”, so the year is implied by its 14 November 2023 post date. ECMWF's newsletters 177 and 178, its science blog and its Severe Event Catalog do not document the case. MIT Technology Review quoted ECMWF's Peter Dueben: “It showed that these models are so good that we cannot avoid them anymore.” Researchers at CIRA, Colorado State University, who have no tie to DeepMind, applied the verification procedure of the National Hurricane Center (NHC) to cyclones from May to November 2023. They found that the track errors of GraphCast-operational and three other AI models were comparable to those of the best operational models, and adding all four improved the NHC track consensus by 11% at 120 h. NHC did not use the AI models in its 2023 operations. The evaluation appeared as a preprint on 8 September 2024 and after peer review in Artificial Intelligence for the Earth Systems on 20 October 2025, with the same 11% consensus gain at 120 h. The authors also found that the AI models' intensity forecasts had no skill against the simplest statistical models, because the models almost always weakened the storms. In its verification report of 30 March 2026, NHC states that the 2025 season marked the first time that it incorporated AI-based models into real-time operations. It reports that the Google DeepMind ensemble mean slightly outperformed NHC at lead times from 12 to 72 h.

Details

AI system

Model

GraphCast.

Role

GraphCast generated weather forecasts.

Configuration

The event concerned GraphCast’s live experimental forecasts on ECMWF charts in 2023. The CIRA evaluation used GraphCast-operational.

Inputs and outputs

GraphCast produced weather forecasts from which a cyclone tracker estimated storm movement; independent researchers evaluated those tracks using NHC verification procedures.

Workflow

AI models initialized from operational NWP analyses and run alongside physics-based models. Verification using the NHC procedure, including early-model conversion and consensus experiments.

Human oversight

The National Hurricane Center (NHC) did not use the AI models in its 2023 operations. No source reports an effect of the evaluated forecasts on warnings. In the 2025 season NHC incorporated AI-based models into real-time operations. Its verification report compares them with its official forecasts. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Benefits

Reach

No source counts beneficiaries. No source reports that a forecaster, an agency or the public gained from these forecasts in 2023. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Duration

The CIRA authors measured track skill for the 2023 season. They report the same gain in their peer-reviewed paper of 20 October 2025. By the 2025 season NHC had brought AI-based models, including a newer Google DeepMind ensemble, into real-time operations. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7]

Measurement

Measurement

NHC verification procedures, including conversion to early models and consensus experiments with and without the AI models. [1]

Setting and sample

Northern Hemisphere tropical cyclones, May to November 2023; four open-source AI models run four times a day from GFS initial conditions. [1]

Measures

Track and intensity forecast error out to 7 days; change in NHC consensus track error. [1]

Results

AI track errors comparable to the best operational models; consensus track error 11% lower at 120 h with all four AI models added, significant at 72 to 120 h; intensity forecasts had no skill. [1]

Additional value from AI

11% reduction in consensus track error at 120 h. [1]

Limitations

One season; the AI models almost always weakened storms, a substantial low intensity bias; track forecasts improved with ECMWF initial conditions in a preliminary test. [1]

Denominators

CIRA ran the models in near-real time and built a database of retrospective runs; NHC first brought AI models into real-time operations in 2025. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Track skill does not extend to intensity: the CIRA preprint reports a substantial low intensity bias. Independent case evaluation (Storm Ciaran) found peak winds underestimated by all AI models tested.
