EchoNext ECG flag finds severe aortic stenosis, leads to TAVR

At Columbia/NewYork-Presbyterian, EchoNext flagged a pre-operative ECG (score 0.6094), triggering an EHR advisory; echo showed LVEF 40% and severe aortic stenosis; cardiac anesthesia, surgery, TAVR.

ⓘ

What happened

Tat et al. (JACC: Case Reports, 4 March 2026) describe an 84-year-old woman with hypertension. She was admitted after a fall with a left femur fracture. Her electrocardiogram (ECG) was analyzed by EchoNext, a deep-learning model for structural heart disease developed at Columbia. The score was 0.6094; the model's high-risk cutoff is 0.6. The score triggered an electronic health record (EHR) advisory. Transthoracic echocardiography showed an ejection fraction of 40% and severe aortic stenosis. The valve area was 0.6 cm2, peak velocity 3.9 m/s and mean gradient 34 mm Hg. Cardiology recommended cardiac anesthesia for the orthopedic operation. The patient then underwent transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), on day 8. The authors call it the first report of an AI-ECG algorithm detecting undiagnosed severe aortic stenosis that led to TAVR.

EchoNext's development paper (Poterucha et al., Nature, July 2025) reports training on more than 1 million ECG and imaging records. It reports that 73% of patients flagged high-risk in prospective evaluation had newly diagnosed disease. All authors are the Columbia developers. Dr Elias reports research support including from Edwards.

Purpose. Screen ECGs for unrecognised structural heart disease and alert clinicians through the electronic health record

AI’s contribution

The EHR advisory from EchoNext prompted the echocardiogram in an asymptomatic patient, according to the developers' own case report. The evidence is developer-authored: the case report and the Nature development paper, with disclosed industry research support. No independent account was found.

Since then
In June 2026 NewYork-Presbyterian announced that EchoNext, commercialized by the spinout Pathway Labs, had received FDA clearance for AI detection of structural heart disease from ECGs, allowing broader deployment beyond the Columbia pilot.EchoNext is the world's first FDA-approved AI detection tool that analyzes electrocardiograms (ECGs) to detect high-risk structural heart disease. source

Further evidence

Setting. EchoNext scoring of a routine ECG. EHR-integrated advisory to the primary team. Cardiology consultation and echocardiography.

Conditions. Model deployed with alerts in the health record. Clinicians act on the advisory before surgery.
