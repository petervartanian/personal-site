EchoNext ECG flag finds severe aortic stenosis, leads to TAVR

echo showed LVEF 40% and severe aortic stenosis

At Columbia/NewYork-Presbyterian, EchoNext flagged a pre-operative ECG (score 0.6094), triggering an EHR advisory; echo showed LVEF 40% and severe aortic stenosis; cardiac anesthesia, surgery, TAVR.

What happened

Tat et al. (JACC: Case Reports, 4 March 2026) describe an 84-year-old woman with hypertension admitted after a fall with a left femur fracture. Her ECG was analyzed by EchoNext, a deep-learning model for structural heart disease developed at Columbia; the earlier verifier recorded a score of 0.6094 (the model's high-risk cutoff is 0.6), which triggered an electronic-health-record advisory. Transthoracic echocardiography showed an ejection fraction of 40% and severe aortic stenosis (valve area 0.6 cm2, peak velocity 3.9 m/s, mean gradient 34 mm Hg); cardiology recommended cardiac anesthesia for the orthopedic operation, and the patient then underwent transcatheter aortic valve replacement (on day 8 per the earlier reason). The authors call it the first report of an AI-ECG algorithm detecting undiagnosed severe aortic stenosis that led to TAVR. EchoNext's development paper (Poterucha et al., Nature, July 2025) reports training on more than 1 million ECG and imaging records and that 73% of patients flagged high-risk in prospective evaluation had newly diagnosed disease. All authors are the Columbia developers; Dr Elias reports research support including from Edwards. The date is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Screen ECGs for unrecognised structural heart disease and alert clinicians through the electronic health record

The system's part

The EHR advisory from EchoNext prompted the echocardiogram in an asymptomatic patient, according to the developers' own case report. Evidence is developer-authored (case report and the Nature development paper), with disclosed industry research support; no independent account was found.
