EchoNext flags an ECG before hip surgery, and doctors find valve disease

At Columbia, the EchoNext deep-learning model flagged an 84-year-old's pre-operative ECG; echocardiography then showed severe aortic stenosis, and she had a valve replacement after her surgery.

The system’s part
The EHR advisory from EchoNext prompted the echocardiogram in an asymptomatic patient, according to the developers' own case report. The evidence is developer-authored: the case report and the Nature development paper, with disclosed industry research support. No independent account was found.
Limits
The echocardiogram and the clinicians established the diagnosis and the treatment. A single case report cannot show that the alert prevented death or surgical complications, and no untreated comparison is available. In the sponsor's validation, the aortic stenosis output had a sensitivity of 54.9% and a positive predictive value of 11.8%.
Costs and harms
The model supplied a reason to investigate an otherwise silent condition. Echocardiography was needed to establish the valve disease and plan care, so the alert was not itself a diagnosis.
Later
Scaled up, 8 June 2026. FDA cleared EchoNext through a traditional 510(k) on 8 June 2026, with Pathwai, Inc. as the applicant. The NewYork-Presbyterian release of 23 June 2026 calls this an approval, but the FDA record shows that it is a clearance as substantially equivalent to an earlier device.

What happened

EchoNext is a deep-learning model for structural heart disease developed at Columbia. Its development paper (Poterucha et al., Nature, July 2025) reports training on more than 1 million electrocardiogram (ECG) and imaging records. It reports that 73% of patients flagged high-risk in prospective evaluation had newly diagnosed disease. Tat et al. (JACC: Case Reports, 4 March 2026) describe an 84-year-old woman with hypertension. She was admitted after a fall with a left femur fracture. Her ECG was analyzed by EchoNext. The score was 0.6094; the model's high-risk cutoff is 0.6. The score triggered an electronic health record (EHR) advisory. Transthoracic echocardiography showed an ejection fraction of 40% and severe aortic stenosis. The valve area was 0.6 cm2, peak velocity 3.9 m/s and mean gradient 34 mm Hg. Cardiology recommended cardiac anesthesia for the orthopedic operation. The patient then underwent transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), on day 8. The authors call it the first report of an AI-ECG algorithm detecting undiagnosed severe aortic stenosis that led to TAVR. The case report comes from Columbia authors who include the model's developers Poterucha and Elias. Dr. Elias reports research support including from Edwards. The journal received the case report on 18 November 2025, so the events took place before then, during pilot testing of EchoNext at Columbia. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) cleared EchoNext through a 510(k) on 8 June 2026, with Pathwai, Inc. as the applicant. In the sponsor's validation, the aortic stenosis output had a sensitivity of 54.9% and a positive predictive value of 11.8%. A NewYork-Presbyterian release of 23 June 2026 calls the clearance an approval. An independent team at Thomas Jefferson University reported in September 2026 that the smaller released EchoNext-Mini model kept most of its accuracy in another hospital's records. In 45,878 Boston patients, its composite area under the curve was 0.790, against 0.820 on the benchmark test set. The team did not test the deployed model or this case.

Details

AI system

Model

The second-generation EchoNext model.

Release

The second generation of EchoNext.

Role

EchoNext analyzed an ECG and prompted further assessment for structural heart disease.

Inputs and outputs

EchoNext analyzed an ECG and generated a structural-heart-disease risk score. A score of 0.6094 exceeded the 0.6 alert threshold and prompted an EHR advisory.

Workflow

EchoNext scoring of a routine ECG. EHR-integrated advisory to the primary team. Cardiology consultation and echocardiography.

Human oversight

Clinicians followed the advisory with echocardiography, diagnosed severe aortic stenosis, planned cardiac anesthesia for hip surgery, and subsequently performed TAVR.
