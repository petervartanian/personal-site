iSciLab tests nose-print AI to register animals and track vaccination

iSciLab describes an AI system, including its AniPuppy app, that identifies animals by nose prints and facial features; it lists Korean and African pilots and reports no outcome.

  • Not rated an organization

    The submission lists a Korean horse-passport pilot and African rabies-tracking tests without a measured improvement in management.

  • Not rated society

    Animal welfare, rabies prevention and reduced illegal trade are proposed effects. No source reports an observed outcome for any of them.

What happened

The biometric registration project uses nose prints and facial features to identify animals without invasive tags. Euronews reported on 1 November 2022 that iSciLab had worked with the South Korean government since 2019 to test its dog nose-print system, with the aim of making it an official registration method by 2024. Aju Korea Daily reported on 29 November 2022 that the Ministry of Science and ICT selected the company's AniPuppy app for a two-year demonstration project in September 2022. In its submission to the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), current as of June 2025, iSciLab lists a Korean horse-passport pilot and African field testing for rabies tracking. It describes intended management and welfare benefits, without quantified identification, vaccination, or disease outcomes. No source reports the outcome of the demonstration project or of the later horse-passport and African pilots.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Phone photographs of an animal’s nose or face were matched to an identity linked with owner and vaccination information.

Workflow

The account does not establish conditions sufficient to reproduce the benefit.

Conditions for replication

Availability

The submission reports pilots in South Korea, Australia, and Ghana within wider product development. A robust mobile platform and broader integration were still planned.
