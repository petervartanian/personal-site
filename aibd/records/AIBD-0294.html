Fitbit Charge 3 signals precede 4 of 5 COVID-19 exacerbations

Kanazawa pilot, 23 COVID-19 patients (2020): Fitbit oxygen-variation signals preceded 4 of 5 exacerbations (sensitivity 80%); one man's persistent signals led to polysomnography showing severe OSAS.

ⓘ

What happened

Yamagami, Nomura and colleagues published the DEXTERITY pilot in JMIR Formative Research on 16 September 2021. They are at Kanazawa University and Japan Community Health Care Organization (JCHO) Kanazawa Hospital. The study ran from August to November 2020. The individual event is undated. Twenty-three patients with PCR-confirmed COVID-19 wore a Fitbit Charge 3 for 30 days. The investigators defined a high “estimated oxygen variation” (EOV) signal as a significant dip and recovery of the oxygen level within the index period. A prediction counted as successful if the signal appeared within two days before an exacerbation. Exacerbation occurred in six patients (seven events). EOV signals preceded four of five analyzed cases (sensitivity 80%, specificity 90%), better than resting-heart-rate signals (50% and 80%).

Separately, patient JCHO-023, a 53-year-old man with hypertension recovered from COVID-19, showed “a long-lasting high EOV signal” from days 18 to 29 without symptoms. The team suspected sleep apnea. A portable polysomnogram (Nihon Kohden SAS-2200) gave a 3% oxygen desaturation index (ODI) of 33.6 per hour, severe obstructive sleep apnea syndrome. The paper reports no treatment for him. The authors note the Fitbit's functions and analysis algorithms were not approved as a medical device at the time. They note that baseline data were short and the five exacerbation cases are too few to generalize. The prediction analysis was defined against outcomes after the fact rather than as live alerts.

Unexpected use. Fitbit's EOV metric summarizes overnight oxygen variation for consumers. The investigators repurposed it to flag COVID-19 deterioration, and the sleep-apnea finding was incidental to that study aim.

AI’s contribution

The device produced the EOV values. The investigators' own rule flagged high signals, and clinicians decided to order polysomnography for the sleep-apnea patient. The paper does not say whether Fitbit's EOV estimation uses machine learning and notes the functions were not approved as a medical device. The COVID-19 prediction figures were computed after the outcomes were known, not from alerts acted on in real time.

Further evidence

Setting. A Fitbit Charge 3 worn for 30 days by patients with PCR-confirmed COVID-19, giving EOV and resting heart rate. An investigator-defined high-EOV signal (a significant dip and recovery of the oxygen level within the index period) reviewed by the study team. Weekly phone calls to discharged patients and access to portable polysomnography.

Conditions. Patients can pair the device with a smartphone app (elderly patients unable to use a smartphone were not enrolled). Enough baseline data to separate abnormal signals from normal variation, which the authors list as lacking in the 30-day window.
