Fitbit Charge 3 signals precede 4 of 5 COVID-19 exacerbations

EOV signals preceded four of five analysed cases (sensitivity 80%, specificity 90%)

Kanazawa pilot, 23 COVID-19 patients (2020): Fitbit oxygen-variation signals preceded 4 of 5 exacerbations (sensitivity 80%); one man's persistent signals led to polysomnography showing severe OSAS.

What happened

Yamagami, Nomura and colleagues at Kanazawa University and the Japan Community Health Care Organization (JCHO) Kanazawa Hospital published the DEXTERITY pilot in JMIR Formative Research on 16 September 2021 (used as the date observed; the study ran from August to November 2020 and the individual event is undated). Twenty-three patients with PCR-confirmed COVID-19 wore a Fitbit Charge 3 for 30 days; the investigators defined a high 'estimated oxygen variation' (EOV) signal as a significant dip and recovery of the oxygen level within the index period and counted a prediction as successful if the signal appeared within two days before an exacerbation. Exacerbation occurred in six patients (seven events); EOV signals preceded four of five analysed cases (sensitivity 80%, specificity 90%), better than resting-heart-rate signals (50% and 80%). Separately, patient JCHO-023, a 53-year-old man with hypertension who had recovered from COVID-19, showed 'a long-lasting high EOV signal' from days 18 to 29 without symptoms; the team suspected sleep apnoea, and a portable polysomnogram (Nihon Kohden SAS-2200) gave a 3% oxygen desaturation index of 33.6 per hour, severe obstructive sleep apnoea syndrome. The paper reports no treatment for him. The authors note that the Fitbit's functions and analysis algorithms were not approved as a medical device at the time, that baseline data were short, and that the five exacerbation cases are too few to generalise; the prediction analysis was defined against outcomes after the fact rather than as live alerts.

Not what it was built to do. Fitbit's EOV metric summarises overnight oxygen variation for consumers; the investigators repurposed it to flag COVID-19 deterioration, and the sleep-apnoea finding was incidental to that study aim.

The system's part

The device produced the EOV values; the investigators' own rule flagged high signals and clinicians decided to order polysomnography for the sleep-apnoea patient. The paper does not say whether Fitbit's EOV estimation uses machine learning and notes the functions were not approved as a medical device; the COVID-19 prediction figures were computed after the outcomes were known, not from alerts acted on in real time.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • A Fitbit Charge 3 worn for 30 days by patients with PCR-confirmed COVID-19, with EOV and resting heart rate extracted
  • An investigator-defined high-EOV signal (a significant dip and recovery of the oxygen level within the index period) reviewed by the study team
  • Weekly phone calls to discharged patients and access to portable polysomnography
What must hold again
  • Patients can pair the device with a smartphone app (elderly patients unable to use a smartphone were not enrolled)
  • Enough baseline data to separate abnormal signals from normal variation, which the authors list as lacking in the 30-day window
