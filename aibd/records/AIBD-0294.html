Fitbit Charge 3 signals precede 4 of 5 COVID-19 exacerbations

Kanazawa pilot of 23 COVID-19 patients (2020): Fitbit oxygen-variation signals preceded 4 of 5 exacerbations (sensitivity 80%); one man's persistent signals led to a finding of severe sleep apnea.

  • 1 a person

    One man received a diagnosis of severe obstructive sleep apnea (3% ODI 33.6/h) from an incidental wearable signal. The paper reports no treatment or follow-up for him.

  • Not rated an organization

    The 80% sensitivity for predicting exacerbation is a retrospective pilot result on five events, which the authors say is too small to generalize. No live alerting or changed care is documented.

What happened

Yamagami, Nomura and colleagues published the DEXTERITY pilot in JMIR Formative Research on 16 September 2021. They are at Kanazawa University and Japan Community Health Care Organization (JCHO) Kanazawa Hospital. The study ran from August to November 2020. The individual event is undated. Twenty-three patients with PCR-confirmed COVID-19 were followed for 30 days. Median Fitbit Charge 3 wear was 19 days. The investigators defined a high “estimated oxygen variation” (EOV) signal as a significant dip and recovery of the oxygen level within the index period. A prediction counted as successful if the signal appeared within two days before an exacerbation. Exacerbation occurred in six patients (seven events). EOV signals preceded four of five analyzed cases (sensitivity 80%, specificity 90%), better than resting-heart-rate signals (50% and 80%). Separately, patient JCHO-023, a 53-year-old man with hypertension recovered from COVID-19, showed “a long-lasting high EOV signal” from days 18 to 29 without symptoms. The team suspected sleep apnea. A portable polysomnogram (Nihon Kohden SAS-2200) gave a 3% oxygen desaturation index (ODI) of 33.6 per hour, severe obstructive sleep apnea syndrome. The paper reports no treatment for him. The authors note that the Fitbit's functions and analysis algorithms were not approved as a medical device at the time. They note that baseline data were short and that the five exacerbation cases are too few to generalize. The investigators computed the figures for predicting exacerbation after the outcomes were known, and no alert of an exacerbation was acted on in real time.

Details

AI system

Configuration

The participant used a Fitbit Charge 3.

Inputs and outputs

The Fitbit supplied oxygen-variation and resting-heart-rate values; investigators applied signal rules and compared them with later clinical deterioration.

Workflow

Persistent oxygen variation in one recovered patient led clinicians to order a sleep study, which diagnosed severe obstructive sleep apnea.

Human oversight

The study team reviewed the high oxygen-variation signals that the investigators' own rule flagged. Clinicians decided to order polysomnography for the patient with suspected sleep apnea. The figures for predicting exacerbation were computed after the outcomes were known, so no alert guided the care of an exacerbation during the study. [1]

Benefits

Distribution and access

The findings apply most directly to patients who could connect the Fitbit to a smartphone and account. [1]

Duration

Follow-up lasted 30 days, with median device wear of 19 days. The sleep-apnea clue persisted from days 18 through 29. [1]

Expected?

No

Measurement

Measurement

Retrospective comparison of investigator-defined high-EOV and high resting-heart-rate signals against recorded exacerbation events. [1]

Setting and sample

23 patients with PCR-confirmed COVID-19 at JCHO Kanazawa Hospital, August to November 2020; five exacerbation cases analyzed. [1]

Measures

Sensitivity and specificity of a high signal within two days of the onset of an exacerbation. [1]

Results

EOV sensitivity 80% and specificity 90%, with a positive predictive value of 9.3%; the abstract gives 50% and 80% for resting heart rate, and the results section gives 50% and 88%. [1]

Additional value from AI

Not isolated. The EOV metric comes from the device, and the signal rule and the decision to order polysomnography came from the investigators. [1]

Limitations

Five exacerbation cases; short baseline data, with each patient's baseline set to the minimum value over the whole study period; device functions not approved as a medical device at the time. [1]

Denominators

Fitbit Charge 3 worn for up to 30 days (median 19 days). [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Patients can pair the device with a smartphone app (elderly patients unable to use a smartphone were not enrolled). Enough baseline data to separate abnormal signals from normal variation, which the authors list as lacking in the 30-day window.

1 follow-up entry. Benefit recurred: 1.
