An Emirati ministry tests AI that flags similar nonprofit names

The Emirati Ministry of Community Empowerment reports that its name check, which uses a fine-tuned Sentence-BERT model to compare Arabic and English nonprofit names, passed acceptance tests.

What happened

The Ministry of Community Empowerment describes a name check for nonprofit registration. A Sentence-BERT model, fine-tuned on Arabic and English, compares proposed names. The system also suggests alternative names. Conventional database queries in SQL, exact matches and rules for prohibited terms remain part of the workflow. In the International Telecommunication Union report published on 29 October 2025, the ministry reports that the name check passed user acceptance tests. Its pilot scenarios covered duplicate detection, prohibited terms and alternative names. The report does not isolate an improvement that comes from the learned component. The ministry expects the system to reduce manual checking. It also expects the system to judge better whether two names are similar in meaning. The ministry's public service page for founding a nonprofit gives a 60-minute application time and a processing standard of 30 working days. That page does not mention an AI check. In a release of 9 July 2025, the ministry says that the registry's AI features have cut processing times, but it gives no figure.
