DeepMind RL controller shapes TCV plasmas and sustains droplets

A deep RL controller commanding all 19 TCV coils produced and held elongated, negative-triangularity, snowflake and twin-droplet plasmas at EPFL's Swiss Plasma Center (Nature, 16 February 2022).

ⓘ

What happened

Degrave et al. (Nature, published 16 February 2022; received 14 July 2021) describe a single deep neural network controller. It learns in simulation to command all 19 magnetic control coils of the Tokamak à Configuration Variable (TCV). TCV is a research tokamak at the Swiss Plasma Center, EPFL. Deployed on the real device, the controller produced and controlled elongated conventional shapes and advanced configurations such as negative triangularity and “snowflake” plasmas. It tracked location, current and shape. In one demonstration it established a snowflake with X-points 34 cm apart and narrowed the separation to 6.6 cm, combined RMSE 3.7 cm. It also sustained “droplets”, two separate plasmas maintained simultaneously in the vessel. MIT Technology Review (16 February 2022) reported that the AI controlled the real reactor without additional fine-tuning.

It quoted independent expert Jonathan Citrin of the Dutch Institute for Fundamental Energy Research and Swiss Plasma Center director Ambrogio Fasoli. The experiment dates are not given.

Purpose. The controller was built to shape and maintain plasmas in TCV from high-level objectives; the configurations produced, including droplets, were its intended use.

AI’s contribution

The reinforcement learning policy directly issued the coil voltage commands during the discharges, so the configurations are its output. The evidence is the peer-reviewed paper by the maker (DeepMind) and the operator (EPFL) plus an independent news report quoting an outside expert. Developer and deployer collaborated on the paper.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    The Swiss Plasma Center gained a controller the paper calls a notable advance for tokamak feedback control. It brought less design effort for new configurations and new droplet experiments.

Since then
DeepMind and the Swiss Plasma Center reported upgraded RL controllers with up to 65% better shape accuracy in simulation and new validating experiments on TCV.We present simulation results that show up to 65\% improvement in shape accuracy, achieve substantial reduction in the long-term bias of the plasma current, and additionally reduce the training time required to learn new tasks by a factor of 3 or more. source

Further evidence

Setting. A high-fidelity simulator (FGE) and equilibrium reconstruction (LIUQE) of the target tokamak available for training. A flexible research tokamak with 19 independently actuated coils. A real-time control system able to run a neural network policy at the required rate.

Conditions. A simulator accurate enough for zero-shot transfer to the device. Operator-specified targets within the device's physical and operational constraints. Access to machine time on a research tokamak.
