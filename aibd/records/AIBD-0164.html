DeepMind RL controller shapes TCV plasmas and sustains droplets

depth 3 for an organization

narrowed the separation to 6.6 cm with a combined RMSE of 3.7 cm

A deep RL controller commanding all 19 TCV coils produced and held elongated, negative-triangularity, snowflake and twin-droplet plasmas at EPFL's Swiss Plasma Center (Nature, 16 February 2022).

What happened

Degrave et al. (Nature, published 16 February 2022; received 14 July 2021) describe an architecture in which a single deep neural network learns in simulation to command the full set of 19 magnetic control coils of the Tokamak à Configuration Variable (TCV), a research tokamak at the Swiss Plasma Center, EPFL. Deployed on the real device, the controller produced and controlled elongated conventional shapes and advanced configurations such as negative triangularity and 'snowflake' plasmas, tracking location, current and shape; in one demonstration it established a snowflake with X-points 34 cm apart and narrowed the separation to 6.6 cm with a combined RMSE of 3.7 cm. It also sustained 'droplets', two separate plasmas maintained simultaneously in the vessel. MIT Technology Review (16 February 2022) reported that the AI controlled the real reactor without additional fine-tuning and quoted independent expert Jonathan Citrin of the Dutch Institute for Fundamental Energy Research and Swiss Plasma Center director Ambrogio Fasoli. The experiment dates are not given; the date used is the paper's publication date.

What it was built to do. The controller was built to shape and maintain plasmas in TCV from high-level objectives; the configurations produced, including droplets, were its intended use.

The system's part

The RL policy directly issued the coil voltage commands during the discharges, so the configurations are the controller's output; the evidence is the peer-reviewed paper co-authored by the maker (DeepMind) and the operator (EPFL) plus an independent news report quoting an outside expert. Developer and deployer collaborated on the paper.

Replication

What produced it. A high-fidelity simulator (FGE) and equilibrium reconstruction (LIUQE) of the target tokamak available for training. A flexible research tokamak with 19 independently actuated coils. A real-time control system able to run a neural network policy at the required rate.

What must hold again. A simulator accurate enough for zero-shot transfer to the device. Operator-specified targets within the device's physical and operational constraints. Access to machine time on a research tokamak.
