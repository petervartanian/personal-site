DeepMind's AI controller shapes tokamak plasmas and holds two at once

DeepMind's deep reinforcement learning controller, commanding all 19 TCV coils, produced and held elongated, negative-triangularity, snowflake and twin-droplet plasmas at EPFL's Swiss Plasma Center.

  • 3 an organization

    The Swiss Plasma Center gained a controller that the authors call a notable advance for tokamak feedback control. The controller reduced the design effort for new configurations and made new droplet experiments possible.

    No societal outcome beyond research progress is documented.

What happened

Degrave et al. (Nature, received 14 July 2021, published 16 February 2022) describe a single deep neural network controller. It learns in simulation to command all 19 magnetic control coils of the Tokamak à Configuration Variable (TCV). TCV is a research tokamak at the Swiss Plasma Center, EPFL. Deployed on the real device, the controller produced and controlled elongated conventional shapes and advanced configurations such as negative triangularity and “snowflake” plasmas. It tracked location, current and shape. In one demonstration it established a snowflake with X-points 34 cm apart and narrowed the separation to 6.6 cm with a combined root-mean-square error of 3.7 cm. It also sustained “droplets”, two separate plasmas maintained simultaneously in the vessel. The experiment dates are not given. MIT Technology Review (16 February 2022) reported that the AI controlled the real reactor without additional fine-tuning. It quoted independent expert Jonathan Citrin of the Dutch Institute for Fundamental Energy Research and Swiss Plasma Center director Ambrogio Fasoli. In a preprint of 21 July 2023, DeepMind and the Swiss Plasma Center reported upgraded controllers with up to 65% better shape accuracy in simulation and new validating experiments on TCV. This follow-up appeared in Fusion Engineering and Design in March 2024. On 21 January 2026, a team from Next Step Fusion and U.S. institutions, with no DeepMind or EPFL authors, reported that deep reinforcement learning controllers kept errors in plasma shape within 1.5 to 2.0 cm on the DIII-D tokamak.

Details

AI system

Autonomy

The learned policy directly issued voltage commands to all 19 magnetic coils during experimental plasma discharges.

Workflow

A high-fidelity simulator (FGE) and equilibrium reconstruction (LIUQE) of the target tokamak available for training. A flexible research tokamak with 19 independently actuated coils. A real-time control system able to run a neural network policy at the required rate.

Human oversight

Researchers trained the controller in simulation and specified target plasma configurations within the tokamak's operating limits before testing it on TCV.

Benefits

Reach

The sources report no count of beneficiaries. The benefit went to one research organization, the Swiss Plasma Center, which operates TCV. [1] [2] [3]

Duration

The droplets were held over the whole 200 ms control window. On 21 July 2023 DeepMind and the Swiss Plasma Center reported upgraded controllers and new validating experiments on TCV. [1] [2] [3]

People and places

Dependencies

The controller required a TCV simulator, magnetic measurements, and a real-time executable running at 10 kHz. Conventional control established the plasma before the learned policy took over.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

A simulator accurate enough for zero-shot transfer to the device. Operator-specified targets within the device's physical and operational constraints. Access to machine time on a research tokamak.
