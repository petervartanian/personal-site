Relation to AI

Pattern-recognition classifiers turned stump muscle signals into predicted phantom movements that a virtual limb carried out. The 2014 authors argue that this overt motor execution drives the relief. In 2024 the same group's randomized trial compared the method with guided motor imagery that decodes nothing and found no difference in pain reduction. The trial does not support a specific benefit from the machine-learning decoding, and the therapy as a whole was followed by large pain reductions in both arms.

Later findings

A double-blind randomized trial in 81 patients at 9 clinics in 7 countries reported that pain fell by 64.5% with machine-learning phantom motor execution and by 68.2% with guided motor imagery. The authors found no evidence that the decoded execution was superior. The pain relief recurred, and the added value of the machine-learning decoding was not shown. [3]

Sources

  1. Case report Developer

    Ortiz-Catalan M, Sander N, Kristoffersen MB, Håkansson B, Brånemark R (2014). Treatment of phantom limb pain (PLP) based on augmented reality and gaming controlled by myoelectric pattern recognition: a case study of a chronic PLP patient. Frontiers in Neuroscience 8:24.

    Eight electrodes were placed by palpation while the patient attempted movements; signals were checked, classifiers trained, and pain assessed after each session. Four- and ten-week changes match the account. Methods: Myoelectric recordings and Intervention; Results

    Original

    The sustained level of pain reported by the patient was gradually reduced to complete pain-free periods.
    Individual and simultaneous phantom movements were predicted using myoelectric pattern recognition and were then used as input for VR and AR environments, as well as for a racing game.

  2. Peer-reviewed study Developer

    Ortiz-Catalan M, Guðmundsdóttir RA, Kristoffersen MB, et al. (2016). Phantom motor execution facilitated by machine learning and augmented reality as treatment for phantom limb pain: a single group, clinical trial in patients with chronic intractable phantom limb pain. Lancet 388(10062):2885-2894.

    Phantom limb pain decreased from pre-treatment to the last treatment session by 47% (SD 39; absolute mean change 1·0 [0·8]; p=0·001) for weighted pain distribution

  3. Peer-reviewed study Developer

    Lendaro E, Van der Sluis CK, Hermansson L, et al., Extended reality used in the treatment of phantom limb pain: a multicenter, double-blind, randomized controlled trial, Pain, 5 September 2024. Max Ortiz-Catalan, who developed the method, is senior author and two authors list Integrum AB; the trial reports that pain fell by about two thirds with machine-learning phantom motor execution and by a similar amount with guided motor imagery that decodes nothing, so it supports the relief and not the part that the decoding plays.

    PLP decreased by 64.5% and 68.2% in PME and PMI groups, respectively.

  4. Peer-reviewed study Independent author Context or contrary evidence

    Vassantachart AY, Yeo E, Chau B, Virtual and Augmented Reality-based Treatments for Phantom Limb Pain: A Systematic Review, Innovations in Clinical Neuroscience 19(10-12):48-57, October 2022. The authors work at Loma Linda University and the Department of Veterans Affairs in Loma Linda, and they state that no funding was provided and that they hold no conflicts of interest; they scored the 2014 case report 9 on the MINORS quality scale and urge caution about the evidence base.

    While results are promising, most publications remain at the case series level, and clinical indications should be cautioned.
