What happened

A 72-year-old man's below-elbow amputation in 1965 left him with 48 years of phantom limb pain, resistant to mirror therapy, drugs, acupuncture and hypnosis. He was treated by a Chalmers University of Technology / Sahlgrenska University Hospital team (both in Gothenburg; the paper does not name the treatment site). Treatment ran for 18 weeks from March 2013. The exact start date is not reported. Stump surface electromyography was classified by myoelectric pattern recognition (linear discriminant analysis and a multilayer perceptron in the open-source BioPatRec platform). It drove a virtual limb shown in augmented reality and a racing game. After four weeks he reported episodes of lower pain intensity, after ten weeks near-absent pain developing into completely pain-free periods. His phantom fist relaxed and he could move the phantom freely (Frontiers in Neuroscience, 2014).

What it was built to do. Treat phantom limb pain by letting the patient execute phantom movements decoded from stump muscle activity

    The sustained level of pain reported by the patient was gradually reduced to complete pain-free periods.

The system's part

The pattern-recognition classifiers turn stump EMG into predicted phantom movements that the virtual limb executes. The authors argue that this engagement of motor circuitry, rather than a mirror of the contralateral limb, is the therapeutic mechanism. So the AI component is central, though the single-case design cannot isolate it.

Replication

What produced it. Decodable residual muscle activity in the stump. Classifier-driven virtual limb that responds to the patient's own effort. Repeated sessions with gamified motor execution.

What must hold again. Patient can produce distinguishable EMG patterns for phantom movements. Weekly or twice-weekly sessions sustained over months. Augmented-reality display of the restored limb.
