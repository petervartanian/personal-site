Rapiscan 3D X-ray CT detects dried marine wildlife at 86-96%

off the scale

detection rates of 95% (shark fin), 96% (seahorse) and 86% (sea cucumber)

Pirotta et al. (2026) trained 3D CNN algorithms on 298 CT scans: detection 95% shark fin, 96% seahorse, 86% sea cucumber with low false alarms; authors say front-line implementation is the next step.

What happened

Pirotta et al., in Frontiers in Ocean Sustainability (published 8 June 2026), used Rapiscan 3D X-ray CT technology to build AI algorithms for autodetection of marine wildlife in mail and air-traveler pathways: 298 scans from 68 samples of dried shark fin, seahorse and sea cucumber, expanded with 3D convolutional neural networks and 3D Threat Image Projection, gave detection rates of 95% (shark fin), 96% (seahorse) and 86% (sea cucumber) with false alarm rates of 2%, 9% and 1%. The authors, who include Rapiscan Systems staff and researchers at Taronga Conservation Society, Macquarie University, NSW DPIRD, the Australian Museum and others, state that the paper 'demonstrates the efficacy and potential impact of AI detection tools to complement existing methods'. In The Conversation (7 June 2026) the lead authors reported success rates of 95%, 95% and 85% and wrote that 'The next step is relying on implementation of these algorithms at the front lines'; the earlier verifier found, via Earth.Org (21 May 2026, not re-fetched here), that the tool is a proof of concept with no operational customs use and that a Buenos Aires seizure it mentioned did not involve the tool. The date used is the journal publication date.

What it was built to do. Automated detection of trafficked marine wildlife in 3D X-ray screening of mail and luggage to support customs inspection; no operational use yet.

The system's part

The AI algorithms are the whole of the reported result (detection and false-alarm rates on CT scans), documented in a peer-reviewed paper and a Conversation article both written by the developers, including a Rapiscan-affiliated lead author; no seizure, inspection or deployment outcome exists, and the sole independent account (Earth.Org, per the earlier verifier) describes a proof of concept.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Rapiscan 3D X-ray CT scanners already used for security screening. Reference specimens of dried shark fin, seahorse and sea cucumber from Taronga and museum collections. Synthetic data expansion with 3D Threat Image Projection. Vendor funding of the research.

What must hold again. Access to CT scanners at mail and airport screening points. Algorithm performance on concealed, mixed consignments. Customs authority adoption. Legal basis for seizure on algorithm alerts.
