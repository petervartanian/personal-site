Rapiscan CT algorithms detect marine wildlife at 86–96% in testing

A vendor-affiliated CT study reported 86–96% detection of three marine-wildlife classes and 13% overall false alarms; testing used original scans and synthetic composites, with no customs outcome.

ⓘ

AI system

System

Rapiscan 3D X-ray CT marine-wildlife detection algorithms (3D convolutional neural networks with 3D Threat Image Projection)

Model

Experimental three-dimensional convolutional neural networks.

Release

The experimental convolutional networks have no published release identifier. [1]

Application developer

Rapiscan Systems Ltd with Taronga Conservation Society Australia and Macquarie University

Role

The networks identified marine-wildlife specimens in CT images of baggage.

Configuration

Researchers trained the networks on CT scans and synthetic images with inserted marine-wildlife specimens.

Inputs and outputs

The networks identified marine-wildlife specimens in CT images of baggage.

Autonomy

Not assessed

Workflow

Researchers scanned marine specimens with a Rapiscan RTT110. They inserted specimens digitally into other CT images to expand the training data and then tested the detection algorithms. [1]

Human oversight

Not assessed

Relation to AI

AI classified objects in CT baggage scans. Most positive test bags used synthetically inserted specimens, and testing drew from the same specimen population. The paper is original research with disclosed Rapiscan involvement, not an independent deployment evaluation. [1]
