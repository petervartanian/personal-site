What happened

Pirotta et al., in Frontiers in Ocean Sustainability (published 8 June 2026), used Rapiscan 3D X-ray computed tomography (CT) technology to build AI algorithms. The algorithms autodetect marine wildlife in mail and air-traveler pathways. The work used 298 scans from 68 samples of dried shark fin, seahorse and sea cucumber. It expanded them with 3D convolutional neural networks and 3D Threat Image Projection. Detection rates were 95% (shark fin), 96% (seahorse) and 86% (sea cucumber), with false alarm rates of 2%, 9% and 1%. The authors include Rapiscan Systems staff and researchers at Taronga Conservation Society, Macquarie University, NSW DPIRD, the Australian Museum and others. They state that the paper “demonstrates the efficacy and potential impact of AI detection tools to complement existing methods”.

In The Conversation (7 June 2026) the lead authors reported success rates of 95%, 95% and 85%. They wrote that “The next step is relying on implementation of these algorithms at the front lines”. Earth.Org (21 May 2026), which is not among the sources, reported that the tool is a proof of concept with no operational customs use. That check also found that a Buenos Aires seizure it mentioned did not involve the tool.

What it was built to do. Automated detection of trafficked marine wildlife in 3D X-ray screening of mail and luggage to support customs inspection; no operational use yet.

The system's part

The AI algorithms are the whole of the reported result: detection and false-alarm rates on CT scans. They are documented in a peer-reviewed paper and a Conversation article, both written by the developers, including a Rapiscan-affiliated lead author. No seizure, inspection or deployment outcome exists. The only independent account, Earth.Org, describes a proof of concept.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. Rapiscan 3D X-ray CT scanners already used for security screening. Reference specimens of dried shark fin, seahorse and sea cucumber from Taronga and museum collections. Synthetic data expansion with 3D Threat Image Projection. Vendor funding of the research.

What must hold again. Access to CT scanners at mail and airport screening points. Algorithm performance on concealed, mixed consignments. Customs authority adoption. Legal basis for seizure on algorithm alerts.
