Rapiscan CT algorithms detect marine wildlife at 86–96% in testing

A vendor-affiliated CT study reported 86–96% detection of three marine-wildlife classes and 13% overall false alarms; testing used original scans and synthetic composites, with no customs outcome.

ⓘ

What happened

Pirotta and colleagues tested AI detection of marine wildlife in three-dimensional X-ray scans. Their June 8, 2026 paper reports 298 scans from 68 specimens, including preserved specimens and fresh shark material. The researchers expanded the scans by inserting specimen images into baggage images. A 512-bag test set contained both original scans and these synthetic composites, drawn from the same specimen population. Table 3 reports detection rates of 95% for shark fins, 96% for seahorses, and 86% for sea cucumbers. The overall false-alarm rate was 13%; the class-specific rates were 2%, 9%, and 1%. Rapiscan supplied equipment and funded staff participation in analysis and writing. The paper demonstrates laboratory detection, with no reported operational seizure or customs evaluation.

Purpose. Automated detection of marine wildlife in CT scans of mail and luggage to support customs inspection; no operational evaluation is reported.

AI’s contribution

AI classified objects in CT baggage scans. Most positive test bags used synthetically inserted specimens, and testing drew from the same specimen population. The paper is original research with disclosed Rapiscan involvement, not an independent deployment evaluation.

Conditions. Access to CT scanners at mail and airport screening points. Algorithm performance on concealed, mixed consignments. Customs authority adoption. Legal basis for seizure on algorithm alerts.
