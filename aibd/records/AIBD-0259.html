Crash warning and automatic braking cut front-to-rear crashes by 49%

In U.S. police-reported crash data, forward collision warning with automatic emergency braking cut front-to-rear crashes by 49%, and automatic braking cut pickup rear-end crash rates by 43%.

Brief from Peter

In a final rule of 9 May 2024, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) required automatic emergency braking on new light vehicles in the United States from September 2029. It found that the systems had reduced injuries and saved lives.1 On 16 November 2022, VOA Learning English reported that PARTS, a partnership of automakers and NHTSA, had found 49% fewer front-to-rear crashes when the striking vehicle had both forward collision warning and automatic emergency braking. It also reported that the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) had found a 43% lower rear-end crash rate for pickups with automatic emergency braking.2 In the peer-reviewed version of the IIHS study, Jessica Cicchino found in her main analysis that automatic emergency braking was associated with a 34% lower risk of rear-end-striking crashes.3 In a second study, published on 6 January 2025 and covering model years 2015 to 2023, PARTS again measured a 49% reduction in front-to-rear crashes.4 The estimates cover the whole system. They do not separate the camera and radar software from the brakes or from the driver who responds to the warning. False positives in Nissan's automatic emergency braking have posed traffic risks to drivers.
