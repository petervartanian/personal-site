FCW with AEB cuts front-to-rear crashes 49%, PARTS study finds

front-to-rear crashes reduced by 49%

VOA Learning English (16 Nov 2022): the automaker-NHTSA Partnership for Analytics Research in Traffic Safety analysed 12 million police-reported crashes in 13 states; IIHS analysed pickups in 25 st...

What happened

VOA Learning English reported on 16 Nov 2022, drawing on Associated Press coverage, that two U.S. studies had quantified the effect of front crash prevention. The Partnership for Analytics Research in Traffic Safety, a collaboration between automakers and the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, examined more than 12 million police-reported crashes in 13 states involving 93 vehicle models from 2015 to 2020 and found front-to-rear crashes reduced by 49% when the striking vehicle had both forward collision warning and automatic emergency braking, with injury crashes down 53%; Stellantis executive Tim Czapp of the PARTS board was quoted. The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety, using police-reported crashes in 25 states from 2017 to 2020, found automatic emergency braking reduced rear-end crash rates for pickups by 43%; IIHS researcher Jessica Cicchino urged automakers to fit every pickup with the feature. The PARTS and IIHS reports themselves were not fetched (an IIHS page guess returned 404); the OECD AI Incidents Monitor lists the coverage (2022-11-15-d32d) as an aggregate benefit. These are different studies from the 2017 IIHS analysis already in the database as AIBD-0008. The date given is the publication date.

What it was built to do. Forward collision warning and automatic emergency braking are built to detect an impending front-to-rear collision and warn or brake.

The system's part

Camera and radar perception triggers warnings and braking; the studies compare crash rates of equipped and unequipped vehicles using police-reported crash data, and the underlying reports were not read.

Replication

What produced it
  • Large multi-state police-reported crash databases linked to vehicle equipment
  • Comparison of equipped and unequipped versions of the same models
  • Industry-regulator data sharing (PARTS) and an insurer-funded institute (IIHS)
What must hold again
  • Equipment status can be identified per vehicle
  • Crash reporting thresholds are comparable across states
