SkinVision flags a mole as high risk; stage 1A melanoma removed

SkinVision rated a 31-year-old's changing arm mole high risk; stage 1A melanoma was found and removed, and he is cancer-free.

AI system

System

SkinVision skin-check app (AI analysis of smartphone photographs of moles, graded low, medium or high risk)

Application developer

SkinVision

Workflow

A smartphone photograph of a changing mole submitted to SkinVision's risk classifier. A high-risk rating with an instruction to see a doctor, acted on by the user. A GP referral and an excision with pathology that confirms and stages the lesion.

Relation to AI

The app's classifier graded a phone photograph of the mole high risk and told him to see a doctor at once. By his account that prompt, not the color change, is what sent him to his GP. He said he only goes to the doctor when absolutely desperate. The referral, excision, biopsy and staging were done by the GP, the dermatologist and pathology, none of whom the Daily Mail quotes. The article does not report the app's score, its version, or whether the 48-hour dermatologist advice it mentions was used.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The lesion is one the classifier can score from a phone photograph. The article reports no score or app version. The user follows the app's advice rather than treating the rating as noise. Clinical follow-up is available quickly enough for early-stage removal. Here the operation came two weeks after referral.

Later findings

No later report on this case was found in the cited sources; SkinVision's site now says the app is not yet available in the United States pending regulatory clearance.

Field

Healthcare - dermatology / melanoma detection. Consumer mobile health app.

Purpose

SkinVision is built to grade a phone photograph of a mole low, medium or high risk. It tells users with a high-risk result to see a doctor, with dermatologist advice sent within 48 hours. This benefit is that intended use.
