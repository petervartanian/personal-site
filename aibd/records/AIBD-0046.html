Scaffolds built with RFdiffusion help solve three protein structures

A Shandong University team used scaffold domains designed with RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN to solve three small-protein cryo-EM structures at 2.29 to 2.50 angstroms, and a fourth without them.

  • 2 an organization

    The Shandong University group obtained a reusable fiducial. With its AI-designed domains, the group solved three structures between 2.29 and 2.50 angstroms for proteins that are otherwise difficult targets for cryo-electron microscopy.

  • Not rated society

    The four structures are public in the Protein Data Bank, and no paper by another group reports using the Trimbody, so no wider outcome is reported.

The system’s part
RFdiffusion generated the backbones of the H3 and TAIL scaffold domains, ProteinMPNN designed their sequences, and AlphaFold2 ranked the candidates. The authors write that the choice of backbone involved a degree of expert-guided selection. People raised the nanobodies, collected and processed the cryo-EM data, and built the models. Three of the four structures used the AI-designed domains. The Galectin10 structure at 2.62 angstroms came from the initial scaffold, iTrimbody, which carries no AI-designed domain, and Protein Data Bank entry 9UBR carries that name. The paper reports one internal comparison. With iTrimbody the Aequorea coerulescens GFP data showed density only for the 5350A trimer. With the AI-designed domains the same protein reached 2.29 angstroms.

What happened

RFdiffusion is a generative model of protein backbones from the University of Washington Institute for Protein Design. It was published in Nature on 11 July 2023 with experimental validation. That validation included a 2.9 angstrom cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) structure of a designed binder-hemagglutinin complex. The structure matched the computational design model to a root-mean-square deviation of 0.63 angstrom. An unaffiliated group at Shandong University and collaborating institutes used RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN to design rigid helical-bundle and TAIL domains for a trimbody fiducial. The group then determined atomic-resolution cryo-EM structures of four small proteins that are normally intractable. The group deposited the Aequorea coerulescens GFP structure in the Protein Data Bank on 1 April 2025. The four structures have been public in the Protein Data Bank since 11 February 2026. On 24 February 2026 the group reported the work in Nature Communications. Human Galectin10 came out at 2.62 angstroms and Aequorea coerulescens GFP at 2.29 angstroms. The Nectin4 IgV domain came out at 2.43 angstroms and Escherichia coli LacY at 2.50 angstroms. Three of the four structures used the AI-designed domains. The Galectin10 structure came from the initial scaffold, iTrimbody, which carries no AI-designed domain. With iTrimbody the GFP data showed density only for the 5350A trimer. With the AI-designed domains the same protein reached 2.29 angstroms. An earlier DARPin-containing cage had determined GFP at a local resolution of 3.8 angstroms without a reliable atomic interpretation. Nature Communications named Alex De Marco, Jan Steyaert and one anonymous reviewer among the reviewers of the paper. The first reviewer in the published peer review file warned that the rigid scaffold may hold target proteins in shapes that they do not take in nature, and both reviewers in the file endorsed the revised paper. Shandong University has filed patent application No. 202512010140.8 on the scaffold constructs, with three of the authors as inventors. The authors wrote that a study of anti-Tim3 nanobodies solved with the Trimbody would be published elsewhere. As of October 2026 no such study has appeared, and no paper by another group reports using the Trimbody.

Details

AI system

Model

RFdiffusion designs protein backbones, and ProteinMPNN designs their amino-acid sequences.

Role

RFdiffusion generated protein backbones, and ProteinMPNN generated amino-acid sequences.

Inputs and outputs

RFdiffusion generated scaffold backbones and ProteinMPNN supplied sequences for rigid domains that held the target-bound nanobodies in a suitable cryo-EM assembly.

Workflow

Publicly released RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN models. A nanobody against each target and a rigid trimbody scaffold design. Cryo-EM data collection and processing by the deploying group.

Human oversight

Researchers selected designs, produced and purified the proteins, assembled target complexes, and checked them through cryo-EM imaging and structure determination.
