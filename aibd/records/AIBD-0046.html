RFdiffusion scaffolds help solve four small-protein structures

depth 2 for an organization, depth 1 for society

Claimed by the parties, 2 sources.

four small-protein structures at 2.29 to 2.62 angstroms

A Shandong University team used RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN scaffolds in a trimbody fiducial to solve four small-protein structures at 2.29 to 2.62 angstroms.

What happened

RFdiffusion, a generative model of protein backbones from the University of Washington Institute for Protein Design, was published in Nature on 11 July 2023 with experimental validation that included a 2.9 angstrom cryo-EM structure of a designed binder-haemagglutinin complex matching the computational design model to 0.63 angstrom r.m.s.d. On 24 February 2026 an unaffiliated group at Shandong University and collaborating institutes reported in Nature Communications that they used RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN to design rigid helical-bundle and TAIL domains for a trimbody fiducial, and then determined atomic-resolution cryo-EM structures of four small proteins that are normally intractable: human Galectin10 at 2.62 angstroms, Aequorea coerulescens GFP at 2.29 angstroms, the Nectin4 IgV domain at 2.43 angstroms and Escherichia coli LacY at 2.50 angstroms.

What it was built to do. Generate de novo protein backbones to order, here to build rigid scaffolds that hold a small target protein still for cryo-electron microscopy.

The system's part

RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN produced the de novo scaffold domains that rigidify the assembly; the deploying authors, who are not the tool's developers, then validated the designs experimentally by determining cryo-EM structures at atomic resolution. The designs originated with the deploying group using the publicly released model.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Publicly released RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN models. A nanobody against each target and a rigid trimbody scaffold design. Cryo-EM data collection and processing by the deploying group.

What must hold again. Publicly released RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN weights. A nanobody against the target protein. Cryo-EM data collection and processing capability.
