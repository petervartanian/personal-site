RFdiffusion scaffolds help solve four small-protein structures

A Shandong University team used RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN scaffolds in a trimbody fiducial to solve four small-protein structures at 2.29 to 2.62 angstroms.

ⓘ

What happened

RFdiffusion is a generative model of protein backbones from the University of Washington Institute for Protein Design. It was published in Nature on 11 July 2023 with experimental validation. That validation included a 2.9 angstrom cryo-EM structure of a designed binder-haemagglutinin complex. The structure matched the computational design model to 0.63 angstrom r.m.s.d. On 24 February 2026 an unaffiliated group at Shandong University and collaborating institutes reported in Nature Communications. They used RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN to design rigid helical-bundle and TAIL domains for a trimbody fiducial. They then determined atomic-resolution cryo-EM structures of four small proteins that are normally intractable. Human Galectin10 came out at 2.62 angstroms and Aequorea coerulescens GFP at 2.29 angstroms.

The Nectin4 IgV domain came out at 2.43 angstroms and Escherichia coli LacY at 2.50 angstroms.

Purpose. Generate de novo protein backbones to order, here to build rigid scaffolds that hold a small target protein still for cryo-electron microscopy.

AI’s contribution

RFdiffusion and ProteinMPNN produced the de novo scaffold domains that make the assembly rigid. The deploying authors are not the tool's developers. They validated the designs experimentally by determining cryo-EM structures at atomic resolution. The designs originated with the deploying group, which used the publicly released model.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    The deploying group obtained a reusable fiducial. It yielded four structures between 2.29 and 2.62 angstroms, for proteins that are otherwise difficult targets for cryo-EM.

  • society

    A methodological advance for structural biology with no identified downstream beneficiaries reported in the source.
