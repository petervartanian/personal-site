SES Water's AI leak alarms cut leak run times by up to 40%

SES Water's Aquasuite Burst compares data from 1,000 flow and pressure sensors with predicted levels and raises leak alarms within minutes; the utility reports that run times fell by up to 40%.

What happened

Sutton and East Surrey (SES) Water supplies 160 million liters a day to 300,000 homes and businesses in parts of Surrey, Kent and South London. Haskoning states in an undated case study that, before the system, an analyst checked deviations in pressure and flow data by eye once a day. Haskoning says that a trial preceded the rollout across SES Water's whole supply area. The case study identifies the software as Aquasuite Burst, and it describes predictive flow-and-pressure models that alert a 24-hour control room. On 11 March 2024 the BBC (Yvette Austin) reported that SES Water was among the first companies in the United Kingdom using AI across its whole network to reduce leaks. Its 1,000 underground sensors monitor flow and pressure. The technology predicts what levels should be. When a leak happens, the system raises an alarm within minutes and engineers are deployed. Innovation manager Jeremy Heath said that pipes are often repaired before customers notice a drop in pressure. Head of asset strategy Liam Ahearne said that the smart network had reduced leakage run rate times by up to 40%. In the case study, Sian Evans of SES Water says that the tool cut the run time of critical burst mains by 40%. Haskoning attributes the 40% run-time reduction specifically to critical burst mains. The BBC noted the regulator Ofwat's figure that about a fifth of water put into the network in England and Wales is lost to leakage. It gave SES Water's 2022/23 loss of 76 liters per property per day. It cited the Angling Trust's criticism that the company's targets were not good enough. The sources include no regulator's report of a specific repaired burst.

Details

AI system

Role

The software compared sensor readings with predicted values to flag possible leaks.

Configuration

The deployment used Aquasuite Burst.

Inputs and outputs

Sensors measured water flow and pressure. The system compared those readings with expected levels and raised alarms for suspected leaks within minutes.

Workflow

Dense sensor coverage (1,000 flow and pressure sensors) across a compact supply area. Predicted-versus-actual comparison raising alarms within minutes. Repair crews dispatched on alarms rather than customer reports.

Human oversight

Engineers investigated and repaired leaks after alarms, sometimes before customers noticed lower pressure.

Benefits

Duration

Haskoning says that a trial preceded the rollout across SES Water's whole supply area, and the BBC reported the result on 11 March 2024. No dated development after March 2024 is recorded, and the vendor's undated case study repeats the 40% figure. [1] [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Sensor density and telemetry sufficient to localize anomalies. Crew capacity to act on alarms quickly. Independent reporting of leakage volumes to confirm the claimed effect.

Evidence

Original evidence

The BBC report relays statements by SES Water staff, and the Haskoning case study is the vendor's own page quoting SES Water. Both 40% figures come from the utility or its vendor. [1] [2]

Purpose

The smart network detects leaks early by comparing sensor data with predicted levels, so that SES Water can send engineers to repair them sooner. Shorter leak run times are the intended result.
