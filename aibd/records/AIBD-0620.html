ChatGPT guidance improves dental students' simulated skill scores

In a 2024 randomized trial with 187 dental undergraduates in China, students who used ChatGPT-3.5 with instructional videos scored 73.12 against 65.54 on a virtual reality skills test.

What happened

Researchers at the School and Hospital of Stomatology, Wuhan University, recruited 192 undergraduate dental students at a Chinese university by 25 October 2024. After eye-tracking calibration, 187 students were randomly assigned by sealed envelopes to learn a clinical operative skill from videos alone or from the same videos with the free ChatGPT-3.5, and all finished the protocol by 2 November 2024. During a one-week learning period the ChatGPT group could ask about doubts and check procedural steps. Both groups were then tested on a desktop virtual reality simulator. The ChatGPT group scored 73.12 against 65.54 for the video-only group (P < .001) and showed lower cognitive load on eye-tracking measures. Students with low spatial ability gained most, 70.20 against 55.41, and the difference among students with high spatial ability was not significant. The authors note that the comparison group had no added support and that they could not check the accuracy of the answers that ChatGPT gave. The Journal of Medical Internet Research published the study on 27 May 2025.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

ChatGPT-3.5 (free version)

Workflow

Students could ask ChatGPT about doubts during a week of video-based skill learning. The group received written guidelines on using ChatGPT before the study.

Measurement

Measurement

Operational skill score on a desktop virtual reality simulator; pupil diameter for cognitive load; questionnaires. [1]

Setting and sample

187 undergraduate dental students at a Chinese university, October to November 2024. [1]

Measures

Operational performance score. [1]

Results

73.12 against 65.54 (P < .001). [1]

Additional value from AI

The gain is attributed to adding ChatGPT to video learning. [1]

Limitations

One-week intervention; comparison group had no added support; accuracy of ChatGPT answers not checked; simulated test. [1]

Denominators

One week of skill learning. [1]

Materials for replication

ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06942130. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Students need access to ChatGPT during practice. Instructors should check the accuracy of the answers that students receive.
