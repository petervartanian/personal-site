DeterTech AI cameras may have cut trespass at Dingwall

Network Rail reported fewer trespass incidents after installing DeterTech AI cameras at Dingwall, alongside signage and campaigns. It supplied no post-installation count and noted a renewed rise.

ⓘ

What happened

Network Rail reported on June 9, 2026 that trespass had declined at Dingwall after it installed AI-powered DeterTech cameras. Monitoring in June 2025 had found an average of five incidents a day. The cameras detect people leaving the designated route, sound warnings, and send images to Network Rail and British Transport Police. Signage, safety campaigns, and minor site changes accompanied the installation. The release gives no post-installation count and says that incidents had begun rising again as the weather improved. It does not isolate the cameras’ effect or document an individual alert that prevented an accident.

Purpose. The cameras are built to detect people leaving the designated route, warn them and alert Network Rail and police in real time.

AI’s contribution

Not assessed. The release credits the reduction to the cameras together with signage and campaigns. Detection counts, false alarms and responses are not reported. The cameras' AI is described only as detecting route departures.

Further evidence

Setting. A known trespass shortcut between two crossings with CCTV evidence from the prior year. Cameras with on-site audio warnings and live relay to the infrastructure manager and police. Concurrent signage and safety campaigns.

Conditions. Warnings deter rather than being ignored. Police and Network Rail respond to live images. Seasonal and school-holiday increases are monitored.
