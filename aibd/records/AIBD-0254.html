Network Rail says DeterTech AI cameras cut trespass at Dingwall

off the scale

Network Rail release (9 Jun 2026): June 2025 data showed five trespass incidents a day between Dingwall Canal North and South crossings; after cameras that detect route departures, issue audio warn...

What happened

In a media release for International Level Crossing Awareness Day on 9 Jun 2026, Network Rail's Scotland's Railway said data from June 2025 had shown an average of five trespass incidents a day at Dingwall, where people used the railway bridge over the River Peffery as a shortcut between the Dingwall Canal North and South level crossings. It stated that following the introduction of AI-powered DeterTech cameras, which detect when someone leaves the designated route, issue an audio warning and send live images to Network Rail and British Transport Police for a rapid response, incidents had reduced, alongside new anti-trespass signage, campaigns and minor improvements; it also said incidents were starting to rise again as the weather improved. Rachel Shaw, Network Rail's head of operational safety, spoke of a big drop in trespass at Dingwall, and BTP Inspector Ashley Forbes said worrying levels of trespass were still being seen. No post-deployment count, installation date or individual detection leading to a train stop or police attendance is given; the release is the deployer's own account; the BBC (10 Jun 2026) and the Ross-shire Journal (9 Jun 2026) relayed the same account, and neither names the supplier or gives a post-installation count. The BBC (2 Jul 2025) had reported Network Rail's CCTV of people using the bridge as a shortcut, with trains passing at up to 65mph. The date given is the publication date.

What it was built to do. The cameras are built to detect people leaving the designated route, warn them and alert Network Rail and police in real time.

not assessed The release attributes the reduction to the cameras together with signage and campaigns; detection counts, false alarms and responses are not reported, and the cameras' AI is described only as detecting route departures.

Replication

What produced it. A known trespass shortcut between two crossings with CCTV evidence from the prior year. Cameras with on-site audio warnings and live relay to the infrastructure manager and police. Concurrent signage and safety campaigns.

What must hold again. Warnings deter rather than being ignored. Police and Network Rail respond to live images. Seasonal and school-holiday increases are monitored.
