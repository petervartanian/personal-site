What happened

Monitoring in June 2025 found an average of five trespass incidents a day at Dingwall, where people used the bridge as a shortcut between the Dingwall Canal North and South crossings. Network Rail then installed AI-powered DeterTech cameras. The cameras detect people leaving the designated route, sound warnings, and send images to Network Rail and British Transport Police. Signage, safety campaigns, and minor site changes accompanied the installation. Network Rail reported on 9 June 2026 that trespass at Dingwall had declined after the installation. The release gives no count after the installation. It says that incidents had begun rising again as the weather improved. It does not isolate the cameras’ effect or document an individual alert that prevented an accident.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The cameras detected people leaving the designated route, issued an audio warning, and sent live images to Network Rail and British Transport Police.

Autonomy

Warnings and image transmission were automatic. Rail staff and police remained responsible for intervention and enforcement.

Workflow

A known trespass shortcut between two crossings with CCTV evidence from the prior year. Cameras with on-site audio warnings and live relay to the infrastructure manager and police. Concurrent signage and safety campaigns.

Human oversight

The cameras send images to Network Rail and British Transport Police when they detect a person leaving the designated route. The release does not describe who reviews those images or how staff respond to them. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Warnings deter rather than being ignored. Police and Network Rail respond to live images. Seasonal and school-holiday increases are monitored.
