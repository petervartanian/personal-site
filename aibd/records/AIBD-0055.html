Neuromotus EMG decoding restores thumb and wrist movement

active thumb extension (4 degrees) and active wrist flexion (6 degrees)

A 72-year-old with a paralysed replanted arm regained active thumb and wrist movement after 18 sessions of ML-decoded motor execution.

What happened

A 72-year-old man whose right arm was replanted after a traumatic above-elbow amputation had no functional recovery after conventional rehabilitation and developed chronic neuropathic pain. His arm had been replanted at Sahlgrenska University Hospital in Mölndal, Sweden; the Mölndal-based Center for Bionics and Pain Research / Sahlgrenska University Hospital (C.A.R.E.) team then delivered 18 sessions of myoelectric motor execution over 84 days, nine of which included sensory training with tactile grids (the paper does not name the treatment site). Eight channels of electromyography were classified by a linear discriminant analysis classifier (Neuromotus, Integrum AB) so that intended movements were decoded from the replanted arm and shown on screen as virtual limbs and games. He regained active thumb extension (4 degrees) and active wrist flexion (6 degrees) and extension (10 degrees), movements absent before treatment; pain fluctuated with no consistent change. Treatment dates are not given, so the publication date (23 November 2024) is used.

What it was built to do. Rehabilitate a highly impaired replanted arm and reduce neuropathic pain using decoded motor intention as feedback

The system's part

The LDA classifier decodes intended movements from weak EMG in a hand and wrist with no active motion, which is the only way the patient could receive real-time feedback on his motor attempts; the authors attribute the regained movement to this motor execution training, with two authors holding a pending patent on the technology.

Replication

What produced it
  • Residual EMG detectable in the impaired limb
  • Classifier trained on the patient's own signals each session
  • Repeated sessions with virtual-limb and game feedback
What must hold again
  • Intact or reinnervated muscle producing separable EMG patterns
  • Clinician-run sessions several times a week
  • Patient motivation to attempt movements without visible motion
