What happened

A 72-year-old man's right arm was replanted after a traumatic above-elbow amputation. He had no functional recovery after conventional rehabilitation and developed chronic neuropathic pain. The arm had been replanted at Sahlgrenska University Hospital in Mölndal, Sweden. The Mölndal-based Center for Bionics and Pain Research / Sahlgrenska University Hospital (C.A.R.E.) team then delivered 18 sessions of myoelectric motor execution over 84 days. Nine included sensory training with tactile grids (the paper does not name the treatment site). Eight channels of electromyography were classified by a linear discriminant analysis classifier (Neuromotus, Integrum AB). Intended movements were decoded from the replanted arm and shown on screen as virtual limbs and games.

He regained active thumb extension (4 degrees) and active wrist flexion (6 degrees) and extension (10 degrees), movements absent before treatment. Pain fluctuated with no consistent change. Treatment dates are not given, so the publication date (23 November 2024) is used.

What it was built to do. Rehabilitate a highly impaired replanted arm and reduce neuropathic pain using decoded motor intention as feedback

The system's part

The LDA classifier decodes intended movements from weak EMG in a hand and wrist with no active motion. That is the only way the patient could receive real-time feedback on his motor attempts. The authors attribute the regained movement to this motor execution training. Two authors hold a pending patent on the technology.

Replication

What produced it. Residual EMG detectable in the impaired limb. Classifier trained on the patient's own signals each session. Repeated sessions with virtual-limb and game feedback.

What must hold again. Intact or reinnervated muscle producing separable EMG patterns. Clinician-run sessions several times a week. Patient motivation to attempt movements without visible motion.
