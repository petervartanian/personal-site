Man regains thumb and wrist movement in a paralyzed replanted arm

A 72-year-old man with a paralyzed replanted arm regained active thumb and wrist movement after 18 sessions in which the Neuromotus system decoded his intended movements.

  • 2 a person

    The patient regained active thumb and wrist movement that conventional rehabilitation had not produced. The gains are small, and pain did not consistently improve.

  • Not rated society

    The paper reports a single case with no follow-up and no use beyond the treating group, so no realized societal outcome is documented.

The system’s part
The LDA classifier decodes intended movements from weak EMG in a hand and wrist with no active motion. That is the only way the patient could receive real-time feedback on his motor attempts. The authors attribute the regained movement to this motor execution training. Two authors hold a pending patent on the technology.

What happened

A 72-year-old man's right arm was replanted at Sahlgrenska University Hospital in Mölndal, Sweden, after a traumatic above-elbow amputation. He had no functional recovery after conventional rehabilitation and developed chronic neuropathic pain. A team from the Center for Bionics and Pain Research and Sahlgrenska University Hospital (C.A.R.E.), based in Mölndal, then delivered 18 sessions of myoelectric motor execution over 84 days. The paper does not name the treatment site. Nine of the sessions included sensory training with tactile grids. A linear discriminant analysis classifier (Neuromotus, Integrum AB) classified eight channels of electromyography. It decoded intended movements from the replanted arm, and a screen showed them as virtual limbs and games. He regained active thumb extension (4 degrees) and active wrist flexion (6 degrees) and extension (10 degrees), movements absent before treatment. Pain fluctuated with no consistent change. The paper reports no follow-up after the final session. Treatment dates are not given. The authors first posted the case as a preprint on 15 January 2024, after the treatment had ended, and the case is dated to that preprint. The journal published the paper on 23 November 2024. The two papers that cite it by October 2026 both list Ortiz-Catalan as an author.

Details

AI system

Model

A classifier based on linear discriminant analysis.

Role

The classifier decoded intended movements from muscle signals and controlled a virtual limb.

Configuration

The Neuromotus system used a classifier based on linear discriminant analysis.

Inputs and outputs

Electrodes recorded attempted muscle movements, and a classifier converted those signals into control of a virtual arm and games.

Workflow

Residual EMG detectable in the impaired limb. Classifier trained on the patient's own signals each session. Repeated sessions with virtual-limb and game feedback.

Human oversight

A therapist guided the movements and adapted the exercises to the patient's residual function.

Benefits

Reach

The case report describes one patient. In a 2025 study by the same group, all six participants with chronic stroke improved upper limb function. [1] [2]

Duration

The treatment consisted of 20 sessions over 84 days: two assessment sessions and 18 training sessions. The paper reports no follow-up after the final session, so it is unknown whether the movement persisted. [1] [2]

Measurement

Measurement

Goniometer range of motion at the first and final assessment sessions and at session 10; weighted pain distribution and numeric pain rating; Semmes-Weinstein monofilament test used in an exploratory way. [1]

Setting and sample

One 72-year-old man with a replanted right arm and no functional recovery after conventional rehabilitation, Sweden. [1]

Measures

Active range of motion of the thumb and wrist; pain levels. [1]

Results

Active thumb extension of 4 degrees, wrist flexion of 6 degrees and wrist extension of 10 degrees; no consistent change in pain. [1]

Additional value from AI

Not isolated; the patient also received sensory training in nine sessions, and no session without the decoder was run. [1]

Limitations

A single uncontrolled case; no follow-up after the final session; two authors hold a pending patent on the technology. [1]

Denominators

18 treatment sessions of about two hours, nine with sensory training, over 84 days. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Intact or reinnervated muscle producing separable EMG patterns. Clinician-run sessions several times a week. Patient motivation to attempt movements without visible motion.
