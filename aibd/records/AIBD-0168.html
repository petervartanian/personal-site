NCAR's DICast cuts Xcel's wind forecast error to 10.8%

NCAR's adaptive forecast-blending system (DICast), used by Xcel Energy since 2009, cut mean wind forecast error from about 18% to 10.8% by 2014; Xcel estimated US$49m saved (IEEE Spectrum 2015).

Costs and harms

More confident forecasts probably let Xcel keep less conventional generation committed, reducing production costs while increasing dependence on accurate wind forecasts and timely operator response.

Editor’s note. The reported savings mechanism is coal-unit decommitment when sustained wind is forecast. A forecast error would leave operators needing replacement generation, so forecast accuracy and dispatch flexibility are consequential constraints.

Citable summary

NCAR's adaptive forecast-blending system (DICast), used by Xcel Energy since 2009, cut mean wind forecast error from about 18% to 10.8% by 2014; Xcel estimated US$49m saved (IEEE Spectrum 2015). Forecast error improved, while Xcel's cumulative US$49 million saving remains an attributed estimate. The adaptive weighting description does not establish exactly which learned technique generated the gain. The later US$10 million annual estimate is not an independent verification of the cumulative total. The forecasting system's contribution to savings is Xcel's own estimate relayed by NCAR authors and reporters. The AI character of the system rests on UCAR's reference to artificial-intelligence research and DICast's adaptive bias correction, not on an explicit machine-learning description. So both the AI status and the causal share are unverified.
