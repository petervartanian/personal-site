What happened

Sue Ellen Haupt and William Mahoney of the National Center for Atmospheric Research wrote in IEEE Spectrum (22 October 2015). NCAR's wind power forecasting system for Xcel Energy blends numerical weather models with the Dynamic Integrated foreCast (DICast) system, used for wind prediction since 2009. DICast constantly checks its predictions against outcomes and adjusts its bias corrections and weightings. The mean forecast error at a typical wind farm fell from about 18 percent in 2008 to 10.8 percent by 2014. Xcel estimated the improvements had saved customers US$49 million so far. A UCAR news item (7 February 2012) said the system saved ratepayers $6 million in 2010 alone. It described the work as building on NCAR research ranging from statistics to artificial intelligence. E&E News (4 December 2017) quoted Haupt that the forecasts could save $10 million a year.

It reported that Xcel operators decommit large coal units when sustained wind is predicted. None of the sources describes the system as machine learning in so many words. The savings are Xcel's estimates reported through NCAR and the press, not a regulator's audit.

What it was built to do. The system was built to forecast wind generation for Xcel's unit-commitment and trading decisions. The cost savings are its intended use.

The system's part

Not assessed. The forecasting system's contribution to savings is Xcel's own estimate relayed by NCAR authors and reporters. The AI character of the system rests on UCAR's reference to artificial-intelligence research and DICast's adaptive bias correction, not on an explicit machine-learning description. So both the AI status and the causal share are unverified.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. Multiple numerical weather models blended with an adaptive statistical layer trained on each wind farm's history. Forecast updates every 15 minutes feeding unit-commitment decisions. A utility with large wind capacity and dispatchable coal and gas units to decommit.

What must hold again. Continuous observed generation data for bias correction. Operators who act on forecast confidence bands. An independent accounting of avoided production costs, absent here.
