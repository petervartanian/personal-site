NCAR's DICast cuts Xcel's wind forecast error to 10.8%

NCAR's adaptive forecast-blending system (DICast), used by Xcel Energy since 2009, cut mean wind forecast error from about 18% to 10.8% by 2014; Xcel estimated US$49m saved (IEEE Spectrum 2015).

What happened

NCAR's wind power forecasting system for Xcel Energy blends numerical weather models with the Dynamic Integrated foreCast (DICast) system. DICast constantly checks its predictions against outcomes and adjusts its bias corrections and weightings. The Xcel, NREL and UCAR authors of a 2011 NREL report state that the NCAR system entered Xcel's operations between November 2009 and April 2010. They found that day-ahead error at Public Service Company of Colorado fell from 18% in 2009 to 14.3% in 2010. A UCAR news item (7 February 2012) said that the system saved ratepayers $6 million in 2010 alone. It described the work as building on NCAR research ranging from statistics to artificial intelligence. MIT Technology Review (23 April 2014) described the software as artificial-intelligence-based and reported that the forecasts saved Xcel nearly as much money in 2013 as in the three previous years combined. Sue Ellen Haupt and William Mahoney of the National Center for Atmospheric Research wrote in IEEE Spectrum (22 October 2015) that DICast had been used for wind prediction since 2009. The mean forecast error at a typical wind farm fell from about 18 percent in 2008 to 10.8 percent by 2014. Xcel estimated that the improvements had saved customers US$49 million so far. E&E News (4 December 2017) quoted Haupt as saying that the forecasts could save $10 million a year. It reported that Xcel operators decommit large coal units when sustained wind is predicted. None of the sources describes the system as machine learning in so many words. The savings are Xcel's estimates, reported through NCAR and the press, and no regulator has audited them.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

DICast combined numerical weather forecasts, checked them against observed conditions or generation, and adjusted its corrections and weights to improve wind-power forecasts.

Workflow

Multiple numerical weather models blended with an adaptive statistical layer trained on each wind farm's history. Forecast updates every 15 minutes feeding unit-commitment decisions. A utility with large wind capacity and dispatchable coal and gas units to decommit.

Human oversight

Xcel operators used the forecasts to decide when to take coal units out of service because sustained wind could supply more electricity.

AI contribution

The authors of the 2011 NREL report compare Xcel's day-ahead forecast error in 2009 and 2010, which measures the whole NCAR system against Xcel's earlier forecasts. No source separates the share of the DICast blending layer from that of the weather models. MIT Technology Review describes the software as artificial-intelligence-based, and none of the sources describes it as machine learning in so many words. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

The NCAR system entered Xcel's operations between November 2009 and April 2010, and by 2014 the mean forecast error at a typical wind farm had fallen to 10.8 percent. By 2017 Xcel was still relying on the NCAR forecasts, which Haupt said saved about $10 million a year in production costs. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
