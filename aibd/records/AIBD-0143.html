ECMWF's AIFS Single goes operational with 5–15% lower errors

ECMWF's AIFS Single forecast model went into operation on 25 February 2025; ECMWF reports 5–15% lower errors than IFS, and two independent studies in China partly confirm the gain.

  • Not rated a person

    No source reports a benefit to a named person, such as a decision changed by an AIFS forecast.

  • 2 an organization

    ECMWF reports errors 5 to 15% lower than the IFS for most variables in 2025, and an independent study in Inner Mongolia found a temperature error about 15.7% lower in summer 2026. That is a measured improvement in a forecasting capability, with weaker results for heavy rain, extremes and cyclone intensity.

  • Not rated society

    The sources report forecast skill only; none documents a realized outcome for the public, such as a warning that worked better because of AIFS.

The system’s part
ECMWF's verification compares AIFS directly with the IFS. The only non-ECMWF cited source (NWS Q&A) confirms NHC evaluated ECMWF's AI system in 2025 but states no performance result. ECMWF is both the developer and the deployer, so its evaluations are the body reporting on its own system.
Limits
The developer's aggregate verification does not establish better forecasts for every variable or event. Under-predicted cyclone intensity and unavailable gust forecasts limit the claim. No measured reduction in casualties or damage follows from the retained comparisons.
Costs and harms
AIFS improved cyclone track forecasts while it tended to underestimate storm intensity. Pan and colleagues found that AIFS underestimated extreme precipitation over China. Zhang and Ma found that the IFS control forecast did better for heavy rain in Inner Mongolia.
Later
Scaled up, 1 July 2025. ECMWF launched the ensemble version, AIFS-ENS, operationally on 1 July 2025, and its 2025 evaluation (May 2026) reports both AIFS Single and AIFS ENS beating the IFS on most parameters.

What happened

The European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) announced that on 25 February 2025 it began producing operational forecasts with AIFS Single, the deterministic version of its Artificial Intelligence Forecasting System. ECMWF runs it alongside its physics-based Integrated Forecasting System (IFS). ECMWF claimed tropical cyclone track gains of up to 20%. Pan and colleagues found that AIFS precipitation errors over China were lower than those of the IFS from April 2024 to March 2025, while AIFS underestimated extremes. On 1 July 2025 ECMWF launched the ensemble version, AIFS-ENS, operationally. The Science Operations Officer of the National Hurricane Center said that ECMWF's AI-driven global system was available for evaluation at the center during the 2025 season. ECMWF's evaluation of 2025 forecasts (8 May 2026) reports that AIFS errors were 5 to 15% lower than those of the IFS for most surface and upper-air variables. Cyclone position errors were substantially smaller, while cyclone intensity was under-predicted. No specific event in which AIFS beat the IFS is documented in the cited ECMWF sources. The Severe Event Catalogue page for Storm Eowyn states that wind gusts were not available for AIFS. In July 2026 a preprint partly written by ECMWF authors restated that machine-learning forecast models “tend to dramatically underestimate intensity” of tropical cyclones. The authors proposed a correction. Zhang and Ma found that the operational AIFS had a temperature error about 15.7% lower than the IFS control forecast in Inner Mongolia in summer 2026, while the IFS control forecast did better for heavy rain. The two studies by Chinese meteorological authors without ties to ECMWF confirm part of the ECMWF claim.

Details

AI system

Model

AIFS Single v1.

Role

AIFS generated weather forecasts.

Configuration

The reported forecasts used AIFS Single v1, operational from 25 February 2025.

Inputs and outputs

AIFS used ECMWF's analyzed weather state to generate a global deterministic forecast on a 28-kilometer grid at launch.

Workflow

Operational AIFS Single runs initialized from ECMWF analyses every six hours. ECMWF's standard headline-score and tropical cyclone verification against the IFS.

Benefits

Reach

The record counts no beneficiaries. The benefit is a forecasting capability at ECMWF, measured as errors 5 to 15% lower than the IFS. No source reports a decision changed by an AIFS forecast. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Duration

AIFS Single has run operationally since 25 February 2025. ECMWF launched the ensemble version, AIFS-ENS, on 1 July 2025. ECMWF's evaluation of 2025 reports that both versions beat the IFS on most parameters. Zhang and Ma found a lower temperature error for the operational AIFS in summer 2026. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Measurement

Measurement

Study 1: Verification of AIFS and IFS precipitation forecasts against station observations and ERA5, with RMSE, mean error, threat scores, equitable threat scores and SEEPS. Study 2: Verification of 2 m temperature and 6-hour precipitation at 6 to 84 hours with Murphy error decomposition and block-bootstrap tests. [7] [8]

Setting and sample

Study 1: 2,415 stations in China, April 2024 to March 2025. Study 2: 117 stations in Inner Mongolia, June to August 2026. [7] [8]

Measures

Study 1: precipitation RMSE and categorical scores at lead times of 1 to 5 days. Study 2: 2 m temperature mean absolute error and precipitation category skill. [7] [8]

Results

Study 1: AIFS RMSE at 120 hours was roughly equal to IFS RMSE at 24 hours; AIFS scored better for heavy rain above 25 mm but over-forecast light rain and scored worse on SEEPS. Study 2: AIFS temperature mean absolute error about 15.7% lower than the IFS control forecast; AIFS better for moderate rain and worse for heavy rain. [7] [8]

Additional value from AI

Study 1: lower precipitation RMSE at 1 to 5 days, with weaker extremes. Study 2: about 15.7% lower temperature error than the IFS control forecast. [7] [8]

Limitations

Study 1: The period falls mostly before AIFS Single v1 became operational, and the study covers one country. The authors link the weak AIFS extremes to weak vertical velocity and water vapor transport. One sentence of the abstract states the RMSE comparison the other way round from the results section. Study 2: One region and one season; the authors compare operational products and state that their findings apply only to the versions, region and season evaluated. [7] [8]

Denominators

Study 1: daily AIFS and IFS forecasts over twelve months. Study 2: operational AIFS forecasts over one summer flood season. [7] [8]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Intensity under-prediction persists per ECMWF and the AIFS-TC preprint. No ECMWF-documented named event where AIFS beat the IFS was found. Event-level superiority remains unverified.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2.

Availability

AIFS Single became an openly available operational service on February 25, 2025, with continuous support for meteorological users.

Evidence

Original evidence

Most sources come from ECMWF itself: two press releases, a newsletter article, a page of its forecast user wiki and a preprint that ECMWF staff wrote in part. Two peer-reviewed studies by Chinese meteorological authors without ties to ECMWF verified AIFS separately. The National Weather Service question-and-answer page states only that ECMWF's system was available for evaluation at the National Hurricane Center during the 2025 season.
