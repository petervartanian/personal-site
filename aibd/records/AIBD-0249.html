South Korea plans AI cameras to warn train drivers of blocked crossings

South Korea's transport ministry plans AI cameras from an unnamed supplier to detect vehicles or pedestrians trapped at crossings and alert drivers, with pilots at Nonsan and Boseong.

What happened

Kyunghyang Shinmun's 최미랑 reported on 11 February 2026 that the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport had drawn up a comprehensive railway-crossing accident-prevention plan. It was to take effect from March 2026. It includes an AI-based intelligent closed-circuit television (CCTV) system, to be installed in railway protection zones nationwide. The system immediately detects a vehicle or pedestrian trapped inside a crossing. It then transmits the information to the driver of an approaching train. Pilot installations were planned within the first quarter at Nonsan in Chungnam and at Boseong, where accidents occurred in 2025. Installation at the 543 national crossings would follow in sequence, depending on the results. The plan also adds fines of up to 70,000 won, after a six-month grace period. They apply to vehicles that fail to stop or that enter while barriers operate. The ministry cited 36 crossing accidents over five years with 14 deaths and 7 injuries, 27 of them due to driver carelessness. No source names a developer. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor labels the announcement an AI hazard. Newspim reported on 10 July 2026 that the ministry still planned to install the AI cameras at the Nonsan and Boseong crossings, after the first-quarter target had passed. On 23 September 2026 the ministry and Korail said that crossing cameras were in pilot operation and that they would consider a phased expansion. No source reports results.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The planned cameras would identify a vehicle or pedestrian trapped inside a crossing and send an alert to the approaching train driver.

Workflow

A national accident-prevention plan following fatal crossing accidents. Pilot sites chosen where accidents occurred the previous year.

AI contribution

The ministry describes AI-based cameras that detect a vehicle or pedestrian trapped inside a crossing and transmit the information to the driver of an approaching train. No source reports a detection or an alert, so the contribution of the AI cannot be assessed. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Duration

The pilot missed its first-quarter target, and on 10 July 2026 the ministry still described the cameras at Nonsan and Boseong as planned. On 23 September 2026 the ministry and Korail said that crossing cameras were in pilot operation. No source reports how long they have run or with what result. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Pilot results support rollout. Alerts reach drivers early enough to stop.

Replication attempts

2 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 1. Outcome unresolved: 1.

Availability

The February announcement described first-quarter pilots in Nonsan and Boseong, followed by a conditional rollout to 543 national crossings. It did not establish that nationwide service had begun.
