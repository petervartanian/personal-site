ML ink detection reads 2,000+ characters of a Herculaneum scroll

recovered 15 columns and more than 2,000 characters of Greek text

ML ink detection let contestants read 2,000+ characters of a sealed Herculaneum scroll; papyrologists confirmed the text (Feb 2024).

What happened

On 12 October 2023 the Vesuvius Challenge announced that contestant Luke Farritor, a 21-year-old student, had read the word ΠΟΡΦΥΡΑϹ ('purple') inside the unopened Herculaneum scroll PHerc.Paris.4 using a machine-learning model trained on the 'crackle' pattern of ink visible in X-ray CT scans. On 5 February 2024 the organizers awarded the $700,000 grand prize to Youssef Nader, Luke Farritor and Julian Schilliger, whose TimeSformer-based ink-detection models recovered 15 columns and more than 2,000 characters of Greek text. Papyrologists Federica Nicolardi, Richard Janko and Robert Fowler reviewed the passages, which discuss pleasure, food and music and are probably by Philodemus. The University of Kentucky, whose virtual-unwrapping software underlies the contest, described the result as a new, readable Herculaneum text.

What it was built to do. Built to detect ink in X-ray CT scans of carbonised Herculaneum papyri so that the text can be read; this benefit was that purpose.

The system's part

The contestants' ink-detection models produced the ink images from which papyrologists read the text; the ink is not visible in the raw CT data. Virtual unwrapping (non-AI) was a necessary precondition, and the readings were validated by an independent papyrological review organized by the contest.

Replication

What produced it
  • High-resolution synchrotron CT scan of a carbonised scroll
  • Virtual unwrapping into flattened segments
  • Ink-detection model trained on labeled crackle/ink texture
  • Independent papyrological review of the output
What must hold again
  • Ink must produce a detectable texture or density signal in the CT data
  • Segmentation must be accurate enough to preserve letter order
  • Readings must be confirmed by papyrologists, not only by model output
