AI ink detection lets scholars read part of a sealed Herculaneum scroll

Ink-detection models that three contestants built on TimeSformer recovered more than 2,000 characters of Greek text inside a sealed Herculaneum scroll, and a later scan confirmed the reading.

The system’s part
The contestants' ink-detection models produced the ink images from which papyrologists read the text. The ink is not visible in the raw CT data. Virtual unwrapping (non-AI) was a necessary precondition. The readings were validated by an independent papyrological review organized by the contest.
Limits
Recovered passages are a demonstrated reading result, but most of the scroll remained unread. Attribution to Philodemus is qualified, and the account does not separate the contribution of ink detection from virtual unwrapping and expert interpretation.
Costs and harms
The sources report no harm. The reading depends on segmentation that preserves letter order. In 2026 the team reported that a new scan shows the ink directly and that this ink matches the 2023 reading.

What happened

On 12 October 2023 the Vesuvius Challenge announced that contestant Luke Farritor, a 21-year-old student, had read ΠΟΡΦΥΡΑϹ (“purple”) inside the unopened Herculaneum scroll PHerc. Paris. 4. He used a machine-learning model trained on the “crackle” pattern of ink visible in X-ray computed tomography scans. On 5 February 2024 the organizers gave the $700,000 grand prize to Youssef Nader, Luke Farritor and Julian Schilliger. Their TimeSformer-based ink-detection models recovered 15 columns and more than 2,000 characters of Greek text. Papyrologists Federica Nicolardi, Richard Janko and Robert Fowler reviewed the passages, which discuss pleasure, food and music and are probably by Philodemus. The University of Kentucky, whose virtual-unwrapping software underlies the contest, called the result a new, readable Herculaneum text. On 5 February 2025 the Bodleian Libraries reported that machine-learning ink detection had revealed columns of text in another scroll, PHerc. 172. On 25 June 2026 the Vesuvius Challenge team announced that it had read all surviving text of a further scroll, PHerc. 1667. In that announcement and in a preprint posted on 27 June 2026, the team reported that a new high-resolution scan of PHerc. Paris. 4 shows the ink directly and that this ink matches the 2023 reading.

Details

AI system

Model

The submission combined a TimeSformer-based model with other architectures.

Role

The models detected traces of ink in CT images of the scrolls.

Configuration

The model was part of the 2023 grand-prize submission to the Vesuvius Challenge.

Inputs and outputs

Ink-detection models processed flattened CT segments of the carbonized scroll and generated images from which Greek letters could be read.

Workflow

High-resolution synchrotron CT scan of a carbonized scroll. Virtual unwrapping into flattened segments. Ink-detection model trained on labeled crackle/ink texture. Independent papyrological review of the output.

Human oversight

Papyrologists Federica Nicolardi, Richard Janko and Robert Fowler reviewed the passages before the organizers awarded the prize. In 2026 the team reported that a new high-resolution scan of the scroll shows the ink directly and that this ink matches the 2023 reading. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Measurement

Measurement

Study 1: Papyrologists read anonymized submissions independently of one another, and a technical team reproduced the submitted code and methods. Study 2: The team rescanned PHerc. Paris. 4 at 2.4 micrometers on the BM18 beamline, segmented directly visible ink in three dimensions and compared it with the output of the 2023 prize-winning model. [1] [6]

Setting and sample

Study 1: Grand Prize submissions received by 1 January 2024 for the scroll PHerc. Paris. 4. Study 2: PHerc. Paris. 4, Institut de France. [1] [6]

Measures

Study 1: Four passages of 140 characters each, with at least 85 percent of characters recoverable. Study 2: Agreement between model-recovered ink and ink visible in the new scan. [1] [6]

Results

Study 1: The winning entry met the criteria and added 11 more columns, for more than 2,000 characters in total. Study 2: The authors state that the new scan confirms the 2023 reading. [1] [6]

Limitations

Study 1: The review team was chosen by the organizers. The scroll was about 5 percent read. Study 2: A preprint without peer review, written by the organizers and one of the 2023 prize winners. [1] [6]

Dependencies

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Ink must produce a detectable texture or density signal in the CT data. Segmentation must be accurate enough to preserve letter order. Readings must be confirmed by papyrologists, not only by model output.
