ChatGPT lists HSP for Phoebe Tesoriere; genetic testing confirms

depth 2 for a person

A Cardiff teacher put her symptoms into ChatGPT after a coma; it listed hereditary spastic paraplegia, her GP called it plausible and genetic testing confirmed it in August 2025 (BBC, WalesOnline).

What happened

Phoebe Tesoriere, 23, from Cardiff, Wales, a former special educational needs teacher, had a limp since childhood and from 2022 was diagnosed with epilepsy, then Todd's paralysis, and repeatedly told her symptoms were anxiety; in January 2025 she fell down stairs and spent months in hospital, and in July 2025 a seizure left her in a coma (three days per BBC Wales, 48 hours per the SWNS report in WalesOnline). After discharge she entered her symptoms into ChatGPT, which returned a list of possible conditions including hereditary spastic paraplegia (HSP). She took the suggestion to her GP, who agreed it was a 'plausible reason'; genetic testing confirmed HSP, in August 2025 according to WalesOnline, which also reports the complex quadriplegic type. The first day of August is used because no day is given. There is no treatment; she uses a wheelchair, attends physiotherapy and is fundraising for a specialist chair. Cardiff and Vale University Health Board said it was 'sorry to hear about Phoebe's experience while in our care' and would not comment on an individual case. The account rests on her own statements to BBC Wales (10 April 2026) and SWNS (6 April 2026); no clinical document was fetched.

Not what it was built to do. ChatGPT is a general-purpose chatbot not built or cleared for diagnosis; the patient used it to generate candidate conditions from her symptom list.

Who benefited

The system's part

By her account the chatbot produced a list of candidates that included HSP; she decided to take it to her GP, the GP ordered genetic testing, and the test made the diagnosis. The prompt, the model version and the full candidate list are not reported. Only news reports quoting the patient were fetched; the health board declined to comment on the case.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A patient with a long list of concrete symptoms (gait, incontinence, sensory loss, reflex changes, seizures) and no settled diagnosis. Entry of the symptom list into a general LLM. A GP willing to test a patient-sourced hypothesis and access to genetic testing.

What must hold again. The true condition is among the candidates the model lists and the patient pursues it. Clinicians confirm by genetic testing rather than accepting the suggestion.
