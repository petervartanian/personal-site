ChatGPT suggests a rare condition that a genetic test confirms

A former Cardiff teacher entered her symptoms into ChatGPT after a coma; it listed hereditary spastic paraplegia, which genetic testing confirmed in August 2025 (BBC, WalesOnline).

  • 2 a person

What happened

Phoebe Tesoriere, 23, from Cardiff, Wales, is a former special educational needs teacher. She had a limp since childhood. From 2022 she was diagnosed with epilepsy, then Todd's paralysis, and was repeatedly told that her symptoms were anxiety. In January 2025 she fell down stairs and spent months in hospital. In July 2025 a seizure left her in a coma (three days per BBC Wales, 48 hours per the SWNS report in WalesOnline). After discharge she entered her symptoms into ChatGPT. It returned a list of possible conditions including hereditary spastic paraplegia (HSP). She took the suggestion to her general practitioner, who agreed that it was a “plausible reason”. Genetic testing confirmed HSP, in August 2025 according to WalesOnline, which also reports the complex quadriplegic type. There is no treatment. She uses a wheelchair, attends physiotherapy and is fundraising for a specialist chair. Cardiff and Vale University Health Board said that it was “sorry to hear about Phoebe's experience while in our care”. It would not comment on an individual case. The account rests on her own statements to SWNS (6 April 2026) and BBC Wales (10 April 2026). No clinical document is among the sources. BBC Wales reported on 5 October 2026 that Tesoriere, then 24 and a student at Cardiff Metropolitan University, has hereditary spastic paraplegia and uses a wheelchair. The same report says that she now lives in Bath; it does not mention ChatGPT.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Tesoriere entered symptoms and received a list of possible conditions that included hereditary spastic paraplegia.

Autonomy

She chose to discuss the suggestion with her GP, who considered it plausible and arranged genetic testing.

Workflow

A patient with a long list of concrete symptoms (gait, incontinence, sensory loss, reflex changes, seizures) and no settled diagnosis. Entry of the symptom list into a general large language model (LLM). A GP willing to test a patient-sourced hypothesis and access to genetic testing.

Human oversight

Tesoriere took the chatbot's suggestion to her general practitioner, who agreed that it was a “plausible reason”. Genetic testing then confirmed the diagnosis. [1] [2] [3]
