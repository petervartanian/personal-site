CMD-1 cuts crisis triage from 9 hours to 8–13 minutes

Cerebral's CMD-1 crisis-message detector screened 102,471 patient chat messages in October 2022 and cut the daily median time to crisis-specialist triage to 8 to 13 minutes from over 9 hours.

The system’s part

Limits
The predeployment baseline is an internal historical comparison, so staffing and workflow changes are not controlled. The prospective labels, keyword-filter exclusions, false negatives, and uncertainty need examination. Faster triage does not establish fewer attempts, hospitalizations, or deaths.
Costs and harms
The prospective positive predictive value was 0.66, so about a third of the messages that CMD-1 surfaced to crisis specialists were not crises.

Nothing later is known. As of 29 October 2026, no source reports whether Cerebral kept CMD-1 running after the October 2022 validation or whether another provider adopted it. The authors name patient outcomes such as hospitalization as future work, and no later paper by the team reports them.

What happened

Cerebral Inc., a national tele-mental-health provider, worked with Stanford University School of Medicine on Crisis Message Detector 1 (CMD-1). CMD-1 is a two-stage natural-language-processing system: key-word filtering, then logistic regression. It flags patient chat messages indicating possible suicidal or homicidal ideation, domestic violence or non-suicidal self-injury. It surfaces them to Cerebral's crisis specialists via a Slack interface. The model was trained on 721 messages (32% potential crises) sent between 1 April 2021 and 1 April 2022. On a retrospective test set of 481 messages, it reached an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.82, a sensitivity of 0.99 and a positive predictive value (PPV) of 0.35. CMD-1 then ran in Cerebral's production systems from 1 October to 31 October 2022 as a prospective validation. The prospective test set had 102,471 messages from that month, and 9,795 of these passed the key-word filter. On that set, CMD-1 reached an AUC of 0.98, a sensitivity of 0.98 and a PPV of 0.66. In the prospective period the daily median time from message receipt to crisis-specialist triage was 8 to 13 minutes. Before deployment it averaged more than 9 hours, per Cerebral's internal analysis. False negatives reached specialists through usual channels. Swaminathan, López and colleagues, of Cerebral and Stanford, published these results in npj Digital Medicine on 21 November 2023. Seven authors report salary and equity in Cerebral and three part-time employment there. Stanford HAI's account of 8 January 2024 gives the reduction as “from 10 hours to under 10 minutes” with 97% sensitivity and specificity. Those figures differ slightly from the paper's. The authors report no downstream patient outcome, such as hospitalization, emergency department use or suicide attempts. As of 29 October 2026, no independent study has measured the system, and no source reports whether Cerebral kept using it after that month.

Details

AI system

Model

A logistic-regression classifier following a keyword filter.

Application developer

Cerebral Inc. With Stanford University School of Medicine collaborators

Role

The classifier selected potentially urgent messages for crisis specialists to review.

Configuration

The deployment used CMD-1.

Inputs and outputs

A keyword filter selected chat messages for a logistic-regression classifier, which sent likely crises to specialists through Slack.

Workflow

Crisis specialists reviewed every flagged message before an intervention. Messages that the system did not flag continued through ordinary human review, and support staff could escalate missed crises.

Human oversight

Crisis specialists review every message that CMD-1 surfaces in a Slack interface and triage it. Messages that the system missed reached specialists through usual channels. [1] [2]

Benefits

Reach

No source counts patients who benefited. The prospective test covered 102,471 messages in October 2022, and 9,795 of these passed the key-word filter. [1] [2]

Duration

CMD-1 ran in Cerebral's production systems from 1 October to 31 October 2022 as a prospective validation. As of 29 October 2026, no source reports whether Cerebral kept using it after that month. [1] [2]

Measurement

Measurement

Timestamps from message receipt to the Slack post by CMD-1 and to triage by a crisis specialist, logged in production; crisis specialists labeled each surfaced message as a true or false positive. [1]

Setting and sample

102,471 chat messages sent by 32,803 patients of Cerebral, a national United States tele-mental-health provider, from 1 to 31 October 2022. [1]

Measures

Daily median time from message receipt to crisis-specialist triage; sensitivity, specificity and positive predictive value of the classifier. [1]

Results

Daily median receipt-to-triage time of 8 to 13 minutes, against over 9 hours on average before deployment; prospective AUC 0.98 and sensitivity 0.98. [1]

Additional value from AI

The before figure is an internal average for all chat messages, so the study does not isolate how much of the change the classifier produced. [1]

Limitations

Company-authored study with no concurrent control; baseline from an unpublished internal analysis; one month of data; no patient outcome measured; prospective positive predictive value of 0.66. [1]

Denominators

All patient chat messages during the deployment month. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Crisis specialists available to act on surfaced messages within minutes. A high-recall filter stage, since messages that do not pass it are classified as non-crises automatically. Message volume and language similar to the training data. The authors note the prospective PPV was 0.66.
