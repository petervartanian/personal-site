A man with a rare blood disorder became well enough for a transplant

Mr. Joseph Coates received three drugs (i) that a consulting physician had considered and (ii) that Every Cure’s early drug-repurposing system had scored highly.

Workflow

The consulting physician recognized similarities between POEMS syndrome and multiple myeloma and reviewed the AI scores for drugs that were used for myeloma. He discussed treatment with the physician responsible for Coates, who assessed the risks and administered the drugs. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]

Human oversight

Fajgenbaum recommended the regimen; Wayne Gao weighed the risks and administered it.

Relation to AI

Fajgenbaum said that he had considered three drugs used for multiple myeloma and that Every Cure’s AI algorithm also scored them highly for POEMS syndrome. In an earlier AAMC interview, he described reviewing the scores and recommending the drugs to Coates’s treating physician. Wayne Gao weighed the risks and administered the treatment. The AI therefore supported the physicians’ assessment of candidate drugs. The sources do not show that it independently devised the regimen or that the benefit would have been impossible without it. The Lancet Haematology commentary contains a brief vignette of a matching unnamed POEMS patient, explicitly labeled unpublished data. No detailed clinical case report, underlying medical records, or case-specific model output was located. The commentary names KGML-xDTD as one of several algorithms in Every Cure’s platform, but it does not establish which algorithm or release was used for Coates. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]

AI contribution

A consulting physician considered three myeloma drugs and reviewed the high scores that Every Cure’s algorithm gave them for POEMS syndrome. The treating physician assessed the risks and administered them. The available accounts do not isolate the AI’s contribution to recovery. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]

Conditions for replication

Clinicians need to assess the patient’s condition, the drugs, and their risks. A high AI score does not establish suitability. In this instance, recovery also depended on a response sufficient to permit a later transplant. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8] [9]
