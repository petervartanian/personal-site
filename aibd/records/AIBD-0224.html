What happened

The date is that of ICE's results release. The operation began on 17 July 2023 and ended in early August. HSI's Cyber Crimes Center hosted investigators from the FBI, U.S. Marshals, Interpol, Europol and 13 foreign agencies to review unidentified child sexual abuse material series. ICE reports 311 probable identifications of previously unknown victims, 14 positive contacts and several confirmed rescues from active abuse. Over 100 leads went to field offices and 25 countries, with arrests already made in the United States and Canada. NBC News reports that HSI officials credited new facial recognition and artificial intelligence technology for fresh leads. Mike Prado of HSI described AI- or machine-learning-enhanced images as a starting point for investigation.

ABC (Australia) reports that the operation used Clearview AI's facial recognition alongside other techniques, citing Queensland Police analyst Scott Anderson. Forbes reports that officials did not confirm Clearview or any specific program. The ICE release itself does not mention AI.

What it was built to do. Identify previously unknown victims and offenders in seized child sexual abuse material so that children can be located and rescued.

The system's part

Official and independent sources agree the surge produced the identifications. The AI's share is described only qualitatively. NBC quotes an HSI official on AI-enhanced images giving investigators a starting point or tentative identification. ABC names Clearview AI's facial recognition as one tool. HSI did not confirm any product, and much of the work was manual contextual analysis (flora, fauna, geolocation). No figure separates AI-derived identifications from the rest.

Replication

What produced it. Large holdings of unidentified abuse-material series concentrated for a time-boxed multi-agency review. Facial recognition against web-scraped reference images plus AI image enhancement feeding human investigators. Lead dissemination through Interpol's ICSE database and partner agencies.

What must hold again. Legal authority to use web-scraped facial recognition (banned for this use in Australia, per ABC). Human investigators to verify tentative identifications before action. Partner agencies able to act on leads.
