Facial recognition helps agents identify 311 probable child victims

U.S.-led investigators credited facial recognition, reported as Clearview AI, and AI image enhancement for leads in a 2023 operation that made 311 probable identifications of child abuse victims.

Brief from Peter

Operation Renewed Hope ran from 17 July 2023 into early August at the Cyber Crimes Center of Homeland Security Investigations (HSI). U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) reported 311 probable identifications of previously unknown child victims and several confirmed rescues from active abuse, and arrests had already been made in the United States and Canada.1 The case bears on the argument over police use of facial recognition, and AI's part in it is hard to weigh. The ICE release does not mention AI. NBC News reported that new facial recognition and artificial intelligence technology had produced fresh leads in formerly cold cases. Mike Prado of HSI said that images enhanced with AI or machine learning can give investigators a starting point.2 ABC News in Australia reported that Clearview AI's facial recognition was used, and Forbes reported that officials did not confirm Clearview or any specific program.3 Crikey reported on 25 January 2024 that Australian case material had been analyzed with Clearview AI, which the Australian Federal Police was not allowed to use.4 The outcomes are ICE's count, and no figure shows how many identifications came from the tools and how many from manual work. In a separate case in Finland, police were reportedly reprimanded after a unit of the National Bureau of Investigation allegedly used Clearview AI to identify potential abuse victims.

Details

AI system

Application developer

Clearview AI (reported by ABC and Forbes; not confirmed by HSI) and unnamed image-enhancement tools

Workflow

Investigators used enhanced images and face-search leads alongside contextual analysis, then sent tentative identifications to partner agencies for verification and action.

Human oversight

An official of Homeland Security Investigations described AI-enhanced images as a starting point for investigation. Much of the identification work was manual contextual analysis of details such as flora, fauna and geolocation. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

Reach

The operation reported 311 probable identifications, 14 positive contacts and several rescues; no count isolates cases found through AI.

Duration

The 2023 operation ran from 17 July to 4 August 2023. HSI repeated it each year through 2026, and ICE counts more than 600 children identified and rescued across the four operations. These later figures come from the deployer. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Legal authority to use web-scraped facial recognition (banned for this use in Australia, per ABC). Human investigators to verify tentative identifications before action. Partner agencies able to act on leads.
