Computer-read chest X-rays speed TB treatment at a Malawi clinic

In the PROSPECT trial at a Blantyre clinic, TB patients offered HIV testing plus chest X-rays read by CAD4TB started treatment a median of 1 day after the visit, against 11 days under standard care.

What happened

Researchers from the Malawi-Liverpool-Wellcome Trust Clinical Research Programme, the Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine and the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine ran the PROSPECT trial at Bangwe Health Centre in Blantyre, Malawi. Between 15 November 2018 and 27 November 2019 they randomly assigned 1,462 adults with cough to standard care, to oral HIV testing with linkage to care, or to HIV testing plus a digital chest X-ray read by CAD4TB version 5 software. People in the X-ray arm whose score was 45 or higher gave sputum for an on-site Xpert test, and a radiologist also read the X-rays remotely. Within 56 days, 15 people in the X-ray arm had started TB treatment, against 5 in the standard-care arm; the authors report that this difference was not statistically significant. Median time to treatment was 1 day in the X-ray arm and 11 days under standard care (hazard ratio 2.86, 95% confidence interval 1.04 to 7.87), and 6 of the 15 patients in the X-ray arm started treatment on the day of the visit, against none of the 5 under standard care. TB was less common among participants than the investigators expected, and follow-up lasted 56 days. The authors estimate that the probability that the X-ray package was cost-effective at US$1,200 per quality-adjusted life year was 0%. The trial tests the X-ray and the software as one package. It does not compare the software with a human reader. Wellcome funded the study, and no author reports a tie to Delft Imaging.

Details

AI system

Reported configuration

CAD4TB v5

Workflow

Adults with cough at an urban primary clinic were offered a digital chest X-ray during the same visit. A CAD4TB score of 45 or higher led to an on-site Xpert sputum test. The clinic TB officer started treatment when the Xpert test was positive.

Measurement

Measurement

Time to TB treatment and same-day treatment from clinic and study records over 56 days; intention-to-treat analysis. [1]

Setting and sample

Bangwe Health Centre, Blantyre, Malawi; 1,462 adults with cough randomized 1:1:1 between 15 November 2018 and 27 November 2019. [1]

Measures

Time to TB treatment; proportion treated the same day. [1]

Results

Median 1 day to treatment against 11 days under standard care; hazard ratio 2.86 (1.04 to 7.87); same-day treatment 6/15 against 0/5. [1]

Additional value from AI

Not isolated; the X-ray and software were delivered as one package. [1]

Limitations

Few TB cases (15, 8 and 5 by arm), a difference in the number treated that was not statistically significant, 56-day follow-up, an open-label design, and a 0% probability of cost-effectiveness at US$1,200 per QALY. [1]

Denominators

497 adults allocated to HIV testing plus digital X-ray with CAD4TB v5. [1]

Materials for replication

Trial registered as NCT03519425. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

X-ray equipment, Xpert testing and trained staff must be available during the visit. The score threshold must suit the local population.

Purpose

CAD4TB was built to score chest X-rays for signs of pulmonary TB so that staff can decide who needs a confirmatory test. Faster diagnosis and treatment of TB was the purpose of its use in this trial.
