Study suggests that parts of Mali could get seven days of flood warning

A retrospective evaluation suggests that Google Flood Hub forecasts could extend some early-action plans in Mali from four to seven days, with frequent false alarms; no early action was measured.

What happened

Researchers evaluated forecasts from Google Flood Hub and the Global Flood Awareness System (GloFAS) against river-flow observations and records of flood impacts in Mali. Four of the seven authors work for the Netherlands Red Cross data and digital team 510. The researchers are independent of Google, although one coauthor works at the Joint Research Centre, which contributes to GloFAS. The authors first presented the result in a European Geosciences Union abstract dated 14 March 2025. They posted a preprint on 7 January 2026, and the journal article appeared on 16 September 2026. Their results suggest that some early-action plans could extend from four to seven days without much loss of forecast skill. Performance varied across districts and thresholds, and no correctly predicted floods were found in the northern districts that were evaluated. At a seven-day lead time, Google Flood Hub’s false-alarm ratio was around 0.8 across discharge thresholds, compared with around 0.5 for GloFAS. The authors describe GloFAS as more balanced in several comparisons. They used a ten-day matching window to compare forecasts with reported impacts, which affects the interpretation of performance. This was a retrospective forecast evaluation, and it did not measure benefits from an implemented early action. No source reports that the Mali Red Cross protocol has adopted these forecasts or a seven-day lead time.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The study compared river-flow forecasts with measured discharge and district-level flood-impact records. The outputs were forecast-skill estimates for different thresholds and warning lead times.

Workflow

Global AI flood forecasts available for ungauged Malian rivers. Evaluation against discharge observations and text-mined district-level impact data across lead times and thresholds.

Human oversight

Researchers assessed the forecasts. Extending a Red Cross early-action trigger from four to seven days remained a proposed operational decision, not a deployment measured by the study.

Benefits

Reach

The study counts no beneficiaries. It evaluated forecasts against past floods, and no source reports an early action taken on these forecasts. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

Retrospective verification of archived reforecasts against river-gauge discharge and district-level flood-impact records, with exceedances merged within a ten-day action window.

Setting and sample

River stations and districts in Mali; discharge analysis and impact analysis from 1 January 2016 to 31 December 2022.

Measures

Probability of detection and false-alarm ratio across lead times and flood thresholds.

Results

Flood Hub and GloFAS keep enough skill beyond a four-day lead time in frequently flooded regions; at seven days Flood Hub's false-alarm ratio is about 0.8 against about 0.5 for GloFAS.

Additional value from AI

Flood Hub detects more floods than GloFAS at return-period thresholds but with more false alarms; the authors describe different strengths for each model.

Limitations

Few gauges with short overlap with observations (2016 to 2018); impact records are sparse and inconsistent; no correctly predicted floods in the northern districts evaluated.

Materials for replication

Impact-data code at github.com/rodekruis/disaster-impact-data-analysis; GloFAS and Google reforecasts are public; Mali gauge data are held under a non-disclosure agreement.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Forecast thresholds and matching windows require local validation; performance differs substantially by district. Frequent false alarms need to be considered alongside the potential for earlier warnings. The proposed change to early-action planning requires implementation and outcome evaluation.
