What happened

A peer-reviewed study in the Journal of Flood Risk Management evaluated Google Flood Hub (GFH), Google's AI-based flood forecasting service. It was published online 16 September 2026, in volume 19, issue 3, September 2026. The authors are researchers from the Netherlands Red Cross 510 data team and Utrecht University. They are also from the EU Joint Research Centre, Politecnico di Milano and the University of Twente. They compared GFH with GloFAS and the Mali Red Cross's existing Early Action Protocol trigger, using discharge observations and district-level impact data. The existing trigger relies on upstream water levels and is limited to a four-day lead time. The authors report that GloFAS and GFH demonstrate sufficient skill for early action beyond a four-day lead time in frequently flooded regions.

They suggest early action plans could operate with a seven-day lead time and span a larger area. No realized early action is documented.

What it was built to do. AI-based riverine flood forecasting for ungauged basins to support early warning and anticipatory action.

The system's part

The study evaluates GFH forecast skill directly against observations and impact data; it does not document any decision or action taken on a GFH forecast.

Replication

What produced it. Global AI flood forecasts available for ungauged Malian rivers. Evaluation against discharge observations and text-mined district-level impact data across lead times and thresholds.

What must hold again. Skill is reported for frequently flooded regions; data-scarce districts remain challenging per the abstract. Adoption into the Early Action Protocol is a suggestion, not a documented change.
