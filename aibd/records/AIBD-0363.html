Whale Safe grades ship cooperation with 10-knot slowdowns

off the scale

Whale Safe rates whale presence from acoustic buoys, sightings and a habitat model and grades ship cooperation with California's voluntary 10-knot zones; no avoided strike is documented.

What happened

Whale Safe, run by the Benioff Ocean Science Laboratory at UC Santa Barbara's Marine Science Institute, is described on its site as a technology-based mapping and analysis tool displaying whale and ship data for the Santa Barbara Channel and the San Francisco Bay region. Its methodology page says the acoustic buoy 'uses a hydrophone (underwater microphone) and a tiny computer to detect and identify sounds in the water', citing Baumgartner et al. 2019, after which 'An analyst then reviews these data and indicates for each 15-minute period whether each of the monitored species was detected'; acoustic, sighting and Blue Whale Habitat Model streams are combined over a five-day window into a Whale Presence Rating, and AIS data are used to compute each vessel's cooperation rate with NOAA's voluntary 10-knot recommendation, graded A+ to F. The homepage states that 'Research demonstrates ships that slow to 10 knots in areas with high whale presence significantly reduce the danger to whales', and the operator score-card page, as rendered by the fetch tool, lists 2026-season cooperation rates by shipping company with letter grades. Partners named on the about page include Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, NOAA's Southwest Fisheries Science Center, The Marine Mammal Center and others. The buoy system's peer-reviewed description (Baumgartner et al. 2019, Methods in Ecology and Evolution) reports false detection rates of 0% to nearly 0% and daily missed detection rates of 12% to 42% for a Massachusetts buoy. No fetched source gives a dated ship slowdown, cooperation change or avoided strike attributable to Whale Safe detections; the date used is the date the pages were read.

What it was built to do. Provide near-real-time whale presence information and public cooperation grades so that ships slow in whale habitat; no attributable outcome is documented.

The system's part

The automated component is the buoy's onboard detection and classification of whale calls (the Baumgartner et al. 2019 system), which Whale Safe's own methodology page says is then reviewed by a human analyst before contributing to the Whale Presence Rating; cooperation grades are computed from AIS data, not AI. Only the maker's pages and the developers' buoy paper were fetched, and none records a dated outcome attributable to the detections.

Replication

What produced it. Moored acoustic buoy with onboard call detection and classification. Human analyst review of detections. Sightings and habitat-model data streams. AIS vessel tracking and public cooperation grading. NOAA voluntary speed reduction zones.

What must hold again. Buoy operation (both regional buoys were listed offline at fetch time). Analyst capacity for review. Shipping company attention to public grades. Evaluation linking ratings to speed changes and strikes, not yet documented.
