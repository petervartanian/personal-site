Costs and harms

The 2019 paper reports false detection rates of 0% to nearly 0%. It reports daily missed detection rates of 12% to 42% for a Massachusetts buoy. In their 2026 paper, the Whale Safe team and its data partners report that the California buoys were offline for more than a year in total in each region. For those periods, the Whale Presence Rating had no acoustic input. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Relation to AI

The automated part is the buoy's onboard detection and classification of whale calls, the Baumgartner et al. 2019 system, and a human analyst reviews its output before it counts toward the Whale Presence Rating. Cooperation grades come from AIS speed data without AI. The team reports in its 2026 paper that the buoys were offline for more than a year in total in each region, so for long periods the rating had no acoustic input. No source isolates what the acoustic detections added to ship slowdowns.

    Analysts review 15-minute summaries; absence of calls is not absence of whales. Five-day lookback combines three inputs. Acoustic Detection Data; Whale Presence Rating

    Original methodology

    Original developer study, NOAA repository copy. Low false detections result from conservative analyst review, with daily misses 12–42%. Review took 30–45 minutes/day/platform in the study. Baumgartner et al.2019,Tables 2–3 and Discussion
