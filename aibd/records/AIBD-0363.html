Whale Safe grades ship cooperation with 10-knot slowdowns

Whale Safe rates whale presence from acoustic buoys, sightings and a habitat model and grades ship cooperation with California's voluntary 10-knot zones; no avoided strike is documented.

What happened

Whale Safe is run by the Benioff Ocean Science Laboratory at the Marine Science Institute of the University of California, Santa Barbara. Its site describes it as a technology-based mapping and analysis tool. It displays whale and ship data for the Santa Barbara Channel and the San Francisco Bay region. Partners named on the about page include Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, the Southwest Fisheries Science Center of the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), The Marine Mammal Center and others. The methodology page cites Baumgartner et al. 2019 and says that the acoustic buoy “uses a hydrophone (underwater microphone) and a tiny computer to detect and identify sounds in the water”. The page continues: “An analyst then reviews these data and indicates for each 15-minute period whether each of the monitored species was detected”. Acoustic, sighting and Blue Whale Habitat Model streams are combined over a five-day window into a Whale Presence Rating. Automatic Identification System (AIS) data from ships are used to compute each vessel's cooperation rate with NOAA's voluntary 10-knot recommendation, graded A+ to F. The homepage states that “Research demonstrates ships that slow to 10 knots in areas with high whale presence significantly reduce the danger to whales”. The buoy system's peer-reviewed description (Baumgartner et al. 2019, Methods in Ecology and Evolution) reports false detection rates of 0% to nearly 0%. It reports daily missed detection rates of 12% to 42% for a Massachusetts buoy. In a paper published on 24 June 2026, the Whale Safe team and its data partners reported that the blue whale habitat model tracked sightings and acoustic detections in both regions. They also reported that the acoustic buoys were offline for 389 days in the Santa Barbara Channel and 474 days off San Francisco during study periods that ended in January 2025, because of technical failures and damage. The operator score-card page lists cooperation rates for the 2026 season by shipping company, with letter grades. On 26 October 2026, the Whale Safe homepage listed both regional acoustic systems as offline. No cited source gives a dated ship slowdown, cooperation change or avoided strike attributable to Whale Safe detections.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The buoy transmitted summaries of candidate whale calls for analyst review. Confirmed calls, sightings, and habitat predictions contributed to presence ratings, while ship-position records supplied speed-compliance grades.

Workflow

Moored acoustic buoy with onboard call detection and classification. Human analyst review of detections. Sightings and habitat-model data streams. AIS vessel tracking and public cooperation grading. NOAA voluntary speed reduction zones.

Human oversight

An analyst checked call evidence before it contributed to a rating. Captains and operators remained responsible for voluntary slowing.

Benefits

Duration

Whale Safe kept running in both regions through January 2025, and its operator page lists grades for the 2026 season. Its acoustic buoys were offline for 389 days in the Santa Barbara Channel and 474 days off San Francisco. On 26 October 2026, the homepage listed both regional acoustic systems as offline. People and places

Dependencies

The acoustic pathway required a hydrophone, onboard processing, satellite transmission, and trained review. The platform also needed sightings, oceanographic model inputs, and ship-tracking data.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Buoy operation (both regional buoys were listed offline when the pages were consulted). Analyst capacity for review. Shipping company attention to public grades. Evaluation linking ratings to speed changes and strikes, not yet documented.
