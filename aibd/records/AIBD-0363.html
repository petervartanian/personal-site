What happened

Whale Safe is run by the Benioff Ocean Science Laboratory at UC Santa Barbara's Marine Science Institute. Its site describes it as a technology-based mapping and analysis tool. It displays whale and ship data for the Santa Barbara Channel and the San Francisco Bay region. Its methodology page cites Baumgartner et al. 2019. It says the acoustic buoy “uses a hydrophone (underwater microphone) and a tiny computer to detect and identify sounds in the water”. After that, “An analyst then reviews these data and indicates for each 15-minute period whether each of the monitored species was detected”. Acoustic, sighting and Blue Whale Habitat Model streams are combined over a five-day window into a Whale Presence Rating. AIS data are used to compute each vessel's cooperation rate with NOAA's voluntary 10-knot recommendation, graded A+ to F.

The homepage states that “Research demonstrates ships that slow to 10 knots in areas with high whale presence significantly reduce the danger to whales”. The operator score-card page on record lists 2026-season cooperation rates by shipping company with letter grades. Partners named on the about page include Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, NOAA's Southwest Fisheries Science Center, The Marine Mammal Center and others. The buoy system's peer-reviewed description (Baumgartner et al. 2019, Methods in Ecology and Evolution) reports false detection rates of 0% to nearly 0%. It reports daily missed detection rates of 12% to 42% for a Massachusetts buoy. No source on file gives a dated ship slowdown, cooperation change or avoided strike attributable to Whale Safe detections. The date used is the date the pages were consulted.

What it was built to do. Provide near-real-time whale presence information and public cooperation grades so that ships slow in whale habitat; no attributable outcome is documented.

The system's part

The automated component is the buoy's onboard detection and classification of whale calls, the Baumgartner et al. 2019 system. Whale Safe's own methodology page says a human analyst then reviews it before it contributes to the Whale Presence Rating. Cooperation grades are computed from AIS data, not AI. Only the maker's pages and the developers' buoy paper are among the sources, and none records a dated outcome attributable to the detections.

Replication

What produced it. Moored acoustic buoy with onboard call detection and classification. Human analyst review of detections. Sightings and habitat-model data streams. AIS vessel tracking and public cooperation grading. NOAA voluntary speed reduction zones.

What must hold again. Buoy operation (both regional buoys were listed offline when the pages were consulted). Analyst capacity for review. Shipping company attention to public grades. Evaluation linking ratings to speed changes and strikes, not yet documented.
