Snake researchers review fewer images with MegaDetector

MegaDetector 5A removed 94% of images from manual review in a Florida snake study, but the hybrid workflow detected fewer snake events overall.

What happened

Miller and colleagues evaluated MegaDetector 5A on 1,485,924 time-lapse images from 42 cameras at three South Florida sites. At a detection threshold of 0.05, 88,863 images remained for human review, a reported 94.0% reduction. Manual review took 265 hours. Computer processing took 27.7 hours, and the authors estimated a 43.5-hour hybrid workflow by adding human review time calculated from the manual review rate. About 16 hours of that total represent estimated human review. The authors did not time the hybrid labor separately. MegaDetector located animals; people assigned snake species in Timelapse2. The hybrid method found 217 independent snake events, compared with 378 manually and 447 across both methods. Snake-image recall was 17.1% for the hybrid method and 96.2% manually. Results differed by species: The hybrid method found five of six Burmese-python events and more North American racer events than manual review. Neither method found every event. The authors report reduced review work with detection losses. They report no management decision, population improvement, or net financial saving.

Details

AI system

Model

MegaDetector, based on YOLOv5. [1] [2]

Release

MegaDetector 5A [1]

Role

MegaDetector detected animals. Timelapse2 supported human review and species identification. [1] [2]

Configuration

Unadapted MegaDetector 5A ran on an Nvidia A100 GPU on UF HiPerGator. The reported comparison used a confidence threshold of 0.05. [1] [2]

Inputs and outputs

Time-lapse photographs were the input. Detection scores and locations selected images for human species identification. [1] [2]

Autonomy

The model filtered images for review. It did not identify snake species or initiate conservation action. [1] [2]

Workflow

Researchers collected images, compared manual review with MegaDetector filtering followed by human review, and reconciled detections from both methods. [1]

Human oversight

A researcher or herpetologist identified species. The same reviewer supported both workflows, and the paper does not report randomization or blinding. [1]

Relation to AI

AI was part of the measured image-filtering workflow. Human review supplied species labels and the comparison data. [1]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The research team that processed the South Florida snake images. [1]

Distribution and access

Performance differed across snake species and sizes. The hybrid workflow retained five of six Burmese-python events but missed the ring-necked-snake event that manual review found. [1]

Expected?

The result concerns the intended use of AI to support wildlife-image analysis. [1]

Measurement

Measurement

The authors timed manual review and computer processing, estimated hybrid human review from the baseline review rate, and compared detections after combining findings from both methods. [1]

Setting and sample

42 cameras at three South Florida sites produced 1,485,924 images between June 20 and July 18, 2022. [1]

Measures

Of 1,485,924 images, 88,863 remained for human review. Manual review took 265 hours; the hybrid workflow was estimated at 43.5 hours. Hybrid and manual review found 217 and 378 independent snake events, respectively. [1]

Results

At threshold 0.05, the hybrid workflow left 88,863 images for human review and had an estimated total duration of 43.5 hours. It found 217 independent events compared with 378 manually. [1]

Additional value from AI

AI directly reduced the image set that people reviewed. The time comparison combines measured compute with estimated human review and does not hold detection quality constant. [1]

Limitations

Human review within the hybrid workflow was estimated. The same reviewer assessed both methods. Ground truth combined their findings and could miss snakes overlooked by both. Results depend on species size, camera conditions, hardware, and threshold. Image recall and independent-event detection use different denominators. [1]

Denominators

The image-review denominator is 1,485,924 images. Detection metrics use the subset remaining after humans, vehicles, and other animals were removed. Combined independent snake events number 447. [1]

Materials for replication

The paper states that the study data are available on request. No public study dataset, analysis-code archive, or supplement is linked. Those unpublished materials were not inspected. [1]

People and places

Dependencies

The study used 42 Reconyx HP2X cameras, UF HiPerGator, an Nvidia A100 GPU, MegaDetector 5A, Timelapse2, and expert human review. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The chosen threshold must retain the species and events that matter to the research question. Available computing and expert review must be included when comparing workflow time and cost. Images, labels, outputs, and timing records must be available to reproduce the comparison. [1]

Availability

The study and versioned model guide are public. The underlying study data are available on request. [1]
