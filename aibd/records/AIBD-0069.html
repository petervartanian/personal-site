From Numbers to Names matches Holocaust survivors to archive photos

The From Numbers to Names face search found Geddy Lee's mother in a photograph from the Bergen-Belsen displaced-persons camp, and survivor Blanche Fixler recognized herself in photos that it found.

What happened

From Numbers to Names (N2N) is a facial-recognition search built by the software engineer Daniel Patt. It indexes roughly half a million digitized Holocaust-era photographs from the US Holocaust Memorial Museum (USHMM) and Yad Vashem. In an interview that The Times of Israel published on 26 June 2022, Patt said that the tool had surfaced a photograph of Mary Weinrib at the Bergen-Belsen displaced-persons camp. She was an Auschwitz survivor and the mother of the Rush musician Geddy Lee. The interview does not give the date of the identification. According to Patt, Lee confirmed the identification and then located further photographs of his mother's extended family in the Yad Vashem collection. On 28 June 2022 The Jerusalem Post published a Jewish Telegraphic Agency story that draws on the interview and adds that Mary Weinrib died in summer 2021. BBC News reported on 12 November 2022 that the tool found the survivor Blanche Fixler in a wartime group photo held by the US Holocaust Memorial Museum, which had listed her as unidentified. Her recognition raised the number of identified people in the photo from three to six. Scott Miller, the museum's director of curatorial affairs, told the BBC that such identifications restore "some semblance of dignity" to the people pictured. On 19 December 2022 NPR reported that Patt had searched using a photo from Twitter, and that Fixler recognized herself, her aunt and her cousin in the photographs that the tool found.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

N2N compared a supplied reference face with faces in roughly half a million digitized Holocaust-era photographs to surface possible matches.

Workflow

Face-embedding similarity search over about half a million digitized USHMM and Yad Vashem photographs. A reference photograph of the relative supplied by the family. Human confirmation by a family member or the survivor.

Human oversight

Patt reports that Geddy Lee confirmed his mother's identity, and Blanche Fixler recognized herself and her relatives. The software proposed candidate matches, and people made the identifications. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

Reach

Two families benefited in the cited cases: Geddy Lee found photographs of his mother and her extended family, and Blanche Fixler found photographs of herself and her relatives. The US Holocaust Memorial Museum went from three to six identified people in one photograph. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

Each identification is a one-time event. The BBC on 12 November 2022 and NPR on 19 December 2022 reported a second identification made with the tool, of Blanche Fixler. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The archive photographs are digitized and indexed by the tool. The relative's face appears in an indexed photograph. A living relative or survivor can recognize and confirm the match.
