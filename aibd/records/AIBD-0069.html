N2N face search finds Geddy Lee's mother in Bergen-Belsen photo

N2N face search found Geddy Lee's mother in a Bergen-Belsen DP-camp photo; Lee confirmed it and found more family photos at Yad Vashem

ⓘ

What happened

From Numbers to Names (N2N) is a facial-recognition search built by software engineer Daniel Patt. It indexes roughly half a million digitized Holocaust-era photographs from the US Holocaust Memorial Museum (USHMM) and Yad Vashem. The Times of Israel reported on 26 June 2022 (report date; identification date not given) that the tool surfaced a photograph of Mary Weinrib. She was an Auschwitz survivor and mother of Rush musician Geddy Lee, at the Bergen-Belsen displaced-persons camp. Lee confirmed the identification and then located further photographs of his mother's extended family in the Yad Vashem collection (Jerusalem Post, 28 June 2022). On 19 December 2022 NPR reported a second case: survivor Blanche Fixler recognized herself, her aunt and her cousin in photographs found with the tool.

Purpose. Identify unnamed faces in Holocaust-era photo and video archives and help descendants find images of relatives

AI’s contribution

The face-similarity search surfaced the candidate photograph. The identification was confirmed by a family member (Lee) or by the survivor herself (Fixler), not by the archive. The further Yad Vashem family photographs were found by Lee following the lead. No archive statement confirming the identification is among the sources.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Material to a family: Lee confirmed a previously unknown photograph of his mother at Bergen-Belsen. He found photographs of her extended family (Times of Israel, Jerusalem Post). Fixler found images of herself, her aunt and cousin (NPR)

  • an organization

    Small gain for archives: an anonymous Yad Vashem photograph acquired an identification, but the sources record no formal archival update

  • society

    Small: two documented identifications among millions of unidentified faces indexed, per the sources

Further evidence

Setting. Face-embedding similarity search over about half a million digitized USHMM and Yad Vashem photographs. A reference photograph of the relative supplied by the family. Human confirmation by a family member or the survivor.

Conditions. The archive photographs are digitized and indexed by the tool. The relative's face appears in an indexed photograph. A living relative or survivor can recognize and confirm the match.
