Observe's AI registers salmon activity rising with gill disease

fish activity rose from 25.6% to 43.6% (Farm A) and 24.9% to 32.6% (Farm B)

University of Stirling study, 2023 season: fish activity rose from 25.6% to 43.6% (Farm A) and 24.9% to 32.6% (Farm B) after gill health deteriorated; proliferative gill disease drove it.

What happened

Researchers at the University of Stirling's Institute of Aquaculture, with co-authors from Observe Technologies (Richmond, UK) and Innovate UK funding in partnership with Observe and AKVA Group, applied Observe's algorithm, a patented convolutional neural network (EP3644717), to feed-camera video in every cage at two commercial Atlantic salmon marine farms in Scotland, with 5 and 4 extra cameras in one cage per farm. The algorithm outputs 'activity' (%), combining fish abundance, speed and shoal cohesion; gill health, operational welfare indicators, mortality and specific feeding rate were scored weekly. Data were collected from 10 May to 10 September 2023 at Farm A and 22 June to 13 October 2023 at Farm B. Gill health issues arose at both farms in summer 2023: mean activity in the two months before was 25.6 ± 10.5% (A) and 24.9 ± 7.0% (B) and rose for two months afterwards to 43.6 ± 15.1% and 32.6 ± 9.6%, with fish moving to the center of the cage; a mixed model found proliferative gill disease the main driver. The paper proposes behavior as a non-invasive welfare indicator; it does not report an alert preceding diagnosis or a changed treatment. The date is the end of Farm B's data collection.

What it was built to do. Extract group behavior (abundance, speed, shoal cohesion) from feed-camera video as a non-invasive indicator of fish health and welfare.

The system's part

The vendor's neural network quantified a behavioral response that coincided with gill disease already identified by weekly scoring; the study shows correlation, not an alert that changed what happened. Vendor staff co-authored the peer-reviewed paper.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it

  • Feed cameras in all cages plus 4-5 extra cameras in one cage per farm
  • Weekly gill scoring, welfare indicators, mortality and feeding-rate records
  • A summer 2023 gill-health episode at both farms

What must hold again

  • Gill disease severe enough to alter shoaling and distribution
  • Camera coverage of the cage volume
