WeChat Pay alert helps a man keep 190,000 yuan from a suspected scammer

WeChat says that its WeChat Pay anti-fraud reminder connected a Yunnan man to customer service, which advised him to cancel a 190,000 yuan transfer to a “goddess” he had met online.

The system’s part
The automated part that the sources describe is the flagging of a risky transaction. The flag shows an on-screen reminder and connects the payer to Tencent Cloud Zhifu, a service that the Monitor calls AI. WeChat's announcement says that Zhang received a call from WeChat Pay customer service, and ChinaZ says that the agent judged the scam "from experience". Both details point to a human agent. Neither report says how the transaction was flagged or whether any model was involved. The sources credit the agent's advice with Zhang's decision to cancel.

Brief from Peter

On 14 December 2021 WeChat announced an “anti-fraud customer service reminder” for WeChat Pay and gave as its example a man in Yunnan, named only as Mr. Zhang.1 ChinaZ reported that Zhang had met a woman on a video platform and that within a week she asked to borrow 190,000 yuan for medical treatment. As he began the transfer, a voice reminder from WeChat Pay appeared, and he received a call from its customer service. The agent on the call judged from experience that this looked like a dating scam and advised him to cancel.2 He canceled the payment. The automated part that the reports describe is the flagging of a risky transaction. Neither WeChat's announcement nor ChinaZ says how this transfer was flagged or whether any model was involved. WeChat and ChinaZ credit the agent's advice with his decision to cancel. No confirmation from the police or a bank is reported, and the reports give no date for the transfer. In September 2022 Xinhua listed the reminder among WeChat Pay's risk warnings and gave no figures on its effect.3

Details

AI system

Autonomy

The system interrupted payment with a warning, but Zhang made the decision to cancel after receiving advice.

Workflow

A risky-payment flag connected Zhang with customer service, whose advice led him to cancel the proposed 190,000-yuan transfer.

Benefits

Duration

The case was a single canceled transfer. In September 2022 Xinhua listed the reminder among WeChat Pay's risk warnings, so the feature was still in place. Xinhua gave no figures on its effect. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The flag fires before the transfer completes. The payer answers the reminder and accepts the advice to cancel. The scam matches a pattern the service recognizes; here a week-old online acquaintance asking for medical money.

Evidence

Original evidence

The Yunnan case is WeChat's own example from its announcement of 14 December 2021, which IT之家 and ChinaZ relay. ZOL reports the launch of the feature without the case. The OECD AI Incidents and Hazards Monitor bundles the case with Shanghai figures from Xinmin. Xinmin credits those figures to police, the communications administration and carriers. Xinhua's report of September 2022 describes a Tencent campaign in Tencent's own terms. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Sector

Purpose

The reminder was built to warn WeChat Pay users during risky transactions and connect them to online customer service that helps judge the risk. Stopping a transfer to a suspected scammer was that purpose.
