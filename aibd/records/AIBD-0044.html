AlphaFold-Multimer predicts a sperm protein needed for male fertility

An AlphaFold-Multimer screen predicted that Tmem81 joins a sperm complex with Izumo1 and Spaca6; the Pauli laboratory then showed that Tmem81 is essential for male fertility in zebrafish and mice.

What happened

The Pauli laboratory and collaborators ran an AlphaFold-Multimer screen. It identified Tmem81 as part of a conserved trimeric sperm complex with the known essential fertilization factors Izumo1 and Spaca6. The prediction was then tested at the bench. The screen and the validation were performed independently of AlphaFold's developer. The Pauli laboratory first posted the result as a preprint on 28 July 2023. A separate team led by Arne Elofsson and Luca Jovine in Sweden used AlphaFold-Multimer to predict the same TMEM81 complex for the mammalian proteins. They posted their preprint on the same day and published the study in eLife on 26 April 2024. The Pauli laboratory published its paper in Cell on 17 October 2024. The authors demonstrated that Tmem81 is essential for male fertility in zebrafish and mice. They showed that Izumo1, Spaca6 and Tmem81 interact in zebrafish sperm and that the human orthologues interact in vitro. They found that complex formation creates the binding site for the egg factor Bouncer in zebrafish. In a preprint of 15 May 2026, the Pauli laboratory reports that the trimer belongs to a larger sperm complex, SPARK, whose parts are each required for male fertility in zebrafish and mice.
