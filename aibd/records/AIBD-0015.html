DeepMind's FunSearch produces a cap set of size 512 in dimension 8

DeepMind's FunSearch, which pairs a large language model with an automated evaluator, produced a cap set of size 512 in dimension 8 and raised the asymptotic lower bound for cap sets to 2.2202.

What happened

On 14 December 2023 Nature published a paper by Google DeepMind researchers with mathematician Jordan Ellenberg (University of Wisconsin–Madison) that describes FunSearch. FunSearch pairs a pretrained large language model that proposes programs with an automated evaluator that scores them, in an island-based evolutionary loop. Applied to the cap set problem, FunSearch produced a program that generates a cap set of size 512 in dimension 8, exceeding the previous best known construction of 496. Ellenberg exploited a symmetry that he noticed in the program's output. FunSearch then found admissible sets that raised the asymptotic lower bound from 2.2180 to 2.2202. The same day, Terence Tao told MIT Technology Review that the approach is a promising paradigm. In a comment dated 11 January 2024, Ernest Davis of New York University, who was not an author, accepted that the constructions are genuine and rated their mathematical importance as moderate. In a survey posted on 5 August 2024, the cap set specialists Ernie Croot, Vsevolod Lev and Péter Pál Pach list the FunSearch bound of 2.2202 as one step in the record. They also cite an unpublished manuscript by Naslund that claims 2.2208. On 11 August 2025 Zhai and colleagues described X-evolve, a method powered by large language models, in an arXiv preprint. In a preprint of 18 June 2026, Paul Hametner and Fred Tyrrell credit FunSearch with the first AI-assisted improvement and give 2.2203, from X-evolve, as the current record.

Details

AI system

Model

Codey, built on PaLM 2. [1]

Provider

Google DeepMind. [1]

Role

Codey generated candidate Python functions for constructing cap sets. [1]

Configuration

The researchers used frozen model weights, temperature 1.0, and top-p 0.95. The cap-set setup used 15 samplers, 140 evaluators, and 10 populations of programs. [1]

Inputs and outputs

FunSearch used problem specifications and prior functions to generate candidate functions and evaluate their scores. [1]

Autonomy

FunSearch generated and tested programs automatically within the researchers’ specification. A separate evaluator rejected invalid results. [1]

Workflow

A problem with a fast, exact evaluator for candidate solutions. An LLM sampling program variants in an evolutionary loop. Human inspection of discovered programs to refine the search.

Human oversight

An exact evaluator rejected invalid constructions, while mathematician Jordan Ellenberg inspected the outputs and used an observed symmetry to redirect the search.

Benefits

Reach

The benefit is knowledge gained by mathematicians who work on the cap set problem, and no source counts them. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

The constructions stand in the record of the problem. X-evolve raised the bound from 2.2202 to 2.2203 in August 2025, and Hametner and Tyrrell gave 2.2203 as the current record in June 2026. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

People and places

Dependencies

The search required an executable representation of candidate programs and a fast, exact test of whether their generated sets satisfied the problem's constraints.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The evaluator must reject invalid constructions exactly. Results must be explicit objects that can be checked independently of the system.
