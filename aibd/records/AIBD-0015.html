What happened

On 14 December 2023 Nature published a paper by Google DeepMind researchers with mathematician Jordan Ellenberg (University of Wisconsin–Madison) describing FunSearch. FunSearch pairs a pretrained large language model (LLM) that proposes programs with an automated evaluator that scores them, in an island-based evolutionary loop. Applied to the cap set problem, FunSearch produced a program generating a cap set of size 512 in dimension 8. The previous best known construction was 496. It found admissible sets that raised the asymptotic lower bound from 2.2180 to 2.2202 after Ellenberg exploited a symmetry noticed in the program's output. Independent commentary by Ernest Davis (NYU, 11 January 2024) accepts the constructions as genuine but rates their mathematical importance as moderate. Terence Tao called the approach a promising paradigm.

What it was built to do. Built to search program space for constructions and heuristics in mathematics and computer science. The cap set constructions were that purpose.

The system's part

FunSearch generated the programs whose outputs are the cap sets and admissible sets. These are explicit finite objects that the evaluator and readers can verify mechanically. The 2.2202 bound depended on a human (Ellenberg) noticing a symmetry in FunSearch's output and refocusing the search. Davis identifies that as the most notable aspect.

Replication

No replication cited.

What produced it. A problem with a fast, exact evaluator for candidate solutions. An LLM sampling program variants in an evolutionary loop. Human inspection of discovered programs to refine the search.

What must hold again. The evaluator must reject invalid constructions exactly. Results must be explicit objects that can be checked independently of the system.
