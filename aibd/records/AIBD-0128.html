KEA's facial recognition app exposes a forged CET admit card

KEA's facial recognition app flagged a woman with a forged CET admit card in Bengaluru on 17 April 2025; she fled before writing the paper.

ⓘ

What happened

On Thursday 17 April 2025 a woman calling herself Tuba Fathima Jameel tried to enter the CET-2025 mathematics paper. The center was Silver Valley Public PU College in Malleswaram, Bengaluru. She had spent nearly an hour in the restroom. Her admit card carried her own pasted photograph and an altered name. It bore the hall ticket number and QR code of a genuine candidate, Tabu Naz. Staff scanned the code and captured her photo with the new Karnataka Examinations Authority (KEA) facial recognition app. It reported that she was not the candidate on record. She fled before the principal was told. KEA executive director H. Prasanna ordered a report with video footage and said legal action would follow.

Purpose. KEA built the app to verify candidates at the hall entrance and prevent impersonation. It scans the admit card's QR code and matches a live photograph against the photo submitted at registration. Exposing this impersonator was exactly that purpose.

AI’s contribution

The app did the detecting. Staff scanned the admit card's QR code and captured the woman's photo. It flagged a mismatch with the photo on record (New Indian Express). It “revealed that she was not the genuine candidate” (The Hindu). The Hindu and the Times of India both report the forged card's contents. It carried her own photograph with the genuine candidate's hall ticket number and QR code. Comparing the card with her face would therefore not have exposed her. The flag depended on the app's match against the registered photograph. Staff then acted, but she fled before the principal was told. No arrest or sanction is attributable to the system. KEA's probe and the promised legal action were still pending in every report.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    KEA stopped a forged-entry attempt in the app's first state-wide run. The Hindu reports that all verifications ran without technical glitches on the second day. The benefit to the authority is a working integrity control across 3,30,808 registrants, demonstrated on one case.

  • society

    Genuine CET-2025 candidates: one person who was not the candidate on record was kept out of a competition for seats among 3.3 lakh registrants. It is a small benefit spread across the cohort. The sources do not say what she meant to gain.

Further evidence

Setting. Admit cards printed with a QR code that resolves to the candidate's registered record and photograph. A photo pasted over the card therefore does not change what the app compares against. A live photograph taken at the hall door and matched by the app against the registered photo before entry. This ran at every center rather than the earlier pilot subset. Center staff instructed to act on a mismatch and escalate to the principal and KEA. Video coverage of the entrance that KEA could call for afterwards.

Conditions. The registered photograph must be of the genuine candidate. The control fails if the fraud is committed at registration rather than at the door. Verification must be completed before the candidate is admitted. This depends on staffing and app reliability at scale. KEA reported no glitches this time. A flagged person must be detained or identified on the spot. Here she fled, so the deterrent rests on the probe and the video footage rather than on an arrest.
