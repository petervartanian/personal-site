KEA's facial recognition app exposes a forged CET admit card

KEA's facial recognition app flagged a woman with a forged CET admit card in Bengaluru on 17 April 2025; she fled before writing the paper.

What happened

On Thursday 17 April 2025 a woman calling herself Tuba Fathima Jameel tried to enter the CET-2025 mathematics paper at the Silver Valley Public PU College centre in Malleswaram, Bengaluru, after nearly an hour in the restroom. Her admit card carried her own pasted photograph and an altered name but the hall ticket number and QR code of a genuine candidate, Tabu Naz. When staff scanned the code and captured her photo with the Karnataka Examinations Authority's new facial recognition app, it reported that she was not the candidate on record. She fled before the principal was told. KEA executive director H. Prasanna ordered a report with video footage and said legal action would follow.

What it was built to do. KEA built the app to verify candidates at the hall entrance by scanning the admit card's QR code and matching a live photograph against the photo submitted at registration, in order to prevent impersonation; exposing this impersonator was exactly that purpose.

Who benefited

  • -2
    organizational

    KEA stopped a forged-entry attempt in the app's first state-wide run, and The Hindu reports that all verifications ran without technical glitches on the second day; the benefit to the authority is a working integrity control across 3,30,808 registrants, demonstrated on one case.

  • -1
    societal

    Genuine CET-2025 candidates: one person who was not the candidate on record was kept out of a competition for seats among 3.3 lakh registrants; a small benefit spread across the cohort, and the sources do not say what she meant to gain.

The system's part

The app did the detecting: when the admit card's QR code was scanned and the woman's photo captured, it flagged a mismatch with the photo on record (New Indian Express) and 'revealed that she was not the genuine candidate' (The Hindu). The Hindu and the Times of India both report that the forged card carried her own photograph with the genuine candidate's hall ticket number and QR code, so comparing the card with her face would not have exposed her; the flag depended on the app's match against the registered photograph. Staff then acted, but she fled before the principal was told, so no arrest or sanction is attributable to the system; KEA's probe and the promised legal action were still pending in every report.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • Admit cards printed with a QR code that resolves to the candidate's registered record and photograph, so a photo pasted over the card does not change what the app compares against.
  • A live photograph taken at the hall door and matched by the app against the registered photo before entry, at every centre rather than the earlier pilot subset.
  • Centre staff instructed to act on a mismatch and escalate to the principal and KEA, with video coverage of the entrance that KEA could call for afterwards.
What must hold again
  • The registered photograph must be of the genuine candidate; the control fails if the fraud is committed at registration rather than at the door.
  • Verification must be completed before the candidate is admitted, which depends on staffing and app reliability at scale; KEA reported no glitches this time.
  • A flagged person must be detained or identified on the spot; here she fled, so the deterrent rests on the probe and the video footage rather than on an arrest.
