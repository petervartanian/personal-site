Facial recognition app flags an impostor at a Karnataka entrance exam

The Karnataka Examinations Authority's facial recognition app flagged a woman with a forged entrance-test admit card in Bengaluru on 17 April 2025; she fled before writing the paper.

  • 2 an organization

    KEA stopped a forged-entry attempt in the app's first state-wide run. The Hindu reports that all verifications ran without technical glitches on the second day. The benefit to the authority is a working integrity control across 3,30,808 registrants, demonstrated on one case.

  • Not rated society

    One person who was not the candidate on record was kept out of the test. The sources report no effect on the 3.3 lakh registrants and do not say what she meant to gain, so the extent of any benefit to the cohort cannot be established.

The system’s part
The app did the detecting. Staff scanned the admit card's QR code and captured the woman's photo. It flagged a mismatch with the photo on record (New Indian Express). It “revealed that she was not the genuine candidate” (The Hindu). The Hindu and the Times of India both report the forged card's contents. It carried her own photograph with the genuine candidate's hall ticket number and QR code. Comparing the card with her face would therefore not have exposed her. The flag depended on the app's match against the registered photograph. Staff then acted, but she fled before the principal was told. No arrest or sanction is attributable to the system. KEA's probe and the promised legal action were still pending in every report.
Limits
The evidence supports a reported detection and referral for investigation, not a final adjudication of identity fraud. One apparent successful match does not establish false-positive rates or system-wide fairness, and the large attendance counts are not validation results.

What happened

On Thursday 17 April 2025 a woman calling herself Tuba Fathima Jameel tried to enter the mathematics paper of the 2025 Common Entrance Test. The center was Silver Valley Public PU College in Malleswaram, Bengaluru. She had spent nearly an hour in the restroom. Her admit card carried her own pasted photograph and an altered name. It bore the hall ticket number and QR code of a genuine candidate, Tabu Naz. Staff scanned the code and captured her photo with the new facial recognition app of the Karnataka Examinations Authority. The app reported that she was not the candidate on record. She fled before the principal was told. H. Prasanna, the authority's executive director, ordered a report with video footage and said that legal action would follow. The authority kept facial recognition and QR-code checks for every candidate at the 2026 Common Entrance Test on 23 and 24 April 2026. On 31 July 2026 Prasanna said that candidates for the civil police constable recruitment exam of 2 August 2026 would enter only after facial recognition verification.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Staff scanned the admit card's QR code and captured a live photograph, which the app compared with the registered candidate's image.

Workflow

Admit cards printed with a QR code that resolves to the candidate's registered record and photograph. A photo pasted over the card therefore does not change what the app compares against. A live photograph taken at the hall door and matched by the app against the registered photo before entry. This ran at every center rather than the earlier pilot subset. Center staff instructed to act on a mismatch and escalate to the principal and KEA. Video coverage of the entrance that KEA could call for afterwards.

Human oversight

Staff performed the check and escalated the mismatch, but the woman left before the principal was informed.

AI contribution

The examination app compared a captured photograph with the registered candidate’s photograph and flagged a mismatch. Comparing the person only with the forged admit card would not have exposed the substitution.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The registered photograph must be of the genuine candidate. The control fails if the fraud is committed at registration rather than at the door. Verification must be completed before the candidate is admitted. This depends on staffing and app reliability at scale. KEA reported no glitches this time. A flagged person must be detained or identified on the spot. Here she fled, so the deterrent rests on the probe and the video footage rather than on an arrest.

Purpose

The Karnataka Examinations Authority built the app to verify candidates at the hall entrance and prevent impersonation. It scans the admit card's QR code and matches a live photograph against the photo submitted at registration. Exposing this impersonator served that purpose.
