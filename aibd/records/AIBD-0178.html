India disconnects more than 82 lakh SIM connections flagged by ASTR

The Department of Telecommunications' ASTR facial-recognition tool flags connections that one person took under different names; more than 82 lakh were disconnected after failed re-verification.

The system’s part
DoT describes ASTR as an indigenous AI and big-data analytic tool. It uses facial recognition to find connections taken by the same person under different names. Disconnection follows a failed re-verification step, so the tool flags connections rather than deciding. Developer and deployer are the same public body (DoT), which wrote the PIB releases.

The government reports more than 82 lakh disconnections after failed re-verification, and a Rajya Sabha reply reported in July 2026 counts more than 88 lakh. No source measures how many of those connections were fraudulent or whether fraud losses fell. No source reports an independent evaluation of ASTR's accuracy or a count of wrongful disconnections.

What happened

The Department of Telecommunications, Government of India, states that it designed and implemented ASTR. ASTR, the “AI and facial recognition powered Solution for Telecom SIM Subscriber Verification”, is to identify and weed out fake, forged and non-bonafide mobile connections. MediaNama reports that ASTR has run since 2021. A Press Information Bureau release from Chennai of 8 May 2023 reported that ASTR identified more than 50,000 non-bonafide connections in Tamil Nadu. It said that operators disconnected all of them and handed point-of-sale details to the Tamil Nadu Police Cyber Crime Wing. MediaNama reported on 9 May 2023 that the department refused its Right to Information request for the ASTR contract as confidential. The Hindu (Press Trust of India, 28 May 2023) quoted a senior official of the department in Patna. The official said that ASTR analysis found at least 21,800 connections exceeding the nine-per-subscriber limit in Bihar and Jharkhand. Around 17,000 of them were disconnected, and operators blacklisted 2,900 points of sale. The OECD AI Incidents Monitor records the Bihar and Jharkhand deactivations as an AI Incident and cites privacy, misidentification and data-protection concerns. Minister of State Dr Pemmasani Chandra Sekhar gave a Rajya Sabha written reply, which the Press Information Bureau posted on 7 August 2025. He said that the department disconnected more than 82 lakh connections through ASTR after they failed re-verification. The cumulative figure is dated to the posting of that reply. MediaNama reported on 24 July 2026 that a Rajya Sabha reply counted more than 88 lakh disconnections. The department's circular of 17 August 2026 orders operators to match subscriber photographs before issuing a SIM. MediaNama notes that the circular sets no image retention limit and no redress for a faulty facial match. On 9 October 2026 The Hans India reported the minister's statement that India had disconnected over 82 lakh fraudulent mobile connections through ASTR. No source reports an independent evaluation of ASTR's accuracy.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

ASTR compared subscriber records to identify connections apparently held by the same person under different names. It produced cases for re-verification and possible disconnection.

Workflow

Subscriber KYC photographs held by operators, matched across names by facial recognition. A legal cap of nine connections per subscriber defining violations.

Human oversight

Authorities analyzed the flagged connections, telecom operators conducted re-verification and disconnections, and police investigated the associated sellers.
