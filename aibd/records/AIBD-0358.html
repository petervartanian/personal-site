TrailGuard AI sends tiger alerts within 30 to 42 seconds

TrailGuard AI cameras sent tiger alerts to researchers and forest officials within 30 to 42 seconds, and officials later warned village leaders by text chat, though not rapidly.

What happened

Dertien et al. reported in BioScience (published 14 September 2023) that 12 TrailGuard AI camera-alert systems ran in the Kanha-Pench corridor, Madhya Pradesh. They ran from mid-May to mid-July 2022 (591 trap nights). The cameras run embedded AI on an Intel Myriad chip to detect tigers and humans and transmit only images of interest. Researchers and forest department officials received tiger notifications 30 to 42 seconds after detection. The authors report that forest officials sent text-chat warnings to village leaders after detections that concerned them, although these messages were not rapid. They also report that the sample was too small to determine an effect on human–wildlife conflict. The paper's second site, Dudhwa Tiger Reserve in Uttar Pradesh, where a unit captured armed poachers, is recorded separately. The Hindu (16 September 2023) reported the Madhya Pradesh deployment. It quoted H.S. Negi of the Global Tiger Forum on plans to incorporate the system in other tiger reserves. AFP (Taipei Times, 6 October 2023) reported the study's claim that the cameras were “immediately effective, picking up a tiger just 300 meters from a village”. No forest department document on the pilot is among the sources.

Details

AI system

Role

The camera’s models detected tigers and people and transmitted selected images.

Configuration

The deployment used the 2022 pilot units.

Inputs and outputs

Embedded detectors selected tiger and human images and sent notifications to researchers and forest officials within 30–42 seconds.

Workflow

Camera units with on-device detectors for tigers and humans placed on trails. Cellular or satellite transmission to officials' phones and email. Forest department staff willing to act on transmitted images. Research consortium funding (Tigers United, Clemson, Khosla grant).

Human oversight

Researchers and forest department officials received the alerts. Forest officials sent text-chat warnings to village leaders after detections that concerned them, although the study says that these messages were not rapid. [1] [2]

Benefits

Beneficiaries

Villagers near tiger movements were intended beneficiaries, while the demonstrated result was rapid awareness among officials and researchers.

Reach

A beneficiary count is not established. The pilot ran 12 camera-alert systems for 591 trap nights, and researchers and forest department officials received the alerts. [1] [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Transmission path within cell range or via the slower protocol. Detector accuracy high enough to avoid alert fatigue. Investigative follow-up by forest and police authorities. Battery life and concealment of units.

3 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 2. Outcome unresolved: 1.
