TrailGuard AI sends tiger alerts within 30 to 42 seconds

unrated a person1 an organizationunrated society

tiger notifications arrived 30 to 42 seconds after detection

BioScience 2023: 12 TrailGuard AI cameras ran in the Kanha-Pench corridor, Madhya Pradesh, mid-May to mid-July 2022 (591 trap nights); tiger notifications arrived 30 to 42 seconds after detection.

What happened

Dertien et al. reported in BioScience (published 14 September 2023) that 12 TrailGuard AI camera-alert systems ran in the Kanha-Pench corridor, Madhya Pradesh, from mid-May to mid-July 2022 (591 trap nights). The cameras run embedded AI on an Intel Myriad chip to detect tigers and humans and transmit only images of interest; researchers and forest department officials received tiger notifications 30 to 42 seconds after detection. AFP (Taipei Times, 6 October 2023) reported the study's claim that the cameras were 'immediately effective, picking up a tiger just 300 meters from a village'. The Hindu (16 September 2023) reported the Madhya Pradesh deployment and quoted H.S. Negi of the Global Tiger Forum on plans to incorporate the system in other tiger reserves. The paper's second site, Dudhwa Tiger Reserve in Uttar Pradesh, where a unit captured armed poachers, is recorded separately as AIBD-0318. No forest department document on the pilot was fetched. The date used is the BioScience publication date.

What it was built to do. Real-time detection of tigers and humans for human-wildlife conflict early warning and anti-poaching; the reported detections and follow-up were the intended use.

The system's part

Embedded AI on the camera selected and transmitted images of tigers and humans within 30 to 42 seconds, the mechanism documented in the developer-affiliated BioScience paper; whether any alert changed what villagers or officials did is not documented.

Replication

What produced it

  • Camera units with on-device detectors for tigers and humans placed on trails
  • Cellular or satellite transmission to officials' phones and email
  • Forest department staff willing to act on transmitted images
  • Research consortium funding (Tigers United, Clemson, Khosla grant)

What must hold again

  • Transmission path within cell range or via the slower protocol
  • Detector accuracy high enough to avoid alert fatigue
  • Investigative follow-up by forest and police authorities
  • Battery life and concealment of units
