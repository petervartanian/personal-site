Origin Orbit trial may have found over 800 Thames Water leaks

What happened

Thames Water trialed Origin Orbit, the satellite leak-detection product of North East England start-up Origin Tech Ltd (founded 2019). The company and the utility describe it as using AI on satellite data to flag suspected underground leaks within a 30-meter search radius. Origin's co-founder John Marsden told the BBC the satellites pass over Swindon roughly every six days. He said they produce points on a map for Thames Water crews. Thames Water's statements say an 18-week trial found over 800 leaks across the network. They were reported by the BBC (20 May 2026) and reproduced in trade press. It saved an estimated 8.7 million liters of water a day, with several leaks running at more than 10 liters per second. On the strength of the trial, Thames Water signed a 13-month contract, launched in March 2026 per the utility's release.

It deploys 20 specialist teams expected to find about 25 leaks a week each, and aims to cut leakage by 100 million liters a day. That makes it the first water company to integrate Origin Orbit into business-as-usual leakage operations. Origin Tech's own site adds a 2026 claim that Origin Orbit identified over 5,000 leaks (73.45 ML/d) across its customers. All figures originate with the utility and the vendor, and no regulator or independent measurement is among the sources. The trial dates are not given. Smart Water Magazine reported the result on 5 May 2026. Cambridgeshire County Council's AI inspection contract (April 2026) and Hertfordshire's ARRES robot (January 2024) have no outcomes on record. They are not among the sources.

What it was built to do. Built to pinpoint non-visible underground leaks from satellite data so that crews can find and fix them. The leaks found in the trial are that purpose.

The system's part

Only the vendor and the utility describe the AI role. The BBC caption says the AI-generated satellite technology detects moisture and movement in the ground, and Origin Tech describes AI-driven satellite insights. The system outputs suspected-leak points that Thames Water crews then locate and repair with conventional methods. The 800-leak and 8.7 million liters a day figures are Thames Water's estimates relayed by the BBC. They are reproduced in the utility's press release (Water Magazine) and trade press. Origin Tech's site adds vendor claims. No independent verification is among the sources.

Replication

What produced it. Satellite passes about every six days over the network, processed into suspected-leak points within a 30-meter radius. An 18-week trial in which Thames Water crews investigated the points with conventional ground methods. A network with a large share of non-visible underground leaks (92% of Origin Orbit finds are non-visible, per the vendor).

What must hold again. Satellite revisit frequency and ground conditions that allow moisture and movement to be detected. Crew capacity to investigate points (20 teams at about 25 leaks a week each under the contract). Repair follow-through so that found leaks become saved water. The utility's method for estimating liters saved per leak, which no independent source has checked.
