Thames Water crews find over 800 leaks flagged by Origin Orbit

In an 18-week trial, Origin Orbit flagged suspected leaks from satellite data, and Thames Water found over 800 leaks, saving an estimated 8.7 million liters a day by the utility's figures.

  • Not rated a person

    No individual customer outcome is documented. The BBC notes that customers never see most of these leaks.

  • 2 an organization

    Material to the utility's leakage operations by its own account. Over 800 leaks were found and an estimated 8.7 million liters a day saved in one trial. A 13-month business-as-usual contract followed. The estimate is the utility's own and unverified.

  • 1 society

    Water was conserved for the public supply. According to the Environment Agency figure that the BBC cites, about 19% of UK water is lost to leakage. The scale claimed is modest and unverified.

What happened

Thames Water trialed Origin Orbit, the satellite leak-detection product of North East England start-up Origin Tech Ltd (founded 2019). The company and the utility describe it as applying artificial intelligence to satellite data to flag suspected underground leaks within a 30-meter search radius. In August 2025 The Times reported that Northumbrian Water technicians used Origin's satellite points to narrow leak searches to a 20-meter radius. The company's founding director told the paper that its leak totals came mainly from trials. Thames Water's statements say that an 18-week trial found over 800 leaks across the network, saving an estimated 8.7 million liters of water a day, with several leaks running at more than 10 liters per second. No source dates the Thames Water trial. On the strength of the trial, Thames Water signed a 13-month contract, launched in March 2026 according to the utility's release. It deploys 20 specialists, with leak-detection crews expected to find about 25 leaks a week each, and aims to cut leakage by 100 million liters a day. That makes Thames Water the first water company to integrate Origin Orbit into business-as-usual leakage operations. Smart Water Magazine reported the result on 5 May 2026. The BBC reported the utility's figures on 20 May 2026, and trade press reproduced them. Origin's co-founder John Marsden told the BBC that the satellites pass over Swindon roughly every six days. He said that they produce points on a map for Thames Water crews. Origin Tech's own site adds a 2026 claim that Origin Orbit identified over 5,000 leaks (73.45 megaliters a day) across its customers. In June 2026 the Irish News reported that NI Water had signed a three-year Origin Orbit contract. On 24 August 2026 Thames Water described the satellite detection as part of its drought-response leak hunting, without reporting leak counts after the trial. New Civil Engineer (25 August 2026) gives the trial's length as 19 weeks, against 18 weeks in the utility's release. All figures originate with the utility and the vendor, and no regulator or independent measurement is among the sources. Cambridgeshire County Council's AI inspection contract (April 2026) and Hertfordshire's ARRES robot (January 2024) have no outcomes on record. They are not among the sources.

Details

AI system

Role

The software analyzed satellite data to identify possible water leaks.

Configuration

The deployment used Origin Orbit.

Inputs and outputs

Satellite analysis generated locations of suspected underground leaks for field crews to investigate. These were search targets, not remote confirmation that a pipe had been repaired.

Workflow

Satellite passes about every six days over the network, processed into suspected-leak points within a 30-meter radius. An 18-week trial in which Thames Water crews investigated the points with conventional ground methods. A network with a large share of non-visible underground leaks (92% of Origin Orbit finds are non-visible, per the vendor).

Human oversight

Specialist crews investigated the suspected locations and arranged conventional leak repair. The utility used trial findings to decide on an operating contract.

Benefits

Duration

The results describe an 18-week trial. A 13-month contract began in March 2026 with a target of 100 million liters a day. On 24 August 2026 Thames Water described the satellite detection as part of its leak hunting and reported no leak counts after the trial. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6] [7] [8]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Satellite revisit frequency and ground conditions that allow moisture and movement to be detected. Crew capacity to investigate points (20 teams at about 25 leaks a week each under the contract). Repair follow-through so that found leaks become saved water. The utility's method for estimating liters saved per leak, which no independent source has checked.
