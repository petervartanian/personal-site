Thames Water says Origin Orbit trial finds over 800 leaks

over 800 leaks across the network, saving an estimated 8.7 million liters of water a day

18-week Origin Orbit trial on Thames Water's network: over 800 leaks found, est. 8.7 million liters a day saved (utility figures); 13-month contract followed, target 100 million liters a day.

What happened

Thames Water trialled Origin Orbit, the satellite leak-detection product of North East England start-up Origin Tech Ltd (founded 2019), which the company and the utility describe as using AI on satellite data to flag suspected underground leaks within a 30-meter search radius; Origin's co-founder John Marsden told the BBC the satellites pass over Swindon roughly every six days and produce points on a map for Thames Water crews. According to Thames Water's statements as reported by the BBC (20 May 2026) and reproduced in trade press, an 18-week trial found over 800 leaks across the network, saving an estimated 8.7 million liters of water a day, with several leaks running at more than 10 liters per second. On the strength of the trial, Thames Water signed a 13-month contract, launched in March 2026 per the utility's release, deploying 20 specialist teams expected to find about 25 leaks a week each and aiming to cut leakage by 100 million liters a day, making it the first water company to integrate Origin Orbit into business-as-usual leakage operations. Origin Tech's own site adds a 2026 claim that Origin Orbit identified over 5,000 leaks (73.45 ML/d) across its customers. All figures originate with the utility and the vendor; no regulator or independent measurement was fetched. The trial dates are not given, so the date used is that of the earliest fetched report of the result (Smart Water Magazine, 5 May 2026). The earlier verifier also named Cambridgeshire County Council's AI inspection contract (April 2026) and Hertfordshire's ARRES robot (January 2024) as having no outcomes; they were not re-fetched.

What it was built to do. Built to pinpoint non-visible underground leaks from satellite data so that crews can find and fix them; the leaks found in the trial are that purpose.

The system's part

The AI role is described only by the vendor and the utility: the BBC caption says the AI-generated satellite technology detects moisture and movement in the ground, and Origin Tech describes AI-driven satellite insights; the system outputs suspected-leak points that Thames Water crews then locate and repair with conventional methods. The 800-leak and 8.7 million liters a day figures are Thames Water's estimates relayed by the BBC and reproduced in the utility's press release (Water Magazine) and trade press; Origin Tech's site adds vendor claims. No independent verification was fetched.

Replication

What produced it
  • Satellite passes about every six days over the network, processed into suspected-leak points within a 30-meter radius
  • An 18-week trial in which Thames Water crews investigated the points with conventional ground methods
  • A network with a large share of non-visible underground leaks (92% of Origin Orbit finds are non-visible, per the vendor)
What must hold again
  • Satellite revisit frequency and ground conditions that allow moisture and movement to be detected
  • Crew capacity to investigate points (20 teams at about 25 leaks a week each under the contract)
  • Repair follow-through so that found leaks become saved water
  • The utility's method for estimating liters saved per leak, which no independent source has checked
