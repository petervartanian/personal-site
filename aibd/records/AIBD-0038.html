ChatGPT proposes ALMS1; Alström confirmed in two business days

ChatGPT proposed ALMS1 for a 44-year-old with lifelong deafness, blindness, cardiomyopathy and CKD; Alström syndrome confirmed in two days.

ⓘ

What happened

Haller and colleagues at the Medical University of Vienna described a 44-year-old man in a letter published in Clinical Kidney Journal on 8 December 2023. He “presented to our nephrological institution with a complex medical history”. It included “progressive hearing loss and amaurosis since shortly after birth”, dilated cardiomyopathy, chronic kidney disease and cirrhosis. The physicians “used ChatGPT to obtain potential genetic targets based on the patient's medical history. One such target was the Alström Syndrome 1 gene (ALMS1)”. A geneticist “within just two business days confirmed that the patient actually possesses two distinct mutations in ALMS1”. Further tests then characterized hypogonadism and metabolic findings consistent with the syndrome. The consultation date is not stated.

Unexpected use. ChatGPT is a general-purpose conversational assistant. Generating candidate genes for a rare multisystem disease is not a function it was built for. This benefit was not its designed purpose.

AI’s contribution

ChatGPT proposed ALMS1 among candidate genes from a physician-written history. The clinicians framed the query and ordered targeted genetic testing, which made the diagnosis.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    A unifying genetic diagnosis at 44 for symptoms present since infancy, enabling targeted follow-up. No curative treatment is reported, so material rather than life-saving.
