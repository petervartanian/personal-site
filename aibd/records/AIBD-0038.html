ChatGPT proposes ALMS1; Alström confirmed in two business days

Alström syndrome confirmed in two days

ChatGPT proposed ALMS1 for a 44-year-old with lifelong deafness, blindness, cardiomyopathy and CKD; Alström syndrome confirmed in two days.

What happened

Haller and colleagues at the Medical University of Vienna described, in a letter published in Clinical Kidney Journal on 8 December 2023, a 44-year-old man who 'presented to our nephrological institution with a complex medical history', including 'progressive hearing loss and amaurosis since shortly after birth', dilated cardiomyopathy, chronic kidney disease and cirrhosis. The physicians 'used ChatGPT to obtain potential genetic targets based on the patient's medical history. One such target was the Alström Syndrome 1 gene (ALMS1)'. A geneticist 'within just two business days confirmed that the patient actually possesses two distinct mutations in ALMS1'. Further tests then characterized hypogonadism and metabolic findings consistent with the syndrome. The date observed is the publication date; the consultation date is not stated.

Not what it was built to do. ChatGPT is a general-purpose conversational assistant; generating candidate genes for a rare multisystem disease is not a function it was built for, so this benefit was not its designed purpose.

The system's part

ChatGPT proposed ALMS1 among candidate genes from a physician-written history; the clinicians framed the query and ordered targeted genetic testing, which made the diagnosis.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • A structured multisystem history entered by physicians
  • Rapid access to targeted genetic testing
  • Clinicians willing to test an LLM-generated hypothesis
What must hold again
  • The LLM's candidate list includes the true gene
  • Confirmatory sequencing is available
