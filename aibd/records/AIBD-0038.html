ChatGPT suggests a gene; Alström syndrome confirmed in two business days

ChatGPT suggested the ALMS1 gene for a 44-year-old man with hearing loss and blindness since infancy and heart and kidney disease; a geneticist confirmed Alström syndrome in two business days.

What happened

Physicians at the Medical University of Vienna saw a 44-year-old man who “presented to our nephrological institution with a complex medical history”. It included “progressive hearing loss and amaurosis since shortly after birth”, dilated cardiomyopathy, chronic kidney disease and cirrhosis. The consultation date is not stated. The physicians “used ChatGPT to obtain potential genetic targets based on the patient's medical history. One such target was the Alström Syndrome 1 gene (ALMS1)”. A geneticist “within just two business days confirmed that the patient actually possesses two distinct mutations in ALMS1”. Further tests then characterized hypogonadism and metabolic findings consistent with the syndrome. Haller and colleagues described the case in a letter that reached Clinical Kidney Journal on 27 November 2023. The journal published it on 8 December 2023. The letter cites the ChatGPT version of 14 September 2023. The authors write that the patient was diagnosed at a very advanced stage with a poor prognosis and that no specific treatment exists for Alström syndrome. Two reviews published in November 2025 and March 2026 cite the case.

AI system

Role

ChatGPT suggested candidate genes from a physician’s written clinical history.

Configuration

The authors identified the ChatGPT interface as the September 14 version.

Inputs and outputs

Physicians entered the patient's multisystem history, and ChatGPT returned possible genetic targets that included ALMS1.

Workflow

A structured multisystem history entered by physicians. Rapid access to targeted genetic testing. Clinicians willing to test an AI-generated hypothesis.

Human oversight

A geneticist confirmed two ALMS1 mutations, and clinicians conducted further testing to characterize the syndrome.
