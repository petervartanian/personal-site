Lawmaker who lost her speech addresses the House in her cloned voice

Representative Jennifer Wexton, who lost her speech to progressive supranuclear palsy, addressed the House on 25 July 2024 through an ElevenLabs clone of her own voice.

  • 3 a person

    A legislator who lost intelligible speech regained the ability to address the House in a voice recognizably her own. She told the Associated Press that it was “more me than I ever could have hoped I could hear again”.

  • Not rated society

    The House Historian states that she was the first Member to speak on the House floor using this type of assistive technology. No source reports a realized outcome for society that followed from that first.

The system’s part
The cloned voice is the AI output. Wexton wrote the content and the AAC device played it. A generic synthetic voice would also have carried the words, so the AI-specific benefit is the restoration of her own voice.
Limits
The benefit is assisted expression using text that Wexton prepared. It is not restoration of biological speech or treatment of her neurological disease, and the account does not establish effects for other patients.
Costs and harms
She told Politico that the voice can sound too formal for conversation and needs a strong signal. The paired AI Incident Database incident 508 concerns abuse of cloned celebrity voices, and the record does not show that such harm occurred here.
Later
Persisted, 31 December 2024. Washingtonian reported on 31 December 2024 that she was still speaking through the cloned voice on her iPad as her term ended. She told Politico in September 2024 that the model could sound too formal for conversation and that she did not use it to ask her husband to pass the ketchup.

What happened

Representative Jennifer Wexton of Virginia was diagnosed in 2023 with progressive supranuclear palsy. ElevenLabs approached her office in spring 2024, and her staff sent over an hour of old recordings. The Associated Press reported that ElevenLabs built the voice from recordings of her congressional speeches and public appearances. On 25 July 2024 she gave a House floor speech for Disability Pride Month. She used an augmentative and alternative communication device playing an AI-generated clone of her voice. The Congressional Record has her saying that progressive supranuclear palsy “has robbed me of my ability to use my full voice”. She said that hearing the recreation was “the most beautiful thing I had ever heard, and I cried tears of joy”. In an interview that Politico published on 9 September 2024, she said that the model was ready in a couple of days and that she first used it at the White House. She also said that it can sound too formal for conversation and needs a strong signal. Andrew Zaleski of Washingtonian spoke with her through the cloned voice for a feature published on 31 December 2024, and he reports that she was still using the voice as her term ended. The Office of the House Historian, which published her oral history on 27 March 2026, states that she was the first Member to speak on the House floor using this type of assistive technology.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The system generated speech in a recreation of Wexton's voice from prepared text, played through her communication device.

Workflow

A corpus of prior recordings in the person's own voice. Consent-based cloning by the vendor. An AAC device and House rules permitting its use on the floor.

Human oversight

Wexton wrote the words, and the augmentative and alternative communication device played them in her cloned voice. Her staff sent the recordings to ElevenLabs. No source describes any other safeguard. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
