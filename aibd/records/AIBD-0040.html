Wexton addresses the House with an ElevenLabs clone of her voice

Rep. Jennifer Wexton, who lost her speech to PSP, addressed the House on 25 July 2024 with an ElevenLabs clone of her own voice.

What happened

On 25 July 2024, Representative Jennifer Wexton of Virginia, diagnosed in 2023 with progressive supranuclear palsy, delivered a House floor speech for Disability Pride Month through an augmentative and alternative communication device playing an AI-generated clone of her voice. The Associated Press reported that ElevenLabs created the voice from recordings of her congressional speeches and public appearances. The Congressional Record records her saying that PSP 'has robbed me of my ability to use my full voice' and that hearing the recreation was 'the most beautiful thing I had ever heard, and I cried tears of joy'. The Office of the House Historian states she was the first Member to speak on the House floor using this type of assistive technology.

What it was built to do. ElevenLabs voice cloning is built to reproduce a person's voice from recordings; restoring her own voice for speech was that purpose.

The system's part

The cloned voice is the AI output; Wexton wrote the content and the AAC device played it. A generic synthetic voice would also have carried the words, so the AI-specific benefit is the restoration of her own voice.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it
  • A corpus of prior recordings in the person's own voice
  • Consent-based cloning by the vendor
  • An AAC device and House rules permitting its use on the floor
What must hold again
  • The speaker retains the ability to compose text
  • Institutional permission for device use
