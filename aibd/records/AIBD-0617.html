TA Pounce chatbot helps Georgia State students earn higher grades

In trials with 2,483 Georgia State students in online Government and Microeconomics courses from 2021 to 2023, the TA Pounce text chatbot raised the share earning an A or B by 4 points.

What happened

Georgia State University tested a course chatbot, called TA Pounce, that ran on the text-messaging platform of Mainstay, in two large asynchronous online courses. In Introduction to American Government (fall 2021, spring 2022 and fall 2022) and Principles of Microeconomics (2022-23 academic year), researchers randomly assigned half of 1,568 and 915 enrolled students to receive two or three scheduled text messages a week. The messages listed due dates, prompted students to finish assignments and encouraged them. The chatbot answered questions from a course knowledge base and passed other questions to the teaching assistant. Katharine Meyer of the Brookings Institution, Lindsay Page of Brown University and coauthors report that the chatbot raised the likelihood that students earned an A or B by four percentage points, from 61 percent in the control group, with similar results in both courses. In Microeconomics, women gained most and men showed no effect. The estimated gain in the numeric final grade, 1.58 points on a control mean of 71.07, was not statistically significant. The authors describe the chatbot as non-generative AI. Several coauthors work at Georgia State. The study appeared as an NBER working paper in July 2026 and in Economics of Education Review.

Details

AI system

Workflow

The chatbot sent two or three scheduled messages a week, tailored to whether a student had finished the previous week's work. A teaching assistant answered the questions that the chatbot could not. The courses were large, asynchronous and online, with few chances to meet the instructor.

Measurement

Measurement

Final course grades and grade categories from university records. [3]

Setting and sample

1,568 students in Introduction to American Government (fall 2021 to fall 2022) and 915 in Principles of Microeconomics (2022-23) at Georgia State University. [3]

Measures

Probability of earning an A or B. [3]

Results

Four percentage points from a control rate of 61 percent. [3]

Additional value from AI

The chatbot service as a whole; the automated answering is not separated from staff-written messages. [3]

Limitations

Numeric final grade effect not significant; men in Microeconomics showed no effect; several coauthors work at the deploying university. [3]

Denominators

Two or three text messages a week for a semester. [3]

Materials for replication

Registry of Efficacy and Effectiveness Studies 8160 and 13760. [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Students must read text messages on their phones. Course staff must keep due dates and the knowledge base current.
