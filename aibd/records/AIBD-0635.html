What happened

The Internal Revenue Service built the Return Review Program to screen individual returns that claim refunds before the refunds are paid and to replace its older Electronic Fraud Detection System. The Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration writes that both systems use predictive models developed with proprietary machine-learning algorithms as their main detection method. In an audit published on 25 September 2017, the Inspector General examined data from the 2015 filing season, in which the IRS's fraud detection systems together identified 700,560 confirmed identity-theft returns worth $3.92 billion. Selections made only by the program's identity-theft models totaled $1.88 billion, or 47.8% of that amount, while selections made only by the older system's models totaled $60 million, or 1.5%. The Inspector General judged that the IRS made a reasonable decision to retire the older identity-theft models, which it did for the 2016 filing season. In a report dated 24 July 2018, the Government Accountability Office relays IRS figures showing that the program prevented more than $6.51 billion in invalid refunds between January 2015 and November 2017, including about $4.4 billion in the 2017 filing season. The dollar figures come from IRS data. The audit counts unique selections by each system and does not compare the program with no screening, and the program also uses business rules besides its models.

Details

AI system

Workflow

The IRS screened every individual return that claimed a refund before paying it. The models used prior-year returns and third-party information returns such as W-2 forms. IRS staff adjusted the models and the fraud score settings to balance fraud caught against legitimate refunds delayed.

Measurement

Measurement

Audit of IRS filing-season data on which detection systems selected each confirmed identity-theft return. [1]

Setting and sample

700,560 confirmed identity-theft returns, 2015 filing season. [1]

Measures

Dollar value of confirmed identity-theft returns selected only by each system. [1]

Results

$1.88 billion (47.8%) unique to the Return Review Program models against $60 million (1.5%) unique to the older system. [1]

Additional value from AI

Not isolated from the program's business rules. [1]

Limitations

Figures come from IRS data; selections by manual processes are not counted; parts of the report are redacted; no comparison with no screening. [1]

Denominators

All electronically filed individual returns claiming refunds. [1]

Materials for replication

None public. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Prior-year returns and third-party data must be available when new returns are scored. Score settings must keep false detections low enough that legitimate taxpayers are not burdened.
