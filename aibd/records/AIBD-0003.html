Brain implant lets a woman speak via an avatar 18 years after a stroke

A speech neuroprosthesis built at the University of California, San Francisco, decoded Ann Johnson's attempted speech into text at a median 78 words per minute and drove a voice and an avatar.

  • 2 a person

    One identifiable person communicated by decoded text, a synthesized voice and an avatar in research sessions at her residence. The paper reports no independent use outside those sessions. Her implant was removed in February 2024.

Deep-learning decoders are the mechanism. They map cortical activity during attempted speech to phonemes, speech units and articulatory gestures, and every output (text, voice, avatar) depends on them.

What happened

In 2005 a pontine infarct left a woman with quadriplegia and anarthria. News reports name her Ann Johnson. She had communicated by letterboard, and the paper reports that her usual head-tracking device gave about 14.2 words per minute. At 47 she took part in the BRAVO trial (NCT03698149) at the medical center of the University of California, San Francisco. In September 2022 a 253-channel electrocorticography array was implanted over her speech cortical areas. Deep-learning recurrent neural networks trained on her attempted silent speech decoded text at a median 78 words per minute. Median word error rate was 25% over a 1,024-word vocabulary. The networks also synthesized speech personalized to her pre-injury voice and animated a facial avatar. The paper ties the text-decoding result to 14 days of data collection shortly after the September 2022 implant and gives no calendar session dates. Metzger and colleagues reported the results in Nature on 23 August 2023. In a Nature commentary of the same day, Nick Ramsey and Nathan Crone, who were not part of the study, called this result and Stanford's a turning point for restoring communication. In February 2024 her implant was removed for a reason unrelated to the trial. She took part in a streaming-synthesis study published in 2025, whose data predate the removal. On 13 August 2025 Berkeley News reported the removal. It also reported that she still writes to the team with her current assistive technology.

Details

AI system

Model

Bidirectional recurrent neural networks trained with connectionist temporal classification loss.

Role

The decoders converted cortical activity into text, synthesized speech, and avatar movements.

Configuration

The system used a 253-channel array on the brain’s surface and a vocabulary of 1,024 words.

Inputs and outputs

Cortical signals from attempted speech were decoded into text, synthesized speech based on Johnson's earlier voice, and movements of a facial avatar.

Workflow

Preserved articulatory representations in speech sensorimotor cortex 18 years after paralysis. High-density ECoG coverage of speech cortex. Deep-learning decoders trained on the participant's attempted speech in under two weeks.

Human oversight

Researchers trained the decoders on the participant's attempted speech and operated the real-time system within the BRAVO trial.

Benefits

Reach

One person benefited: the single participant in this study, a woman with anarthria after a 2005 stroke, in the BRAVO trial at the University of California, San Francisco. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5] [6]

Duration

Measurement

Measurement

Real-time decoding of silently attempted sentences into text, scored by word error rate and words per minute; synthesized speech and avatar output also assessed. [1]

Setting and sample

One woman with anarthria and quadriplegia 18 years after a brainstem stroke, BRAVO trial (NCT03698149), sessions at her residence in 2022 and 2023. [1]

Measures

Words per minute and word error rate. [1]

Results

Median 78.3 words per minute with a median word error rate of 25% over a 1,024-word vocabulary. [1]

Limitations

One participant, and the authors are the developers. Results come from research sessions, and the paper reports no independent use. [1]

Materials for replication

Code on GitHub (UCSF-Chang-Lab-BRAVO/multimodal-decoding); data under restricted access on request. [1]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Participant can attempt speech and retains cortical speech representations. Array remains stable with adequate signal quality. Research infrastructure available for training and real-time decoding.
