Brain implant lets a woman speak via an avatar 18 years after a stroke

A speech neuroprosthesis built at the University of California, San Francisco, decoded Ann Johnson's attempted speech into text at a median 78 words per minute and drove a voice and an avatar.

Costs and harms

The personalized voice and animated face probably conveyed identity and expression beyond text alone. Each output still needed its own accuracy checks; the text decoder’s median word error rate was about 25%.

Editor’s note. The study uses distinct decoding pathways for text, speech audio, and facial movement. Expressive presentation can add value, but the text error rate cannot be treated as the measured error rate of every output.
