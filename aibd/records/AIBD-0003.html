UCSF neuroprosthesis lets Ann Johnson speak through an avatar

253-electrode ECoG and deep-learning decoder let Ann Johnson, mute since a 2005 stroke, produce text, synthesized speech and an avatar

ⓘ

What happened

At UCSF Medical Center, a 47-year-old woman with quadriplegia and anarthria from a pontine infarct in 2005 was in the BRAVO trial (NCT03698149). News reports name her Ann Johnson. A 253-channel electrocorticography (ECoG) array was implanted over speech cortical areas in September 2022. Deep-learning recurrent neural networks trained on her attempted silent speech decoded text at a median 78 words per minute. Median word error rate was 25% over a 1,024-word vocabulary. They synthesized speech personalized to her pre-injury voice and animated a facial avatar (Metzger et al., Nature, 23 August 2023). She had earlier communicated by letterboard. The paper ties the text-decoding result to 14 days of data collection shortly after the September 2022 implant and gives no calendar session dates. So the implant month (1 September 2022) is the observation date.

Purpose. Built to restore communication by decoding attempted speech from cortical surface recordings into text, synthesized speech and avatar animation. That was the intended benefit and is what occurred.

AI’s contribution

Deep-learning decoders are the mechanism. They map cortical activity during attempted speech to phonemes, speech units and articulatory gestures, and every output (text, voice, avatar) depends on them.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Material benefit to one identifiable person: fast text, synthesized voice and avatar communication demonstrated in research sessions (run at her residence). The paper reports no independent use outside those sessions.

Since then
The same participant went on to use a streaming synthesis approach, reported in Nature Neuroscience, that cut the decoding delay from about eight seconds per sentence to under a second.Ann, who also participated in the 2023 study, shared with researchers how her experience with the new streaming synthesis approach compared to the earlier study's text-to-speech decoding method. source

Further evidence

Setting. Preserved articulatory representations in speech sensorimotor cortex 18 years after paralysis. High-density ECoG coverage of speech cortex. Deep-learning decoders trained on the participant's attempted speech in under two weeks.

Conditions. Participant can attempt speech and retains cortical speech representations. Array remains stable with adequate signal quality. Research infrastructure available for training and real-time decoding.
