AI knowledge graph proposed baricitinib in Feb 2020; ACTT-2 showed faster recovery and COV-BARRIER lower 28-day mortality (a secondary endpoint); FDA and WHO endorsed it.

What happened

On 4 February 2020 BenevolentAI staff published a Lancet correspondence reporting that the company's machine-learning-built knowledge graph identified baricitinib, a rheumatoid-arthritis JAK inhibitor, as a candidate against 2019-nCoV because it binds AAK1, a regulator of viral endocytosis. Randomised trials then tested that hypothesis. ACTT-2 (1,033 patients, enrolled 8 May to 1 July 2020) reported median recovery of 7 days versus 8 (rate ratio 1.16, 95% CI 1.01-1.32), and 10 versus 18 days among patients on high-flow oxygen or non-invasive ventilation. COV-BARRIER (1,525 patients) did not meet its primary endpoint of disease progression (27.8% versus 30.5%, odds ratio 0.85, p=0.18); its 28-day all-cause mortality, a secondary endpoint, was 8% versus 13% (hazard ratio 0.57, nominal p=0.0018). FDA authorized baricitinib with remdesivir on 19 November 2020; WHO strongly recommended it on 14 January 2022.

What it was built to do. Drug repurposing: mine a machine-learning-built biomedical knowledge graph for approved drugs that might block a new coronavirus.

Who benefited

The system's part

The AI's role was hypothesis generation only: BenevolentAI's knowledge graph ranked baricitinib among six high-affinity AAK1-binding approved drugs and the authors published the prediction. It did not design, run or analyze the trials. The demonstrated clinical benefit was established by randomized controlled trials (ACTT-2, COV-BARRIER) conducted by investigators unconnected to BenevolentAI, and judged by FDA and WHO.

Replication

What produced it. A machine-learning-built knowledge graph queried for approved drugs likely to block viral endocytosis through AAK1. A new pathogen without a treatment and trial investigators willing to test a repurposing hypothesis. Hospitalised patients requiring oxygen, in whom the effect was measured in randomized trials.

What must hold again. Hospitalised COVID-19 patients needing supplemental oxygen or more. Baricitinib given per protocol alongside remdesivir or corticosteroids. SARS-CoV-2 epidemiology and standard of care of 2020-2021.
