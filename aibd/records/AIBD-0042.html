An arthritis drug helped patients with severe COVID-19

Researchers used the Benevolent Platform to identify baricitinib as a candidate treatment for COVID-19.

ⓘ

What happened

BenevolentAI researchers reported in February 2020 that their knowledge graph had helped them identify baricitinib, an arthritis drug, as a possible treatment for COVID-19. They proposed clinical testing after reviewing its effects on inflammation and proteins involved in viral entry. In ACTT-2, 1,033 hospitalized adults received remdesivir with either baricitinib or a placebo. Median recovery took 7 days with baricitinib and 8 days with the placebo.

COV-BARRIER enrolled 1,525 adults. By day 28, 8% of patients receiving baricitinib had died, compared with 13% receiving a placebo. Both groups received standard care. Mortality was a secondary outcome. The trial did not meet its primary outcome of reduced disease progression, so its mortality result was not conclusive under the planned procedure for multiple comparisons.

The subsequent RECOVERY trial enrolled 8,156 patients. Mortality, its primary outcome, was 12% with baricitinib and 14% with usual care alone.

Purpose. Researchers searched for existing drugs that might treat COVID-19, using a biomedical knowledge graph that combines databases with information extracted from scientific papers.

AI’s contribution

BenevolentAI researchers used the Benevolent Platform to search biomedical relationships and examine networks of proteins. They selected baricitinib after reviewing its targets, dosage, and pharmacology. The clinical investigators then tested the drug. None of these trials compared discovery with AI against discovery without it. The original correspondence and methods paper document AI-assisted selection. FDA authorization and WHO guidance concern the treatment.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Fewer patients died with baricitinib in COV-BARRIER and RECOVERY. RECOVERY confirmed lower 28-day mortality as its primary outcome in 8,156 patients.

  • an organization

    ACTT-2 reported median recovery of 7 days with baricitinib and 8 days without it. That could free hospital capacity. A separate economic model estimated lower hospital costs, but neither establishes realized staffing or cash savings.

  • society

    WHO recommended baricitinib with corticosteroids for severe or critical COVID-19. That recommendation supports wider access to a treatment that reduced deaths in trials. Worldwide uptake and population-wide effects were not measured.

Since then
RECOVERY independently found lower 28-day mortality with baricitinib. WHO had recommended its use with corticosteroids for severe or critical COVID-19 in January 2022. The recommendation does not establish worldwide uptake.

Further evidence

Setting. BenevolentAI researchers combined a biomedical knowledge graph with expert review to select baricitinib. Clinical investigators separately tested the drug alongside the care available in 2020 and 2021.

Conditions. The clinical comparisons concern hospitalized patients and the eligibility criteria of each trial. Background treatment differed: remdesivir in ACTT-2, and mostly corticosteroids in COV-BARRIER and RECOVERY. Reproducing the AI selection would also require the January 2020 graph, software configuration, and researchers’ decisions. The complete snapshot is not public.
