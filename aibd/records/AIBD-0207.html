Workflow

A chain-wide facial recognition layer on existing CCTV (rolled out from April 2024, extended to all 83 outlets after August 2025). Staff upload a suspect's face, and managers get app alerts when that person re-enters any branch. A human trigger: a stock check on 10 September 2025 that revealed missing bottles. Staff then reviewed the footage and uploaded the face the same day. A repeat offender who came back to the same outlet at noon the next day, which gave the alert something to fire on. A manager on the premises with his phone, able to intercept at the self-checkout kiosk. A police and court process (the STAR program, an amalgamated theft charge) that turned the identification into restitution and a sentence.

Relation to AI

The system's documented contribution came after a human detection. On 10 September 2025 a staff member found discrepancies in a stock check. Colleagues reviewed CCTV and saw Tan leaving without paying. They uploaded her face. The Straits Times reports that the system “then automatically identified the other instances” of theft. That turned one observed theft into an amalgamated charge covering seven visits. On 11 September the system's alert reached the retail manager's phone when she entered. He intercepted her at the self-checkout kiosk with three unscanned bottles. The interception, the bag check, the police call, the prosecution and the restitution were human and institutional. No source names the vendor or the model, gives an accuracy figure, or reports a false match in this case.

AI contribution

After staff identified a theft, facial-recognition software found footage of earlier visits and alerted a manager when the same person returned to the store.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The system identifies, it does not detect. Stock counts or staff still have to notice the first theft. Sheng Siong's CEO implied as much when he said the human eye had missed cases. Someone must act on the alert within minutes. The gain here was an interception before she left the store, not the alert itself. The match must be right and lawful. A wrong face flagged at the door would produce a wrongful stop. No source reports a false match in this case. None names the vendor or any accuracy figure.

Purpose

Sheng Siong rolled out its AI-driven facial recognition CCTV system in April 2024 as a measure against shoplifters. It worked with the Singapore Police Force under its Shop Theft for Retailers (STAR) program. In August 2025 it announced an extension to all 83 outlets. Staff upload the photograph of a suspect identified from footage. The system alerts store managers' phones when that person enters any branch. Sheng Siong said that thefts per store rose from 4.91 in 2023 to 8 in 2024. Its CEO attributed the rise to catching cases that the human eye had missed. The system was designed to identify repeat shoplifters and flag their return.
