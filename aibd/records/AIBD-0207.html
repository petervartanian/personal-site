Sheng Siong's facial recognition catches a repeat wine thief

Sheng Siong's facial recognition matched a wine thief to six prior thefts and flagged her return; arrest, restitution, eight days' jail.

ⓘ

What happened

Catherine Tan Li Eng, 51, took 19 Jacob's Creek wine bottles worth about S$556 from the Sheng Siong supermarket at Block 622D Punggol Central, Singapore. She bagged them unscanned at self-checkout from 2 to 11 September 2025. On 10 September staff found stock discrepancies, reviewed closed-circuit television (CCTV) and uploaded her face to the chain's AI-driven facial recognition system. It automatically identified the earlier thefts. When she returned at noon on 11 September the retail manager got a phone alert. He stopped her at the self-checkout kiosk and found three unscanned bottles. Police arrested her. On 12 May 2026 District Judge Brenda Chua jailed her for eight days on an amalgamated theft charge after full restitution of about S$470.

Purpose. Sheng Siong rolled out its AI-driven facial recognition CCTV system in April 2024 as a measure against shoplifters. It worked with the Singapore Police Force under its Shop Theft for Retailers (STAR) program. In August 2025 it announced an extension to all 83 outlets. Staff upload the photograph of a suspect identified from footage. The system alerts store managers' phones when that person enters any branch. Sheng Siong said thefts per store rose from 4.91 in 2023 to 8 in 2024. Its CEO attributed the rise to catching cases the human eye had missed. Identifying a repeat shoplifter and flagging her return was exactly its designed purpose.

AI’s contribution

The system's documented contribution came after a human detection. On 10 September 2025 a staff member found discrepancies in a stock check. Colleagues reviewed CCTV and saw Tan leaving without paying. They uploaded her face. The Straits Times reports that the system “then automatically identified the other instances” of theft. That turned one observed theft into an amalgamated charge covering seven visits. On 11 September the system's alert reached the retail manager's phone when she entered. He intercepted her at the self-checkout kiosk with three unscanned bottles. The interception, the bag check, the police call, the prosecution and the restitution were human and institutional. No source names the vendor or the model, gives an accuracy figure, or reports a false match in this case.

Beneficiaries

  • an organization

    Sheng Siong's gain was small and specific. It kept three bottles on 11 September 2025. It got full restitution of about S$470 for the rest of the 19 bottles (about S$556 in all). A seven-visit theft series at one outlet ended. The amount is minor for an 83-outlet chain. The first detection was a human stock check and a manual review of CCTV, not the system.

Further evidence

Setting. A chain-wide facial recognition layer on existing CCTV (rolled out from April 2024, extended to all 83 outlets after August 2025). Staff upload a suspect's face, and managers get app alerts when that person re-enters any branch. A human trigger: a stock check on 10 September 2025 that revealed missing bottles. Staff then reviewed the footage and uploaded the face the same day. A repeat offender who came back to the same outlet at noon the next day, which gave the alert something to fire on. A manager on the premises with his phone, able to intercept at the self-checkout kiosk. A police and court process (the STAR program, an amalgamated theft charge) that turned the identification into restitution and a sentence.

Conditions. The system identifies, it does not detect. Stock counts or staff still have to notice the first theft. Sheng Siong's CEO implied as much when he said the human eye had missed cases. Someone must act on the alert within minutes. The gain here was an interception before she left the store, not the alert itself. The match must be right and lawful. A wrong face flagged at the door would produce a wrongful stop. No source reports a false match in this case. None names the vendor or any accuracy figure.
