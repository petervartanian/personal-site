Sheng Siong's facial recognition catches a repeat wine thief

Sheng Siong's facial recognition system matched a wine thief to six prior thefts and flagged her return, which led to her arrest, restitution and an eight-day jail term.

  • 1 an organization

    Sheng Siong's gain was small and specific. It kept three bottles on 11 September 2025. It got full restitution of about S$470 for the rest of the 19 bottles (about S$556 in all). A series of thefts over seven visits to one outlet ended. The amount is minor for a chain of 83 outlets. The first detection came from a human stock check and a manual review of closed-circuit television (CCTV) footage.

The system’s part
The system's documented contribution came after a human detection. On 10 September 2025 a staff member found discrepancies in a stock check. Colleagues reviewed CCTV and saw Tan leaving without paying. They uploaded her face. The Straits Times reports that the system “then automatically identified the other instances” of theft. He intercepted her at the self-checkout kiosk with three unscanned bottles. The interception, the bag check, the police call, the prosecution and the restitution were human and institutional. What happened

Catherine Tan Li Eng, 51, took 19 Jacob's Creek wine bottles worth about S$556 from the Sheng Siong supermarket at Block 622D Punggol Central, Singapore. She bagged them unscanned at self-checkout from 2 to 11 September 2025. On 10 September staff found stock discrepancies, reviewed closed-circuit television (CCTV) and uploaded her face to the chain's AI-driven facial recognition system. It automatically identified the earlier thefts. When she returned at noon on 11 September the retail manager got a phone alert. He stopped her at the self-checkout kiosk and found three unscanned bottles. Police arrested her. The Straits Times reports that the alert and the interception happened at noon on 11 September 2025. A similar case followed at the chain's Sin Ming Avenue outlet, where the system alerted staff on 1 October 2025 to a woman who had stolen on 12 visits. On 12 May 2026 District Judge Brenda Chua jailed Tan for eight days on an amalgamated theft charge after full restitution of about S$470. The woman caught at the Sin Ming Avenue outlet was jailed for one week on 8 October 2026.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Staff uploaded a face from reviewed theft footage, and the system alerted the manager’s phone when the woman returned.

Autonomy

The manager checked the bags and called police after the alert.

Workflow

A chain-wide facial recognition layer on existing CCTV (rolled out from April 2024, extended to all 83 outlets after August 2025). Staff upload a suspect's face, and managers get app alerts when that person re-enters any branch. A human trigger: a stock check on 10 September 2025 that revealed missing bottles. Staff then reviewed the footage and uploaded the face the same day. A repeat offender who came back to the same outlet at noon the next day, which gave the alert something to fire on. A manager on the premises with his phone, able to intercept at the self-checkout kiosk. A police and court process (the STAR program, an amalgamated theft charge) that turned the identification into restitution and a sentence.

Human oversight

Staff found the initial discrepancy, reviewed the footage, and uploaded the photograph. A manager intercepted the customer and contacted the police.

AI contribution

After staff identified a theft, facial-recognition software found footage of earlier visits and alerted a manager when the same person returned to the store.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The system identifies, it does not detect. Stock counts or staff still have to notice the first theft. Sheng Siong's CEO implied as much when he said the human eye had missed cases. Someone must act on the alert within minutes. The gain here was an interception before she left the store, not the alert itself. The match must be right and lawful. A wrong face flagged at the door would produce a wrongful stop. No source reports a false match in this case. None names the vendor or any accuracy figure.

Sector

Retail loss prevention and law enforcement. Supermarket shop-theft detection: facial recognition on store CCTV with mobile alerts to managers.

Purpose

Sheng Siong rolled out its AI-driven facial recognition CCTV system in April 2024 as a measure against shoplifters. It worked with the Singapore Police Force under its Shop Theft for Retailers (STAR) program. In August 2025 it announced an extension to all 83 outlets. Staff upload the photograph of a suspect identified from footage. The system alerts store managers' phones when that person enters any branch. Sheng Siong said that thefts per store rose from 4.91 in 2023 to 8 in 2024. Its CEO attributed the rise to catching cases that the human eye had missed. The system was designed to identify repeat shoplifters and flag their return.
