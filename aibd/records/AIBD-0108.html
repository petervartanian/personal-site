Two Tesla Model X cars stop as an oak falls; eight walk away

Two Tesla Model X cars stopped as an oak fell across the A31 in Storm Dennis; eight occupants walked away, and one driver credits Autopilot's emergency braking.

  • 3 a person

    A 400-year-old oak struck two cars at the hood. Eight people walked away with, at worst, two black eyes, a bump on the head and bruising. Laurence Sanderson and the Mirror call the braking life-saving. That claim rests on his account, and no telemetry or official assessment is reported. The second driver was unsure whether the brakes were enabled.

What happened

On the evening of Saturday 15 February 2020, during Storm Dennis, a 400-year-old oak fell across the A31 at Sturminster Marshall, Dorset. It hit two Tesla Model X cars going in opposite directions. Laurence Sanderson of Brentwood, Essex, was driving his wife Anna and three children to a half-term holiday. The other car was a Tesla loan vehicle driven by Josh Whitelock of Wimborne, with Kitty McConnell and her mother Julia. It also stopped suddenly. Anna Sanderson had two black eyes and bruising. Nobody was seriously hurt. That night Laurence Sanderson wrote to Tesla and Elon Musk on Twitter: "Autopilot kicked in and saved us." On 17 February 2020 he told the Mirror that the car was on Autopilot, that the emergency braking kicked in and that the trunk landed on the hood. On the same day Fred Lambert, the editor in chief of Electrek, questioned the drivers' account. He argued that Tesla's emergency braking could not detect a tree that fell from beside the road. He suggested that the drivers may have braked themselves, and he called for the vehicle logs. On 19 February 2020 Whitelock told the Bournemouth Echo that he was not sure whether the car's automatic brakes were enabled. He credited the car's other safety features. The same day the Mirror reported that Elon Musk had replied: "Glad you're all ok." On 25 February 2020 Anna Sanderson repeated the family's account on BBC London. No source reports that the logs were released.

Details

AI system

Role

The driver reported that the system applied emergency braking.

Configuration

Both vehicles were Tesla Model X cars, including one loan car. The installed Autopilot software was not identified.

Autonomy

Sanderson reported that automatic braking acted while his Model X was on Autopilot. Automatic intervention in Whitelock's loan vehicle remains uncertain because he did not know whether its automatic brakes were enabled.

Workflow

Automatic Emergency Braking active on a Tesla Model X whose cameras and radar register an obstacle entering its path. A hazard that arrives in front of the car, here a tree falling across the carriageway, rather than onto the roof from above. A stop short of the point where the trunk lands, leaving the bonnet rather than the cabin to take the impact.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The system has time to detect and brake, which Sanderson put at about a second. The vehicle's structure withstands the part of the impact that still lands. Whitelock credited the car's other safety features. Occupants are belted. The Whitelock passengers were sore from the seatbelts.
