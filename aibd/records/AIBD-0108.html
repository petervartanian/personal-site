Two Tesla Model X cars stop as an oak falls; eight walk away

Two Model X cars stopped as an oak fell on the A31 in Storm Dennis; eight occupants walked away, both drivers credit the emergency brakes.

AI system

System

Tesla Autopilot driver-assistance suite with Automatic Emergency Braking, on two Model X cars

Release

Exact release not established.

Application developer

Tesla

Role

The driver reported that the system applied emergency braking.

Configuration

Both vehicles were Tesla Model X cars, including one loan car. The installed Autopilot software was not identified.

Workflow

Automatic Emergency Braking active on a Tesla Model X whose cameras and radar register an obstacle entering its path. A hazard that arrives in front of the car, here a tree falling across the carriageway, rather than onto the roof from above. A stop short of the point where the trunk lands, leaving the bonnet rather than the cabin to take the impact.

Relation to AI

By Laurence Sanderson's account his car was on Autopilot and the emergency braking stopped it so the trunk hit the bonnet rather than the cabin. Josh Whitelock said he was not sure whether his loan car's automatic brakes were enabled and credited its other safety features. No Tesla data, police finding or engineering assessment of whether either system braked, or whether the drivers would have stopped in time themselves, is reported. Elon Musk's reply, “Glad you're all ok”, acknowledges the outcome, not the mechanism.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The system has time to detect and brake, which Sanderson put at about a second. The vehicle's structure withstands the part of the impact that still lands. Whitelock credited the car's other safety features. Occupants are belted. The Whitelock passengers were sore from the seatbelts.

Later findings

Later coverage on 19 February 2020 only added Elon Musk's reply that he was glad everyone was OK; both cars were written off and no further development was reported.

Entry details

Field

Road vehicle crash avoidance. Automotive.

Purpose

Tesla's Autopilot hardware and software provide driver assistance and active safety features including Automatic Emergency Braking. It detects obstacles the car may hit and applies the brakes. Stopping short of a falling tree is that intended use as the drivers describe it.
