What happened

On the evening of Saturday 15 February 2020, during Storm Dennis, a 400-year-old oak fell across the A31 at Sturminster Marshall, Dorset. It hit two Tesla Model X cars going in opposite directions. Laurence Sanderson of Brentwood, Essex, was driving wife Anna and three children to a half-term holiday. He told the Mirror the car was on Autopilot, the emergency braking kicked in and the trunk landed on the bonnet. The other car, a Tesla loan vehicle driven by Josh Whitelock of Wimborne with Kitty McConnell and her mother Julia, also stopped suddenly. Whitelock told the Bournemouth Echo he was not sure its automatic brakes were enabled. Anna Sanderson had two black eyes and bruising. Nobody was seriously hurt.

What it was built to do. Tesla's Autopilot hardware and software provide driver assistance and active safety features including Automatic Emergency Braking. It detects obstacles the car may hit and applies the brakes. Stopping short of a falling tree is that intended use as the drivers describe it.

The system's part

Not assessed. By Laurence Sanderson's account his car was on Autopilot and the emergency braking stopped it so the trunk hit the bonnet rather than the cabin. Josh Whitelock said he was not sure whether his loan car's automatic brakes were enabled and credited its other safety features. No Tesla data, police finding or engineering assessment of whether either system braked, or whether the drivers would have stopped in time themselves, is reported. Elon Musk's reply, “Glad you're all ok”, acknowledges the outcome, not the mechanism.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. Automatic Emergency Braking active on a Tesla Model X whose cameras and radar register an obstacle entering its path. A hazard that arrives in front of the car, here a tree falling across the carriageway, rather than onto the roof from above. A stop short of the point where the trunk lands, leaving the bonnet rather than the cabin to take the impact.

What must hold again. The system has time to detect and brake, which Sanderson put at about a second. The vehicle's structure withstands the part of the impact that still lands. Whitelock credited the car's other safety features. Occupants are belted. The Whitelock passengers were sore from the seatbelts.
