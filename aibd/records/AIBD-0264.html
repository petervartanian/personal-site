FOX matches or beats manual fitting of cochlear implants in a trial

In a randomized crossover trial in Brussels, newly implanted patients fitted with the FOX software understood speech better at 3 and 12 months, and all manual-arm patients kept the FOX map.

  • 2 a person

    Newly implanted patients had better speech intelligibility at 3 and 12 months. Experienced users had median gains of 10% in speech scores.

  • 1 an organization

    The authors report that the FOX approach was equivalent or superior in “hearing performance, comfort, and resources”.

  • Not rated society

    The Brussels studies each come from a single center, and no source reports an outcome across a population.

The software proposes MAP settings from measured outcomes. The trials compare it with manual fitting and attribute the measured gains to the FOX maps. All three papers share co-author P. J. Govaerts. Improved speech scores did not always mean greater comfort: 63% of patients in the FOX arm preferred their manual map for comfort.

What happened

Two 2019 case studies from the Saint-Luc group in Brussels reported improved word recognition with AI suggestions in one of two adults. Two US studies followed, by authors outside the Saint-Luc and Eargroup teams. Waltzman and Kelsall (2020) reported that FOX maps matched clinician maps on average in 55 experienced adults. Zwolan and colleagues (2021), two of them Cochlear Americas staff, reported scores similar to traditional programming in 31 newly implanted patients, with 43% fewer programming appointments. They state that Otoconsult developed FOX. Wathour et al. (Ear and Hearing, published online 21 December 2022) then ran a one-year randomized crossover trial. It enrolled 24 newly implanted cochlear-implant patients at Cliniques universitaires Saint-Luc, Brussels. It compared manual fitting with the Fitting to Outcome eXpert (FOX). From 3 months the FOX group showed less variability and better speech intelligibility in quiet and in noise. At 3 months its median phoneme scores were 50%, 70% and 50% at 55, 70 and 85 dB SPL, against 45%, 50% and 40% in the manual group. After crossover, 100% of the manual group kept the FOX map and 82% performed better with it. Of the FOX group, 63% preferred their manual map for comfort. A companion study (Otology & Neurotology, 18 January 2023) gave 47 experienced users a FOX map. It found median improvements of 10 dB at 6,000 Hz and of 10% in phonemic discrimination and speech scores after 15 days of take-home use. The two US studies found results similar to those in Brussels. P. J. Govaerts co-authored all three Saint-Luc papers. The cited sources do not establish whether any author is affiliated with Otoconsult. In November 2025 Otoconsult's site described FOX as an outcome-based application assisting audiologists and AI-FOX as the successor of a deterministic first-generation tool.

Details

AI system

Configuration

The studies used FOX fitting software in 2022 and 2023. The sources do not establish whether this was the later AI-FOX generation.

Inputs and outputs

Measured hearing outcomes informed proposed cochlear-implant settings that clinicians could apply as a new map.

Autonomy

Audiologists selected and applied the proposed settings, and patients’ comfort and preferences influenced which map they retained.

Workflow

Outcome measurements (thresholds, loudness scaling, speech scores) fed to the FOX recommender. Audiologists applying the recommended MAPs.

Human oversight

FOX proposes MAP settings from measured hearing outcomes, and audiologists apply the recommended maps. The sources describe no other safeguard. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

Reach

The Brussels trial enrolled 24 newly implanted patients, and the companion study gave 47 experienced users a FOX map. The two US studies covered 55 experienced adults and 31 newly implanted patients. No source counts patients fitted with FOX in routine care. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The Brussels trial ran for one year, and its authors report better speech intelligibility at 3 and 12 months. No source reports whether Saint-Luc kept using FOX after the trial, although Otoconsult still offered AI-FOX in November 2025. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Pure-tone thresholds, loudness scaling, spectral discrimination, word recognition in quiet and noise and speech tracking at 3 and 12 months; Mann-Whitney tests with Holm correction; map choice after crossover.

Setting and sample

24 patients receiving a first cochlear implant at Cliniques universitaires Saint-Luc, Brussels, randomized to FOX or manual fitting for one year, then switched.

Measures

Median phoneme scores at 40 to 85 dB SPL and in noise; share of patients keeping each map.

Results

Better speech intelligibility in the FOX arm at 3 and 12 months (p < 0.05); after crossover all manual-arm patients kept the FOX map and 82% performed better with it, while 63% of FOX-arm patients kept the manual map for comfort.

Additional value from AI

The randomized comparison attributes the difference in measured hearing to the FOX maps.

Limitations

Small single-center sample with no masking listed in the registry; comfort favored manual maps in the FOX arm; a co-author is from the Eargroup, whose results Otoconsult presents as FOX's clinical evidence.

Denominators

One year of fitting in each arm.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Clinicians accept and apply FOX recommendations. Patients tolerate the map (63% of FOX-arm patients preferred manual maps for comfort).

Replication attempts

4 follow-up entries. Benefit recurred: 4.

Evidence

Original evidence

The three Saint-Luc papers share the co-author P. J. Govaerts, and the developer's identity comes from Otoconsult's own site. The 2021 study by Zwolan and colleagues comes from a separate US group, two of whose members are Cochlear Americas staff. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]
