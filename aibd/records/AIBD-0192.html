FAO and Penn State launch Nuru to confirm fall armyworm damage

FAO announced on 25 June 2018 a talking Android app built with PlantVillage to confirm fall armyworm damage offline and feed FAO's FAMEWS; no outcome data in the fetched sources.

What happened

On 25 June 2018 FAO announced from Rome that it and Pennsylvania State University had developed and launched Nuru, a talking app using machine learning that runs offline on a standard Android phone: a farmer holds the phone next to an infested plant and Nuru confirms whether fall armyworm caused the damage. FAO said the pest first appeared in Africa in 2016 and spread across sub-Saharan Africa in 2017, threatening the food security of more than 300 million people; Nuru was to complement FAO's Fall Armyworm Monitoring and Early Warning System (FAMEWS) app, with data validated by national focal points flowing into a global platform, and was to add Swahili, French and Twi. The New York Times later reported that this fall armyworm tool served as the blueprint for the eLocust3m app of 2020. The fetched sources document the launch and design only; no evaluation of accuracy or of any outcome for farmers was found.

What it was built to do. Confirm fall armyworm damage on maize from a phone held next to the plant, offline, and feed reports into FAO's FAMEWS monitoring system.
