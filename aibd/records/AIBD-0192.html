FAO and Penn State launch Nuru to confirm fall armyworm damage

Nuru, a machine-learning app from Penn State's PlantVillage and FAO, launched on 25 June 2018 to confirm fall armyworm damage offline on Android phones; no source reports an outcome for farmers.

What happened

On 25 June 2018 the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) announced from Rome that it and Pennsylvania State University had developed and launched Nuru, a talking app using machine learning. It runs offline on a standard Android phone. In FAO's description, a farmer holds the phone next to an infested plant and Nuru confirms whether fall armyworm caused the damage. FAO said that the pest first appeared in Africa in 2016 and spread across sub-Saharan Africa in 2017. It threatened the food security of more than 300 million people. Nuru was to complement FAO's Fall Armyworm Monitoring and Early Warning System (FAMEWS) app. Data validated by national focal points would flow into a global platform. The app was to add Swahili, French and Twi. PlantVillage wrote on 9 July 2018 that in Nuru's first two weeks it had tested the app with scientists in FAO field offices in Ethiopia, Zambia and Zimbabwe and with colleagues in Ghana and Burundi. Farmers in Kenya and Uganda had also used it. PlantVillage wrote that the beta app was underestimating fall armyworm damage, likely because of how the phone was held. Mongabay reported on 26 October 2018 that developers were still working on the fall armyworm version and that FAO planned to link it to FAMEWS by the end of 2018. The New York Times later reported that this fall armyworm tool served as the blueprint for the eLocust3m app of 2020. No source reports a test of the fall armyworm model's accuracy or an outcome for farmers. The published studies of Nuru's accuracy concern cassava.

Details

AI system

Workflow

FAO-Penn State collaboration under FAO's five-year fall armyworm program launched October 2017. Android phones able to run the model offline.

Human oversight

The Food and Agriculture Organization planned that national focal points would validate the data before it entered a global platform. No source reports that this validation took place for Nuru. [1] [2] [3] [4]

AI contribution

The sources document the launch and use of Nuru in seven African countries in its first two weeks. The makers wrote that the beta app underestimated fall armyworm damage. No source evaluates the model's accuracy or reports a farmer's decision that it changed. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Benefits

Duration

The sources on the fall armyworm app cover the four months after the launch on 25 June 2018. Two weeks after the launch, PlantVillage reported use in seven African countries. On 26 October 2018 Mongabay reported that developers were still working on the fall armyworm version. No later source reports continued use. [1] [2] [3] [4]

People and places

Dependencies

Nuru ran on a standard Android phone without an internet connection for the plant assessment. The proposed reporting link to FAMEWS depended on a separate validation and data-transfer process.
