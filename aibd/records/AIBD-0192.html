FAO and Penn State launch Nuru to confirm fall armyworm damage

FAO announced on 25 June 2018 a talking Android app built with PlantVillage to confirm fall armyworm damage offline and feed FAO's FAMEWS; no outcome data in the sources.

ⓘ

What happened

On 25 June 2018 FAO announced from Rome that it and Pennsylvania State University had developed and launched Nuru, a talking app using machine learning. It runs offline on a standard Android phone. A farmer holds the phone next to an infested plant and Nuru confirms whether fall armyworm caused the damage. FAO said the pest first appeared in Africa in 2016 and spread across sub-Saharan Africa in 2017. It threatened the food security of more than 300 million people. Nuru was to complement FAO's Fall Armyworm Monitoring and Early Warning System (FAMEWS) app. Data validated by national focal points would flow into a global platform. It was to add Swahili, French and Twi. The New York Times later reported that this fall armyworm tool served as the blueprint for the eLocust3m app of 2020. The cited sources document the launch and design only.

No evaluation of accuracy or of any outcome for farmers was found.

Purpose. Confirm fall armyworm damage on maize from a phone held next to the plant, offline, and feed reports into FAO's FAMEWS monitoring system.

Since then
Two weeks after release, PlantVillage reported that Nuru's fall armyworm feature had been used with FAO field offices and farmers in seven African countries.So, in these two weeks, we have been testing Nuru with scientists who work in UN FAO field offices in Ethiopia, Zambia, Zimbabwe and with colleagues in Ghana and Burundi. Nuru was also used by farmers in Kenya and Uganda. So, in just two weeks Nuru was used for fall armyworm in 7 African countries! source

Further evidence
