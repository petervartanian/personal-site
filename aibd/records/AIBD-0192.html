What happened

On 25 June 2018 FAO announced from Rome that it and Pennsylvania State University had developed and launched Nuru, a talking app using machine learning. It runs offline on a standard Android phone. A farmer holds the phone next to an infested plant and Nuru confirms whether fall armyworm caused the damage. FAO said the pest first appeared in Africa in 2016 and spread across sub-Saharan Africa in 2017. It threatened the food security of more than 300 million people. Nuru was to complement FAO's Fall Armyworm Monitoring and Early Warning System (FAMEWS) app. Data validated by national focal points would flow into a global platform. It was to add Swahili, French and Twi. The New York Times later reported that this fall armyworm tool served as the blueprint for the eLocust3m app of 2020. The sources on file document the launch and design only.

No evaluation of accuracy or of any outcome for farmers was found.

What it was built to do. Confirm fall armyworm damage on maize from a phone held next to the plant, offline, and feed reports into FAO's FAMEWS monitoring system.

Replication

What produced it. FAO-Penn State collaboration under FAO's five-year fall armyworm program launched October 2017. Android phones able to run the model offline.

What must hold again. Farmers hold the phone next to an infested plant. National focal points validate reports through FAMEWS.
