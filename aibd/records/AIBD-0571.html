New Delhi researchers propose AI rain forecasts to time irrigation

The Indian Agricultural Research Institute proposes AI rainfall forecasts for its automatic basin-irrigation system to skip watering when rain is expected; their saving is unmeasured.

  • Not rated an organization

    Farm operations are intended to avoid unnecessary irrigation using AI rainfall predictions. No measured operational gain from this proposed component is reported.

  • Not rated society

    Reduced water use and more sustainable farming are intended. The extent of savings attributable to the proposed AI component is unassessed.

What happened

The Water Technology Centre at the Indian Agricultural Research Institute (ICAR-IARI) in New Delhi built a basin-irrigation controller that uses soil-moisture sensors and preset moisture thresholds. The use case states that this threshold controller was tested and deployed at the institute's research farm. It names Monalisha Pramanik, Manoj Khanna and P. S. Brahmanand of the Water Technology Centre as contacts. The team proposes using AI rainfall predictions to control the irrigation gates. A forecast of rain for the next day would close the gate. Otherwise, the motor could open it when soil moisture is low. The team plans pilots across crops and climatic zones. Water savings and other benefits from the proposed AI extension remain unmeasured. A cited water-saving figure comes from separate research on the threshold controller.

AI system

Autonomy

The existing controller opens and closes irrigation gates at preset moisture thresholds. The proposed AI extension could prevent watering before forecast rain. [1] [2] [3]

Workflow

The proposed design combines AI rainfall forecasts with soil-moisture readings and irrigation-gate control.
