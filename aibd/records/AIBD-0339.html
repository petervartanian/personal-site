What happened

Kettering General Hospital, a National Health Service (NHS) foundation trust, asked the NHS AI Lab Skunkworks team (NHSX) for support exploring AI for bed management. The Skunkworks funded a 14-week project with Faculty, an applied AI supplier selected through the Home Office's Accelerated Capability Environment (ACE). The team used five years of pseudonymized patient administration data and one to two years of patient-flow data. It built a virtual hospital and a Bayesian model forecasting how many patients with given characteristics would present. It compared greedy allocation, Monte Carlo tree search and reinforcement learning for suggesting beds. Only the greedy agent was integrated into the web interface because of performance issues with the tree search. Reinforcement learning was not developed within the project.

The resulting proof of concept was tested and reviewed by the hospital. A human scheduler still makes the final bed-allocation decision. It was released as open source on the NHSX GitHub pages. The README places it at Technology Readiness Level 4, not intended for deployment without further development and compliance with the UK Medical Device Regulations 2002. The NHS case study (10 December 2021) says the hospital would work with Faculty to bid for funding to develop and operationalize the system. The GOV.UK ACE case study (12 October 2023) lists potential benefits only. No quantified results and no live use are documented, so this is a general benefit. The GitHub README gives a 12-week initiative launched in May 2021, while the NHS and GOV.UK pages say 14 weeks.

What it was built to do. Built to forecast bed demand and suggest bed allocations to the hospital's bed-management team. The proof of concept demonstrated that purpose without a measured outcome.

The system's part

The AI components forecast demand and suggest beds with an explanation, while the scheduler makes the final decision. Only the greedy agent was integrated, and the tool was tested and reviewed by the hospital rather than deployed. All read accounts come from the sponsoring public bodies (NHS AI Lab case study, GOV.UK ACE case study) and the NHSX GitHub README. So this is the deployer side describing its own pilot. No vendor-authored or independent evaluation is among the sources. The NHS page is available only through the Internet Archive because the transform.england.nhs.uk domain no longer resolves.

Replication

What produced it. Five years of pseudonymized patient administration data and one to two years of patient-flow data under an information governance process. A virtual hospital modeled on key wards with a Bayesian demand forecast. A greedy allocation agent integrated into a web interface with explainable suggestions. Skunkworks funding and an ACE-procured supplier for a 14-week build.

What must hold again. Centralized, real-time patient-flow and admissions data across all specialties, which the hospital lacked. Reconfigurability by staff for changes in beds, ward layouts and admission peaks. Compliance with the UK Medical Device Regulations 2002 before any deployment. Funding to develop and operationalize the system beyond the proof of concept.
