Faculty's Kettering bed tool is open-sourced but not deployed

A proof of concept that Faculty built with NHS AI Lab Skunkworks in 2021 forecasts bed demand and suggests beds; Kettering General Hospital tested it, and no source reports live use.

What happened

Kettering General Hospital, a National Health Service (NHS) foundation trust, asked the NHS AI Lab Skunkworks team (NHSX) for support exploring AI for bed management. The Skunkworks funded a project with Faculty, an applied AI supplier selected through the Home Office's Accelerated Capability Environment (ACE). The GitHub README gives a 12-week initiative launched in May 2021, while the NHS and GOV.UK pages say 14 weeks. The team used five years of pseudonymized patient administration data and one to two years of patient-flow data. It built a virtual hospital and a Bayesian model that forecasts how many patients with given characteristics would present. It compared greedy allocation, Monte Carlo tree search and reinforcement learning for suggesting beds. Reinforcement learning was not developed within the project. Only the greedy agent was integrated into the web interface, because of performance issues with the tree search. The integrated greedy method operates independently of the displayed demand forecast. A human scheduler still makes the final decision on bed allocation. In a webinar reported by HTN Health Tech News on 20 September 2021, the hospital's digital director said that the project was halfway through and that the hospital hoped to put the product into day-to-day use. The hospital tested and reviewed the resulting proof of concept. It was released as open source on the NHSX GitHub pages. The README places it at Technology Readiness Level 4 and says that it is not intended for deployment without further development and compliance with the UK Medical Device Regulations 2002. The NHS case study of 10 December 2021 says that the hospital would work with Faculty to bid for funding to develop and operationalize the system. The GOV.UK case study by ACE, dated 12 October 2023, lists potential benefits only. It names integration with hospital systems as a next step after user testing. No quantified results and no live use are documented. As of 29 October 2026, no source reports that the system went into use.

Details

AI system

Configuration

The web interface paired a demand forecast with a separate greedy allocation agent. The allocation suggestions did not use that forecast. [1] [3]

Autonomy

A human scheduler retained the final bed-allocation decision.

Workflow

Five years of pseudonymized patient administration data and one to two years of patient-flow data under an information governance process. A virtual hospital modeled on key wards with a Bayesian demand forecast. A greedy allocation agent integrated into a web interface with explainable suggestions. Skunkworks funding and an ACE-procured supplier for a 14-week build.

Human oversight

A human scheduler makes the final decision on bed allocation. The tool offers bed suggestions with an explanation. The README says that the tool is not intended for deployment without further development and compliance with the UK Medical Device Regulations 2002. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Centralized, real-time patient-flow and admissions data across all specialties, which the hospital lacked. Reconfigurability by staff for changes in beds, ward layouts and admission peaks. Compliance with the UK Medical Device Regulations 2002 before any deployment. Funding to develop and operationalize the system beyond the proof of concept.
