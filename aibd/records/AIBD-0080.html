qXR flags lung nodules on unrelated X-rays; five early cancers confirmed

Qure.ai's qXR flagged lung nodules on chest X-rays taken for other reasons; computed tomography and biopsy confirmed early-stage lung cancer in five adults, and four had surgery.

What happened

CREATE (NCT05817110) was a prospective multicenter study. The deep-learning software qXR (Qure.ai) was adopted for routine use on all chest X-rays across participating centers from April 2023 to December 2024. The parent study's main paper was published on 19 January 2026 by authors who include AstraZeneca and Qure.ai staff. It states that a site radiologist confirmed each qXR flag before enrollment. A case series in Cureus in March 2026 describes five individuals found through the AI pathway, none with respiratory symptoms. The case series names neither the centers nor their cities. Its clinical authors are at Hacettepe University (Ankara, Turkey) and with Aarthi Scans and Sri Ramachandra Medical College and Hospital (Chennai, India). In each case, qXR flagged suspicious pulmonary nodules on X-rays taken for non-respiratory reasons. The patients were a 57-year-old man screened before anti-tumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF) therapy for ulcerative colitis, a 64-year-old man with fever, a 65-year-old woman at a smoking-cessation consultation, and two men aged 35 and 75 at annual health checks. In all five cases the reviewing radiologist or pulmonologist agreed with the AI finding. Computed tomography (CT) and biopsy confirmed early-stage lung cancer in all five. Four underwent curative surgery, two with adjuvant chemotherapy, and one chose chemotherapy alone. The case series does not state whether the original X-ray reports noted the nodules. The trial registry, updated on 19 August 2026, lists the study as active, with completion expected on 6 December 2026.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

QXR screened routine chest radiographs for nodules and flagged suspicious findings on films obtained for non-respiratory reasons.

Workflow

QXR running on routine chest X-rays ordered for non-respiratory indications. A pathway from AI flag to CT or PET-CT and biopsy.

Human oversight

According to the parent study's main paper, a site radiologist confirmed each qXR flag before enrollment. In all five cases a radiologist or pulmonologist agreed with the flag, and clinicians confirmed cancer with computed tomography and biopsy before choosing treatment. [1] [2]

Relation to AI

QXR flagged a nodule on each X-ray ordered for an unrelated reason. A radiologist or pulmonologist then reviewed the flag, agreed with it and recommended low-dose CT, with or without PET-CT; biopsy followed. The report does not say whether radiologists had reported the nodules before the flag. The AI's contribution thus rests on the authors' judgment that the cases would otherwise have been missed or delayed. One of ten authors is a Qure.ai employee and the study was funded by AstraZeneca.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

The report concerns five patients with confirmed early-stage lung cancer: four underwent surgery, and one chose chemotherapy alone.

Reach

Five adults received a diagnosis of early-stage lung cancer, and four of them had curative surgery. The parent study's main paper covers 712 flagged nodules and does not report how many were cancers. [1] [2]

Duration

In its second phase, the parent study follows participants for up to two years after the first computed tomography scan. The trial registry lists 6 December 2026 as the expected completion date. No survival or follow-up data for the five patients have been published. [1] [2]
