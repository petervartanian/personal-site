qXR nodule flags lead to five early-stage lung cancer diagnoses

qXR flagged nodules on X-rays done for other reasons; CT/biopsy confirmed early-stage lung cancer in five adults; four had surgery.

ⓘ

What happened

CREATE (NCT05817110) was a prospective multicenter study. The deep-learning software qXR (Qure.ai) was adopted for routine use on all chest X-rays across participating centers from April 2023 to December 2024. Five cases were found through the AI pathway. The paper names neither the centers nor their cities. Its clinical authors are at Hacettepe University (Ankara, Turkey) and with Aarthi Scans and Sri Ramachandra Medical College and Hospital (Chennai, India). The March 2026 Cureus report describes five individuals, none with respiratory symptoms. In each, qXR flagged suspicious pulmonary nodules on X-rays taken for non-respiratory reasons. They were a 57-year-old man screened before anti-TNF therapy for ulcerative colitis and a 64-year-old man with fever. Also a 65-year-old woman at a smoking-cessation consultation and two men aged 35 and 75 at annual health checks.

Computed tomography (CT) and biopsy confirmed early-stage lung cancer in all five. Four underwent curative surgery, two with adjuvant chemotherapy, and one chose chemotherapy alone. Whether the original X-ray reports noted the nodules is not stated.

Purpose. qXR is built to detect abnormalities including pulmonary nodules on chest X-rays. Flagging incidental nodules is its intended function.

AI’s contribution

qXR's nodule flag on X-rays ordered for unrelated reasons prompted low-dose CT, with or without PET-CT, and biopsy in each case. The report does not say whether radiologists had independently reported the nodules. The AI's contribution thus rests on the authors' judgment that the cases would otherwise have been missed or delayed. One of ten authors is a Qure.ai employee and the study was funded by AstraZeneca.
