BrainGate wireless iBCI lets T5 and T10 use a tablet at home

Two men with tetraplegia used a wireless BrainGate iBCI at home for web browsing, apps and typing, with 24-hour home recording.

ⓘ

What happened

A BrainGate pilot-trial study (Simeral et al.) appeared in IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering in 2021. Authors are at Brown University, Massachusetts General Hospital, Providence VA Medical Center and Stanford University. Two men with tetraplegia from C4 spinal cord injury, T5 (63) and T10 (35), used a 192-electrode intracortical brain-computer interface (iBCI). Wireless transmitters replaced its cables. Sessions were in their homes. A Kalman filter decoder and a linear discriminant classifier converted neural activity into cursor velocity and clicks. Both controlled an unmodified commercial tablet to browse the web and run apps. T5 typed 196 correct characters in 14.6 minutes (13.4 per minute) in Notepad. Neural data were recorded continuously from T10 for 24 hours at home. Calendar dates are not given.

Purpose. Restore computer access and communication for people with paralysis by decoding intended movement from motor cortex

AI’s contribution

Machine-learning decoders (Kalman filter and linear discriminant classifier) are the mechanism that translates intracortical activity into cursor movement and clicks. Wireless hardware enabled home use. The decoded control is what produced the tablet and typing capacity.

Beneficiaries

  • a person

    Two identifiable participants gained tablet control, web browsing and typing at home. Use was within supervised research sessions rather than independent daily use, so the gain is material but not yet life-changing.

  • society

    First human demonstration of wireless broadband iBCI use at home; a step toward practical deployment rather than a population-level change.

Further evidence

Setting. Chronically implanted microelectrode arrays with usable spiking signals. Daily Kalman filter and linear discriminant decoder calibration. Wireless transmitters removing the cable tether.

Conditions. Stable array signal quality years after implantation. Research staff available to set up sessions. Participant retains intention-to-move signals in motor cortex.
