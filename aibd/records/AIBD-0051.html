What happened

A BrainGate pilot-trial study (Simeral et al.) appeared in IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering in 2021. Authors are at Brown University, Massachusetts General Hospital, Providence VA Medical Center and Stanford University. Two men with tetraplegia from C4 spinal cord injury, T5 (63) and T10 (35), used a 192-electrode intracortical brain-computer interface (iBCI). Wireless transmitters replaced its cables. Sessions were in their homes. A Kalman filter decoder and a linear discriminant classifier converted neural activity into cursor velocity and clicks. Both controlled an unmodified commercial tablet to browse the web and run apps. T5 typed 196 correct characters in 14.6 minutes (13.4 per minute) in Notepad. Neural data were recorded continuously from T10 for 24 hours at home. Calendar dates are not given; the publication date is used.

What it was built to do. Restore computer access and communication for people with paralysis by decoding intended movement from motor cortex

The system's part

Machine-learning decoders (Kalman filter and linear discriminant classifier) are the mechanism that translates intracortical activity into cursor movement and clicks. Wireless hardware enabled home use. The decoded control is what produced the tablet and typing capacity.

Replication

What produced it. Chronically implanted microelectrode arrays with usable spiking signals. Daily Kalman filter and linear discriminant decoder calibration. Wireless transmitters removing the cable tether.

What must hold again. Stable array signal quality years after implantation. Research staff available to set up sessions. Participant retains intention-to-move signals in motor cortex.
