Abridge scribe eased exhaustion for UW Health clinicians in a trial

In a UW Health randomized trial of 66 practitioners, the Abridge ambient scribe lowered work exhaustion scores by 0.44 points and cut note time by 0.36 hours a day.

What happened

UW Health, the academic health system of the University of Wisconsin, ran a 24-week stepped-wedge randomized trial of the Abridge ambient AI scribe in ambulatory clinics in Wisconsin and Illinois between 21 August 2024 and 27 March 2025. Sixty-six practitioners were randomly assigned to three six-week sequences for starting the scribe. Of 71,487 notes, 27,092 were drafted with the scribe. Scribe use lowered work exhaustion and interpersonal disengagement on the Stanford Professional Fulfillment Index by 0.44 points on a five-point scale (95% confidence interval 0.25 to 0.62). Time spent on notes fell by 0.36 hours a day. The rise in professional fulfillment did not reach the trial's significance threshold. The drop in work outside work lost significance after the authors removed extreme values. The authors report that note quality scores stayed high and that billing diagnostic codes improved. UW Health bought the licenses under a standard vendor agreement, and Abridge provided no funding.

Details

AI system

Workflow

The scribe ran on practitioners' Apple devices through Epic Haiku. Practitioners had at least 20 ambulatory encounters a week.

Measurement

Measurement

Stanford Professional Fulfillment Index; EHR audit logs for note time; PDSQI-9 note quality; coder review of billing codes. [2]

Setting and sample

66 practitioners in UW Health ambulatory clinics in Wisconsin and Illinois; 71,487 notes. [2]

Measures

Work exhaustion/interpersonal disengagement score; hours a day on notes. [2]

Results

-0.44 points exhaustion (95% CI -0.62 to -0.25); -0.36 hours a day on notes (95% CI -0.55 to -0.17). [2]

Additional value from AI

0.36 fewer hours a day on notes. [2]

Limitations

Small sample, one health system, survey outcomes, fulfillment change not significant, no patient-reported outcomes. [2]

Denominators

38% of notes generated with the scribe. [2]

Materials for replication

ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06517082; statistical analysis plan and code in a public Git repository. [2]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Practitioners adopt the scribe for a large share of notes. Note quality holds up under review.
