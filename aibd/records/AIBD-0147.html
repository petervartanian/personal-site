Taichung's AI smoke cameras flag a man who kept burning roadside waste

The Taichung Environmental Protection Bureau's AI smoke-recognition system flagged roadside waste burning in Dadu District, and police identified a 74-year-old repeat burner, who faces a fine.

  • 2 society

    The bureau says that the case removed “a potential pollution source threatening air quality” for residents near Hugan Road in Dadu District. Chinese Television System and Public Television Service describe repeated burns at the same spot, next to weeds, in February, July and August 2025. No exposure figures or air-quality measurements are reported. The benefit is therefore material but local and unquantified.

  • 1 an organization

    The bureau and the Wuri Precinct police solved a case of repeated burning that had stalled because the motorcycle plate was too faded to read. The system's alert sent inspectors to the site, and its video supplied the core evidence. The bureau presents the case as proof that its “smart technology city” enforcement works.

What happened

In March 2025 Taichung's Environmental Protection Bureau received a complaint about smoke from a dumping spot beside Hugan Road, Dadu District. The bureau then installed its AI smoke-recognition system, air micro-sensors and cameras at the site. The Central News Agency reports that shortly after 08:00 on 4 July 2025 the instruments flagged abnormal air quality. Inspectors found dumped waste burning. The system recorded the same man in July and August. His motorcycle plate was too faded to read. So inspectors collected neighbors' closed-circuit television and dashcam footage. A Wuri Precinct officer recognized a 74-year-old man surnamed Zhao, who confessed. On 13 September 2025 the bureau announced a fine of up to NT$100,000 under the Air Pollution Control Act. It asked the land manager to clear the site. The bureau told Public Television Service that the site already had cameras, which may have had blind spots, and that the dumped waste had built up over a long time. On 26 February 2026 the United Daily News reported the bureau's own figures for the whole program. Open-burning alerts had fallen 59.7 percent and complaints had fallen 43.4 percent. By August 2026 the bureau reported nearly 170 AI smoke-detection systems across Taichung.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Cameras and air-quality sensors monitored a known dumping and burning hotspot. Alerts directed inspectors to a fresh incident, but the system could not read the faded motorcycle plate.

Workflow

Fixed AI smoke-recognition cameras and air micro-sensors installed at a known open-burning hotspot after a public complaint, with automatic alerts to inspectors. Inspectors able to reach the site while the fire burned. They could assemble third-party CCTV and dashcam footage when its video could not read the faded plate. Local police (Wuri Precinct Dadu Station) familiar enough with residents to match the recorded build, clothing and gait to a known 74-year-old resident. A legal basis for penalties (Air Pollution Control Act Article 32) and a land manager that could be asked to clear the dump site.

Human oversight

Inspectors verified the burning on site. Residents supplied additional footage, and a police officer identified the suspect, who reportedly admitted the acts.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The AI alert must reach staff who can respond while the fire is burning and who have the mandate to enter the site. Identification must not rest on the camera alone. Plate recognition failed here and human evidence-gathering closed the case. The offender must be identifiable and reachable. A stranger with no local ties would likely not have been recognized.

Sector

Purpose

The bureau introduced AI smoke-recognition cameras in 2022 to recognize abnormal smoke around the clock at open-burning hotspots. They send automatic alerts so that inspectors can locate and stop pollution sources and reduce fire risk. Detecting and recording this roadside burning was that intended use.
