ChatGPT's first-aid advice may have saved a teen in Assiut

A Facebook post of 24 May 2025 credits ChatGPT with first aid. Assiut Faculty of Medicine dean Alaa Attia says that the youth had no phosphine symptoms and said he threw the pill into water.

ⓘ

AI system

System

ChatGPT (OpenAI), described in the post as 'an advanced AI model and a limited-use trial version of GPT-5'

Model

A trial version of GPT-5, according to the cousin’s post.

Workflow

A layperson prompting ChatGPT for first aid during a suspected aluminum phosphide ingestion. Paraffin oil bought from a pharmacy and given before and, by the cousin's account, during hospital care.

Human oversight

Not assessed

Relation to AI

ChatGPT's output (advice to give paraffin oil, then nine bottles in turn with breathing exercises) is documented only by the cousin's own post. The chat transcripts were not published (“too many to publish all”, per Masrawy's summary of the post). Dean Alaa Attia contradicts its effect: he says that the patient had no clinical signs of phosphine poisoning and denied swallowing the pill. Dr Nermin Hamdi's report that Assiut University denied the case involved the grain pill also contradicts it. Dr Mahmoud Amr says that oil is a recognized first aid but not a cure. Whether the hospital followed any of the AI's instructions is unsupported by any source other than the post.

Conditions for replication

That the pill was swallowed at all, which the treating dean says the patient denied. Clinical confirmation of phosphine poisoning and of any effect of the oil. Assiut's paraffin-oil-plus-acetylcysteine protocol is described by the dean as experimental and not officially adopted.

Later findings

25 May 2025. The next day the dean of Assiut University's Faculty of Medicine told Masrawy that the youth showed no clinical signs of phosphine poisoning and had categorically denied swallowing the pill, and on 26 May a toxicologist told Al Jazeera Mubasher that the claim that paraffin oil rescues such a case has no scientific basis.
