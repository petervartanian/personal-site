Hospital disputes that ChatGPT helped save a teenager from poisoning

A relative credited ChatGPT's paraffin-oil advice with saving a teenager from a grain pill, but the medical dean at Assiut University said that the teenager showed no signs of poisoning.

What happened

Islam Adel, a commercial-control inspector, wrote a Facebook post at 1 a.m. after “24 hours” had passed since his cousin Mohamed Yousry (17) swallowed part of a “grain pill” (aluminum phosphide). The ingestion date is not fixed by any source. The pill came from samples that Adel had seized and kept at the family's old house. The cousin took it after a quarrel with his father. Adel writes that the Abnoub central hospital refused the case. He writes that within minutes he asked ChatGPT for the best course. It advised paraffin oil to coat the pill and limit phosphine release. The youth drank a bottle from a pharmacy before they went to the poison reception of Assiut University Hospital. Doctors there said that there was no treatment protocol and that the case would die. He then gave instructions from “an advanced AI model and a limited-use trial version of GPT-5”. Nine bottles of paraffin oil were given in turn with breathing exercises. The post says that the youth stayed fully conscious and without acute toxic symptoms through the night, and that a blood test for mitochondrial poisoning came back negative. The cousin stayed conscious, vomited for hours and was discharged stable. El Balad reproduces the post (24 May 2025, 12:39). Dostor (24 May 2025, 22:40) reports the post and the reply of Dr. Alaa Attia, dean of the Faculty of Medicine at Assiut University. He said that precise medical examinations showed no signs or symptoms of swallowing the pill. The youth denied taking it and admitted throwing it into a water basin. The dean added that the hospital is researching paraffin oil with acetylcysteine as an adjunct. The protocol is experimental and not officially adopted. Masrawy (25 May 2025) quotes the dean as saying that the youth, about 17, showed no clinical symptoms of phosphine poisoning. The team questioned him, and he categorically denied swallowing the pill. He cited a family dispute over travel to Italy. Dostor (25 May 2025) adds the views of the toxicologist Dr. Mahmoud Amr, founder of the National Poison Center. He says that oils are a necessary first aid in grain-pill poisoning. Al Jazeera Mubasher (26 May 2025) quotes Amr as saying that the claim that paraffin oil rescues such a case “has no scientific basis”. It quotes Dr. Nermin Hamdi, a toxicologist at Cairo University, as saying that Assiut University denied that the case involved the grain pill. Hamdi adds that giving oil counts only as first aid and does not treat the poisoning. The OECD Monitor lists 13 syndications. Al Arabiya's syndication is not available. A 2024 meta-analysis of randomized trials found that paraffin-oil gastric lavage in hospital lowers mortality from aluminum phosphide poisoning, on low-quality evidence.

Details

AI system

Model

A trial version of GPT-5, according to the cousin’s post.

Editor’s note. The identification comes from the cousin’s post. The existing sources provide no model output or vendor confirmation.

Role

The chatbot supplied treatment suggestions, according to the patient’s cousin.

Inputs and outputs

The cousin reported asking for emergency guidance and receiving suggestions about paraffin oil. No complete conversation transcript is available to establish the response in context.

Workflow

A layperson prompting ChatGPT for first aid during a suspected aluminum phosphide ingestion. Paraffin oil bought from a pharmacy and given before and, by the cousin's account, during hospital care.

Human oversight

The treating hospital assessed the teenager directly and reported no signs of phosphine poisoning, contradicting the cousin’s interpretation of the chatbot advice.

Benefits

Beneficiaries

No verified treatment benefit is established for the teenager. His stable discharge does not demonstrate that the advice treated aluminum phosphide poisoning.

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

That the pill was swallowed at all, which the treating dean says the patient denied. Clinical confirmation of phosphine poisoning and of any effect of the oil. Assiut's paraffin-oil-plus-acetylcysteine protocol is described by the dean as experimental and not officially adopted.
