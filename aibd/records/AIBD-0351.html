Phone data helps halve GiveDirectly's cost per added enrollee in Malawi

GiveDirectly's MobileAid model scored phone records to choose 3,052 of 12,800 recipients in a Malawi pilot, and the hybrid approach halved the cost per added enrollee compared with in-person surveys.

  • Not rated a person

    Recipients received cash, but no source reports a gain for recipients that the phone-data targeting itself produced.

  • 1 an organization

    The World Bank paper, drawing on GiveDirectly's 2023 synthesis, reports that the hybrid approach cost about half as much per added enrollee as in-person surveys alone, a cost saving for the program.

The system’s part
In the Khongoni pilot a machine-learning model scored phone records to estimate consumption, and people below the cutoff could enroll by USSD; 3,052 of the 12,800 recipients joined this way, within 56 hours of ID validation. GiveDirectly staff then surveyed the same villages door to door to enroll people the phone method missed. The World Bank authors credit the hybrid with halving the variable enrollment cost. They also report GiveDirectly's estimates of 17% targeting error for the model against 13% for the in-person test, and they judge that the model did little better than random choice at the 80% cutoff.
Limits
The halving applies to the variable cost per added enrollee, so it does not show a halving of total program cost. The share enrolled by phone does not show targeting accuracy or recipient welfare, and the World Bank authors judge that the model did little better than random choice at the 80% cutoff. No independent evaluation of the pilot is cited.
Costs and harms
Lower enrollment cost came with less accurate targeting than in-person proxy-means tests and dependence on telecom access.
Later
Nothing later is known. GiveDirectly says that it is now working with the Government of Malawi to fill gaps in the urban social registry with phone metadata. As of 29 October 2026 its research page lists the related urban study by Emily Aiken, Joshua Blumenstock and CEGA as still under way, with no results.

What happened

GiveDirectly's MobileAid approach uses machine learning on mobile-phone usage patterns to identify people in need and pay them by mobile money. GiveDirectly says that it has evaluated the method across four countries since 2021 (Togo, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Malawi and Bangladesh). In Malawi it ran a Canva-funded pilot in Khongoni, in rural Lilongwe, and GiveDirectly says that the pilot launched in March 2022. A World Bank discussion paper by Yuko Okamura, Tim Ohlenburg and Emil Tesliuc (March 2024) describes the pilot. Drawing on GiveDirectly's internal data, the authors report that 3,052 of 12,800 recipients were chosen with phone data and that the hybrid approach cost about half as much per added enrollee as in-person surveys alone. They also judge that the phone model did only slightly better than random choice when most people were eligible. GiveDirectly's 27 January 2025 post (Nirali Desai and Vera Lummis) describes interviews with 67 recipients across Malawi and Kenya in February and October 2024. The interviews, which name Mzuzu, asked how to explain targeting by AI and machine learning. The authors found that scenario-based explanations were the most understandable. GiveDirectly's 4 September 2025 post (Vera Lummis) says that in Malawi it ran a hybrid program that identified and enrolled 35% of recipients via phone metadata and 65% in person. That share differs from the World Bank count of 3,052 of 12,800 recipients. The post says that the hybrid halved the cost of identifying and enrolling each additional person compared with a fully in-person operation, and it links that claim to the World Bank paper. GiveDirectly says that it is working with the Government of Malawi to fill gaps in the urban social registry with phone metadata. A study with the Government of Malawi, U.C. Berkeley and U.C. San Diego is expected to report in 2026. It asks whether phone metadata can flag people whose poverty deepens after a shock.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

Mobile-phone usage metadata was used to estimate need, identify some recipients and support enrollment for mobile-money transfers.

Workflow

Access to mobile network operator metadata for the target population under a data agreement. A machine-learning model relating phone usage patterns to poverty, trained on survey data. Mobile-money rails to enroll and pay identified people. An in-person survey component (65% of enrollments) alongside the phone-metadata component.

Human oversight

GiveDirectly staff surveyed the same villages door to door to enroll people whom the phone method missed. In GiveDirectly's interviews, recipients found scenario-based explanations of the targeting the most understandable. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Benefits

Reach

The pilot paid 12,800 recipients, and 3,052 of them were chosen with phone data. GiveDirectly's 2025 post puts the share enrolled via phone metadata at 35%, which differs from that count. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Duration

The cost figure describes a single pilot that launched in March 2022. On 4 September 2025, GiveDirectly said that it was working with the Government of Malawi on the urban social registry. As of 29 October 2026, no result of that work is reported. [1] [2] [3] [4] [5]

Measurement

Measurement

Comparison of three targeting methods (saturation, in-person proxy-means test, tele-targeting plus in-person survey) applied in different areas, with model accuracy checked against a 2,000-household consumption module; figures from GiveDirectly's 2023 internal synthesis. [4]

Setting and sample

Canva-funded GiveDirectly pilot in TA Khongoni, rural Lilongwe, Malawi; 12,800 recipients in 2022, 3,052 chosen by tele-targeting; about 19,400 USSD registrants. [4]

Measures

Variable cost per person enrolled; time from registration to enrollment; targeting error. [4]

Results

Variable cost per enrollee about 50% of in-person only; enrollment within 56 hours of ID validation; targeting error 17% for tele-targeting against 13% for the in-person test. [4]

Additional value from AI

The model cut enrollment cost and time but was less accurate than the in-person test and only slightly better than random choice at the 80% cutoff. [4]

Limitations

Data come from the deployer's internal synthesis; no confidence intervals; about 35.8% of households owned a phone and phone owners were about 20% wealthier; high fixed cost for data agreements and ground-truth surveys. [4]

Denominators

One enrollment round in Phase 1 of the pilot, 2022. [4]

Materials for replication

GiveDirectly 2023 internal slides 'Internal Learning Synthesis: Malawi Khongoni Pilot-Teletargeting Retrospective' (not public). [4]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

Telecom partnership and data-sharing agreement in place (GiveDirectly cites 6–18 months set-up). Phone ownership and usage among the poor population. Recipients' understanding and consent, which GiveDirectly found requires scenario-based explanation. Model accuracy acceptable for the program (GiveDirectly reports phone metadata below in-person proxy-means tests).
