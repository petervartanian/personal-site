GiveDirectly's MobileAid may have halved enrollment cost in Malawi

GiveDirectly posts (2024–2025) describe phone-metadata AI/ML targeting in Malawi: 35% of recipients enrolled via phone data, cost per enrolee halved; no date, count or independent source.

ⓘ

What happened

GiveDirectly's MobileAid approach uses machine learning (ML) on mobile-phone usage patterns to identify people in need and pay them by mobile money. Its 4 September 2025 post (Vera Lummis) says it has evaluated the method across four countries since 2021 (Togo, DRC, Malawi and Bangladesh). In Malawi it ran a hybrid program identifying and enrolling 35% of recipients via phone metadata and 65% in person. It says this halved the cost of identifying and enrolling each additional person compared with a fully in-person operation. It is working with the Government of Malawi to fill gaps in the urban social registry with phone metadata. A study with the Government of Malawi, U.C. Berkeley and U.C. San Diego is expected to report in 2026. It asks whether phone metadata can flag people whose poverty deepens after a shock.

Its 27 January 2025 post (Nirali Desai and Vera Lummis) describes interviews with 67 recipients across Malawi and Kenya in February and October 2024. The interviews, naming Mzuzu, asked how to explain AI/ML targeting. It finds scenario-based explanations most understandable. No cited source gives the Malawi program's dates, location, number of recipients or an independent evaluation.

Purpose. Identify people in poverty from mobile-phone metadata with machine learning so that cash can be delivered faster and more cheaply. The claimed benefit (cheaper, faster enrollment) is that purpose.

AI’s contribution

Only the maker's account is among the sources. GiveDirectly says machine learning on phone metadata identified and enrolled 35% of recipients in a Malawi hybrid program. It says this halved the cost per additional enrolee. It gives no date, place, recipient count or accuracy for Malawi. The Government of Malawi, U.C. Berkeley and U.C. San Diego study is described as pending (findings expected in 2026). Likewise, the explain-AI page names no deployment, date or outcome.

Further evidence

Setting. Access to mobile network operator metadata for the target population under a data agreement. A machine-learning model relating phone usage patterns to poverty, trained on survey data. Mobile-money rails to enroll and pay identified people. An in-person survey component (65% of enrollments) alongside the phone-metadata component.

Conditions. Telecom partnership and data-sharing agreement in place (GiveDirectly cites 6–18 months set-up). Phone ownership and usage among the poor population. Recipients' understanding and consent, which GiveDirectly found requires scenario-based explanation. Model accuracy acceptable for the program (GiveDirectly reports phone metadata below in-person proxy-means tests).
