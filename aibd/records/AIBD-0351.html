GiveDirectly says MobileAid halves enrolment cost in Malawi

35% of recipients enrolled via phone data, cost per enrolee halved

GiveDirectly posts (2024-2025) describe phone-metadata AI/ML targeting in Malawi: 35% of recipients enrolled via phone data, cost per enrolee halved; no date, count or independent source.

What happened

GiveDirectly's MobileAid approach uses machine learning on mobile-phone usage patterns to identify people in need and pay them by mobile money. Its 4 September 2025 post (Vera Lummis) states that it has evaluated the method across four countries since 2021 (Togo, DRC, Malawi and Bangladesh), that in Malawi it ran a hybrid programme identifying and enrolling 35% of recipients via phone metadata and 65% in person, that this halved the cost of identifying and enrolling each additional person compared with a fully in-person operation, that it is working with the Government of Malawi to fill gaps in the urban social registry with phone metadata, and that a study with the Government of Malawi, U.C. Berkeley and U.C. San Diego on whether phone metadata can flag people whose poverty deepens after a shock is expected to report in 2026. Its 27 January 2025 post (Nirali Desai and Vera Lummis) describes interviews with 67 recipients across Malawi and Kenya in February and October 2024 on how to explain the AI/ML targeting, naming Mzuzu, and finds scenario-based explanations most understandable. No fetched source gives the Malawi programme's dates, location, number of recipients or an independent evaluation, so the date observed is the MobileAid post's publication date.

What it was built to do. Identify people in poverty from mobile-phone metadata with machine learning so that cash can be delivered faster and more cheaply; the claimed benefit (cheaper, faster enrolment) is that purpose.

The system's part

Only the maker's account was fetched. GiveDirectly states that machine learning on phone metadata identified and enrolled 35% of recipients in a Malawi hybrid programme and halved the cost per additional enrolee; it gives no date, place, recipient count or accuracy for Malawi, and the Government of Malawi, U.C. Berkeley and U.C. San Diego study is described as pending (findings expected in 2026). The earlier verifier found the same: the explain-AI page names no deployment, date or outcome.

Replication

What produced it
  • Access to mobile network operator metadata for the target population under a data agreement
  • A machine-learning model relating phone usage patterns to poverty, trained on survey data
  • Mobile-money rails to enrol and pay identified people
  • An in-person survey component (65% of enrolments) alongside the phone-metadata component
What must hold again
  • Telecom partnership and data-sharing agreement in place (GiveDirectly cites 6-18 months set-up)
  • Phone ownership and usage among the poor population
  • Recipients' understanding and consent, which GiveDirectly found requires scenario-based explanation
  • Model accuracy acceptable for the programme (GiveDirectly reports phone metadata below in-person proxy-means tests)
