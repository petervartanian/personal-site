What happened

Officers of the Seyhan District Police Directorate in Adana, Turkey, stopped a suspicious man during a street check in the Sucuzade neighborhood. He said he had no identity document and, by DHA's account, gave contradictory statements. So the district's Crime Prevention and Investigation Bureau took him to the station for fingerprint and facial recognition checks. The facial recognition system identified him as Süleyman Abül, 38, a fugitive convict wanted to serve 10 years, 1 month and 10 days for robbery. İHA and DHA published this in the early hours of 4 March 2025 without stating the day of the stop. The OECD Monitor dates it 3 March. DHA said he would go to prison once processing was done.

What it was built to do. Police facial recognition systems exist to identify people against identity and wanted-person records. Matching an unidentified detainee to a fugitive convict is that function, not a side effect.

The system's part

The İHA and DHA reports credit the facial recognition system with the identification. The man produced no ID, and the match against police records returned his name and outstanding sentence. DHA's version says he was taken in for both fingerprint and facial recognition checks, so fingerprints might have identified him regardless. What the system is attested to have contributed is the identification itself, not time saved. The reports name neither the system nor its developer and give no match score. No official is quoted by name.

Replication

No attempt to repeat it has been recorded.

What produced it. A street identity check in which the detainee could not or would not produce identification and gave contradictory answers. A facial recognition system at the station linked to records that hold wanted fugitives' photographs and outstanding sentences.

What must hold again. The face match must be confirmed against identity records before anyone is handed to prison. The reports describe processing continuing after the match. Fingerprint checks were available in parallel, so the system's contribution is the identification, not a capability police otherwise lacked.
