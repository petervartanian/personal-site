Facial recognition in Adana identifies a wanted robbery convict

A police facial recognition system, which no report names, identified a man stopped in Adana without identification as a robbery convict wanted to serve a 10-year sentence.

  • 2 an organization

    The Seyhan unit established at the station that a man who said that he had no identity document and gave contradictory statements was a fugitive convict. He had a robbery sentence of 10 years, 1 month and 10 days outstanding, and could go to prison. The gain was material and confined to a single case for one police unit.

  • 1 society

    One convicted robber who had evaded a long sentence was returned to custody. The reports give no evidence of any further harm prevented, so the public benefit is small and diffuse.

The system’s part
The İHA and DHA reports credit the facial recognition system with the identification. The man produced no ID, and the match against police records returned his name and outstanding sentence. DHA's version says he was taken in for both fingerprint and facial recognition checks, so fingerprints might have identified him regardless. What the system is attested to have contributed is the identification itself, not time saved. The reports name neither the system nor its developer and give no match score. No official is quoted by name.
Limits
The reports credit facial recognition with an identification but also describe fingerprint checks. They do not establish the match score, false-match risk, time saved, final legal processing, or crime prevented. Identification and public-safety benefit are different claims.
Costs and harms
No source reports a cost or harm in this case. The reports give no match score. The linked incident from Buenos Aires describes false matches that reportedly led to wrongful stops. These reports show no such error here.

Nothing later is known. No report later than the 4 March 2025 agency stories was found; they say only that he would be handed to prison after processing.

What happened

Officers of the Seyhan District Police Directorate in Adana, Turkey, stopped a suspicious man during a street check in the Sucuzade neighborhood. He said that he had no identity document and, by the account of the Demirören News Agency (DHA), gave contradictory statements. So the district's Crime Prevention and Investigation Bureau took him to the station for fingerprint and facial recognition checks. The facial recognition system identified him as Süleyman Abül, 38, a fugitive convict wanted to serve 10 years, 1 month and 10 days for robbery. DHA said that he would go to prison once processing was done. The İhlas News Agency (İHA) and DHA published this in the early hours of 4 March 2025 without stating the day of the stop. The OECD Monitor dates the case to 3 March.

Details

AI system

Inputs and outputs

The station's facial recognition system compared the detained man's face with police identity records and returned a wanted convict's identity.

Workflow

A street identity check in which the detainee could not or would not produce identification and gave contradictory answers. A facial recognition system at the station linked to records that hold wanted fugitives' photographs and outstanding sentences.

Human oversight

Police officers stopped the man, took him to the station, and continued processing after the facial match.

Benefits

Reach

No source counts beneficiaries. The direct gain went to one police unit, which identified one fugitive convict. The reports give no evidence of any further harm prevented. [1] [2] [3]

Follow-up

Conditions for replication

The face match must be confirmed against identity records before anyone is handed to prison. The reports describe processing continuing after the match. Fingerprint checks were available in parallel, so the system's contribution is the identification, not a capability police otherwise lacked.

Evidence

Original evidence

The three reports relay two news agency stories. Habertürk and Haberler.com carry the story of the İhlas News Agency (İHA), and NTV carries the story of the Demirören News Agency (DHA). No report quotes an official by name. [1] [2] [3]
