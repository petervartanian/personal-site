Method

Admission

A record is admitted when a source reports that an AI system produced a beneficial impact. Nothing is discarded for weak evidence; the strength of the evidence is recorded as confidence and can be filtered on. Whether the benefit was what the system was built to do is recorded on every record and is not a condition of entry.

Kinds of event

A record describes one of four kinds of event. A benefit created or restored: an advantageous real-world change, with an identifiable system, a real deployment, an identifiable beneficiary and an observed outcome. A specific harm prevented: a dangerous pathway was operating, the intervention occurred, and the evidence supports that it changed what happened. A protective response whose prevention is unproven: the safeguard acted, but no defensible counterfactual shows that harm would otherwise have occurred. A general benefit without an event: launches, simulations, accuracy studies, aggregate claims and cohorts without an individually attributable changed outcome; these are kept in the evidence base and are not placed on the depth scale.

Depth

Each stakeholder group that benefited is rated from -1 to -5. The number is the depth of the benefit for that group, and every rating carries its reason in full, so that a reader can see the judgment and not only the number. A group may be left unrated. Every rating is checked against its sources in a verification sweep, and any correction is recorded in the record's revisions.

The system's part

What the system contributed to the benefit is assessed separately from whether the benefit occurred, and may be left unrated. A system that was present is not thereby responsible.

Evidence and confidence

Every record cites its sources, each marked with its kind and with who wrote it: the system's makers, its deployer, the beneficiary, or someone independent of all three. Confidence is computed from them: high when an independent peer-reviewed study, case report, regulatory or court document or report supports the record; medium when the independent material is a news report, preprint or dataset, or when the makers', deployer's or beneficiary's own account is a peer-reviewed study, case report or official report; low when the only sources are press releases, announcements or other material from the makers, the deployer or the beneficiary. A registry label is one source among others.

Briefs

Some records carry a brief from Peter H. Vartanian that distils the evidence and names its gaps, so that a reader deciding whether to act on a case can see what is established and what is not. The date of each brief is in the record's revisions.

Replication

Every record states which conditions produced the benefit and which of them would have to hold again. Attempts to make the benefit happen again are recorded with their setting, model version, and outcome, and drawn on the record's line of time. An outcome that nobody followed up is shown as unknown.

Paired incidents

Where an incident database records a comparable harm to the same stakeholder group, the record links to it, so that harm and benefit can be read on one axis.

Not admitted

Candidates left out are duplicates of a record, events in which no AI system took part, topics with no case to record, and harms. Each is listed on the Not admitted page with the reason, and the verifiers' full outputs are kept in the repository under data/review. Weak evidence does not keep a candidate out; it lowers the record's confidence.

Coverage

Counts on this site describe the database's coverage. They do not measure how beneficial AI is, and a trend in records is a trend in reporting.

Versions

Each record carries a version and a revision history that names the source and the editor of every change. Cite the version.
